*After her daughter's overdose, a woman begins collecting electrical cords from demolished buildings, convinced their hum contains messages, while the city collapses around her in ways she no longer notices.*

Rachel found the first cord in the Target where she used to work. Electric blue. The thick kind they use for industrial equipment. The store was closing—all of them were except Walmart—and they let employees take things.

She took the cord.

At home, she plugged it into nothing. Just held the ends. Listened to it somehow. Could hear something. Not with her ears.

Pearl had been dead six weeks. Overdose. Blue lips when Rachel found her. Same blue as the cord, which couldn't be coincidence.

Started collecting them. Blue cords only. From Circuit City (demolished). RadioShack (long gone). The Sears where her mother had worked. Each cord hummed different. E flat. C sharp. Making chords out of cords. She wrote this down on receipts. Kept every receipt. Evidence of what, she didn't know.

"Mom, you need to eat," her son Michael said. When had he gotten so tall? When had his voice changed? She'd missed things. Been collecting cords while he became someone else.

"I'm documenting," she said.

"Documenting what?"

She showed him the notebook. Hundreds of receipts taped in. Drawings of cord patterns. Musical notation she didn't understand but wrote anyway.

"This is crazy."

"No. This is Paraploo."

The word just came out. Made no sense. Made perfect sense. The sound the cords made when she held them all at once.

Michael left. Went to his father's. She kept collecting.

The city was failing around her. First the regional bank. Then the Ford plant. Then everything except Walmart, always except Walmart. But she only noticed the buildings after they emptied. That's when the cords became available.

Found twelve in the abandoned medical complex where Pearl had been born. Cerulean. Azure. Midnight. Each a different blue, each humming Pearl's name differently.

Started weaving them. Not basketry. More like—she didn't know. Like the pattern behind patterns. Like if you could see electricity's skeleton. Her living room filled with it. This structure of blue cords going nowhere, conducting nothing, singing silently.

The eviction notice came. She taped it in the notebook. More evidence. Everything was evidence now.

"You can't live like this," the social worker said. Young woman. Clean clothes. Looked like Pearl would have looked if Pearl hadn't found heroin, hadn't found that specific blue that kills.

"I'm not living. I'm collecting."

"Collecting what?"

Rachel gestured at the cords. Hundreds now. The room looked like the inside of a computer that had learned to grieve.

"This is hoarding."

"This is music."

The social worker left. Everyone left eventually. Except the cords.

She found one at the demolished plaza—thick as her wrist, the kind they use for those mansion servant robots that rich people bought before everyone realized robots were just another thing to break. The cord had fake skin around it. Realistic. Disturbing. She peeled it off in strips. Saved those too.

Started hearing news through the cords somehow. The mayor indicted. The hospital closing. Her son getting into college (how? when?). All of it humming through blue plastic, making sense in ways that sense never had.

"We at Paraploo are just collecting things right now," she told the reporter who came. Someone had called about the crazy woman with the cords. Human interest story.

"We?"

Rachel looked around. Just her. But also Pearl in every blue cord. Also everyone who'd been laid off from the stores she salvaged from. Also the robots whose skin she'd peeled. Also.

"We," she confirmed.

The reporter took photos. Would run them with the headline "Local Woman's Unique Grief Response" or something equally wrong.

Rachel kept collecting. Even after the power was cut. Even after the house was condemned. The cords didn't need electricity to sing. They were the electricity. They were what happened to power after it died.

Found the last one in Pearl's room. Had been there all along. Thin. Child-sized. From some toy that sang songs, taught numbers, promised futures that never came.

Plugged it into the weave. Everything connected now. The whole room humming with it. The sound of:
- Pearl at three, electrocuted by joy
- Pearl at thirteen, sparking with possibility  
- Pearl at twenty-three, blue lips, no pulse, the electricity leaving

Rachel sat in the center of her collection. Receipts papering the walls—evidence of every transaction that led here. Cords weaving through everything, the pattern finally visible.

Not a pattern.
A chord.
Electric blue.
Singing what couldn't be said.
Paraploo.

The demolition crew found her like that. Said she was humming. Same frequency as the cords. Said the whole room sounded like something breaking and being born at the same time.

They had to cut her out. The cords had grown into her somehow. Or she'd grown into them. Hard to tell. The newspapers called it "tragic." The internet called it "art." 

Michael called it mom.

She's in a facility now. Still collecting receipts. Still humming in E flat—Pearl's last breath, the note everything makes when it stops.

Still saying "Paraploo" when asked what she's doing.
Still not wrong.

*What we collect while ignoring everything else becomes the everything we ignored, humming with the specific frequency of our loss, making songs from the cords that connected nothing to nothing until suddenly they connected everything to everything, too late to matter, too late to stop, exactly on time.*
