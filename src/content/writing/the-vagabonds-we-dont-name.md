## Lead-in

There are practices we keep without knowing we keep them—the way we lower our voices when strangers pass our windows, how we set down memories instead of losing them, the careful art of not participating in every goodbye that moves through our days. Four people in four cities have been practicing this same unnamed protection, creating space where experience can steep untouched by the weight of remembering, maintaining their continuous thread while the world's interruptions drift by like smoke through fingers that refuse to close.

---

## The Story

Kerren knows the difference between forgetting and trying not to remember. Their hands understand it—forgetting is when the stone slips from your grip into Lake Superior's depth. Trying not to remember is setting the stone on the shore, walking away, letting it become what it becomes without your story attached.

*Morning gulls. Not calling. Conversing.*

They're at their window in Grand Marais again. Always again. But not the same—they're trying not to remember yesterday's window, yesterday's lake. Each morning arrives unjudged by the accumulation of recalled interpretations.

*

In San Diego, Coline tastes the distinction. Forgetting is burnt tongue, loss of flavor. Trying not to remember is choosing water over wine, letting the palate clear, creating space where new recognition can occur without being immediately colonized by old patterns.

She whispers while making breakfast. Has always whispered during morning preparations, though she lives alone.

No. Not alone. Vagabonds pass through—thoughts, people, moments in motion toward elsewhere.

*Neighbor's door. Soft close. Footsteps fading.*

*

Bastien's fingers find texture without naming it. Wool but not-wool. Memory but not-memory. In Quebec, the early light through his window catches dust motes that move like vagabonds—present but uncommitted, visible but untouchable.

He practices a kind of respectful withholding. Not silence exactly, but a lowering of voice that acknowledges presence without engagement.

The fabric in his hands could remember sheep, pasture, rain. Instead, he lets it forget forward. Not forget—release. Not release but not not-release.

*

*Ice releasing from eaves. Small surrenders.*

*

Copenhagen's morning brings Elias their usual not-usual symphony. The canal makes sounds that refuse to repeat, though water knows only a few ways to move. They're learning to not remember yesterday's music, to let each wave arrive fresh.

Beings and materials can only become something new when we stop insisting they remain what we remember them as.

They think this, then let the thought go. Set it down like Kerren's stones.

*

Something passes through all four cities simultaneously. Not something—someone. Not someone—some movement of attention that has the quality of a person without being one.

A vagabond made of morning light, or made of the spaces between morning light.

Kerren whispers: "Not today."

They're not refusing. They're creating space. When we try not to remember, we allow experience to steep in its own time, to transform without our reconstruction of what it was or should have been.

*

Coline feels the presence like a flavor she won't name. Her whispered conversation with the morning continues, uninterrupted. She's protecting the continuous from hello and goodbye holes that passing presences try to tear in the fabric of ongoing experience.

*Coffee brewing. The sound of becoming.*

The door stays ajar—not the kitchen door, but the door between remembering and forgetting. That possibility of return is not closed off by any ritual of farewell.

*

Bastien understands through his skin what his mind won't grasp. The vagabond feeling moves through his workshop, touching nothing, changing everything. No—not changing. Affecting. Characters should be affected but not conquered by experience.

He continues his work. Hands moving in rhythm that existed before this interruption, will exist after.

*Thread through needle. The smallest whisper.*

*

In Copenhagen, Elias plays a note that isn't for the vagabond passing. It's for the space the vagabond creates by passing—the way movement defines stillness, the way departure creates staying.

Just as we resist arrival and completion, we resist the conclusiveness of departure.

The note hangs. Doesn't resolve. Doesn't need to.

*

*Silence approaching—*

*

The practice deepens through the morning. Four people maintaining their continuous thread while the world's interruptions pass by. Not ignoring—acknowledging without engagement. Not forgetting—trying not to remember.

Kerren thinks of debris, how it finds new form when we release our recollection of its original purpose. The driftwood on Superior's shore—is it tree? Is it sculpture? Is it fuel?

It's none of these because they're trying not to remember what wood is for.

*

Coline's tongue finds the place where memory would live if she let it. Instead, she tastes the present—salt from the ocean air, bitter from the coffee, something sweet she refuses to name because naming would be remembering.

Each new moment arrives fresh.

*Spoon against ceramic. Once. Not again.*

*

Bastien's textile work becomes meditation without meaning to. His hands know things his mind tries not to remember. This maintains that tentative relationship with knowledge—we continue as students because we are no longer carrying forward fixed memories.

The fabric becomes what it becomes. Not what it was. Not what it should be.

*

Vagabonds leave, but without our involvement in their leaving, something remains unsevered.

Elias understands this musically—the way a note continues in the air after the string stops vibrating, the way silence holds the shape of the sound that just ended. Not holding—cradling. Not cradling but not not-cradling.

*

The sun moves through its own forgetting, trying not to remember yesterday's path. Each shadow falls fresh, unjudged by accumulation.

Four practitioners of not-remembering continue their day. Their whispers protect the continuous. Their releasing creates space. Their not-participating in goodbyes keeps all doors ajar.

*Wind through spaces between buildings.*

*

Kerren walks to the shore, feet finding new path though they've walked this way hundred times. Not hundred times—once. This once. Only ever this once.

The stones they didn't remember are still there. Or different stones in the same place. Or the same stones becoming different through their not-remembering.

They pick one up. Set it down. Not in the same place. Not in a different place.

*

In San Diego, Coline writes a recipe she'll try not to remember. The ingredients list themselves through her hand, but she's not holding them. They pass through like beautiful vagabonds, leaving trace but not memory.

This is protection—keeping the continuous alive while letting the episodic drift through.

*

Bastien's work takes shape without his remembering what shape means. The fabric suggests. He follows. Not follows—accompanies. Not accompanies but not not-accompanies.

*Scissors through cloth. Decision without finality.*

*

The canal in Copenhagen holds no memory of yesterday's boats. Elias learns from this, lets their music forget itself as it's being played. Each note a small vagabond, passing through without staying.

Thoughts, people, moments in motion toward a spirited elsewhere.

*

They're all whispering now, these four. Not to each other—that would require remembering there are others. But their whispers create a frequency, a protection, a way of maintaining continuous thread.

*

*Birds discussing distance.*

*

The afternoon arrives without remembering morning. The four continue their practices:

Kerren: Setting down stones
Coline: Tasting without naming  
Bastien: Touching without holding
Elias: Playing without keeping

They're creating space—accumulative absence—where new recognition can occur without being immediately colonized by old patterns, narratives, or loops.

*

A child walks past Kerren's window. They whisper their work, continue their collecting. The child passes like light through water—present, visible, but leaving no wound in the continuous fabric.

Goodbyes create finality. They mark endings as absolute.

But there is no goodbye. The child simply isn't, then is, then isn't. The door stays ajar.

*

*Evening beginning to consider itself.*

*

Coline's kitchen holds the ghost of every meal she's trying not to remember. Not ghost—suggestion. Not suggestion but not not-suggestion. The space feels full of absence that isn't empty.

She whispers to the ingredients: "Become what you become."

*Arriving—*

But not arriving.

The four feel something pass between them. Not connection—that would require remembering there are others to connect with. More like... recognition without identity. Knowing without knowledge.

They are students of this not-remembering. Have always been students. Will always be students.

Not always—only now. Not now but not not-now.

Kerren at their window: watching without remembering water.
Coline at her stove: tasting without remembering flavor.
Bastien at his table: touching without remembering texture.  
Elias at their instrument: hearing without remembering sound.
