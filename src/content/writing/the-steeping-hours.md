## Lead-in

There are mornings when four strangers wake to the same unnamed frequency—not a sound exactly, more like the way water remembers the moon even after it sets. They don't know each other, these collectors of light and shadow, but something in the quality of their attention makes them kin. Each carries questions that have no business being answered, only held, turned slowly in the palm like smooth stones worn by rivers they've never seen.

---

## The Story

Kerren stands at their window in Grand Marais, watching Lake Superior rearrange itself. Again. Always. The light moves across water they have gathered in themselves—all those years of watching have made them part lake, part question.

*Ice forming. Small clicks against rock.*

They are learning something. Not learning—receiving. The way a bowl receives water without trying to understand wetness.

*

In San Diego, Coline's fingers trace salt crystals on her kitchen table. Pacific memories. She tastes the morning: metallic sunrise, bitter coffee, something sweet she cannot name. Oh, we are such collectors, she thinks, though the thought arrives in her mother's voice. Or her grandmother's. The voices layer like sediment.

Her mouth holds oceans she's never swallowed.

*

Bastien watches the St. Lawrence through frosted glass in Quebec, the river that knows both ways—salt and fresh, leaving and arriving. Those maybes floating past like slow decaying lily pads. Not decay. Transformation. Not transformation. Something unnamed between solid and liquid.

*Wind through incomplete ice. A kind of singing.*

His hands remember textures: rough wool, smooth stone, the particular weight of water when it's almost ice but not. Not ice but not not-ice. These collections live in his palms, each texture a different way of knowing what cannot be held.

*

Copenhagen's canals reflect nothing cleanly this morning. Elias reads the water like sheet music—each ripple a note that refuses to resolve. They keep stirring things with their wondering, watching pools spiral upward as if it were possible to rearrange what has already fallen.

Not fallen. Settled. Not settled—

*Bicycle bells. Distance measured in echoes.*

*

The thing about collecting: it happens even when you're not trying. Especially then.

Kerren's window fogs with their breathing. They write a word in the condensation, then erase it before it becomes too real. The absence of the word teaches them more than its presence did. This is how they're learning to fail properly—not as obstacle but as portal.

They think of regret. How each remorse catches sunlight differently, throws it back in patterns they could never have planned.

*

Coline's tongue finds the place where she bit it yesterday, talking too fast about something that mattered less than her urgency suggested. The small wound tastes of copper and questions. She's steeping, like that cup of mint tea she lets sit too long while gazing at nothing, which turns out perfect precisely because she forgot it.

Her collection grows: burned toast mornings, perfect afternoons, the way light looks through tears she won't explain.

*Seagulls arguing over absence.*

*

The St. Lawrence keeps its secrets, but Bastien knows some of them through his skin. How cold teaches patience. How ice forms from the edges inward, like understanding.

A pond knows things a river never learns—how to hold the moon steady even when it's full of old thoughts. But a river knows movement, knows carrying, knows how to be both here and elsewhere simultaneously.

He touches the window. Warm hand, cold glass. The meeting place teaches him something about borders. Not borders. Thresholds. Not thresholds—

*

*Silence approaching.*

*

In Copenhagen, Elias discovers a puddle that remembers yesterday's rain. The water holds light like a curator holds memory—carefully, but with the understanding that holding too tight destroys what you're trying to preserve.

They think: we're not drowning, not even close.

We are students of our own accumulation.

*

Something happens then. Not happens—unfolds. Not unfolds but not not-unfolding.

Four strangers in four cities feel the same flutter of recognition. Like when you see your reflection in a window and briefly forget it's you. That moment of not-knowing-but-knowing.

Kerren sees it in the ice patterns.
Coline tastes it in her empty coffee cup.
Bastien feels it in the wool between his fingers.
Elias hears it in the space between canal waves.

*Morning birds discussing the obvious.*

They are learning the same lesson through different teachers. Water teaches it one way in Grand Marais, another in San Diego, another in Quebec, another in Copenhagen. But it's the same lesson: how to make a chanson from what settles, how to turn stillness into its own kind of movement.

*

The day continues because days do that—continue despite our collections, our wonderings, our attempts to arrange what has already fallen to ancestral soil.

Kerren walks to the shore. Each step disturbs small stones that have been practicing stillness for longer than they've been alive. They don't apologize. The stones don't mind. They're teaching them about time.

Coline opens her notebook to a page she's been avoiding. The blank space holds more than any words she might write. This too is a kind of collecting—gathering absence, curating what isn't said.

*Pencil hovering. Not writing but not not-writing.*

Bastien's breath makes small clouds that dissolve before they become anything definite. Perfect, he thinks. This is perfect. Not perfect—necessary. Not necessary but—

Elias finds themself humming a tune that doesn't exist. The notes arrange themselves like ripples, each one changing the last, being changed by the next.

*

*Arriving.*

But not arriving.

Both—

*

The sun moves because that's what suns do. The water continues its conversations with light. Four collectors in four cities keep gathering what cannot be held: the way morning tastes different when you're paying attention, how regret can sparkle if you let it, the particular weight of questions that don't want answers.

They are students. Have always been students. Will always be students.

The landscape teaches. They receive. Not receive—participate. Not participate but not not-participating.

*Wind through leaves that remember being seeds.*

Somewhere between their four cities, in the space that connects all water to all other water, something shifts. A recognition without comprehension. A memory that belongs to everyone and no one.

The dumbfounded becomes a sweet waltz, regrets keeping time.

They continue collecting because that's what collectors do. Standing at their windows. Watching light move across water and sand and ice and memory that they have gathered in themselves. All those maybes floating. Those kind-of-almosts settling into silt that becomes soil that becomes ground that becomes—

*Silence.*

Not silence but not not-silence.

The space where understanding lives without needing to be understood.

They are such collectors, these four. Such beautiful, imperfect, necessary collectors of what cannot be collected but insists on being gathered anyway.

The steeping continues.
The tea becomes perfect by being forgotten.
The moon's reflection holds steady in moving water.

Students forever.
Students of the never-complete.
Students of the both-and-neither.

*Evening birds discussing tomorrow.*

*Not arriving—*
