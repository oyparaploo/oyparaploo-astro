Listen—I've been collecting names like other people collect bottle caps or reasons to worry. Not normal names. Names that feel wrong and absolutely right at the same time, like philosophy in action.

**The French Hybrids live in my head:**

Marlène Silt waits tables, carrying melancholy sediment in her apron pockets. Jean-Debris (you say it zhahn deh BREE, makes garbage elegant) drives the Tuesday night garbage truck, interrupting family dinners with curious observations about what people throw up. Or away. Both.

C. Beaumont is the pause on a beautiful mountain—she exists only between words. Troy Rousseau, primitive philosopher meets bridge dweller, sleeps under the overpass reading Kant by flashlight.

Ohm Lie Why carries whimsical wreckage in a violin case. Never opens it.

**Then the almost-normals:**

Lint Chin accumulates neutral flowing stories at the laundromat. Mo Delacroix stands at every crossroads, helping creatures decide. Veronica Spool unwinds true shadow and light from her fingertips—party trick, medical condition, nobody asks.

Pascal Cue: dove waiting for a philosophical palm and subdued pause. (I don't know what this means but I see him clearly—white suit, bus station, forever.)

Margaret Pearl guards the threshold of a building that was demolished in 1987. Still shows up daily. Branch Holloway grows through hollow paths, leaving notes in trees like this one.

**The legendary:**

Ourtour Vessel contains everything and nothing. Doro Loom knits gifts of yarns that become true when worn. Marie Debris Johnson (bitter garbage, common son) inherited both names and uses them like weapons. Will Beau Silt turned a beautiful field into beautiful silt into beautiful nothing. Georges Gardener, earth worker, follows sparks instead of seeds.

**Where they live:**

In stories that feel real enough to ground a narrative but carry that transformative undercurrent. One shows up as a nightclub singer who witnesses a marble crime but only hums about it. Another repeatedly interrupts Thanksgiving asking why we throw so much away.

Someone lives in pauses between possibilities.

These names carry insights about worlds. About ours. I write them down because they demand to exist, even if they don't. Yet.

If you've read this far, you probably have names like this too. People who don't exist but should. Or do exist but shouldn't. Same thing really.

*—Found pinned to this tree, October morning, signed only "Oyparaploo"*

**P.S.** Names can carry entire worlds. Handle with care.
