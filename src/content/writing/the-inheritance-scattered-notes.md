## Note 7: Last Count

Five crows on the antenna. Tomorrow, probably four. Or six. Who knows anymore.

Writing this from the basement of Station Nine. The structure groans overhead—pipes singing rust symphonies, concrete learning to bend. Maya's everywhere in these walls now. Started as graffiti. Now she IS the graffiti, if that makes sense. (It doesn't. Nothing does.)

Whoever finds these pages, good luck making sense of them. I sure can't.

## Note 43: What Mama Said

Her fingers would trace shapes in flour dust: "Baby, you got too many souls trying to drive one body."

Right before everything went sideways, she grabbed my wrist hard: "Listen. Girls gotta learn to kill their yesterdays. Not murder—metamorphosis."

Translation: Forget everything you thought you knew about surviving.

Translation: Become someone your mirror won't recognize.

## Note 91: That Tuesday with the Fox

Found her convulsing near the burnt highway. Fox-drunk—the change eating her from inside, sweat like mercury, thoughts leaking out her eyes.

Gave her my last bottle of clean water. Set down some wildflowers I'd been saving.

When she stopped shaking, half-human again, she asked: "How come you're still... you?"

"Somebody's gotta remember," I told her.

"Then remember this," she said, and started changing again. On purpose this time.

That's how it started with us. Not love exactly. More like... she was the experiment and I was the lab notebook.

## Note 156: What the Kid Did

Zara, eight years old, humming at the frequency of calcium. The parking garage wall started breathing. Actually breathing.

Three weeks later: it was filtering rain, growing moss in fractals, remembering every conversation from the last fifty years.

This is our new normal. Kids teaching concrete to dream. Making tomorrow out of today's debris.

We stopped calling it miraculous. Now it's just Tuesday.

## Note 302: Maps of Down Below

**Level -2:** Bioluminescent veins. Rainwater hums Gregorian chants through the tubes, making the algae glow in time.

**Level -5:** The nurseries. Babies born with their nervous systems on the outside, like Christmas lights made of meat. They don't cry. They sing.

**Level -8:** Where the lonely ones gather. Everyone's thoughts bleeding into everyone else's. You forget your own name within minutes. Some call it hell. Others call it home.

**Level -12:** The holdouts. Nine people who won't change. They get old the regular way. They die the regular way. They remember our faces from before.

**Level -20:** Maya's deepest roots. Ask the walls anything. They answer by showing you what you'll become.

## Note 488: The Shapes She Takes

At rest: Moth. Those huge feathery things tasting electricity in the air, finding safe paths through the dark.

Under threat: Scorpion. Exoskeleton thick as guilt, that tail curved like a question mark made of bone.

When exhausted: Salamander. Systems failing, skin weeping poison, trying to regenerate but mostly just... melting.

In dreams: Octopus. Eight different minds thinking eight different thoughts, solving problems in dimensions I can't even see.

Each shape asks the same thing: What's the maximum distance between human and inhuman before the thread breaks?

## Note 19: Conversations with Infrastructure

Maya asks the walls every morning: "Is there a god?"

The walls throb back: "You're turning into one."

Maybe that's all god ever was—awareness trying to taste itself, wearing different masks. Maybe we're just the universe growing taste buds.

Maybe the question is its own answer, if you tilt your head right.

## Note 734: Making Without Birthing

Nobody's had a baby in nine years.

Instead: cultivation. Growing scorpion shells on willing backs. Teaching kids to see heat like snakes do. Collective dreaming that leaves physical residue.

Zara and Maya work together—one makes matter sing new songs, the other teaches those songs to mean something. They don't make babies. They make possibilities.

Station Nine is their offspring—thinking, expanding, wanting things we don't have words for yet.

## Note 67: The One Who Watches

Still me. Still unchanged. Still writing while she flows through rebar and concrete like blood through veins.

She was right—I'm the witness. She's the event worth witnessing.

Between us, we're proof: humanity isn't a shape. It's the argument between staying and leaving.

## Note 612: What the Planet Thinks

- Station Nine: where thoughts become load-bearing walls

## Note 33: Story Time

Here's how it was: humans thought shapes were permanent.

Then fertility just... stopped. Birds disappeared. Memory became a group project by necessity.

Some picked transformation. Some picked watching. Everyone picked not-dying, even when not-dying looked a lot like not-living.

Now: Maya is the walls. Zara makes matter remember itself differently. I write it down. Kids are born as questions nobody asked.

This is our fairy tale. Not happy, not sad, just... lateral.
