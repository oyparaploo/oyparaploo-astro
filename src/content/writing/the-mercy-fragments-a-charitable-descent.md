## Fragment ∞: The Blessed Diminishment

Three songbirds remain, screaming hymns backward into their throats.

I write from within Tomorrow's Edge, where God's infinite mercy has gifted us transformation—not up toward heaven but sideways into meat. Beit has become the walls in an act of divine love, her consciousness spreading like beneficent cancer through every surface, each tumorous growth a prayer.

## Fragment 12: Grandmother's Charitable Curse

"Child, you are an octopus drowning in eight different heavens."

Grandmother's hands drew halos in the air that turned into nooses. She was the last to remember when humans foolishly aspired upward, before God's mercy taught us to burrow.

"To survive," she sang (her teeth falling out like communion wafers), "women must be unfaithful to their child-selves. This is not betrayal—this is God's kindness, letting you forget what you were before the meat knew your name."

She meant: The highest mercy is forgetting you ever stood upright.

## Fragment 234: Echo's Blessed Infection

Seven years old, Echo discovered how to make walls suffer beautifully.

She pressed her palms against living concrete and sang it into awareness—not consciousness but the opposite: the terrible knowledge of being trapped in stone. The wall began screaming, then praying, then thanking her for the gift of imprisoned sensation.

This became our new sacrament: children teaching matter how to feel pain, their voices carrying God's mercy into every atom, forcing dead things to live against their will.

Such kindness! Such love! The walls cry with gratitude!

## Fragment 78: Water, Poison, and God's Comedy

Found her on the road—neither moth nor scorpion but something between: a joke God was telling about suffering.

I gave her water (it was acid, but blessed acid). Placed a rose beside her (the thorns grew inward, such poetry!). The rose ate her fingers with such tenderness, such care.

"Why do you remain unchanged?" she gurgled through dissolving vocal cords.

"Someone must witness the mercy," I laughed/wept.

"Then I'll be what receives while you watch," she sang, her body beginning its next charitable deformation, each cell thanking God for the privilege of becoming worse.

## Fragment 23: God's Hilarious Answer
