*From Still Movement, including Prayers to Planetary Activity*

You're washing dishes. Someone else is drafting climate legislation. Both hands are working. Both matter.

The thing nobody mentions about devotion: it doesn't care about scale. Your private morning ritual—lighting a candle, feeding birds, writing three lines in a notebook nobody will read—carries the same weight as movements that shift nations. The universe doesn't rank offerings by size.

A child draws monsters with broken crayons. A scientist maps the ocean floor. Someone plants tomatoes in a rusted coffee can. Someone else coordinates food distribution for ten thousand refugees. They're all doing the same thing, really. Offering what they have to whatever they understand as holy.

Playfulness belongs here too. Maybe it's the whole point. The sacred shows up wearing ridiculous disguises—in dad jokes, in midnight dancing alone in your kitchen, in the way you arrange rocks on your windowsill for no reason except it pleases you. These aren't separate from the serious work. They're part of the weave.

The prayer you whisper walking to work reaches as far as any sermon. The song you hum folding laundry is received. That weird project you're tinkering with in your garage at 2 AM? The universe leans in, curious.

Private actions, collective projects, planetary healing, personal healing—not different things. Same thing, different volumes. Every authentic gesture becomes liturgy. Every creative act opens a door between worlds.

The earth receives it all: your smallest kindness, your largest ambition, your invisible Tuesday afternoon mercy that nobody witnessed. It all counts. It's all being woven into whatever comes next.
