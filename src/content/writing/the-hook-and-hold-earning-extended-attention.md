## Start with Dessert

The first point is your audition. If you nail it, they'll give you more time. If you fumble it, they're already checking out mentally. So start with your strongest, most immediately valuable insight—not throat-clearing or context-setting. Give them the thing they didn't know they needed to hear, the insight that makes them think "I never saw it that way."

This first point should be complete enough to stand alone. If they only heard this one thing, they'd still walk away with value. You're not holding good stuff hostage but proving worth immediately. Like a free sample that's actually the premium product.

## Making Them Complicit

After delivering that strong opening point, acknowledge what just happened: "Now that changes things, doesn't it?" or "If that's true—and it is—then we need to talk about what this means." 

Once they've nodded yes to one thing, they're invested in implications. They're not observing anymore; they're participating. You can say directly: "If you found that useful, let me show you how deep this goes..." You're not just adding points but revealing layers, each building on their agreement with the first.

## Creating Necessary Incompleteness

"That principle I just shared applies to three other areas you haven't considered..." Now they need to know what those areas are. Or try the complication move: "But here's where it gets tricky..." You've given them truth, now you're showing nuance. People love nuance after clarity—first make it simple, then sophisticated. They feel smart for following along.

Sometimes you create a cliffhanger from your strong point: "This technique generated incredible results, but also led to an unexpected problem..." You've opened a loop that needs closing.

## The Rhythm of Attention

After that first strong point, lower your energy slightly—not boring, but less intense. If you maintain peak intensity, it's exhausting. Peak with the first point, then create a valley for them to rest while building to the next peak. Like music—you need dynamics or it all becomes noise.

During that recovery, you're laying groundwork for the next big point. You're not wasting the valley but using it to prepare.

## The Callback Architecture

Keep referencing that first strong point throughout: "Remember what I said about X? That applies here too..." The first point becomes the anchor everything else attaches to. It makes everything feel connected, not random. You're building a system, not listing points.

This rewards their attention. They feel smart for remembering, seeing connections. They become pattern-seekers, actively looking for how things connect.

## Your Reputation in Miniature

That first point better be actually good—not just confident or well-delivered but genuinely valuable. False advertising kills everything. If you promise value and deliver fluff, they're gone forever.

Choose carefully: What's the one thing you could tell them that would make them think "I need to hear everything this person has to say"? The one thing that proves you see something they don't, know something they need, understand something they're struggling with.

Deliver it like a gift, not a tease. Full value, no holding back. Then build from that generosity, layer by layer, each point earning the right to the next. Until they're not just willing to listen—they're eager to. Because you've proven with that first point that their attention is an investment, not an expense.
