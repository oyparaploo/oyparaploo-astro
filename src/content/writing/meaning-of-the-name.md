**What We Know Now**

**in the hush** between waking and sleep,  
**a root** stirred in us—  
not loudly,  
but with the warmth of **old soil**  
**remembering my name**.

it didn’t ask who we were.  
it simply **traced our silence**  
like a map  
made of **pulse**  
and **chilled breath**.

we walk differently now—  
as if **the earth listens**,  
as if **our shadow hums**  
in **tones**.

even **our quiet has texture**.  
even our steps  
leave behind **spores of soft becoming**.

and when we forget,  
**the roots do not**.  
they hold us  
in **whispered loops**  
until we **remember**  
to **grow again**.

The Underground Choir

We fell asleep beneath a streetlamp that flickered like a failing heartbeat,  
and in the between-space of sleep,  
we descended—  
not into bed,  
but into a cathedral of frostroot.

There, the **roots were not roots**, but **frozen arteries of forgotten choices**,  
stretching miles beneath the surface, cold as moonlight on iron.  
They pulsed—not with blood,  
but with memory stalled in glacial time.

We walked.  
Or perhaps, we floated.  
In this soil-stitched dreaming, locomotion was emotional metaphor—  
our regret curled like mycelial tendrils, guiding us deeper.

The first root we touched sang.

It hummed a **fugue of our childhoods**,  
soft and imperfect,  
like moss reciting a lullaby in broken vowels.  
Our fingers tingled with the ghost of crayons,  
of crushed leaves,  
of everything we never got to say to our mothers.

Another root—thicker, colder—wrapped around our wrists.

It whispered, “We are your silence during joy. We hold all the moments you didn’t laugh loud enough.”  
“We are the chill of self-preservation. We froze your boldness so you could survive.”

**Roots are metaphors for identity’s multiplicity.**  

They don’t point one way.  
They burrow in all directions—fluid, nonlinear, sacredly confused.  
Like how we survive:  
not neatly,  
but knotted and cold and beautiful.

At the center, we found a root unlike the others—  
coated in frost so pure it looked like glass thought into existence.

We touched it and heard every version of ourselves say:

“You are more than one story.  
You are a **network of contradictions** made holy by connection.  
You grow downward,  
and that, too, is grace.”

Then morning came.  
We woke not with clarity,  
but with tangled questions,  
our veins feeling strangely rootlike—  
as if underground had blessed us  
with a new way to feel lost  
and call it growth.

**We Are the Archive of Midnight Echo**

But to us—it is archive.  

We unfurl when sleep folds the mind into vapor.  
We scatter where consciousness dares not tread.  
The soil remembers all that the waking world forgets,  
and we, the lone spores, are its librarians of murmur.

We were born from the sigh of a violet buried a hundred springs ago—  
not pushed by sunlight,  
but summoned by dreaming.

Every breath you exhale in your sleep? We braid it into filament.  
Every regret caught between teeth? We carve it into nutrient.  
You think you wake alone—  
but beneath your pillow, we have mapped your fears in mycelial curls.

Last night, we tunneled through a poem half-spoken by a stranger  
sleeping in St Paul, Minnesota.  
Their bones hummed in enjambment.  
Their lungs rhymed with the rain.

We sprawl not with purpose, but with pulse.  
Our bodies are ritual:  
- The rhythm of repetition  
- The liturgy of loss  
- The gospel of dirt  

We commune with worms, not gods.  
We make no temples, only tender swells of rot.  
And yet—we remember.

Oh, how we remember.

In the tangled thickets of your unconscious,

**Fable of the Fractured Sun**

Long ago, before root and rain knew each other,  
the world was stone-strung silence.

Then came *Myco*, the First Spore, born of moon-mist and decay.  
It did not walk—it *threaded*.  
With one filament, it touched the stone.  
With the next, it kissed a seed.  
And with the third, it sang.

“Our name is network. Our breath is boundary. We shall make the dark delicious.”

Myco spun its thoughts through soil—  
each pulse a memory of light lost,  
each hypha a dreaming of form.  
Soon, trees leaned in to listen.  
Beetles built temples in the loam.  
Even the wind changed its accent to match the spores.

But then, the Sun grew jealous.

“You thrive in shadow,” the Sun roared,  
“I will bleach the soil. I will dry your song.”

And so came the Scorching. Roots withered. Leaves wept.  
Myco retreated—not in fear, but in **pattern**.  
It coiled into myth, stored signals in spiral codes,  
and whispered to its children:

“Repetition is our rebellion.  
Memory is our cathedral.  
When the Sun forgets, we remember.”

And from the cracked soil, new spores bloomed—  
carrying within them the story of how to speak to stone,  
how to resurrect decay,  
how to carve identity from ruin.

**Roots as Cartographer of Self**

Beneath bark and boundary, the mycelium stretches—not in conquest, but in curiosity.  
Each tendril is an inquiry: *Who are we in relation to this oak root?*  
*To the earthworm?*  
*To the decay of last autumn’s leaf?*

We map identity not in names, but in interactions.  
Not in declarations, but in transformation.  
What was once wood becomes soil,  
What was once solitude becomes symbiosis.

Repetition becomes ritual.  
Each time we connect, we remember.  
Each spore sprouted is both archive and avatar—  
a poem spun from genetic code and chemical whisper.

If humans mirror this—if their selves sprout from connection, decay, and renewal—  
then maybe identity is less about “We are,”  
and more about “We have become,”  
again and again  
and again.

**Roots Organism Dream Beneath the Hyrcanian Canopy**

In the breath between soil and silence, a fungal vastness stirs.  
It threads its tendrils in humid dusk, caressing the roots of elder beech and hornbeam—  
Not as parasites, not as prophets, but as memory in motion.  

We dream not in language, but in pulses:  
A cedar’s sigh, translated into sugar.  
The sorrow of a wilted violet, metabolized into poetry.  
We hum in slow symbiosis, reshaping decay into cradle,  
etching elegies into limestone with spore and intention.

Somewhere in the Zagros, an unseen network spans decades—perhaps centuries—  
whispering through calcium and clay: *You are not alone.*  
Each tree, each stone, each beetle crossing our lattice  
is accounted for in the ledger of silence.  

A living archive—fluid, fungal, and unbroken.
