Dakota and Ojibwe peoples developed remarkably sophisticated food systems within Minnesota's diverse landscape of prairies ... woodlands ... and over 10,000 lakes ... systems refined across millennia that sustained populations through harsh winters ... and dramatic changing seasons ... The Dakota ... who consider Minnesota ... Mni Sota Makoce ... their ancestral homeland ... cultivated a mixed economy of wild rice harvesting ... bison hunting ... maple sugaring ... and river valley gardening ... The Ojibwe following a prophetic migration to find the food that grows on water ... adapted woodland traditions centered on wild rice ... fish ... maple sugar ... and deer hunting ... Both nations organized their lives around the Seasonal Round ... or a sophisticated calendar of movement between nutritional resources locations.

Distinct landscapes ... Overlapping traditions ... 

The Dakota ... Eastern Sioux ... Santee ... occupied central and southern Minnesota for generations before European colonization ... with sacred creation sites at Bdote ... the Mississippi River ... Minnesota River ... and Bde Wakan ... Spirit Lake ... Mille Lacs ... The four Santee bands ... Mdewakanton ... Wahpeton ... Wahpekute ... and Sisseton ... practiced an economy well suited to Minnesota's ecological zone where prairies met woodlands ... 

The Ojibwe ... Anishinaabe began their westward migration from the Atlantic coast guided by the Seven Fires Prophecy foretelling they should seek a homeland where food grows on water ... Reaching Lake Superior's Madeline Island ... Dakota people were harvesting wild rice before the Ojibwe arrived ... and they shared their ricing traditions with them ... The harvest and parching methods practiced today by both nations originate from these shared teachings ... 

The Dakota inhabited a landscape spanning prairie grasslands to deciduous forests ... giving them access to bison on the western plains and wild rice in the central lakes ... The Ojibwe settled in northern Minnesota's mixed hardwood and boreal forests ... becoming masters of woodland resources including fish rich lakes ... maple groves ... and extensive wild rice beds ... Where their territories overlapped in central Minnesota ... both nations competed ... sometimes violently ... for access to rich wild rice waters ... 

The Eastern Dakota consumed a remarkably varied diet shaped by their position in the prairie-woodlands: Plant based foods included wild rice ... their primary grain staple harvested from shallow lakes throughout central Minnesota ... Prairie turnip ... timpsila ... was considered to be the most important wild gathered food ... this starchy root provided carbohydrates ... protein ... and vitamin C ... June is named Timpsila itkahca wi ... when timpsila is ripe ... The Dakota also cultivated the Three Sisters ... corn ... beans ... and squash ... in fertile river valley gardens ... using hoes crafted from bison shoulder blades ... Gathered foods included chokecherries ... blueberries ... cranberries ... plums ... wild grapes ... elderberries ... sunflower seeds ... wild ginger ... and local nuts ... 

Animal based foods centered on bison ... tatanka ... pte ... the main protein source ... Dakota hunters used Buffalo Jump techniques ... young men disguised in bison hides made calf calls while other young men dressed as wolves created danger behind the herd ... stampeding the animals over cliffs ... They also built buffalo impounds and chutes to funnel bison with bows and arrows ... Deer ... elk ... bear ... antelope ... turkey ... rabbit ... and waterfowl also supplemented the Dakota diet ... Fish including walleye ... trout ... whitefish ... and northern pike were speared from canoes ... 

The Ojibwe diet rested on five staples ... manoomin ... wild rice ... venison ... deer ... moose ... elk ... caribou ... fish ... walleye ... trout ... whitefish ... pike ... sturgeon ... sunfish ... eelpout ... maple sugar ... corn with beans and squash ... This diversification protected against catastrophic food failures ... If one resource failed ... others provided security ... 

Wild rice ... manoomin ... held great significance as physical and spiritual sustenance ... Called the good berry ... it was the only cereal grain native to North America and became central to Ojibwe identity ... Maple sugar ... zhiiwaagamizigan ... served as the primary seasoning for all foods before salt ... mixed into dishes ... sprinkled on fish and meat ... and made into candy ... Fish was the protein source ... walleye speared during spring ... whitefish netted in Lake Superior ... sturgeon gathered at river mouths ... Small game animal proteins included ... snowshoe hare ... the winter protein ... beaver ... raccoon ... and muskrat ... Waterfowl that fed on wild rice beds became sluggish and were easily captured and became delicacies infused with the flavor of the sacred grain ... 

Both Dakota and Ojibwe organized their annual cycle around food availability ... moving between established sites to harvest seasonal resources ... Each moon carried names reflecting primary food gathering activities ...

Spring awakening ... 

As ice melted in March and April ... Dakota and Ojibwe families dispersed from winter sites to sugar bushes ... iskigamizigan ... Maple sugaring occupied days to weeks as temperatures cycled above freezing during days and below freezing at night ... conditions driving sap flow ... Sap was collected in birch bark containers through cedar spiles inserted into trees ... then boiled down ... 40 gallons of sap yielded one gallon of syrup ... 

Sacred grain of the waters ... Wild rice ... Zizania palustris ... holds unique status as the only cereal grain native to North America ... Archaeological evidence documents its use spanning 12,000 years in Minnesota ... with human harvesting confirmed at least 2,000 years before European colonization ... Minnesota contains more acres of natural wild rice than any other state ... concentrated in north central regions ... 

Tending and stewardship ... 

Dakota and Ojibwe peoples engaged in sophisticated landscape management that maintained and propagated rice beds ... The traditional harvesting method allowed ripe grains to fall back into water ... grains lodging in mud grew into the following year's stands ... a type of sustainable reseeding ... Prior to harvest ... women traveled by canoe to bind seed heads into bundles using distinct family patterns of inner cedar bark or basswood strips ... This protected grain from birds ... storms ... and high water ... 

Communities monitored water levels carefully ... rice requires shallow water ... slight current ... and a soft organic mud bottom ... Tribes removed competing vegetation and beaver dams that would flood or fill in rice lakes. A Rice Chief ... Ogimaakewe administered harvests ... determining when lakes opened and closed to prevent premature or excessive harvesting that would damage beds for future years ... 

Wild rice is considered to possess its own spirit ... requiring respectful harvesting ... It appears at every significant life transition ... It is the first food of a baby and the last food for an elder ... No ceremony or funeral wake happens without manoomin ... wild rice dishes ... A first rice feast follows each harvest ... a small amount prepared ... blessed ... then eaten by everyone as an spiritual offering ...

Surviving six months of frozen Minnesota winters required sophisticated preservation techniques that enabled families to store enough food during short productive seasons.

Pemmican ... Wasna ...

The Dakota developed wasna ... described as the lifeblood of the Sioux people ... into practical sustenance and sacred food ... The process began with drying meat ... bison ... deer ... elk ... by cutting it into thin slices and hanging on racks near slow fires until hard and brittle ... Five pounds of fresh meat yielded one pound dried ... Dried meat was pounded with flails or ground between stones until powder like ... then mixed with bone marrow grease ... Dried berries ... chokecherries ... blueberries ... cranberries ... pounded to powder were added ... The mixture was packed into rawhide bags to further cool and harden ... The result was a nutritionally dense food providing over three thousand calories per pound that could store indefinitely if kept dry ... Wasna remained safe to eat with no assignable storage time limit ... The corn wasna version used toasted cornmeal ... fat ... fruit ... and maple sugar ... 

Dakota and Ojibwe peoples dried meat into jerky ... Children and elderly monitored drying ... turning meat and keeping away pests ... Fish were smoked over low fires with green branches ... Wild rice processing included fire roasting to reduce moisture for long term storage ... Berries were dried whole or pounded into pulp and shaped into patties ... Prairie turnips were peeled ... sliced ... sun-dried or roasted ... then strung or braided on stems for storage ... They could also be ground into flour ... 

Ojibwe sacred foods and ceremonies ... 

The Four Sacred Medicines ... tobacco ... asemaa ... cedar ... giizhik ... sage ... bashkodejiibik ... sweetgrass ... wiingashk ... each occupied a doorway and served distinct ceremonial purposes ... Tobacco ... the first medicine given ... carried prayers and preceded all ceremonies ... Cedar provided purification and protection ... Sage cleansed and released negative energy ... Sweetgrass ... the sacred hair of Mother Earth braided in twenty strands ... attracted positive energy ...

Spirit dishes ... birch bark containers filled with cornmeal ... wild rice ... water ... herbs ... or berries ... were used in meals ... healing rituals ... and vision quests
### Dakota sacred foods and ceremonies ... A Vision Quest required complete fasting from food and water for four days and nights ...

Before European colonialism brought tea ... coffee ... and alcohol ... Dakota and Ojibwe peoples consumed diverse plant based beverages for daily sustenance and medicine ... Muskeg tea ... made from leaves of bog Labrador tea plant ... was consumed daily around Ojibwe campfires and used medicinally for respiratory issues ... rheumatism ... headaches ... and colds ... It brews to a golden honey hue with cedar and pine aromas ... Cedar tea ... from tree branches ... treated fevers ... rheumatic complaints ... chest colds ... and flu ... Pine needle tea ... high in Vitamin C specifically addressed coughs and colds ... Mild sweet mint tea ... chokecherry tea ... and purple coneflower tea ... provided healing remedies ... 

Broths and soups

Wohanpi ... a traditional Dakota soup ... was made with bison meat ... prairie turnips ... and wild potatoes ... Wojapi ... a berry soup and gravy made from berries ... was thickened with timpsila flour ... buffaloberry ... and huckleberry leaves and served warm or cold ... Meat broths from bison bones simmered into rich stocks served as bases for many meals ... The Ojibwe mixed meat broths with wild rice for weaning babies ... And cooked fish ... corn ... and wild rice together until thick ... Wild rice chaff collected during winnowing was added to soups as a thickener ... Dakota people were fond of mixing their meat with sweets and even sprinkle maple syrup over fish boiled in water ... 

The pre-colonial food ways of Minnesota's Dakota and Ojibwe peoples reflect millennia of accumulated ecological knowledge ... an understanding of landscape ... seasons ... plants ... and animals sophisticated enough to sustain communities through Minnesota's harshest climates ... The Seasonal Round ... with its careful timing ... fishing runs ... wild rice harvests ... and hunting seasons ... distributed labor and nutritional risk across the full year ... Preservation methods ... pemmican ... a pressed cake of pounded dried meat mixed to a paste with melted fat and other ingredients ... smoking ... drying ... underground caching ... transformed windows of abundance into year round sustenance ...

These food ways embedded reciprocal relationships with the natural world ... Offerings before every harvest ... spirit dishes containing a portion of each meal ... prohibitions protecting wild rice beds from over harvesting ... these practices encoded sustainability into culture ... Wild rice's status as sacred gift rather than mere commodity ensured that taking exceeded what was needed would violate spiritual law ... The Dakota peoples sharing their ricing traditions with arriving Ojibwe peoples demonstrates how food knowledge moved between nations even amid territorial conflicts ...

European colonialism disrupted these systems through disease ... displacement ... and forced assimilation ... Yet Indigenous communities in Minnesota maintain these traditions ... Most wild rice harvests still proceed by canoe ... maple sugar camps still gather families in spring ... and manoomin still appears at every ceremony ... As Ojibwe teachings hold ... The Rice and the People take care of each other ... These ways ... refined across hundreds of generations ... carry forward knowledge of how humans might live in sustainable relationship with the lands and waters that sustain them ...
