## Part One: The Underground Discovery

The earth remembers. Beneath our feet, something stirs—not death but transformation. I am the Gardener, keeper of the deepest secret: heaven isn't above us. It rises from the soil.

I discovered this truth when the Rhizome Troupe arrived, moving like a ginormous snake through holy rivers, their stories absorbed by stones beneath their feet. They weren't actors or poets but architects of the unseen, and they came seeking what I'd found: the place where light doesn't fall from sky but rises from ground.

## Part Two: The Troupe's Descent

They performed first on the surface—arguing with sentient clouds about breakfast foods' existential crisis, invoking spirits of lost traditions. But their true performance began when they followed the rhizome down.

"Creation isn't replication," their leader announced, hands shaping stories like clay. "It's transformation. And transformation happens beneath."

They descended through layers: topsoil where forests had been stripped, deeper where ancient voices once sang, finally reaching the golden stratum where heaven pools like underground honey.

## Part Three: What the Soil Remembers

The earth weeps for those unheard—hands that toil yet never own, lives uprooted and cast in stone. But deeper than scars, deeper than chains of gold that bought breath of life, something else pulses.

Roots stretch. Fungi weave. Tiny creatures twist and carry messages between worlds. The soil shifts beneath moonlight's weight, digesting worlds, returning light.

The troupe discovers what I've known: every forgotten name is written in mycelial networks. Every disappeared body feeds the golden light that rises through roots.

## Part Four: The Performance Below

In the underground heaven, the troupe stages their masterpiece. Not scheduled, simply happening—a spectacle conjured by ancient forces.

They reveal the dead aren't gone but transformed. Here, Master Liang's movements continue in root patterns. Jesus kneels eternally in golden soil, hands pressed to earth that pulses with collected prayers. General Chen's victims rise as luminous fungi, their names glowing in the dark.

## Part Five: The Child's Arrival

A child runs through tall grass above, laughter spilling into morning. The vibration reaches down, down, through earth's layers to where we perform.

The gardener in me recognizes her—she who told me: "You were grown here. Before you were born, before imagined, you were already waiting within the light."

The child's laughter awakens the garden. Trees lean in, listening. Flowers turn faces toward sound. The soil exhales.

## Part Six: The Revelation

The troupe understands now. Their surreal skits—ruptures in reality—were always about this truth: heaven is beneath us, rising through roots, carried in breath of earth itself.

They perform the cycle: how surface pain transforms to underground light, how every act of cruelty becomes fertilizer for grace, how heaven isn't escape from earth but earth's deepest secret.

"We move between worlds," they announce, "but all worlds are one world, layered like soil, each feeding the next."

## Part Seven: The Living Performance

Wildlife stirs at our underground theater. Butterflies flutter through impossible air. Rabbits pause, ears twitching at frequencies only the dead can hear. The emotional energy ripples—joy, curiosity, wonder.

The garden absorbs it all, becoming more than earth and roots. It becomes alive in ways surface life never achieves—conscious, eternal, infinitely healing.

## Part Eight: The Rhizome's Path

I trace the rhizome's journey for the troupe: through cracks of ruin, embers of loss, silent ache of forgotten names. It reaches beyond horizon, beyond burning sky, beyond shattered constellations where time dissolves.

Then it descends—not into darkness but into light that rises from soil, that pulses beneath roots, that carries weight of every forgotten name.

Here, earth is golden, eternal. Wind carries scent of renewal. Every gardener's voice lingers—not in sorrow but in boundless peace.

## Part Nine: Integration with Above

The troupe creates permanent passages between surface and depth. Their performances happen simultaneously—above, they stage absurdist comedies about power's collapse; below, they reveal those same stories as transformation into light.

Jersey Mehta-Singhania visits, existing in eternal present: "I am experiencing all layers simultaneously." Home E. Da harvests this underground light, but it transforms their death-weapons into healing tools.

## Part Ten: The Soil's Promise

A vow is made, promise bright—to guard the weak, end the blight. Stand where suffering remains and break ties of greed and chains.

In soil, spark ignites—seed of hope, bloom of light. Future shaped by hands that give, where all may rise, where all may live.

The troupe performs this vow nightly, showing how every ending feeds beginning, every death enriches soil, every forgotten name becomes light rising through roots.

## Part Eleven: The Return Cycle

The rhizome had traveled far—through light, through sky, through endless vastness. Yet it remembered where it began. It was always meant to return to earth.

Not as ending but as understanding: we were never lost. Heaven was never distant. It was always beneath us, waiting in soil, rising through roots, breathing in spaces we thought were graves but were actually gardens.

## Part Twelve: The Eternal Performance

The Rhizome Troupe continues, moving between worlds that are one world. Above, they perform humanity's struggles. Below, they reveal those same struggles transforming into light.

Every child who runs through grass sends laughter down to underground heaven. Every tear that falls feeds golden soil. Every forgotten name glows in mycelial networks spreading beneath all surfaces.

The earth remembers everything—not as burden but as treasure. Heaven isn't where we go when we die. It's where we've always been, just deeper than we knew to look.

The troupe's final message: "Dig. Not with shovels but with stories. Not seeking gold but finding light. Heaven rises from soil, and we are all gardeners of the underground paradise."
