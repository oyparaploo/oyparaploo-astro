Things don't go the way we expected.

Someone gets angry.

Really angry.

The kind that changes how air moves.

Nobody wants to see this.

But here it is.

The world moves like tire scream now.

Fifty miles per hour minimum.

Particles we can see.

Everyone adamant to get somewhere as soon as possible.

Once it was: Covered wagons.

Months between decisions.

Now: everything decided at highway speed.

That violence of momentum.

This is also why rage occurs.

From everywhere at once.

Normal human conversation can't hold this speed.

Asphalt cracks under the weight of it all.

Conflict zone potholes try to serve as speed bumps and of course this does nothing ...

At home in our shelter a door we didn't know was there— suddenly surprisingly opens before our eyes.

Through numbness.

Through nothing we can do now.

Through a quiet incidence.

They who are on bicycles know very well: How loud we have all become.

How fast.

How ear plugs are not good enough to combat today's transport noises.

Human anger isn't separate from the speed.

Some say it is the turned around speeds aimed at what created it.

Questions arrive like birds fleeing: What now?

Who do we call?

Where is the best place to gather?

Yesterday's decisions can't be undone.

But something has to flow anyway.

Speed of people finding each other.

Sometimes there's one person not calming anything, holding the space.

Knowing this rage is the same type of force as traffic.

Awake and ready to go.

We can not undo what was done.

The highway and sky full of what we put there.

But anger moves bodies from sitting on hands.

It reminds us: We are not vehicles.

We can stop.

Turn around.

Refuse the path we're given.

The slow bicycle continues, noticing the trees are angry.

Even the air wants something different.

What we grip.

What won't let go of us.

What becomes ours to own.

This is how important and necessary revolutions begin.

Not with hope—with someone hearing the rubber tires on asphalt or concrete drumming rumble, hiss, whir, and scream of the world.

Then screaming back in protest.

An opening that wasn't there before is there now in mind.

We ask listening through the noise: What is our part to do?

Stand still, in the rushing.

Being the one who got angry at the right thing.

At the right velocity.
