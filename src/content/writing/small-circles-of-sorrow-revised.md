**Small Circles of Sorrow (Revised)**

*"A visitor's witnessing"*
_________________

I learned something today from someone's Instagram story: "Hosting grief night. Bring nothing but your sadness. We're not fixing anything." *The post got 47 hearts in five minutes.* Everyone wants permission to stop pretending.

Invite a few people to your kitchen. *The ones who've seen you break, who don't need your performance.* Say: tonight we're talking about what we've lost. Set ground rules. *Like a support group but with wine and real tears.* We're not solving anything.

Sorrow isn't a problem needing solutions. *It's the dishes in the sink you're too tired to wash, too guilty to leave.* It's presence awaiting witness. When we witness grief, we add human warmth. *Like when someone says "that fucking sucks" instead of "stay strong."*

There's an old principle about keeping things warm to transform. *Like sourdough starter, like compost.* When we approach grief with coldness, it hardens into depression. *Becomes that weight on your chest every morning, the reason you can't answer texts.*

The first move is bringing warmth. *Not advice, not yoga recommendations - just presence.* The friend who comes over and doesn't talk. Who matches your breathing. *Who brings takeout and doesn't mention you're wearing yesterday's clothes.*

Grief wants company. *It's why support groups exist, why comment sections become confessionals.* From humanity's beginning, we've grieved together. *Irish wakes. Jazz funerals. Sitting shiva.* But now, under capitalism's isolation, we schedule breakdown between meetings. *Crying in bathroom stalls. Paying strangers to listen.*

We need to find our way back to small circles. *Kitchen table sized. Group chat sized.* Where we discover our griefs are tangled together. *Your climate anxiety mixing with my parent's diagnosis.* Different verses of the same exhausted song.

The communal cup needs regular emptying. *Like taking turns being the one who falls apart this week.* Not just for personal healing - though that matters. *Like breathing matters.* But so we can love fiercely in collapsing times. So we can show the young ones that feeling is still allowed. *That breaking is survivable when witnessed.*

Under this innocent spiritual goodness, shared grief becomes prayer. *The most honest prayer there is.* Just saying: this happened, this matters, this hurts. *And having someone text back at 2 AM: "me too, we're here."*

Still learning that "how are you" is too big a question now. *Still discovering that everyone's lying about being okay.* Still finding our way back to the communal cup.
