*Lead-in:*
Written on napkins from the diner near the intersection. Different pens, different days, some parts in pencil already fading. The waitress says whoever wrote this never finished their coffee, just kept writing and crying and writing. She saved the napkins because she said it felt important. Or maybe she said impossible. Her accent makes it hard to tell the difference.

Morning after everyone showed up and tried to organize and failed.

Yuki comes back. Had this whole speech planned about how she doesn't belong, doesn't really hear pressure ghosts, just has regular synesthesia plus desperation.

But before she can start, Lulu grabs her arm. "Help me with this?"

"With what?"

"I don't know. The recordings. I'm hearing something but maybe it's nothing. You see sound, right? Tell me if you see what I'm hearing."

That's it. No big moment. Just one confused person asking another confused person for help.

The elder gardener starts doing these walks. Takes three or four people around the block. Points at trees.

"This one's grieving."

"How do you—"

"Seventeen senses."

"But I only have five."

"You have more. You just don't know their names."

Half the people think he's profound. Other half think he's senile. Maybe both. Definitely both.

The Goon Ox does this thing where it lets people touch its orange hair while it wobbles. 

Tamika tries it first. "Oh fuck," she says. "Oh fuck, I hear—is that—?"

"Pressure ghosts," someone says.

"No, that's just traffic."

"Same thing maybe?"

She keeps her hand on the orange hair for twenty minutes. When she lets go, she's crying. Won't say why.

They try to do Desta's humming as a group. Tamika leading because she used to teach dance or choir or something.

"Everyone hum at—what's the frequency, Desta?"

Desta shrugs. Writes: "Purple"

"That's not a frequency."

Writes: "It is for me"

Twenty people trying to hum purple. Sounds like broken refrigerators having a conversation. But something happens. People walking by stop. Look confused. One woman asks, "What is that?"

"What's what?"

"That—feeling? Sound? I don't know. It's like the air is sad."

Here's the thing nobody wants to admit: it's falling apart daily.

Karim keeps leaving, saying it's too organized. Then coming back because alone is worse.

The gardener forgets people's names. Calls everyone "seedling." Some find it endearing. Others find it condescending.

Yuki and Lulu argue about what they're measuring. Yuki sees purple. Lulu hears subsonic. Neither can prove they're perceiving the same thing.

"Maybe we're both just making it up," Yuki says.

"So what if we are?" Lulu says. But she looks scared when she says it.

New people keep showing up. Some woman named—I forget. Says she can teach everyone to expand perception.

"How?"

"Through movement and breath and—"

"That's just yoga."

"No, it's—"

"It's yoga."

She leaves. Comes back the next day. Teaches yoga. Nobody complains because at least it's something to do while wobbling.

The puppets are still here. Nobody knows who brought them. They're too big for the intersection. Keep getting in the way. But moving them feels wrong. Like moving gravestones.

Someone starts using them for protests. Not organized protests. Just standing with a giant puppet outside the bank. The puppet holds a sign: "I see what you're hiding."

Security doesn't know what to do. Can't arrest a puppet. Can't ignore it either.

Daniel's sensors keep breaking. He thinks the intersection has some kind of field that disrupts electronics.

"Or maybe my equipment's just shit."

"Maybe both."

"Definitely both."

He keeps taking measurements anyway. Numbers that mean nothing. Or everything. Can't tell.

Thursday—or some day—huge fight.

Started with resources. Who gets to use the intersection when. Whose practice is "real." Whether they need structure or whether structure kills what they're building.

"We need some kind of—"

"No leaders!"

"I'm not saying leaders, just—"

"Organization is violence!"

"Everything's violence to you!"

"Because everything IS violence!"

The Goon Ox lies down in the middle of everyone arguing. Just plops down. Orange hair spreading out like spilled paint.

Everyone stops. Looks at this creature refusing to participate in human bullshit.

"Maybe," someone says quiet, "we just take turns?"

"That's organization."

"Soft organization?"

"Soft violence."

But they try it anyway.

Janae starts writing something. Crosses it out. Writes "1. Everyone's confused." Stares at the paper. Adds "2. That's okay." 

Gives up. The Ox wobbles away. She writes "3. That."

Some days nothing happens. Everyone just sits around the intersection feeling stupid.

"Are we doing anything?"

"Are we supposed to be doing something?"

"I thought we were building alternatives."

"To what?"

"Everything?"

"That's too much."

"So we're doing nothing?"

"That's too little."

They sit in silence. Desta hums. Someone joins. Someone else. Not purple this time. Gray maybe. The frequency of giving up but still being here.

A reporter shows up. Wants to write about "the community of expanded perception."

"We're not—"

"We don't call ourselves—"

"There's no community, just—"

Everyone talks at once. The reporter looks overwhelmed. Asks to interview the leader.

Twenty people point at the Goon Ox.

The Ox is asleep. Or pretending to be asleep. Orange hair covering its eyes.

The reporter leaves without a story. Or with too many stories. Same thing.

Here's what I learned:

Nobody knows what they're doing. Not Desta with her humming. Not Karim with his checkpoint trauma. Not the gardener with his seventeen senses. Not even the Goon Ox.

Especially not the Goon Ox.

But they keep showing up. Keep trying to teach each other things they don't fully understand. Keep arguing about whether they're building something or just wobbling near each other.

Maybe that's enough. Maybe that's everything. Maybe asking if it's enough is the wrong question.

Someone new arrives. Young guy, nervous, says he thinks he might hear pressure ghosts but probably not, probably just anxiety, probably should leave.

Janae says, "That's normal. We're all probably wrong. Want to touch the Ox's hair?"

He does. Starts crying immediately. "Oh. Oh they're real. Or I'm crazy. Or both."

"Both," everyone says together. First time they've agreed on anything.

The work—if it's work—continues. Interrupted by fights, confusion, people leaving, people returning, the Goon Ox lying down when things get too organized.

Today someone's teaching breathing. Tomorrow someone's measuring nothing with broken sensors. Next day everyone argues about whether yesterday mattered.

But the intersection persists. The wobbling continues. The orange hair keeps catching light like a flag that says: "We don't know what we're doing but we're doing it together."

Maybe that's all revolution is. People who don't know what they're doing standing near each other.

*Coda:*
Communities don't learn to wobble together. They wobble near each other and call it community because "people wobbling in proximity" sounds too pathetic to be revolutionary. But maybe pathetic is what we need. Maybe admitting we don't know how to fix anything is the first step. Maybe the Goon Ox's orange hair is the only flag worth following—not because it leads anywhere, but because it's honest about being lost. The intersection isn't building alternatives. It's just where people go when alternatives have already failed and you need somewhere to fail together.
