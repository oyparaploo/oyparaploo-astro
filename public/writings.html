Writings

Forty-seven short writings, sorted into seven groups so you can start anywhere.

What Paraploo Is

  • The Book
  • This World of Paraploo

    An overview of the whole practice: invented language, recurring characters, its own rules, and marks between drawing and writing.

  • COUNTLESS: Always on the Ready to Move and Flow

    How the practice began as an itch, and what the names Paraploo and The Elder stand for.

  • Fun, with a Severed Half that Reads as ploo

    Explains the "ploo" in the name Paraploo through French-sounding words for rain, pleasing, and growth.

  • Spirit's Storied Shelter and Other Tops for Heads

    On the umbrella folded inside the name Paraploo, shelter without walls, with French phrases throughout.

  • Our Journey's Sad to Exhilarating Deep Dive

    A long account of the site as an umbrella dome, teardowns, play over work, and being an outsider.

  • Settle That Value, Floating Over the Land and Sea

    A long statement of how the work is built, what an Elder is, and the outsider test.

  • Mon'Day-key, in a Mist

    One paragraph describing "personnel experience" as play, nature, and well-being.

The Ink Marks

  • Paraploo's Marked Body of Playful Works

    The longest description of the ink-mark drawings: unreadable script, varying density, no focal point.

  • About Our Training That Includes Countless Anämehliēz

    Short paragraphs on marks that refuse to be an alphabet, and on respelling the word anomalies.

  • Every Paraploo Title Is Volunteered and Not Automatically Assigned

    Why the artworks here all have titles, with a short history of untitled art.

  • Paraploo's Floating Quarry

    Nine short sections separated by asterisks about a quarry, stolen ground, clocks, and research.

  • Exactly What the Dôme Wants

    An alphabetical list of 135 single words, from Accord to Youthfulness, with almost no other text.

  • Warm Gray Zone

    Describes the practice's dark, low-lit mood in film-noir terms, then notes that warm gray means compassion.

Breath and Dots

  • Why The Three Dots Everywhere

    Explains why every text here uses three dots instead of periods: breath, flow, and fun carrying weight.

  • What This One Slightly Hesitant Yes Is Made Of

    Swallowed air, belches, and gas as a way of saying what a "yes" and the dots are made of.

  • With Honesty

    Capitalized lines on leaving a work unfinished rather than sanding off its grit, ending "We All Do Better."

  • Underground Electric Charge Network

    Single lines, many with invented syllables, about a voice buried in ordinary speech and a seed in a basket.

Making the Work

  • A Little Bend Worth Enjoying

    A short note on working with a machine that cannot love back, and enjoying careful work anyway.

  • Hope Never Takes a Break

    A caution about labels and risk in the body of work, ending on hope as the win.

  • Unlabeled Disciplined Meaningful Organism

    Lines addressed to a machine, then a reply reading them as an ethic: discipline over swagger, accept the unlabeled.

  • Unexpected Favorable: What Is or Qu'est-ce que

    A list of half-finished questions, then a self-review of voice, titles, and dates, ending with Spirit's verdict.

  • We Just Pulled Our Best Carving Instrument Out of a Med-innocent Leather Tool Case

    Two paragraphs on a day's honest fun, Saint Francis, and merciful discipline.

  • Wearables Not Fitting the Body

    The writer's wife questions the site word "Dispatches"; the reply weighs replacements such as Passages and Doorways.

  • Keep The Bones the Same Spirit

    Loose sound-driven lines about memory, logs, and love, with a closing note on truth without dressing.

  • No Single Safe Direction

    Reading the grain of wood as a lesson in how people and trees behave under pressure.

  • Dissecting Didier's Cooling Eccentricity

    Short paragraphs with a joke about early risers and questions about staying odd, gentle, and cheap.

  • Bluff Stones Under a Giant Invisible Dome

    A reply about seven color words, elder voices, and building something with bluff stone under a clear dome.

Songs and Greetings

  • Good Day, Ever Which Way

    Greetings to the sun and horizons, then notes on greeting as a ritual, ending with a farewell blessing.

  • From The Back Love Song

    A long love song asking someone to come down from a tree, with stains, theft, and songbirds.

  • WELCOME BACK CAW

    One short paragraph in invented syllables about Haneda airport and summer flights.

  • Dew for Counting Daughters

    Five lines about sunrise, three daughters, and mended nests.

  • Yes, We're All In

    Softness as least dangerous: lambs, water carving a valley, invented syllables, and a melancholy piano song.

  • We Would Love to Get Inside Their Mind

    Five short passages with a boat, an anchor, an owl call, and an aching wrist.

  • Quintessence's Nonphysical Seat and Bleachers

    A short list of words like Upside Elder and Warm Trans-Parent, then a call to stand and read together.

  • Tip Top Crawling with Improv

    Colors and Character take a phone call, then lines on living with impermanence and finishing a drink together.

Jokes and Wordplay

  • Airport Wrong Windpipe Question or Incident

    A small dialogue at an airport explaining the phrase "wrong windpipe," with its French equivalent.

  • DAIRYAIRE

    Six joke lines about the human backside, ending in "Haha."

  • Their Hand is a Mess

    A short mock warning about a wrecked lifestyle that finds no problem.

  • Mercy, what is the meaning of this?

    Word play on bad news and used goods, moving through Lakota kinship and exile toward clemency.

  • Message Personal, Message Personnel

    A short bilingual note with invented syllables, wearables worn on city streets, and a need for fresh air.

The World Outside

  • Better Place

    Describes an underground network of roots and fungus as a shared place where breakdown feeds everything.

  • Exposing This Kind of Neglect

    Argues that governments commit the neglect they charge parents with, ending with questions in French and English.

  • Size of a Nation's Relative Swallow

    Short lines on climate change: melting ice, fires, acid seas, and not solving it this month.

  • Coloring for Outsider Institutions

    Colors matched to unflattering meanings such as yellow-deceit and beige-piety, with gray singled out.

  • This Meeting is About a Lot of Things

    A numbered list of future project ideas, then notes on land, mapping jobs, and secrets kept by works.

  • Another Question Please

    Notes on growing, losing control, and sensing with the whole body, likened to weaving on a loom.

  • Never On Such A Pile or Bag

    On pricing artwork: why fixed prices fail, and why haggling is a form of hospitality.
