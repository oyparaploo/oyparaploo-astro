Paraploo

The five figures called when the work is stuck are usually given as a roster and a set of functions. They also have addresses.

Marie Lafert is in the Skagit Valley north of Seattle, in a small house backing onto a creek, and the studio is a converted shed twenty feet from the kitchen door heated by a wood stove — clay on shelves along one wall, wheel in the middle, kiln outside under a roof. Fifty years at it. What Marie knows is that the hands understand the clay before the mind has language for it, and that the explanation would betray the listening. Resistance is information. When something pushes back, it's telling you about itself. And the line that reverses the order everybody assumes: the plan comes after. The plan is an explanation of what your hands already did.

Herb Sellers is in Santa Fe in an adobe house bought thirty years ago when Santa Fe was still affordable, courtyard with three apricot trees and a stone bench, library filling the entire back room. What Herb knows is that none of it worked. Thousands of books, dozens of credentials, and the understanding never arrived. The library lied. What arrived instead was the admission of not understanding, which is why Herb is the one to call when a piece has become an essay: the response does not make the piece smarter, it makes it more honest.

Nell Cahen runs fifteen seats outside Quebec City, two services a night, four nights a week, trained three years in Lyon and came back because Lyon was not home. What Nell knows is the middle. The carrot is not a soup and the soup is not just carrots, and the recipe describes the two ends and skips the part where the change happens. How long does it take? However long it takes. The sauce tells you when it's done. Not the clock. Not you.

Jean Amadan is between Minneapolis and Provence, born in the one and teaching in the other for forty years, going back several months a year. Plays piano. Composes when asked. Mostly listens. The wife died seven years ago and there has been no music since. What Jean knows is the negative space, and the knowing has a date on it: when my wife died, I learned this — presence is what is left when the obvious is removed. Which is why Jean is called for endings, and why the instruction is to end on what is not there.

Hahnu Youn is eighty-three and has migrated through many places and has no house in the sense the other four have houses. The practice does not own her. She does not belong to the practice. Weathered like an elder tortoise, refreshingly innocent, carrying seeds in her pocket for when I need reminding that I do not know what alive looks like. She gives no advice. She asks, and sometimes she does not answer, and the non-answer is treated as information rather than as absence — the question was not ready.

The four with addresses are asked about structure: the opening, the overthinking, the middle, the ending. The one without an address is asked what the piece is for, and whose land it lives on, and what it owes.

And the figures are known to be made. The making is not concealed and does not reduce them. The claim is narrow and it is testable: imagining what a specific named figure would say produces different answers than sitting alone produces. Not better answers. Different ones, arriving from a position the maker does not otherwise have access to — which is the only sense in which any of it needs to be real, and the practice says so.
