Middle Eastern Noir Cinema Soundtracks on Spotify

A NOTE ON SILENCE

Middle Eastern noir cinema operates differently from every other tradition in this collection. The defining directors — Nuri Bilge Ceylan (Turkey), Abbas Kiarostami (Iran), Asghar Farhadi (Iran) — deliberately use minimal or no score. Once Upon a Time in Anatolia is a 157-minute murder procedural with virtually no music. A Separation won the Oscar with ambient sound as its primary texture. The absence of score is the score. This makes the tradition harder to collect on Spotify but more aligned with the Oyparaploo practice than any other — the meaning arrives from the materials, not from the explanation. The wind is the soundtrack. The darkness is the soundtrack. The silence between two men looking for a body they buried drunk is the soundtrack.

Where scores do exist, they tend to be spare, acoustic, rooted in regional instruments, and used so rarely that each musical moment carries the weight of an entire orchestral score in another tradition.

ISRAEL

FRANK ILFMAN

Israeli-born composer. The most internationally recognized Israeli noir scorer. Old-school orchestral darkness in a genre dominated by electronics.

Big Bad Wolves (2013) — Quentin Tarantino's pick for best film of 2013. Black comedy horror-thriller about a suspected child killer's abduction and torture. The score won the Saturn Award for Best Music. Baroque, dark-fairy-tale orchestral work recorded with the London Metropolitan Orchestra. 18 tracks on Spotify. The main theme contrasts minor and major chords — a noir-ish mood with dark whimsy reminiscent of Prokofiev's "Peter and the Wolf." The film opens with five minutes of silence over slow-motion children playing, scored by Ilfman as "larger-than-life" orchestral noir.

Rabies / Kalevet (2010) — Israel's first slasher film. Ilfman's first collaboration with directors Keshales and Papushado.

Gunpowder Milkshake (2021) — international action noir with the same director team. Ilfman scoring their Hollywood crossover.

OPHIR LEIBOVITCH

Israel's most prolific film composer. Over 100 scores. Three Israeli Academy Award nominations.

Foxtrot (2017) — Samuel Maoz's war-grief noir. Venice Film Festival Grand Jury Prize. A father told his soldier son is dead. The military checkpoint as existential trap. Leibovitch won the Israeli Oscar for Best Music.

Magic Men (2014) — an Israeli man brings his elderly Greek father back to the Greek island where he was hidden during WWII. Memory noir.

IRAN

PEYMAN YAZDANIAN

Asghar Farhadi's primary composer. The scores are so spare they border on ambient — piano, silence, then piano again. The restraint is the architecture.

A Separation / Jodaeiye Nader az Simin (2011) — the first Iranian film to win the Academy Award. A marriage disintegrating in Tehran. Yazdanian's score is barely present — a few piano phrases across two hours. What music exists is devastating because of how much silence surrounds it. Search "Peyman Yazdanian A Separation" on Spotify.

The Past / Le Passé (2013) — Farhadi's French-language film. Yazdanian scoring an Iranian director working in Paris. The displacement is in the music.

About Elly (2009) — a group of friends at a seaside cottage. Someone disappears. Yazdanian's score is the sound of a vacation turning into a nightmare so slowly nobody notices.

A Hero (2021) — Farhadi's return to Iran. A man on temporary release from debtor's prison. The moral complexity scored with Yazdanian's characteristic restraint.

HOSSEIN ALIZADEH

Master of the tar and setar (Iranian stringed instruments). His film scores bring traditional Persian music into cinematic darkness. A 54-track "Persian Movie Soundtracks: Cinematic Iran" playlist exists on Spotify (1.7K saves) featuring his work alongside other Iranian film composers.

MAJID ENTEZAMI

Iran's most prolific post-revolution film composer. Over 80 films. Oboist and conductor. His work includes The Glass Agency (1998) — an Iranian hostage thriller that functions as pure noir.

TURKEY

NURI BILGE CEYLAN (as sound designer)

Ceylan's films use virtually no composed score. The sound design IS the music — wind across the Anatolian steppe, car engines in darkness, voices in empty rooms. This is the most radical noir soundtrack tradition in the world: the refusal of music entirely.

Once Upon a Time in Anatolia / Bir Zamanlar Anadolu'da (2011) — Grand Prix at Cannes. Men searching for a body at night. No score. The sounds of wind, cars, birds. "For a movie with virtually no music, the soundtrack was nonetheless powerful and haunting." Not on Spotify because there is nothing to put there. The film IS the soundtrack.

Winter Sleep / Kış Uykusu (2014) — Palme d'Or winner. A hotel owner in Cappadocia. Schubert's Piano Sonata No. 20 is the only significant musical presence — classical music used as a character's emotional armor, not as score.

About Dry Grasses / Kuru Otlar Üstüne (2023) — Cannes Best Actress winner. A teacher trapped in a remote Anatolian town. The sound of snow. The sound of isolation.

Three Monkeys / Üç Maymun (2008) — the closest Ceylan comes to scored noir. A family covering up a crime. The heat and silence of Istanbul.

LEBANON

KHALED MOUZANAR

Nadine Labaki's composer and husband. The Lebanese film music tradition's most internationally visible figure.

Capernaum (2018) — a 12-year-old boy suing his parents for being born. The score uses traditional Middle Eastern instruments alongside orchestral elements. Cannes Jury Prize winner. The score moves between tenderness and Beirut street-level survival. Search "Khaled Mouzanar Capernaum" on Spotify.

Where Do We Go Now? / Et Maintenant, On Va Où? (2011) — a Lebanese village where Christian and Muslim women conspire to prevent their men from fighting. The score blends Arabic folk with dark comedy.

THE SEED OF THE SACRED FIG (2024)

Mohammad Rasoulof's Iranian film made in secret and smuggled out of the country. Rasoulof escaped Iran to premiere at Cannes, where it won a Special Prize. Set during the 2022 Iranian protests. A judge's family fractures as the state demands compliance. The sound design — protest chants, gunfire, domestic arguments, phone footage — functions as the score. Real footage of the Mahsa Amini protests woven into the fiction. The most politically dangerous noir film in any tradition. Search for the soundtrack on Spotify — it may exist as a limited release.

IT WAS JUST AN ACCIDENT (2025)

Jafar Panahi's latest — inspired by his own imprisonment. Winner of the top prize at Cannes 2025. Panahi, who was banned from filmmaking by the Iranian government, made the film anyway. Nominated for two Oscars in 2026. The score details are not yet widely documented — search Spotify for availability.

THE GAP AND THE TRADITION

Middle Eastern noir does not have a gap the way Korean or Danish noir does — because the tradition was never built on composed scores. The silence IS the tradition. Ceylan has made five films since 2008 and none of them have a soundtrack album because the soundtrack is the world the camera is pointed at. Farhadi's scores exist but are so minimal they function as punctuation, not paragraphs. The Israeli tradition (Ilfman, Leibovitch) is the exception — fully scored, orchestral, closer to the European model.

The future of Middle Eastern noir scoring may be in the protest footage itself — the real sound of the real streets, as in The Seed of the Sacred Fig. The crime is not fictional. The investigation is the camera. The soundtrack is whatever the microphone picks up while the world is burning.

A WORKING PLAYLIST ORDER

A Separation (Peyman Yazdanian) → Big Bad Wolves (Frank Ilfman) → Foxtrot (Ophir Leibovitch) → Capernaum (Khaled Mouzanar) → Persian Movie Soundtracks playlist (various) → A Hero (Peyman Yazdanian)

Then silence. Put on Once Upon a Time in Anatolia without music. Listen to the wind.

The sequence moves from Iranian domestic noir through Israeli dark-fairy-tale noir through Israeli war-grief noir through Lebanese survival noir through the Persian tradition through moral-complexity noir and arrives at the Turkish tradition that proves the most powerful noir soundtrack is no soundtrack at all. Six temperatures plus the temperature of silence, which is the coldest and most honest of them all.
