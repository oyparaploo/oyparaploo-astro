Italian Noir Cinema Soundtracks

Otto Paraploo

Italian Noir Cinema Soundtracks on Spotify

ENNIO MORRICONE

The maestro. Over 500 film scores across six decades. His giallo and noir work sits alongside the westerns as his darkest, most experimental output. Died in 2020 at 91. His Spotify catalog is enormous — start with the giallo work and the crime scores, not the westerns.

L'uccello dalle piume di cristallo / The Bird with the Crystal Plumage (1970) — Dario Argento's debut. The score that broke Morricone internationally outside westerns. Slinky lounge noir bleeding into dissonance. "Theme for Julia" is the door in.

Indagine su un cittadino al di sopra di ogni sospetto / Investigation of a Citizen Above Suspicion (1970) — Elio Petri's masterpiece. A police commissioner murders his mistress and investigates his own crime. The score is sardonic, strutting, corrupt — the music sounds like the system that protects the killer.

La corta notte delle bambole di vetro / Short Night of Glass Dolls (1971) — Morricone's giallo at its most abstract. Dissonant, subconscious, jittering between rhythm and chaos.

Una lucertola con la pelle di donna / Lizard in a Woman's Skin (1971) — The main theme is divine. Psychedelic noir. Bossa nova rhythms, sharp dissonance, surreal beauty — desire and dread in the same phrase.

La tarantola dal ventre nero / Black Belly of the Tarantula (1971) — orchestral giallo. The most classically noir of the set.

The Thing (1982) — John Carpenter hired him specifically because of the giallo work. The score proved Morricone's darkness could cross the Atlantic.

A 13-track fan playlist exists on Spotify: "Ennio Morricone - Themes - Giallo."

GOBLIN

Prog-rock band whose arrangements defined the sound of Dario Argento's 1970s thrillers. Turned suspense into something almost danceable. The band that proved horror and noir share a heartbeat.

Profondo Rosso / Deep Red (1975) — the masterpiece. "Mad Puppet's Laugh" opens with a children's lullaby that becomes a prog-rock assault. The main theme is the most recognizable piece of Italian noir music ever written. Full album on Spotify.

Suspiria (1977) — not strictly noir but the score is the architecture that giallo-horror is built on. Whispered vocals, celesta, bouzouki, and the word "witch" chanted over prog-rock thunder.

Tenebre (1982) — Simonetti, Pignatelli, and Morante reunited without calling themselves Goblin. Synthesizer-driven, leaning into early 80s electronic textures. Minimized bass, favoring melodic atmosphere.

Zombi / Dawn of the Dead (1978) — Goblin's score for the Italian cut of George Romero's film. Shopping mall noir. Consumerism as horror.

Squadra Antigangsters (1979) — "Disco China." Poliziotteschi funk — the Italian crime genre where cops are as violent as criminals. The sleaziest Goblin gets.

Search "Goblin" on Spotify — their discography is extensive and well-represented.

PIERO PICCIONI

Jazz-based noir. Moody orchestrations for poliziotteschi crime flicks. Wah-wah funk for exploitation cinema. His son Jason found the master tapes in 2020 and the reissues have been revelatory. Ghostface Killah sampled him. DJ Khaled sampled him. The hip-hop world found him before most film critics did.

Camille 2000 (1969) — futuristic jazz noir. "Pearls" is an organ-led breakbeat perfect for sampling. The blueprint for hip-hop from deep in the Italian past.

Le Mépris / Contempt (1963) — Godard's film starring Brigitte Bardot. Piccioni's score is melancholy and gorgeous. (Note: this is technically a French-Italian co-production scored by an Italian — the borders blur.)

La Decima Vittima / The 10th Victim (1965) — pop-art noir. Marcello Mastroianni. The score swings between lounge cool and existential dread.

PIERO UMILIANI

The first full jazz soundtrack in Italian cinema. His library music and film scores have been sampled by Knxwledge and Curren$y. The man who proved Italian noir could swing.

I soliti ignoti / Big Deal on Madonna Street (1958) — Mario Monicelli's comedy-heist classic. Upbeat jazz that shifts the genre from neorealist angst to something lighter and darker at the same time.

Svezia, inferno e paradiso / Sweden: Heaven and Hell (1968) — the source of "Mah Nà Mah Nà" (yes, that song). But the rest of the score is moody, exotic, strange. Library music that became noir.

BRUNO NICOLAI

Morricone's closest peer. They studied together at the conservatory. Where Morricone painted in widescreen, Nicolai worked closer to the psyche. His giallo scores slither.

Tutti i colori del buio / All the Colors of the Dark (1972) — haunting vocal performances. "Bambole" and "Suggestione" balance tension and melody. Seductive horror.

La coda dello scorpione / The Case of the Scorpion's Tail (1971) — classic giallo scoring. Nicolai at his most suspenseful.

STELVIO CIPRIANI

Poliziotteschi specialist. The sound of Italian street-level crime cinema in the 1970s.

La polizia sta a guardare / The Great Kidnapping (1973) — gritty crime jazz. The Italian cop movie at its rawest.

Tentacoli / Tentacles (1977) — nature-horror noir with orchestral sweep.

FABIO FRIZZI

Lucio Fulci's composer. Dream-state noir — scores that feel like hallucinations.

Sette note in nero / The Psychic (1977) — "Tracce Sul Muro." One of the most beautiful pieces of Italian film music, hidden inside a giallo.

Paura nella città dei morti viventi / City of the Living Dead (1980) — Fulci's nightmare logic scored by Frizzi's nightmare music.

STEFANO SOLLIMA + MODERN ITALIAN NOIR

The contemporary inheritor. His father Sergio directed poliziotteschi in the 1970s. Stefano brought Italian noir into the 21st century.

Suburra (2015) — dark Roman crime. The soundtrack mixes electronic and orchestral.

Romanzo Criminale (TV series, 2008–2010) — the Italian crime series that revived the genre. Multiple composers.

Gomorra / Gomorrah (TV series, 2014–2021) — Matteo Garrone's film (2008) and the series based on Roberto Saviano's book. The series scores are on Spotify.

Adagio (2023) — Sollima's most recent noir film. Rome burning. Dark, gloomy, melancholic. Search for the soundtrack on Spotify.

THE GAP — 2023 to 2026

Italian noir cinema continues but the scored tradition thinned. Adagio (2023) was the last major Italian noir with a traditional score. Garrone's Io Capitano (2023) was migration drama, not noir. Morricone is gone. Goblin reunites for anniversary performances but no new film work. The poliziotteschi era is history. The giallo tradition lives in vinyl reissues — dozens of albums being repressed yearly — but not in new productions. The Italian noir soundtrack is the richest archive of the five countries, but it is largely an archive now.

A WORKING PLAYLIST ORDER

Investigation of a Citizen Above Suspicion (Morricone) → The Bird with the Crystal Plumage (Morricone) → Big Deal on Madonna Street (Umiliani) → Camille 2000 (Piccioni) → All the Colors of the Dark (Nicolai) → Deep Red (Goblin) → Suspiria (Goblin) → The Psychic (Frizzi) → Lizard in a Woman's Skin (Morricone) → Tenebre (Simonetti/Pignatelli/Morante) → Suburra (various) → Adagio (various)

The sequence moves from corrupt-system noir (1970) through giallo-jazz through heist-comedy noir through pop-art noir through psyche-noir through prog-rock horror-noir through dream-state noir through synthesizer noir through modern Roman noir. Fifty-three years of Italian darkness in twelve temperatures. The deepest catalog of all five countries — and the one most likely to surprise you, because the names between Morricone and Goblin are where the real discovery lives.
