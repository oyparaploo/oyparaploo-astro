From The Milk Is Still on the List and the List Is Still on the Counter and the Counter Is Still in the Kitchen

Someone else is scrubbing at a mark on a fork. The mark is a scratch. The scratch has been there since 2019. It's 2025 now. That's six years. 2,190 days. 52,560 hours of the scratch existing. Someone has tried to clean it approximately 2,190 times. That's once a day for six years. Sometimes twice a day. Sometimes the same person tries twice in one washing. Scrub. Look. Still there. Scrub harder. Look. Still there. The scratch doesn't care about effort. The scratch transcends effort. The scratch is eternal.

Was it always scratched? Was there a before-scratch era? Nobody remembers. Everyone remembers differently. The fork remains.

While coffee brews, someone forgets they're out of milk. They forgot yesterday. They'll forget tomorrow. The coffee brews anyway. Coffee doesn't need permission. Coffee doesn't care about milk.

The bird doesn't read the news. The bird didn't read the news yesterday. Won't read it tomorrow. It just builds. Built yesterday. Will build tomorrow. Twig by twig by twig. Each twig the same. Each twig different. The paradox of twigs.

People buy notebooks thinking this time they'll stay organized. They bought the same notebook last month. A different notebook that was the same notebook. They'll buy another same different notebook next month. The notebook industry stays in business partly because of morning list-makers who make the same lists. By Thursday, the handwriting has degraded into hieroglyphics. It degraded by Thursday last week. It will degrade by Thursday next week. Thursday is when handwriting gives up. Wednesday's hope becomes Thursday's scrawl.

The List That Lists the Listed Lists

Dental appointment Tuesday—but which Tuesday? Last Tuesday? This Tuesday? Next Tuesday? The Tuesday that exists in the eternal now of unclear scheduling?

The 14th remains the 14th while they remain uncertain about the 14th.

"Buy milk" goes on the list. "Buy milk" was on yesterday's list. "Buy milk" was on the list three days ago. The milk isn't bought. The list doesn't care. The list holds "buy milk" with infinite patience. The list has held "buy milk" before. The list will hold "buy milk" again. Sometimes it's "buy almond milk." The specification changes. The not-buying remains constant. The list accumulates versions of the same unbought milk. Regular milk. Almond milk. Oat milk. All unbought. All on lists.

They spend ten minutes sketching their coffee mug. The handle looks wrong. It looked wrong yesterday. It will look wrong tomorrow. Like a weird ear. Or a broken thumb. Or something that isn't quite either. They keep drawing anyway. The drawing doesn't improve. It doesn't need to.

What they don't mention: roughly one in ten people doing this will realize partway through they're wearing mismatched shoes. One gray, one black. One navy, one black. The colors are close enough. Everything is close enough. Nobody notices. Everybody notices. Nobody cares. The shoes don't match. The shoes have never matched. The shoes will never match.

You'll spend half of it composing arguments against people you'll never actually confront. The same arguments you composed yesterday. The same people you didn't confront yesterday. Won't confront tomorrow. The arguments get refined. More pointed. More devastating. More useless.

A neighbor is putting out trash bins. The same neighbor. Different trash, same bins. Or same trash, different day. They nod at each other. Both of them do that polite smile-grimace that isn't quite either. It's not a smile. It's not a grimace. It's the thing between. The thing that has no name. They did this yesterday. They'll do this tomorrow. This is the extent of their interaction, but it registers as human contact. This counts. It counted yesterday. It will count tomorrow. Neither knows if you're supposed to do this every single time or if once a week covers it. The social contract remains unclear. They follow it anyway.

The polite grimace-smile exchanged between neighbors who don't know each other's names happens approximately 3 million times per day in suburbs worldwide. That's 3 million uncertain facial expressions. 3 million people wondering if this is required. 3 million yesterday. 3 million tomorrow. The aggregate uncertainty of suburban acknowledgment.

At any given moment during work hours, someone is eating lunch while watching a utility crew where one person digs while three others supervise. This ratio has been consistent across all cultures and time periods where utilities have existed. One Mesopotamian dug while three Mesopotamians supervised. One Roman dug while three Romans supervised. One person will dig in 2075 while three supervise. The eternal equation of supervised digging.

This seems about right for most group projects. One does. Three watch. Sometimes four watch. Never fewer than three.

Their friend texts: "Did you see that article about—"
They reply: "Let's talk about literally anything else."
The friend sends back: "Fair. I found a recipe for banana bread that uses mayo."
They discuss this for ten minutes. Neither of them bakes. Neither baked yesterday. Neither will bake tomorrow. The banana bread remains theoretical. Has always been theoretical. Will always be theoretical.

No one knows why banana bread specifically comes up so often. It came up yesterday. It will come up tomorrow. Banana bread is the universal deflection. The safe harbor of conversation. Banana bread with mayo. Without mayo. The mayo variable in the banana bread equation that produces no banana bread.

Or a debate about whether that actor was in that other thing. They weren't. They weren't yesterday. They won't be tomorrow. But the debate continues.

That evening, they water a houseplant they've been neglecting. The same plant. The same neglect. The same watering that's both too late and too early. Never on time. It's somehow both dying and growing a new leaf. It was dying and growing yesterday. It will be dying and growing tomorrow. Plants are confusing. Plants have always been confusing. Plants will always be confusing.

Ask a plant person to explain this and they'll say something about nitrogen. It's not nitrogen. It's never nitrogen. Nobody knows what nitrogen does. They said nitrogen yesterday. They'll say nitrogen tomorrow. They don't know either. They're just guessing.

The "one good thing that happened today" journal technique, recommended by therapists, frequently includes entries like "remembered to eat lunch" and "answered that email from last week." The bar is on the floor. The bar was on the floor yesterday. The bar will be on the floor tomorrow. We step over it. We've always stepped over it. "The car started"—cars are supposed to start. "Found matching socks"—socks exist in pairs. "Plant didn't die"—the plant is dying, just slowly. These count.

Burned the rice a little. Burned it yesterday too. Will burn it tomorrow. It's fine. It was fine. It will be fine.

They try to write "Finished the project report" then cross it out because they actually only finished most of it. Most of it yesterday. Most of it tomorrow. Never all of it. They write "Remembered to eat lunch." Eating is not an achievement. Except it is.

Partly cloudy. Always partly cloudy. Even when it's not partly cloudy, it's partly cloudy somewhere. The universal partly cloudy.

Someone new will discover they've been using the wrong coffee maker setting. Someone discovered this yesterday. Someone will discover this tomorrow. It's always being discovered, never remembered. The coffee maker keeps the secret.

The Milk Remains (On the List, Unbought, Eternal)

The list is patient. Has always been patient. Will always be patient. The list has nothing but time. The milk has already expired anyway.

The plant has grown another confusing leaf. Lost another. Net zero. Always net zero. The math of houseplants.

The coffee is black because the milk wasn't bought. Was black yesterday. Will be black tomorrow.

A neighbor walks by with their dog. The same neighbor. Different dog. No, same dog. They wave. This too is news—that people still wave.

The milk is still on the list. Was on the list. Will be on the list. The list is still on the counter. Was on the counter. Will be on the counter. The counter is still in the kitchen. Was in the kitchen. Will be in the kitchen. The kitchen is still in the house. The house is still standing. Was standing. Will stand tomorrow. Probably.

glued back together with coffee without milk and notebooks full of illegible reminders and plants that won't commit to living or dying
