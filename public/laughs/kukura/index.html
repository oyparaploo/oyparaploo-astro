From Kukura

Paloma whispered kukura to the doves each morning, though they couldn't understand her any more than the mail carrier could. The birds tilted their heads at the sound. They knew it meant seeds were coming.

B. W. Otto had driven eight hours on roads that forgot they were roads.

"Please," she said, setting up her equipment while a chicken investigated her shoelaces, "say 'peace dove' in every language you know."
Paloma laughed. Not cruel, not bitter. The laugh of someone asked to empty the ocean with a cup.

The soap opera languages, where everyone's tears looked perfect.

The doves—ordinary, shitting on her clean washing, oblivious to their symbolism—exploded into air. One flew directly into the window. Another landed on Otto's recorder, leaving a white streak across the microphone.
