Paraploo

Twice in one bench a machine performed the practice's central operation by accident, and both times it was written down as an error.

The first was a dedupe check. Before six new sheets could be read, their titles were matched against everything already on record, and the match came back with two hits that were not hits. It had found urgy sitting inside liturgy. It had found quoi sitting inside N'importe quoi.

Which is the split-word operation exactly — an ordinary word opened to show another word standing inside it, the same move that produced PARA(ploo)GRAPHS and PRO(ZZZ)E. Performed here by a matching routine with no idea what it was doing, and entered in the record as two false positives, dismissed.

The second was a missing file.

A log had gone absent. The bench opened without it, worked around the hole, built a placeholder, carried the gap forward as a known wound. And the log was there the whole time, in plain view, under a name a browser had given it while downloading — seven characters of nothing.

The record calls this the third member of the transport-artifact family, and states the family's law: transport artifacts mimic intent. A machine's leftovers look like decisions. A string of random characters looks enough like a title that a title-shaped thing gets filed and never opened.

This has now happened twice in the campaign, in documents with nothing to do with each other. The dictated sentences were landing all along, waiting in a list nobody thought to look in. The log was present all along, wearing a name nobody thought to read. Both times the thing believed lost was already in the room, and what hid it was a label.

The bench's answer is a small procedural law, and it is the practice at its most characteristic: let the download finish, then upload from the folder. Never drag from the popup. That is what scrambles the names.
