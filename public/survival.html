Survival of Humanity

There is a sound that has been running so long we stopped hearing it. It says the work is pointless because the ending is already on its way, and it has said this every day for so many days that it no longer sounds like a sentence. It sounds like the room. We take it for silence and take the weight of it for weather.

We cannot argue with it. Every argument goes into the same ledger it keeps, one more entry weighed against the ending, and the ledger always wins because it is kept in the head and the head is where arguing happens. The machine that frightens us lives there too. It has no hands. It wins every contest of results, and the ledger is a contest of results.

So the way out was never an answer. It was a change of room. When the hand goes down to the pen, the hand is not weighing anything. A finger on paper cannot hold a number. It is the one place in us the ledger cannot enter and the one place the machine cannot follow. That is the whole of the method and the whole of the mercy: not a thought that beats the sound, but a hand that leaves the room where the sound is.

But nothing can be set down that was not first noticed as carried. So the entrance is this: hear the sound again. Then go find the hands.
