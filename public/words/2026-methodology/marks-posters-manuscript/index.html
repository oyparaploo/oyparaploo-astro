The opening: the first morning

The record begins on October 18, 2026, with this entry, quoted as it was written that morning:

> Marks poster, first still — October 18, 2026
>
> Drawing: No Name Terre, catalogue 20968 (LOCKED MARKS\MOST RECENT (Three Volumes)\VOLUME THREE (60)\No Name Terre.tif, 27745 x 20081, 16-bit grey). Category Pond.
> Photograph: LOCKED PHYSICAL ACTUAL\LIGHT FOR MARINE HUES (40)\Forelight For A Play Goldmarinelife 4.TIF (6000 x 4000, 16-bit, Adobe RGB). Trimmed a little at the sides to cover the drawing's shape; never stretched.
>
> Method (make_poster.py, in this folder): the drawing's own ink tones decide the mix, never a pure black cut. Inside the ink the photograph is sharp, colour raised 1.2, deepened 42 per cent. Outside it is blurred (width/360) and paled 66 per cent toward warm paper, colour 0.55. Within a wide soft reach of the marks (width/260) the blur and paleness ease back (paled 42 per cent), so the space around the marks is drawn in by them. Nothing is generated; every pixel is the drawing or the photograph.

What the project set out to do, in Reach's words from that first day: large posters of the Marks drawings, about four by five feet, to sell, in a technique that is new. The drawing filled with and surrounded by photographic material from his own photographs, so that it reads as one intricate, lucid, sharp, textural image, photographic on top of photographic, never a cutout, never a known craft. His reason: "the patterns are very strong but graphic and simple," and "a larger audience loves to see more detail as textures and colours interacting, as with Van Gogh, expressionistic pictures with an obvious sense of hope." He called the flowing-pieces direction, found that same evening, a possible first in the world of art.

The rules he set on October 18 held through everything that followed, and where one was changed, the change is recorded in its section. Every pixel is his drawing or his photograph; nothing generated. The drawings are never redrawn, reshaped or thresholded. Nothing on the print is ever out of focus. Every photographic piece is used at its own pixels, only cut and turned, never enlarged. Nothing is asked of Reach except judging what he sees on screen. His originals are only read, never written to.

Two later rulings changed the shape of the work. On October 21 he set aside the four-by-five-foot size: the finished pieces have no fixed size in feet, but a finished piece is never smaller than 16K (12,800 by 16,000 pixels at 350 pixels per inch), later restated as about three by four feet. On October 29 his law for the whole drawing: a Marks picture is one design and is never cropped. And on November 2 the Ring final was ruled at 8K, 8,000 by 6,460 pixels at 300 pixels per inch, because at that size the museum photograph's pixels were crisp where at full size they were soft.

The telling of October 18 for any reader is HOW-IT-CAME-ABOUT-2026-10-18.md in the preview folder.

Date: October 18, 2026.

2. Scraps, dark marks on a pale ground

Date: October 18, 2026.

What it does: thirty scraps of 5,374 by 4,000 pixels are cut at random places from random photographs of one Placings set and laid on a jittered six-by-five grid, every scrap at its own pixels. The poster is divided among them by nearest scrap centre, the edges wandered by smooth noise and melted over about thirty pixels. Inside, outside and near the marks the treatment is as in method one.

Reference and drawing: Drink The Ocean Before You Burn Up over the Goldmarinelife photographs. Made at full print size: drink-the-ocean-SCRAPS-PRINT-60x43in-300ppi.tif, 18,000 by 12,956 pixels, 16-bit, 1.1 gigabytes, rendered in twelve bands of 1,100 rows.

Reach's word: this answered his scraps ruling. He then asked whether dark marks on light or the reverse was better, which gave method three. It breaks the focus rule (the ground is out of focus), so it stays in the technique book as "scraps-pale", run only by name.

4. Combed blur

Date: October 18, 2026.

What it does: a look test only, at one sixth of print size. The direction of the strokes nearby is read from the drawing, smoothed over about three hundred pixels; the soft photograph is averaged along those stream lines, and the more the strokes agree in direction, the more the haze is combed.

Reference and drawing: Drink The Ocean Before You Burn Up. Files combed-preview-*.

Why it stopped: Reach ruled the same day, no out-of-focus print. "Every part of the poster stays sharp; blur is out, including the combed blur." He had liked it, and the rule was kept anyway. It stays as "combed-blur", by name only, with no print-size file.

Page and scripts: index.html in the preview folder, and technique-book.html, which says its close piece is the look test doubled. Script flow_preview.py. Checked October 19: remakes exactly, all four pictures.

5. Lit, everything in focus

Date: October 18, 2026.

What it does: the answer to the no-blur ruling. Around the marks the scraps stay at full sharpness, their colour halved, dimmed eighty percent toward near-black where unlit and thirty-eight percent where the marks' light is strongest. The light map is the drawing's own ink carried along the stroke directions plus a little spread straight out from each line, made at one sixth size; it is light, not detail, so it is enlarged smoothly. Inside the marks the photograph is full and bright.

Reference and drawing: Drink The Ocean Before You Burn Up. Full print file drink-the-ocean-LIT-PRINT-60x43in-300ppi.tif.

Reach's word, October 18: it still reads as an image layer with a cutout over a light version of the same image. He gave two directions: the details flow within the line art, moving differently from the background but staying in focus; and the line art made of a different photograph, married into the background photograph, the two layers slightly interacting, one version where the background is affected by the line art and one where the line art is affected by the background. Those two directions gave methods six to eight.

Page and scripts: index.html in the preview folder; drink-lit-*. Scripts light_map.py, then scrap_poster.py with MODE lit. Checked October 19: remakes exactly. In the technique book as "lit".

6. Flowing pieces in the lines, and the thirteen iterations

Date: October 18, 2026, evening and night.

What it does: each mark is filled with sharp pieces cut from photographs at their own pixels, about sixty-four print pixels across and about three times longer along the stroke. Each piece is taken from a part of a photograph whose grain runs strongly one way and turned so that grain runs along the direction of the stroke at that spot, read from the drawing at the scale of one stroke; one photograph per neighbourhood so the grain carries on. Around the marks, the marine scraps, sharp, dimmed and lit by the marks.

Reference and drawing: Drink The Ocean Before You Burn Up, a 3,600 by 2,400 print-size piece at x 6,600, y 4,600; pieces from the ten Ignore The Fallen Leaves photographs; the ground from the Goldmarinelife scraps.

7. Streams under the lines

Date: October 19, 2026.

What it does: the ground is built from sharp pieces at their own pixels, narrow and long, laid in streams that echo the marks outward: close to each stroke they run alongside it, farther out they loosen into the drawing's own wide currents, the way Van Gogh's sky circles his stars. A direction field is read from the drawing; about four hundred evenly spaced stream lines are traced through it; every ground pixel joins its nearest stream, so the streams tile the ground with no gap; each stream is cut along its length into pieces, long where straight, shorter where it bends; one photograph runs the length of each stream; each piece's colour is drawn toward the broad colour of the old marine ground at that spot. Two versions: "the ground answers the lines" (streams hug each stroke and take some of the line's colour and light) and "the lines answer the ground" (streams keep their own currents; the lines take part of the ground's colour).

Reference and drawing: Drink The Ocean Before You Burn Up, the same test piece, under each of the three line treatments, so six pictures. Across the Grain and Dabs failed on the first try (the blue streams read as the shapes; the dabs sank into the teal) and were remade with a darker ground and brighter lines.

Reach's word: the background is cut so it moves with the line art, "but the line art still seems to be of another layer as opposed to setting into the background, having a different kind of texture that flows naturally in the spaces and or shapes between and around the marks and lines." The overall image must feel without a doubt unified.

Page and scripts: backgrounds.html in the preview folder, opened by OPEN-MARKS-POSTER-BACKGROUNDS.bat; files background-NN-name.jpg and -close.jpg. Scripts streams.py and lines.py (lines.py makes the three line treatments exactly as iterations 01, 11 and 13; labels.py the labels). The settings per line treatment are in NOTES.md and in techniques.py. Checked October 19: all six remake exactly once Streams 06's true recipe was found (its ground had been painted with the Across the Grain contrast).

8. Shapes between the marks: the calm ground and its twelve variations

Date: October 19, 2026.

What it does: every space between and around the marks becomes one shape (the open ground divided where it narrows; 119 shapes on the test piece). Each shape is made of one photograph, a texture unlike the lines. Inside each shape the narrow sharp pieces run in bands that follow the shape's own outline from the marks inward to its middle; each band is cut along its length into pieces, the joins staggered from band to band. The edge of each mark is the first seam of the ground beside it, so the line sits in the surface, not on it. Two versions again: "the ground answers the lines" (pieces of the line's own photographs take part of the first bands around each mark) and "the lines answer the ground" (about a quarter of the line's pieces are cut from the photograph of the shape beside them).

Reach's word on the six: he asked which Claude liked most and least; Claude ranked shape-02 (Straw Current, the lines answer the ground) first and named two faults in all six, the left third gone muddy and the pocket middles reading as flat slabs. Reach: "take your favourite, lighten that left side and cut the pocket middles into finer bands, so every shape keeps flowing to its centre."

The rounds that followed, each one change on his word: shape-07, Straw Current, lighter, finer bands (the ground's broad colour drawn toward the blue-grey of the middle; bands kept thirty pixels wide to the middle of every pocket; a six percent step of light from band to band). Then, after Claude named two things still to judge and Reach said "Yes, I agree with you": shape-08, Straw Current, calm ground. Photograph spots that carry small dark flecks are simply not chosen; the cleanest spots of the macro photographs were found to be outside their focus, which Reach saw at once ("gorgeous, but it's slightly out of focus, especially the background"), and was fixed with a denser spot list, a sharpness test and Lanczos turning; the line pieces on the left settle to the line's own gold. This is beautiful and new, and it stays exactly as made." And: "Seen whole they look like a magazine cut into tiny fragments, which is why the whole needs technique two." 9. Grain and Colour, the whole look

Date: October 19, 2026. Parked the same day.

What it is: Reach's vision for the whole, in his words recorded before any work: "an odd dream of fragmented sharpness and lovely soft gestures," with a third element, "fine details that are crisp, that can come from any type of natural material, Mother Earth material, that is more granular, so as to not disturb the simplicity." Technique two, the whole: no fragments at all. The picture is a few large, soft fields of colour that breathe into each other at their edges, like Rothko's brushstrokes; the drawing is where one colour meets another, and it must lead from across a room. The surface everywhere is one crisp natural grain from his photographs at its own pixels, so sharp it seems at first like real grains on the paper. The softness lives only in the colour and the light; the grain underneath is one continuous photograph, never blurred, never enlarged, never cut into pieces. Then an animated slideshow that dives from the whole into the close-up look, twelve dives for the twelve fragment versions.

What was done: step one, the grain search, over all 4,390 photographs in LOCKED PHYSICAL ACTUAL and 762 in the archive. The verdict, plainly: nothing in the archive is fine enough. The truest sand grain is in Holy Stoney 21, but its clear stretches are a twelfth of the test piece. The sheet of six candidates is grain-candidates-2026-10-19.jpg in the preview folder.

Reach's word on the sheet: "There must be a better way to do this. For what we are looking for my photographs I can tell right now are not going to be good enough for providing the type of realism that we will need." The flatbed scanner was offered and set aside by him: "A scan of material on the flatbed of my scanner will produce flat lighting, not natural looking lighting." The sand-tray photograph with one low light was offered and waits.

Why it stopped: his ruling, October 19: "Let's just abandon this whole recording grains of sand concept. I may revisit it, and most likely will revisit it down the road, because I like the idea of using a variety of materials photographed close up. But for now I just want to work with, artistically work with, the photographic content that I have, and try to have that accomplish the dream." Grain and Colour, the scanner and the sand-tray photographs are parked, not dropped. Steps two and three of the vision wait.

Scripts: scratch\poster-work\grain-search (screen.py, screen2.py, arch_thumbs.py, test_full.py with full.csv, make_sheet.py).

To make this at 8000 wide: nothing can be built until Reach photographs the grain himself: a shallow tray of fine sand or soil, the camera on a tripod pointing straight down, one low light from one side so every grain casts its own small shadow, frames overlapping by a third at the same height and light; one frame covers a test piece, about ten cover a whole picture, joined along the grain's own lines. Then step two as written in the vision: grain-01-NAME.jpg beside shape-08.

The soft-joins setting: Reach saw a jagged edge in every close view. The page was fixed first (one picture pixel on one screen pixel), and the edge was still there, because every join where one photographic piece meets the next is a hard one-pixel stair-step. SOFT=1 softens every join across about one pixel: each piece reaches one pixel past its edge, and along the join the two pieces share the pixels, each giving its own colour in the measure of how much of each pixel lies on its side. Nothing is blurred; away from the joins every pixel is exactly as before. One join on shape-08 was shown before and after, enlarged four times and at print size. The setting is off unless asked for (--soft in a batch) so every proven recipe still remakes exactly. Reach's word on the join is not recorded.

11. The massive experiment, rounds one to three

Date: October 19, 2026.

12. The surveys of four hundred, one to four

Date: October 21, 2026 (the fourth survey built October 21, picked October 22).

What it does: Reach turned the day toward completely different types of approaches to combining the marks with all kinds of non-marks pictures (photographs, symmetrias, collages, anything with a different look), across many black and white drawings, to learn which combinations work best. The ruling that the open spaces stay open still governs the posters; the surveys are tests.
