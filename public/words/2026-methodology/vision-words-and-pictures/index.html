Paraploo — The Vision for Words and Pictures

Written September 21, 2026, from Reach's own statements in the chat "Picture pages full run." This is a guiding document. It records the vision, the rules that protect it, and the order of work. Where Reach's exact words are quoted, they are in quotation marks. Everything else is a plain restatement for him to correct.

1. The vision, in Reach's words

The website should become "something that will be very valuable for the visitor."

A visitor arrives, looks at a menu, and is "able to know what's on the website and then be able to get to their interest." When they get there, they find "profound writing, mingling with this gorgeous imagery."

The archive holds "over 2,000 writings," many of them "very entertaining and meaningful," with "a combination of light humor" and "very deep concerns, existential concerns even." The aim is to get that accessible literary content "to mingle with the visual content in appropriate and surprising ways." Reach's judgment: "that's going to be the most organic way that will be most appealing for the visitor."

There is also the story of the making. Long manuscripts on the evolution, method and voice of Paraploo exist so that "the visitor is able to hear the story as art and then also have it be narrated as how the art came about."

The meaning must be "very accessible for every type of person, common speak." And the whole site must "look really professional."

2. What the visitor experiences

1. Arrives and understands within seconds what kind of place this is.
2. Reads a menu whose words a stranger understands at a glance, and sees what is here.
3. Goes straight to their interest in one or two clicks.
4. Meets the art: a whole picture, never cropped, on a quiet page, inside a slideshow if they want one.
5. Meets the writing beside it: a sentence or two of Reach's own words that echoes, contradicts, or surprises the picture. One click opens the whole piece.
6. Can hear how it came about: one quiet link opens the passage that tells the story of that work's making.
7. Wanders: from picture to writing, from writing to picture, from either into the story of the making, and back. Never trapped on one page.

3. The three streams

1. The pictures — about 15,000 on the site: abstract, semi-abstract, figurative and realistic.
2. The finished writing — over 2,000 pieces. The literary art itself.
3. The story of the making — the long manuscripts on how Paraploo evolved, its method and its voice. The narration.

The art comes first. The narration is always one click behind it, never in front of it.

4. The rules that protect the vision

- The words are Reach's, verbatim. Passages are lifted exactly. Nothing is reworded, summarized or invented by a model. A model chooses and proposes; it does not author what the visitor reads.
- Reach rules everything. Pairings come to him in a simple review list: picture, passage, kind of fit. He keeps or cuts. Only kept pairs reach the site. Only writings he has already ruled ready for the site are eligible.
- Three kinds of fit, and one kind that is banned.
- Echo: the passage and the picture share a feeling.
- Counterpoint: a funny passage beside a grave picture, or a heavy line beside a playful one. This is where the mix of humor and deep concern does its work.
- Surprise: no obvious link, but something sparks.
- Banned: the literal match (a dog poem beside a dog). It is the dull kind.
- Common speech. Every word a visitor meets, including menu words and labels, is plain: short sentences, nothing a person would have to look up, no art language.
- Silence is allowed. Not every picture gets words. A writing may earn one to five pictures. Several thousand pictures speak; the rest stay quiet. Silence keeps the pairings special.
- The picture is always whole. Never cropped, anywhere.
- Nothing is said twice on a page, and no file names show. Catalogue numbers are the public face of every picture.
- Professional finish. One template, one look, everywhere.

5. Ideas raised on the way, and where they stand

- Fable closely reading every image and writing what it perceives. Kept as a tool, not as published text. A model's perception is raw material: it helps describe and sort pictures so matches can be found. It is not what the visitor reads.
- One thread of loss and healing as plain-spoken prayer under every image. Reach adjusted away from this toward his own existing writings. Held, not dropped: loss and healing can be one of the concerns the matchmaking looks for, and could later become a guided path through the kept pairs.
- The worry that a cheaper model cannot perceive intuitively. Answered by design: the cheaper model never interprets. It only describes what is visible and applies themes that Fable has already named. Reach judges the result on a small trial before anything scales.
- Myths. Raised by Reach on September 21, after this document was first written. The archive already works like a personal mythology: its own invented names and tongue, marks that come before or after language, an elder's voice, animals, and an origin story. Loss and healing is the oldest mythic shape: going down and coming back. Use the shape, never the label — the word "myth" belongs nowhere a visitor can see it. Use only Reach's own stories; nothing borrowed from Greek, Norse or biblical myth is laid over the work. To be built into the master plan.

6. How it gets carried out, in order

1. Foundation: every picture has its own page. Stage two of the picture-pages run, finished and pushed September 21 (commit f36a5bfa). Every picture has a catalogue number. Every passage will later hang on that number.
2. Proof. One short read-only check that counts every picture against every page (in progress September 21).
3. The archive master plan. One document, written once, at Fable's highest effort: how the whole archive is sorted conceptually, what a visitor meets first, how the three streams find each other, when a page offers the story of the making and when it stays silent, and the common-speech standard. Trigger phrase in a fresh chat: "archive master plan." Reach supplies the folders where the writings and the making manuscripts live.
4. Three indexes, built once.
- Writings index (Fable, High): for each piece, the two or three passages that can stand alone, the mood (light, deep, or both), the concerns, the concrete things in it.
- Making index (Fable, High): the passages that tell how particular works or bodies of work came about.
- Pictures index (Sonnet): a plain note of what is visible and how it feels.
5. The trial. Twenty writings against one gallery. Fable proposes pairs and looks at the actual finalist pictures. Reach judges whether the matches feel appropriate and surprising. Trigger phrase: "words and pictures."
6. The small site build. A kept passage appears quietly beneath its picture with "Read the whole piece." The writing's page shows that picture with a link into its gallery. "How this came about" opens the making passage. Sonnet builds it.
7. The menu and the visitor's guide. Built once there is something worth being guided to, so the labels describe the real experience.
8. Scale, gallery by gallery, starting with the galleries Reach cares about most. Review lists arrive in batches he can rule in one sitting.

7. Who does what

- Fable, highest effort: the master plan only. One document.
- Fable, High: reading the writings, reading the making manuscripts, choosing passages, matchmaking.
- Sonnet, Low: describing pictures, building pages and menus, moving files, checks. This is most of the volume and costs several times less.
- Reach: the vision, the thread, and the final ruling on every pair.

8. Risks named ahead of time

- Sameness. Thousands of pairings on similar concerns will start to repeat. Cure: variety of fit, humor kept in the mix, and silence.
- The literal match creeping back in. Cure: the ban is written into every matchmaking instruction.
- A model's voice creeping in. Cure: verbatim only, and Reach reads everything before it goes up.
- Cost. Cure: Fable only where judgment is needed; trials before scale; gallery by gallery over weeks, not one sweep.
- The 20,000-file ceiling. After the full picture run the site holds about 13,900 files. Passages ride on existing pages and add no files, but the next big picture batch needs a decision first.

9. Decisions still open for Reach

- Which galleries and which writings go first.
- Whether a passage shows beneath the picture at all times or appears on a gentle hover or click.
- Whether the slideshow later carries an occasional passage between pictures.
- Whether loss and healing becomes its own guided path.
- The menu words, once the master plan is written.
