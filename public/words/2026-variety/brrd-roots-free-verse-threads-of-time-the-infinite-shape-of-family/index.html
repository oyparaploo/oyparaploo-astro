Steel, Sweat and Tariffs

Threads of Time

Brrd Roots ~ Free-Verse
Threads of Time:
The Infinite Shape of Family

This is a story—
not of fences fixed in formality,
but of hearts beating boldly in darkness and light,
bonds that bend and endure,
and love, as fluid and fierce as ocean tides.

It’s a heartbeat—
steady, shifting, soaring,
a rhythm written in humanity’s resilience—
its faults, its flourishes,
its infinite ability to rise.

Why does the conservative right long so fiercely
for the nuclear family of the 1950s?

Picture this:
a suburban street serenaded by cicadas,
houses humbly standing like sentinels,
their windows glowing with golden light.

Dad strides inside, his briefcase brimming,
Mom moves with measured grace,
the roast resting, steaming softly
as though praying toward the heavens.

Children’s laughter spills like stardust
into the shadows of dusk,
a melody of innocence,
fireflies blinking as night blooms.

For many conservatives,
this isn’t just memory—
it’s anchor, sanctuary, salvation
etched deep in nostalgia’s embrace.

(And let’s marvel at Mom’s mastery:
dinner ready, chaos calm—
a balancing act worthy of applause.)

But behind the fence lies complexity.

Picture a neighbor—
a widow threading days together,
her hands weathered by shifts,
her whispers weaving bedtime tales of bravery,
each story carrying the weight of entire universes.

Or imagine another home—
its walls thick with unspoken truths.
A daughter admits her secret,
but her words meet frost
instead of flame.

Her tears carve invisible rivers
down cheeks bent in hope,
while her father busies himself with faucets,
as though plumbing could mend hearts.

(And yes, duct tape’s reputation for fixes is… overstated.)

The nuclear family rose in dawn’s splendor—
a hymn sung to post-war prosperity.

Fathers tread pathways to office desks,
their steps sure, steady, certain.
Mothers, cloaked in quiet strength,
curated lives of ritual and routine,
each meal, each chore
sacred in its offering.

Together, they seemed indomitable—
a foundation fixed in certainty.

Yet beneath polished perfection,
cracks ran deep.
Not everyone’s light was made to fit its mold.

Picture Linda, 1955—
pearls shimmering, apron snug,
her movements elegant, precise.

But under moonlit silence,
her tears fell,
each drop catching the constellation’s glow—
dreams deferred, visions dimmed.

“If I bake one more casserole,”
she whispers,
“I might just fly away.”

And Timothy—
his footsteps tracing the same lawn,
his mower’s hum rising like prayer,
his gaze fixed on horizons untouched.

“Is this all there is?” he wonders,
“Grass clipped, days repeated, fences painted
without end?”

The universe doesn’t answer.
But his lawn gleams immaculate—
his longing lingering softly in its shadow.

Faith wrapped the nuclear family in certainty.

To many,
it wasn’t just structure;
it was design, divine, destined—
etched in holy scripture’s blueprint.
Its balance ordained,
its harmony sealed.

Yet even sacred blueprints carried echoes.
Doubt whispered beneath faith’s glow:
“Is this enough? Is this all?”

Politically, the nuclear family burns like a beacon.

Amid upheaval,
it promises simplicity—
refuge, return, resolution.

But beacons cast shadows long enough
to oversimplify the truth—
that families bloom
in infinitely diverse forms.

Beyond the fences lives humanity.

Picture Danielle—
a mother stretching toward sunrise,
her mornings cracked with chaos.

She forgets lunch money,
she raises her voice,
and guilt clings quietly by dusk’s light.

But her love remains—fierce, bold, unbending—
binding her family in resilience.

Picture Aaron and Michael—
fathers fumbling with braids
beneath starlit skies.

“Dads, this is crooked,”
their daughter giggles.
“Do you even care?”

Their laughter carries through constellations,
imperfect yet infinite,
their bond written among the stars.

Imagine Sarah—
her laughter muted in her solitude,
her shoulders heavy with loneliness
as her son picks cereal bowls day after day.

But then he smirks,
his face soft against her worst joke:
“What do you call a cow with no legs? Ground beef!”

“Mom,” he says,
“your jokes are bad.
But I kinda love them.”

The nuclear family feels like anchor—
steady, sure.
Yet anchors, too, can stall the tides.

To rise,
families must loosen grips,
embracing their infinite forms,
boundless expressions.

Beyond the fences,
stories bloom wildly.

Picture Lydia—
her laugh loud enough to scatter stars
across uncharted skies.

She didn’t match the mold,
but she mapped new constellations
from light only she could see.

Family isn’t diagram,
isn’t conformity.

It’s laughter over burnt dinners,
apologies whispered in shared beds,
mismatched socks folded in love.

Progress doesn’t erase tradition—
it magnifies it,
inviting families to flourish—
wide, wild, wondrous.

Let’s not just admire the past.
Let’s learn.

The beauty of family breathes
in its ability to adapt,
in its unyielding resilience.

Family is infinite—
a sacred thread binding struggle to hope,
woven deeply into what endures.
