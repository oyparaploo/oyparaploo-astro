Taking Words Apart

Strident to Unpeasant Enemy of Words

( no not unpleasant )

We are against words ... It took six years and 1,820 sheets of our own handwriting to be sure ...

The fight started on the page ... A word would come out of the pen and sit there, pleased with itself, swinging its feet ... and we put a line through it ... The next one we scribbled on until it was a black knot ... A knot cannot be read ... We have tied thousands ...

Some words would not die on the spot, so we roped them ... one word in a loop ... three words in a loop ... a whole family in one long loop shaped like a sausage ... Then a line from the loop clear across the page, with an arrow on the end, so the rest could see where they were headed ... It was not enough ... Words got through ... “Birds” got through and sat there, plain as a bird ... “Duck” got through and went to sit with “Birds” ... “Stains” got through and sat by itself ...

Then one of us held a page up to the window ...

The words on the back side showed through the paper ... faint ... gray ... and every one of them backwards ... They had their loops and their lean ... They could not say a thing ...

The back of the page had been doing our work for years and had never once been thanked ... We sent a card ... blank on both sides ... Then we made it the law ... Any word that can still be read gets picked up, turned to face the other way, and set down right where it stood ... We want them to know where they are ... Anybody with a mirror and a free afternoon could get them back ... we are counting on people being busy ... They don’t answer to their names now ... We tried ... Whistling gets a little further ... “And” turned around looks like a small animal with its tail up ... “The” looks like a chair somebody tipped over ...

After that we tore ... pages into scraps the size of a hand, then a thumb, then smaller ... A word torn in half does not grow back ... What we had left was loops, hooks, sticks and springs ... One scrap had nothing on it but a coil, like the cord of an old telephone with nobody on either end ... We threw the whole mess at a wall ... It stuck ...

From across the room it looks like every gnat in the county has come to the same window ... Up close there is nothing in it to read ... We checked ... Checking meant trying to read, which was hard on all of us ... One of us enjoyed it and had to be taken outside ...

What would not stick we swept into a heap ... The wall behind the heap has eyes in it ... They are faint, in pencil, and none of us will own up to them ... They follow the broom ... The heap came up higher than our heads ... Two pages rode up out of the top nearly whole, at a tilt ... and the top one has “say” on it, big enough to read from the door ... We let it stand ... Every dump needs a flag ... this one can say “say” until it rots ... The whole heap looks like the town dump after a snow ... Wonderful Garbage ... We stood there and loved it ...

Then we got ambitious ... more dead words than we knew what to do with ... We packed a load of them down so tight they turned back into wood, with grain and knots ... Paper comes from wood, so we call that one sending them home ... With the rest we built ... a street corner with an awning and a flight of steps ... a bridge ... islands ... a mountain with snow on it ... and a stand of trees that had already burned, which saved time ... A skull turned up in the middle of all this ... Nobody had ordered a skull ... We kept it ... There is not a street sign in the whole town ... There is one sign ...

Then we took the paper away ... That was the big one ... Without paper the words had nothing to stand on, and down they went ... We had a well ready ... They went in around the rim and down in rings ... round and round ... the big loud ones at the top ... the small ones under them ... and the smallest at the bottom, where it is white and quiet and nothing is written ... We stood at the edge and looked down for a long time ... One of us waved ...

Some we blew up ... They flew out from the middle in every direction at once, and they have not landed ... Somebody is standing in that one ... small ... drawn in pen ... arms down ... looking the other way ... Whoever it is has not been hit yet ... None of us put anybody there ...

A few we pinned to a pinwheel and left for the wind ... The wind does not read either ... We get along ...

Others we laid down flat in long rows, and that turned out to be a sea ... It has a sun on it, half up, made of a 2, a 4, a 6 and some dots ... Numbers are somebody else’s fight ... they can stay ... The rows run back one behind another, out to where a row is too small to see ...

Our best country is the gray one ... packed with writing from one edge to the other, so tight that nothing in it can move ... Through the middle runs one white river with next to nothing on it ... a few dashes ... one x, floating on its back ... It goes all the way back to where the land stops ... Above that is the sky, and there is not a mark on it ...

Here is the part we did not plan ... The pen came through fine ... It does not say anything now, and it is having the time of its life ... It runs all over everything, faster than before, looping and jabbing and going off the edge ... It comes back with nothing in its mouth ... so proud ...

People come and look ... They say it looks like weather ... They say fur, smoke, a flock of something ... Somebody small says noodles, and is the closest ... Then they ask what it says ... We say “Nothing” ... Then we have to go and lie down, because that was a word ...

So is all of this ... We know ... Every word here is on loan, and Friday they go down the well ... Our own handwriting came first because it was closest ... English handwriting in general is next ... After that comes European and American text, the printed kind, which stands very straight and thinks that will save it ... Then, when we can catch them, Latin American words spoken out loud ... Those are quick ... and there is no paper to tear ... We have a butterfly net and high hopes ...

Unpeasant is not a word ... That is why we wear it ... Strident was the start ... the scribbling stage ... the loud one ... We have since made unpeasant ... There was a small party, with no speeches ...

One word is still standing ... It is on the sign, in capital letters, in a place we built out of everything we wrecked ... It says ASKING ... The one we took outside comes back to look at it most days ... We are not going to answer it ...
