Discipline of This That and The Other Thing

← Words / 2026 Variety

Temporary absence is the only kind that tells the truth because permanent absence is just death and permanent presence is just surveillance ... but temporary — that's the breath between heartbeats, the door that swings both ways, the elder who steps out of the room so the children will finally say what they actually think and then steps back in carrying a plate of something that answers the question nobody had the courage to finish asking ... and those harmonious veins running underneath the mosaic aren't decorative, they're circulatory, they're keeping the whole textile alive the way a system of roots keeps a prairie alive long after someone paved over the visible part and called it a parking lot ... the glimpses of specific isolated memories are the most dangerous material because isolation is where memory gets honest, where it stops performing for the family reunion version and says actually that afternoon smelled like iron and sage and the screen door didn't latch ... and a floating-into-a-frame accidental angel is what happens when craft becomes so quiet it looks like luck, looks like something that just appeared, the way a face appears in a stain on a wall and the committee says it's a miracle but the wall knows it was just weather and time and the particular calcium content of the water that ran down it for forty years ... and that Dakota elder woman's goat milk crate is the archive, the original filing system, wooden and splintered and holding everything that institutional preservation will never touch because it doesn't have a barcode ... the rotisserie wrist doesn't rotate for applause it rotates because the meat will burn on one side if it doesn't and feeding people is not a metaphor it is the thing itself ... and the subsistence cuisine disappearing gradually is disappearing because someone replaced the recipe with a supply chain and the supply chain doesn't know what the wrist knows and doesn't care to learn ... so the adobe brick wall stands there sun-dried and holding its own temperature the way only earth buildings can, cool inside when the world outside is burning, and behind it the recipes aren't secret because someone locked them away, they're secret because nobody with a clipboard ever thought to ask the right woman the right question at the right time of day ... and getting your arms wet up to the pits means you're not reaching in politely, you're submerged past the point of keeping your clothes clean, past the point of observation, you're in it now and whatever you pull out is going to smell like you were there and that smell doesn't wash out and wasn't meant to.

Zaca endurance is not the kind that gets a documentary ... it's the kind that gets a callus so deep the skin stops distinguishing between the hand and the tool and the material being worked ... sewing that doesn't announce its stitches because the stitch is not the point, the holding together is the point, the way a wound closed by a grandmother's thread heals differently than a wound closed by a surgeon's staple — both shut but only one remembers who did the shutting ... and weaving is just organized stubbornness, thread over thread over thread refusing to be ordinary string, refusing to stay singular, insisting on becoming cloth through repetition so patient it looks like meditation to people who have never had to make the thing their family sleeps under ... and carving takes away, carving is the only art that makes by removing, the finished figure was always inside the wood and the carver's job was to know what didn't belong and cut it free, which is the opposite of accumulation, the opposite of everything a market economy taught us about value ... and those hand-mottled gloves — mottled because the dye came from the earth and the earth doesn't produce uniform color, the earth produces this brown next to that brown next to a brown that is almost purple if the iron content was high enough in that particular handful of soil — erased from historical narratives not because they were lost but because the narrative needed clean hands, needed the gloves off, needed the labor to look mechanical so the product could look inevitable ... Oaxaca pinching abstract signifiers is the moment the living object became a sign for itself, the moment someone looked at a carved devotion figure and said this represents something instead of saying this is something, and that pinch — that small academic gesture of lifting the thing out of its life and holding it up to the light of theory — cracked it, the way you crack a seed casing except nothing grew from this crack, the smashed objects of devotion didn't break because they were fragile they broke because the frame they were forced into was the wrong shape and the wrong size and insisted on being the right one ... and the empty gloves are the permanent exhibit, the display case with nothing in it except the shape of where hands used to be, labor still visible as a negative space the way a footprint in dried mud outlasts the foot by centuries ... and the wearers' presence recurs because you cannot erase a body that made something, you can remove the name and burn the record and pave over the workshop but the object still holds the torque of the wrist that shaped it, still carries the pressure of specific fingers, and those fingers belong to someone whose grandchildren are still alive and still sewing and still weaving and still carving in places the system labeled irrelevant because irrelevant is what you call something you failed to convert into currency.

Gleaning is what you do after the harvest when the official gathering is finished and the machines have gone home and what remains is whatever the system considered too small or too bruised or too close to the dirt to be worth bending down for ... and that is where the actual nutritional content lives, in the partially intact, in the history that has a hole in it the size of a deliberate omission but the hole has edges and the edges tell you exactly what shape the missing piece was ... anti-artful is the corrective, the refusal to make the remains beautiful because beautiful is what got them put behind glass in the first place, beautiful is the category that separated the object from the hands that made it and the mouth it was meant to feed, and archaeological remains that resist artfulness are just saying we are evidence not decoration, read us like a crime scene not a gallery wall ... and wrestling with systemic manifestations is the longest fight because the system doesn't hold still long enough to get a grip on, it shifts its weight every time you think you've pinned a shoulder, it renames itself, it puts on a lanyard and calls itself a conference, and the wrestler's body remembers every hold that almost worked which is its own kind of archive more reliable than the minutes of any meeting ... so the issues become tactile because abstraction is where power hides, abstraction is the fog machine, but the moment you make something you can touch you've given grief a handle and a handle is the beginning of a tool and a tool is the beginning of refusing to accept the way things are ... and what is missing gets a shape from what is present the way a mold holds the negative of the thing it was built to cast, and reclamation doesn't mean restoration because restoration pretends the damage didn't happen and reclamation walks in with the damage still showing and says this is mine now including the parts you broke ... and disappearance as strategy is the elder move, the move that understands presence can be confiscated but absence cannot be seized, you cannot arrest someone who isn't there, you cannot catalog what refuses to appear, and that disruption — that hole in the archive where the living thing used to be — that hole has more gravitational pull than the shelves around it ... and all of this, every bit of this accumulated weight and witness and systemic wrestling and loss registered in the hands, arrives finally and inevitably at its truest form which is a round crocheted horse keister doily — because someone's great-aunt made that, made it with thread and a hook and time that nobody was paying her for, made it to protect a chair from the sweat of a workhorse's rump, which is the most anti-artful purpose imaginable, and the crochet pattern is a mathematics older than the university that would later call it folk craft, and the round shape is the shape of every calendar that predated the grid, and the horse's keister is the part of the horse that faces you when the horse is doing its work and moving forward, which means the doily honors the direction of labor not the direction of display ... and if the chaos of everything that was taken and smashed and erased and systematically rendered irrelevant can be translated into anything at all, it would be translated into exactly this — a small round handmade thing that was never meant to be important and outlasted everything that was.

Totemic punctures are not holes they are mouths ... openings that speak in a frequency the merchant understands in his stomach before his ledger catches up because the merchant's vulnerability is not theoretical it is the space between what he owes and what he is owed and that space has a perimeter and the perimeter is not a wall it is a membrane, a skin that lets certain things through and holds other things back the way a market stall's canvas holds rain but not wind and the merchant stands inside that partial shelter knowing the difference between covered and protected ... and economic instability is not a condition it is a weather system, it moves through a community the way a pressure front moves through a valley and the ones who feel it first are the ones closest to the ground, the ones whose love of community is not a slogan on a grant application but is the actual reason they left their door unlocked so the neighbor's kid could eat something before school, and that unlocked door is the economy, the real one, the one that runs on trust and Tupperware that never gets returned and doesn't need to be ... and the Tabasco hawk vessels are the point where utility and ceremony refuse to separate, palm leaves because the palm gives without dying, gives its leaves the way an elder gives advice which is to say continuously and without expecting the advice to be followed, and copper screens because copper is the metal that changes color with exposure to air which means the vessel is a clock, it tells you how long it has been breathing, green means years, bright means new, and the hawk is the observer who sees the field from above and knows where the mice are before the mice know they've been seen ... and those vessels conjure temporal thresholds because a threshold is not a wall or a door it is the strip of floor where you are neither inside nor outside, where you belong to both rooms for exactly as long as your foot is on it, and temporal thresholds are the moments between eras, the Tuesday between the old economy and the new one, the afternoon between the last generation that knew how to weave palm and the first generation that was told palm weaving was not a marketable skill ... and forming a permeable relic is the act of making something holy that leaks, something sacred that admits air and rain and doubt because impermeability is for tombs and the relic is not dead it is just old and still porous and still in conversation with whatever passes through it ... and the crotch pain is real, is the body's accounting system, the place where the nerves converge and report what carrying and straddling and bearing down actually costs the flesh, and if you've ever sat on a wooden bench for nine hours selling what you made with your hands you know the crotch doesn't lie, the crotch is the most honest critic of any economic system ever designed ... and the flagellation rope is the same object as the doily is the same object as the hemp is the same object it has always been — fiber twisted by hands into a thing that can hold or a thing that can punish depending entirely on who is gripping the end of it, and it doubles because everything in a subsistence economy doubles, the pot that cooks the food also heats the room, the rope that ties the load also disciplines the child, the vessel that carries water also carries prayer, nothing is single-use because single-use is a luxury invented by people who never had to make one object do the work of five ... and the punctures remain, totemic, speaking, each one a small mouth in the perimeter saying we were here and the copper is green now and the hawk is still watching and the merchant's stomach still knows before his books do.
