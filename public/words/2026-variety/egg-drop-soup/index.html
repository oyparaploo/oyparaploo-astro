Egg Drop Soup

← Writings / 2026

Reach: What's cooking, Paraploo?

Brian: Egg Drop Soup.

Spirit: Show me the actual recipe, Angelina.

Four cups chicken or vegetable broth
A teaspoon of grated ginger
A teaspoon of soy sauce
One tablespoon cornstarch stirred into two tablespoons cold water
Three eggs, beaten
White pepper
A few drops of toasted sesame oil
Two scallions sliced thin
Salt

1. Bring the broth to a simmer with the ginger and soy.
2. Stir in the cornstarch water until the broth turns glossy.
3. Keep it at a bare simmer, stir slowly in one direction, pour the eggs in a thin stream, and leave it alone for thirty seconds.
4. Off the heat, pepper, sesame oil, salt.
5. Scallions on top.

Brian: Do you approve this recipe?

Reach: Yes, it holds up. The proportions are the standard: one tablespoon of cornstarch to four cups of broth gives it just enough body to hold the egg in ribbons, instead of allowing it to sink into shreds. And three eggs for that volume makes for a generous and heavy bowl. If you want it lighter/more brothy, drop to two eggs; everything else is the same. Note: the broth must be at a bare simmer, not a boil, when the eggs go in. A rolling boil will break the eggs into a cloudy scramble.

Brian: Ah, yes.

Paraploo, The Elder: Egg soup is the first soup learned and the last one we forget. Broth, an egg, a hand pouring slow. We have watched a boiling pot turn a good egg into rags or tatters. We tell this to the young ones and some of them will nod and still boil it. And after that they know. Some one will tell us that there are far better soups. Yes, there are. But this one is three basic things and a cherished wood spoon, and we make it during worst days...
