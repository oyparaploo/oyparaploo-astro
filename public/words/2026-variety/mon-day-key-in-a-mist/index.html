Locked August 4, 2026 · shelf #36 · crowned by the Mason

The determination, delegated once. The Mason handed the
what-is-it call to the Quarry for this piece alone. Ruled: a piece,
whole and finished — it closes, it does one complete job, it arrives
already in the elder voice. The delegation was the Mason's carve;
the lock and the crown stayed his.

The crown. Mon'Day-key, in a Mist — carved from the piece's own
close: Monkey opened into Mon'Day-key, three words folded into
one sound (Monday · monkey · key). The mist arrived with the crown,
nowhere in the body — the title adds weather the piece never names.

Quarry flag on the crown, logged not hidden. In a Mist sits
near a standing jazz composition of the same name (Beiderbecke,
1927) — proximity noted the same way the CAW television flag was.
The practice's jazz veins run deep; the echo may be the point.
The crown stands as carved unless the Mason re-rules.

The heartstring reads, on record before the crown. The Quarry's
diagnostic named where the piece plucks (beings in the first
breath · the solo-time line · the single admission of difficulty ·
the close) and where it runs coolest (the brochure middle, no being
in frame · the bonding vein, promised and unmined). The Mason
carved nothing — the piece locked as it arrived. **The restraint is
the ruling.**

A quarry version was set beside it (plain voice, periods, per
the foil exercise that same bench). The close would not survive

held; the exercise opened the door to method amendment M-A1,
ruled the same bench.

Grammar and punctuation pass. Run before the crown, clean —
zero fixes proposed. Terminal ellipses law honored start to close;
no periods, semicolons, or colons; ellipsis spacing correct
throughout.

Protected, never to be corrected. Mon'Day-key ·
