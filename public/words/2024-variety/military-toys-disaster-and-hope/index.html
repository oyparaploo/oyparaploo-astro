Military Toys Disaster and Hope

Words / 2024 Variety

What are the symbolisms of natural and artificial/material interpretations of damaged plastic military toys on a theater of war battleground?

The music of visual art teaches us that repaired brokenness is gold. We can be mended and transformed. Leonard Cohen’s quote, “There is a crack, a crack in everything. That’s how the light gets in,” emphasizes that brokenness can lead to positive growth. Cracks can become channels for illumination and understanding.

On the other hand, plastic toys, especially those representing military themes, carry a different kind of symbolism. They evoke innocence, playfulness, and imagination, but when damaged or broken, they reflect the impact of conflict on childhood innocence. These toys, once whole, now bear scars, much like the real-life war experiences.

While most plastic toys are pretty durable, they have a lasting environmental impact, persisting in landfills and oceans. Their brokenness reflects our collective responsibility to repair and protect our planet.

When child plastic military toys are placed in the context of a battleground, they become poignant. Their damage reflects the toll of war. The stark contrast between playthings and the harsh reality of war highlights the fragility of life.

The interpretation of damaged toys invites self-reflection. Can we find beauty in brokenness? Repairing, whether with empathy or compassion, becomes a process with the capacity to endure. The play of natural and artificial materials within the theater of war invites us to honor ways to mend our world.
