Humans Proving Something.

Words / 2024 Variety

Why do humans feel the need to prove themselves or prove something constantly?

Have you ever felt like you need to prove yourself? It's a familiar feeling that many of us struggle with, and all sorts of emotional and psychological factors can influence it. So let's dive into some of the underlying reasons that drive this need to prove ourselves:

** Feeling vulnerable and insecure:
When we feel vulnerable or insecure, we often seek validation from others to avoid rejection or judgment. If we don't have a strong sense of self-worth, we may constantly look for external validation, and proving ourselves becomes a way to feel valued and accepted.

** Comparing ourselves to others:
It's natural for us to compare ourselves to other people. When we see others as more successful or accomplished, we need to prove our worth to measure up. With social media, this has become even more common, as people post their most impressive moments and make us feel like we're not doing enough.

** Fear of criticism:
The fear of criticism or negative judgment can drive us to prove ourselves, especially if we're perfectionists who feel like any mistake could ruin our reputation.

** Childhood experiences:
Our childhood experiences shape our beliefs, and if we grew up feeling like we weren't accepted or criticized, we might carry that need for validation into adulthood.

** Identity and achievement:
Our identity is often tied to what we've accomplished, and proving ourselves validates our chosen roles ( like our careers or relationships ). Achievements give us external validation, and we want to show that we're good enough and successful.

** Fear of rejection:
Some of us are sensitive to rejection and feel we must constantly prove our worth to avoid rejection. Proving ourselves also ensures we're accepted within our social circles since we all want to feel like we belong.

** Cultural and societal norms:
We're taught that success is based on merit, and we must prove ourselves to get rewards. Competitive environments also make us feel we need to outperform others to succeed.

Recognizing these patterns can help us learn to accept ourselves and stop constantly seeking validation from others.
