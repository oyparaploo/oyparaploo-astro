MONTH OF MARCH

Words / 2024 Variety

Opposites Of Madness ~ Calm, Balance, Deep Spiritual Richness, Mindfulness.

Why is March spiritually and soulfully such a rich and fertile month?

March, the third month of the year, holds deep spiritual significance that is rooted in both history and astrology. Let’s explore why it is considered special:

Transition and Renewal:
March marks the transition from winter to spring in many parts of the world.
It symbolizes a time of renewal, rebirth, and growth.
Just as nature awakens from its slumber, so does the human spirit, seeking fresh beginnings.
Balance and Equilibrium:
The month begins with the spring equinox (around March 21-22), when day and night are equal.
This balance signifies a time to start fresh and make changes in life.
New Beginnings and Fresh Starts:
March encourages us to shed old habits and beliefs.
It’s an opportunity to embrace new paths, ideas, and opportunities.
Cleansing and Purification:
As winter fades, March invites us to cleanse our minds and hearts.
Let go of negativity and make space for positivity.
Fertility and Creativity:
Spring is associated with fertility and growth.
Use this energy to nurture your creative endeavors.
Courage and Boldness:
March inspires courage to step into the unknown.
Be bold in pursuing your dreams and aspirations.
Reflection and Self-Examination:
Take time to reflect on your journey.
Evaluate your progress and realign your intentions.
Growth and Expansion:
Just as flowers bloom, allow yourself to grow.
Expand your horizons and embrace the possibilities.
Remember, March isn’t just a month on the calendar; it’s a canvas for spiritual transformation and awakening.

What does it mean to have Springtime Faith and Celebration blood running through our veins?

Springtime Faith and Celebration intertwine like the delicate tendrils of blooming vines, infusing our veins with life and purpose. Let us explore this poetic tapestry:

Springtime Faith:
Spring arrives, shedding winter’s cloak. It is a season of awakening, hope, and resurrection.
Faith, too, blossoms anew. It is not mere belief; it is the sap that nourishes our spiritual roots.
In our veins, springtime faith flows:
Like the sap rising in trees, it stirs our dormant dreams.
It whispers, “The barren shall bloom; the broken shall heal.”
We trust in unseen promises—the thaw after frost, the dawn after darkness.
Our hearts unfurl like petals, reaching for the sun of divine assurance.
Celebration Blood:
Imagine our veins as rivers of celebration blood:
It carries the rhythm of joy, the melody of gratitude.
With each beat, it sings:
Of daffodils dancing in meadows.
Of birds weaving nests, celebrating life’s continuity.
Of lambs leaping, symbolizing innocence and renewal.
Our veins become ribbons of festivity, adorned with laughter and wonder.
The Blood of Christ:
In this poetic weave, we find the crimson thread—the blood of Christ:
It flows through our veins, not as mere plasma, but as redemption.
His veins bled on a rugged cross, birthing a fountain of grace.
We plunge into that flood, shedding guilt, emerging cleansed.
His blood, like sap, revives our dormant spirits, grafting us into eternity.
Springtime Communion:
Picture a communion cup filled with springtime faith and celebration blood:
The wine—a symbol of His sacrifice—tastes of earth’s renewal.
The bread—a broken seed—promises resurrection.
We sip, we chew, we remember:
The tomb’s stone rolled away.
The lilies trumpeting life.
The risen Christ, our eternal spring.
So let our veins carry this sacred blend: faith, like sap, rising from roots deep within, and celebration, like a songbird’s trill, echoing through our souls.

Does the putting on of ash to our forehead mean that our will body one day return to ashes?

Certainly! The practice of applying ashes to the forehead holds deep spiritual symbolism, especially during Ash Wednesday, which marks the beginning of the Lenten season. Let’s explore its meaning:

Ash Wednesday and Lent:
Ash Wednesday is observed by many Christians, especially within the Catholic Church.
On this day, a priest applies a smudge of ash on people’s foreheads in the shape of a cross.
As the ashes are applied, the phrase “remember that you are dust, and to dust you shall return” is often spoken.
This ritual serves as a powerful reminder of our mortality and the need for repentance.
Origins of Ashes:
The ashes used on Ash Wednesday typically come from the burning of palm branches from the previous year’s Palm Sunday celebrations.
After Palm Sunday, the palm branches are collected, dried, and burned to create the ashes used in the Ash Wednesday service.
Symbolism:
The ash symbolizes several profound truths:
Humility: We recognize our humble origin—“from dust”—as human beings.
Repentance: It calls us to turn away from sin and seek forgiveness.
Mortality: Just as the palm branches were once green and vibrant but now reduced to ashes, our bodies will also return to dust after death.
Hope: Despite our mortality, we look forward to resurrection and eternal life through faith in Jesus Christ.
Spiritual Reflection:
As the ash is traced on our foreheads, we contemplate our journey:
From dust to life.
From sin to redemption.
From mortality to hope.
In summary, the ashes remind us of our earthly existence, our need for repentance, and the promise of resurrection. They connect us to the cycle of life, death, and eternal life through Christ.

Can you tell me about the history of giving things that we like a lot up for Lent?

Certainly! The tradition of giving up something for Lent has a rich history within Christian observance. Let’s delve into its origins and significance:

Early Origins of Lent:
The practice of Lent can be traced back to the 2nd century.
During this period, there is evidence of a 40-day fasting and preparation leading up to Easter.
Lent was observed as a time of reflection, repentance, and spiritual discipline.
Development in the Early Church:
By the 4th century, the observance of Lent became more formalized within the Christian community.
The rules around Lent were initially strict:
People were allowed to eat only one meal a day, and that too in the evening.
Meat, fish, and animal products were off-limits during this period.
Purpose of Lenten Sacrifice:
The 40 days of Lent symbolize the 40 days that Jesus fasted in the desert while being tempted by Satan.
Christians participate in Lent to:
Imitate Jesus’ sacrifice and self-discipline.
Prepare their hearts for the celebration of Easter.
Reflect on their own lives, seek forgiveness, and grow spiritually.
Giving Up Something Meaningful:
During Lent, Roman Catholics traditionally give up something that means a lot to them.
This act of sacrifice mirrors Jesus’ own self-denial during his desert fast.
Common examples of things people give up for Lent include:
Sugary foods
Swearing
Social media
Meat on Fridays (some Eastern Orthodox Christians eat vegetarian or vegan on certain days)
Creative Lenten Traditions:
Throughout history, Christians have found creative ways to observe Lent:
Pinatas: Originally a Lenten tradition, pinatas represented the Seven Deadly Sins. Defeating them led to heavenly rewards (in the form of candy).
Pretzels: Shaped like folded arms in prayer, pretzels were popular during Lent when fasting required giving up meat and dairy.
Eels: Some monasteries collected rent in eels during Lent, as they were abstaining from meat. Medieval monks ate eels to suppress sexual thoughts while fasting.
Hot Cross Buns: These buns with a cross on top became a traditional dessert during Lent.
In summary, giving up something we like during Lent serves as a reminder of our mortality, our need for repentance, and the promise of resurrection through Christ. It’s a time of self-reflection, discipline, and spiritual growth.

What is the Ethiopian coptic Christian lent diet exactly?

In the Ethiopian Coptic Christian tradition, the Lenten diet during the Great Lent (which lasts for 7 weeks) is characterized by strict fasting. Here are the key aspects of this Lenten diet:

Plant-Based Diet:
The Ethiopian Coptic Christians adhere to a vegan diet during Lent.
They abstain from all animal products, including:
Meat
Fish
Eggs
Dairy (such as milk, cheese, and butter)
Allowed Foods:
The Lenten diet primarily consists of:
Whole grains
Cereals
Green leafy vegetables
Legumes (such as lentils, peas, and beans)
Fruits
Traditional Ethiopian Dishes:
A common dish during Lent is wat, a local stew.
Wat is typically served with enjera, a sour flatbread made from teff, a tiny, round grain.
Spiritual Significance:
The Lenten diet serves as a form of self-discipline and spiritual preparation.
By abstaining from certain foods, Ethiopian Coptic Christians seek to:
Purify their bodies
Focus on prayer and reflection
Emulate Jesus’ fasting in the desert
In summary, the Ethiopian Coptic Christian Lenten diet emphasizes simplicity, plant-based foods, and spiritual devotion. It is a time of self-denial and drawing closer to God.

Can pruning trees during spring represent Spiritual blessings of Self renewal?

Certainly! Pruning trees during spring can indeed symbolize spiritual blessings of self-renewal. Let’s explore this metaphor:

Physical Pruning and Spiritual Symbolism:
Pruning is a horticultural practice where branches, buds, or roots are trimmed to improve the health, growth, and productivity of plants.
When we apply this concept spiritually, it becomes a powerful metaphor for how God shapes us to grow in faith and bear spiritual fruit.
Spiritual Pruning:
Just as a skilled gardener prunes a tree to remove dead or unproductive branches, God prunes our lives:
Removing hindrances: God cuts away unfruitful areas of our lives so we can thrive spiritually.
Character refinement: The process can be painful, but it helps remove fleshly habits and worldly attitudes.
Increased intimacy with God: Pruning draws us closer to God as we surrender to His loving care.
Biblical Basis:
Jesus used the metaphor of pruning in John 15:1-8:
He is the vine, and we are the branches.
Every branch that bears fruit, He prunes to bear more fruit.
Abiding in Him leads to spiritual vitality and fruitfulness.
Self-Renewal and Spiritual Growth:
Pruning allows us to shed what no longer serves us:
Dead habits
Unhealthy relationships
Worldly distractions
It creates space for new growth, deeper faith, and a closer walk with God.
Celebrating Renewal:
As spring brings forth new leaves and blossoms, we celebrate the renewal of life.
Similarly, spiritual pruning leads to self-renewal, transformation, and a flourishing soul.
So, when you see a pruned tree in spring, remember that God’s loving hands are at work, shaping you for greater spiritual abundance.

What does "I Am The True Vine" mean historically?

Certainly! The phrase “I am the true vine” holds deep historical and spiritual significance. Let’s explore its meaning:

Biblical Context:
The statement “I am the true vine” is attributed to Jesus and is found in the Gospel of John, specifically in John 15:1-8.
In this passage, Jesus uses the metaphor of a vine to convey profound spiritual truths to His disciples.
Symbolism:
Vine: In the Old Testament, Israel is often symbolized as a vine. For example, Psalm 80 describes Israel as a vine brought out of Egypt and planted in the Promised Land.
True Vine: By declaring Himself as the “true vine”, Jesus emphasizes His divine nature and role as the source of spiritual life and nourishment.
Branches: Jesus refers to His disciples as the branches connected to Him. Just as branches draw sustenance from the vine, believers draw their spiritual life from Christ.
Spiritual Significance:
Unity: The vine and branches illustrate the intimate union between Jesus and His followers. We are connected to Him, drawing our spiritual vitality from His life.
Fruitfulness: Jesus emphasizes that fruitfulness is essential. A branch that remains connected to the true vine bears fruit, while one that doesn’t is removed.
Pruning: God, the vinedresser, prunes the branches to enhance their fruitfulness. Pruning involves removing dead or unproductive parts, symbolizing spiritual growth and refinement.
Abiding in Christ:
Jesus encourages His disciples to abide in Him:
By remaining connected to Him through faith, prayer, and obedience.
By allowing His words to dwell in them.
By relying on His strength and grace.
Eternal Life and Glory:
Jesus promises that those who abide in Him will bear much fruit and glorify God.
The ultimate goal is eternal life—a life rooted in Christ and bearing the fruit of love, joy, peace, and righteousness.
In summary, Jesus as the true vine signifies our dependence on Him for spiritual life, growth, and fruitfulness. As we abide in Him, we experience transformation and eternal significance.
