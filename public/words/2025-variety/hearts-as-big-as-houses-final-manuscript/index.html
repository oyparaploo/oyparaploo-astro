Play · 12 minutes

Hearts As Big As Houses

← Words / 2025 Variety

Final Manuscript

A Play in Two Acts

By [ B. Otto Will ]

CHARACTERS

SEGAL - 30s, counts emotions like inventory, works as hospital administrator
BREUT - 40s, bitter former social worker, self-medicates
CONSTANCE - 20s, sees future grief, works at hospice
CHERHAL - 50s, former monk, seeks meaning in suffering
YOUNG WOMAN - 22, newly awakened empath
CHORUS - 4-6 performers representing collective emotions

SETTING

A liminal space between therapy and theater. Chairs that become cities, walls that breathe, a massive translucent suitcase center stage.

TIME

Present day, moving toward an uncertain future.

ACT ONE

Scene 1

AT RISE: SEGAL alone, dragging an enormous suitcase (6' x 4', semi-transparent, on wheels). Sound of breathing, amplified.

SEGAL
(to audience)
Forty-three. That's this morning's count. Forty-three separate griefs from my apartment building. Mrs. Chen's lymphoma in 4A—that's a sharp seven out of ten. The teenager in 2C cutting again—steady four, been there for months. New couple in 5B, their baby won't stop crying because he feels what they won't say out loud—that they're drowning—

BREUT enters SR, already mid-conversation with herself.

BREUT
—and the subway driver's thinking about jumping in front of his own train, which is ironic, and the woman next to me is planning her divorce but hasn't told her wife yet, and the kid with the violin is auditioning for Juilliard tomorrow and already knows he won't get in—

SEGAL
Only forty-three?

BREUT
(noticing her)
What?

SEGAL
I said, only forty-three? Try a subway car at rush hour.

BREUT
You count them.

SEGAL
You don't?

BREUT
I used to.
(bitter laugh)
Then I ran out of numbers.

CONSTANCE enters backward from SL, as if pulled.

CONSTANCE
Thursday. It's going to happen Thursday.

SEGAL
What's happening Thursday?

CONSTANCE
I don't know yet. But it's pulling me.
(turns to them)
Do you ever feel tomorrow's pain today?

BREUT
I can barely handle yesterday's.

CHERHAL enters from US, sits in lotus position DC.

CHERHAL
Time is a construct. Pain is eternal.

BREUT
Oh good. The monk's here.

CHERHAL
Former monk. The monastery asked me to leave.

SEGAL
Why?

CHERHAL
I kept feeling God's loneliness.

Silence.

CONSTANCE
Is that why we're here? Group therapy for the overly sensitive?

SEGAL
My insurance mandated it. "Emotional dysregulation disorder," they called it.

BREUT
My therapist fired me. Said I was making HER depressed.

CHERHAL
I came because the walls in my apartment started weeping.

CONSTANCE
Literally?

CHERHAL
Metaphorically. But the water damage was real.

Scene 2

They arrange chairs in a circle. The suitcase remains center.

SEGAL
Should we... introduce ourselves?

BREUT
Hi, I'm Breut, and I'm an empath.
(mocking AA)
It's been zero days since I absorbed someone else's trauma.

CONSTANCE
That's not funny.

BREUT
Nothing's funny when you feel everyone's pain. Humor's just scar tissue.

SEGAL
I'm Segal. I work at Metropolitan Hospital. Administration. I thought being around suffering would desensitize me.
(pause)
It didn't.

CONSTANCE
Constance. Hospice care. I help people die. Sometimes before they know they're dying.

CHERHAL
And I'm Cherhal. I seek to understand why we were given hearts too big for our bodies.

BREUT
"Given." Like it's a gift.

CHERHAL
What else would you call it?

BREUT
A curse. A cosmic joke. A manufacturing defect.

SEGAL
Can we return it?

CONSTANCE
(holding out empty hands)
I tried. The gift shop's been closed for years.

Scene 3

They begin to move, dragging invisible weight.

SEGAL
Every morning—

ALL
(overlapping)
Every morning—

SEGAL
I wake up already full.

BREUT
Stuffed with other people's disasters.

CONSTANCE
Dreams that aren't mine.

CHERHAL
Sorrows I didn't earn.

Movement becomes synchronized, exhausted.

SEGAL
The weight accumulates. Like sediment.

BREUT
Like compound interest on a loan you never took out.

CONSTANCE
I see the deaths before they happen. Next Tuesday, the barista at my coffee shop—brain aneurysm. She's twenty-three. She's picking out wedding dresses this weekend.

SEGAL
Don't tell her.

CONSTANCE
I never do. What would I say? "Excuse me, but you're going to die before your macchiato gets cold"?

BREUT
You could save her.

CONSTANCE
How? Drive her to the hospital for a scan she doesn't need? For a future that might not happen if I interfere? I tried that once.

CHERHAL
What happened?

CONSTANCE
She died in the car accident I caused rushing her to the ER.

Silence.

Scene 4

BREUT
(pulling out pill bottle)
Xanax. Klonopin. Ativan. The holy trinity of not giving a fuck.

SEGAL
Does it work?

BREUT
(dry swallow)
Ask me in twenty minutes.

SEGAL
I tried meditation.

CHERHAL
And?

SEGAL
Turns out when you quiet your mind, everyone else's gets louder.

CONSTANCE
I tried boundaries. Built walls so high I couldn't see over them.

CHERHAL
What happened?

CONSTANCE
The feelings learned to climb. Or dig. They always find a way in.

CHERHAL
Because we're houses with no locks.

BREUT
Broken houses.

SEGAL
We are not broken.

BREUT
(standing, aggressive)
We ARE broken. Look at us! I haven't slept through the night in five years. Segal counts suffering like sheep. Constance sees death everywhere. And you, Cherhal, you think there's some cosmic purpose to feeling everyone's agony?

CHERHAL
Yes.

BREUT
Then you're more broken than any of us.

Scene 5

BREUT's pills kick in. She moves differently, looser but emptier.

BREUT
You know what? I feel better. I feel... nothing. Sweet nothing.

SEGAL
Your eyes look dead.

BREUT
Better than feeling everyone else dying.

CONSTANCE
(to SEGAL)
She's fading.

SEGAL
I know. I can feel her... disconnecting.

CHERHAL
This isn't healing. It's hibernation.

BREUT
Call it what you want. I'm taking a vacation from caring.

She starts to exit.

CONSTANCE
If you leave now—

BREUT
What? What happens if I leave?

CONSTANCE
(seeing the future)
You take the whole bottle. Tonight. 11:47 PM.

BREUT
(stops)
Good.

SEGAL
No—

BREUT
Why not? Why shouldn't I stop feeling EVERYTHING for EVERYONE all the time?

SEGAL rolls up her sleeve, showing scars.

SEGAL
Because I tried. Last year. Swallowed every pill in my medicine cabinet. You know what happened? Even unconscious, I absorbed the paramedic's divorce, the nurse's dead son, the janitor's—

BREUT
Stop.

SEGAL
We can't stop. That's the point.

Scene 6

CHERHAL
What if we're not broken? What if we're evolution?

BREUT
(sarcastic)
Evolution toward what? Voluntary extinction?

CHERHAL
Toward connection. Real connection. When humans lived in small tribes, empaths helped survival. We knew when someone was sick before symptoms showed. We felt danger approaching. We bound the group together through shared feeling.

CONSTANCE
But now?

CHERHAL
Now there are too many. The tribe is seven billion strong. Our nervous systems haven't caught up.

SEGAL
So we're obsolete.

CHERHAL
Or essential. In a world growing numb, we're the ones who still feel.

BREUT
Pretty words. But I still wake up at 3 AM feeling someone's cancer three blocks away.

SEGAL
Someone's always dying three blocks away.

CHERHAL
Yes. And someone's always being born. Someone's always falling in love. We feel that too.

BREUT
The pain's louder.

CHERHAL
Only because we've been taught to listen for sirens instead of lullabies.

Scene 7

CHERHAL
Let's try something. Close your eyes.

BREUT
Group meditation? Really?

CHERHAL
Not meditation. Recognition. Close your eyes and feel one good thing. Just one.

They reluctantly close their eyes.

CHERHAL
What do you feel?

SEGAL
(eyes still closed)
The NICU nurse... she's singing to the premature twins. They're going to make it.

CONSTANCE
My downstairs neighbor is writing a love letter. His hands are shaking. He's seventy-three. He's been alone for twenty years.

BREUT
(struggling)
I can't—

CHERHAL
Feel deeper. Under the pain.

BREUT
(voice breaking)
The... the kid with the violin. He's playing for his grandmother. She has Alzheimer's but she remembers this song. She's humming along.

They open their eyes, changed.

CONSTANCE
It's still there. The joy. Under everything.

SEGAL
Like finding clean water at the bottom of a dirty well.

Scene 8

Suddenly, all four clutch their chests.

ALL
(gasping)
What—

CHERHAL
Something's happening.

SEGAL
Something big.

CONSTANCE
Multiple casualties. Downtown. Now.

BREUT
I can't—it's too much—

They're overwhelmed, moving as if drowning.

SEGAL
Forty... fifty... hundred...

CONSTANCE
So much fear—

CHERHAL
Breathe. We breathe through it.

BREUT
I can't breathe when two hundred people are suffocating!

Lights shift dramatically. CHORUS enters from all sides, becoming the city's pain.

CHORUS
(overlapping whispers building to screams)
Help me—
Where's my daughter—
I can't feel my legs—
Please—
God—
Anyone—

The four empaths are on their knees, overwhelmed.

Scene 9

CHERHAL struggles to stand.

CHERHAL
We can absorb this. Transform it.

BREUT
It'll kill us.

CHERHAL
Maybe. Or maybe we're exactly what's needed right now.

SEGAL
I'm already at capacity—

CONSTANCE
(seeing)
If we don't... more will die. The ripples of panic will—

CHERHAL
Then we hold it. Together.

They slowly join hands, forming a circle around the suitcase.

SEGAL
My heart—

BREUT
—is as big as a house—

CONSTANCE
—as big as a city—

CHERHAL
—as big as necessary.

They breathe together, the CHORUS's screams slowly transforming into a hum.

BLACKOUT

END OF ACT ONE

ACT TWO

Scene 1

Days later. The four sit exhausted. The suitcase now glows faintly.

BREUT
We did it.

SEGAL
We absorbed a city's trauma.

CONSTANCE
And we're still here.

CHERHAL
Changed, but here.

BREUT
I can still feel them. All of them. But it's... different.

SEGAL
Like the pain has been composted. Turned into something else.

CONSTANCE
I saw something. When we were connected. A future where—

CHERHAL
Where what?

CONSTANCE
Where everyone feels like we do. Where empathy isn't a disorder but the norm.

BREUT
That sounds like hell.

CONSTANCE
Or heaven. Imagine if everyone felt the consequences of their actions immediately. Through others.

Scene 2

A YOUNG WOMAN enters, nervous.

YOUNG WOMAN
Is this the... support group? For people who feel too much?

SEGAL
How did you find us?

YOUNG WOMAN
I felt you. During the attack. Four lights in all that darkness. I followed the feeling.

BREUT
You're one of us.

YOUNG WOMAN
I don't want to be. Can you fix me?

CHERHAL
We can't fix what isn't broken.

YOUNG WOMAN
But I'm exhausted. I'm twenty-two and I feel ancient. I wake up crying from dreams that aren't mine. I can't ride the subway without having panic attacks. I—

CONSTANCE
We know.

SEGAL
We all know.

YOUNG WOMAN
So how do you bear it?

The four exchange looks.

BREUT
Badly. With medication and bitter humor.

SEGAL
By counting and categorizing until it feels manageable.

CONSTANCE
By working with the dying, where future pain and present pain meet.

CHERHAL
By believing it means something.

YOUNG WOMAN
Does it? Mean something?

Scene 3

CHERHAL
(to Young Woman)
Sit. Breathe. Feel.

YOUNG WOMAN
I don't want to feel anymore.

CHERHAL
Then feel that. Feel the not-wanting.

The Young Woman sits. The others form a circle around her.

SEGAL
The first thing to know is you're not alone.

BREUT
Even when you're alone, you're not alone. That's the problem.

CONSTANCE
And the solution.

YOUNG WOMAN
I don't understand.

CHERHAL
Understanding comes later. First, acceptance.

YOUNG WOMAN
Accept that I'll always be in pain?

SEGAL
Accept that you'll always be open.

BREUT
Accept that your heart is too big for your body.

CONSTANCE
Accept that you'll see death before it arrives.

CHERHAL
Accept that you are not broken.

ALL
We are not broken.

Scene 4

More people enter gradually—different ages, backgrounds.

SEGAL
They keep coming.

BREUT
Every day, more.

CONSTANCE
I saw this. In the vision. A network of us.

CHERHAL
The meditation teachers call it "spontaneous awakening."

BREUT
The doctors call it "mass hysteria."

SEGAL
The news calls it "the empathy epidemic."

CONSTANCE
What do we call it?

CHERHAL
Evolution.

The stage fills with people, all moving with the same exhausted grace.

Scene 5

BREUT
You know what pisses me off? They want us to be fixed. To stop feeling so they can stop feeling guilty.

SEGAL
The hospital board suggested I take a leave of absence. Said I was "too emotionally involved" with patients.

CONSTANCE
The hospice director wants me to stop predicting deaths. Says it's "disturbing to families."

CHERHAL
And yet they come to us when they need to feel. When their numbness becomes unbearable.

YOUNG WOMAN
So what do we do?

BREUT
We resist.

SEGAL
By staying soft.

CONSTANCE
By refusing to close.

CHERHAL
By being houses with no locks in a world of fortresses.

Scene 6

They begin teaching the newcomers.

SEGAL
First, learn to count. Not to control, but to acknowledge.

GROUP
One sorrow, two sorrows, three...

BREUT
Then learn to laugh. Not because it's funny, but because absurdity is armor.

GROUP
(attempting dark humor)
My heart is a mansion... a condemned mansion...

CONSTANCE
Learn to see time as fluid. Future pain, past pain—it all exists now.

GROUP
(overlapping)
Yesterday, today, tomorrow...

CHERHAL
And finally, learn to breathe. Together.

They all begin synchronized breathing.

Scene 7

CONSTANCE suddenly stands.

CONSTANCE
It's happening.

SEGAL
What?

CONSTANCE
The thing I saw. It's starting now.

Lights shift. The CHORUS returns, but different—singing instead of screaming.

CHORUS
We are not broken
We are breaking open
Hearts as big as houses
In a world of closed doors

CONSTANCE
Everyone. Everywhere. All at once. Feeling everything.

BREUT
That's impossible.

CONSTANCE
Look.

Projections of faces around the world, all touching their hearts.

CHERHAL
The barriers are falling.

SEGAL
Between self and other.

BREUT
Between my pain and yours.

CONSTANCE
Between nations. Between hearts.

Scene 8

The suitcase begins to pulse with light.

YOUNG WOMAN
What's happening?

CHERHAL
We're becoming unnecessary.

BREUT
What?

CHERHAL
When everyone's an empath, no one is. We're not special anymore.

SEGAL
(realizing)
We're normal.

They approach the suitcase together.

CONSTANCE
All this weight we've been carrying...

BREUT
For everyone else...

SEGAL
They can carry their own now.

CHERHAL
And share the load.

They open the suitcase. Brilliant light pours out.

Scene 9

Each character steps forward, speaking in their native language, then English.

SEGAL
(Hebrew first)
אנחנו לא שבורים...
We are not broken. We were never broken. We were just early.

BREUT
(French first)
Nous ne sommes pas brisés...
We kept the doors open when everyone else was closing them. We held the weight until they were strong enough to share it.

CONSTANCE
(Mandarin first)
我们没有破碎...
I see the future clearly now. A world where feeling is not weakness. Where hearts are all as big as houses.

CHERHAL
(Arabic first)
لسنا مكسورين...
This was our purpose. To be the bridge between the old world of isolation and the new world of connection.

The CHORUS joins, speaking in multiple languages.

ALL
We are not broken. We are not broken. We are not broken.

Final Scene

Dawn light. The four sit in simple chairs. The suitcase is gone.

SEGAL
I woke up this morning and felt... quiet.

BREUT
Not empty. Just... proportional.

CONSTANCE
I can still see tomorrow. But it doesn't hurt.

CHERHAL
Because everyone carries their own tomorrow now.

YOUNG WOMAN enters.

YOUNG WOMAN
Is this still the support group?

SEGAL
Support for what?

YOUNG WOMAN
For people who feel... everything?

BREUT
(smiling)
That's everyone now.

CONSTANCE
But yes. Still a support group.

CHERHAL
Just bigger.

YOUNG WOMAN
How much bigger?

ALL
As big as necessary.

Lights slowly fade as more people enter, filling the stage, breathing together.

END OF PLAY

PRODUCTION NOTES

Running Time: Approximately 90-100 minutes without intermission

Set Requirements:
- Raked platform (15 degrees)
- Oversized translucent suitcase (6' x 4') with LED integration
- Projection-capable upstage scrim
- Flexible seating units

Technical Requirements:
- 16-channel sound system with surround capability
- 48-channel lighting board
- Projection mapping system
- Haze machine
- 8 wireless microphones

Special Considerations:
- Multilingual coaching required for Scene 9 of Act Two
- Choreographed breathing patterns throughout
- Physical theater training recommended for ensemble
- Consider cultural consultants for authentic vocal techniques if incorporating production guide variations

Note on Casting:
The play benefits from diverse casting to reflect the universal nature of empathic experience. Consider actors with musical/vocal training for potential incorporation of the 18 variations from the original concept.
