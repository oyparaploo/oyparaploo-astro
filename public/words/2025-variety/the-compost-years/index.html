## A Memoir in Fragments

### Fragment 1: The Divorce Papers
**Eugene, Oregon - 2019**

Found them in the compost bin. Not metaphorically. Lisa had thrown the unsigned divorce papers in with the coffee grounds and rotting lettuce. When I asked why, she said, "Everything decomposes eventually."

We'd been attending couples therapy with a woman who called herself a "relationship composter."

### Fragment 2: The Mushroom Phase
**2020**

After Lisa left, I grew oyster mushrooms in the closet. Started a YouTube channel: "Divorced Dad's Fungi Journey." Seven subscribers. My daughter commented: "Dad this is embarrassing please stop."

The mushrooms grew anyway. They didn't care about subscriber counts.

### Fragment 3: Mom's Diagnosis
**2021**

Mom forgot my name but remembered I was afraid of earthworms. "The worm boy," she called me at the memory care facility. Accurate. I'd spent forty-three years avoiding soil.

Now I visit her garden. The tomatoes grow wild. Worms everywhere. I pick them up, show her. She laughs like I've performed magic.

### Fragment 5: The Border Crossing
**2023**

Smuggled my mother's garden soil to Canada. She'd died in spring. Canadian customs asked what was in the jar.

"My mother," I said.

They looked at the soil. "Ashes?"

"Compost."

They let me through. Scattered her in Stanley Park. Illegal. Beautiful. She feeds Douglas firs now.

### Fragment 6: Online Dating
**2024**

Profile bio: "Composts emotionally. Literally grows mushrooms. My mother became trees."

Three matches:
- GardenGoth92: "My ex is buried in my vegetable patch (joking) (maybe)"
- SoilSister: "I ferment everything. EVERYTHING."
- DecomposingDan: "Want to compare bacteria cultures?"

Met SoilSister for coffee. Real name: Sarah. Actual soil scientist. Showed me microscopic footage of fungi trading nutrients.

"It's like Venmo for trees," she explained.

I fell in love immediately.

### Fragment 7: The Return
**Portland - Now**

Sarah and I bought the empty lot where my childhood home burned down. Previous owners couldn't get anything to grow. We know why: soil remembers trauma.

We're patient. Adding compost weekly. The neighbors think we're crazy, hauling bags of food waste to an empty lot.

But last week: a seedling. Unknown species. Growing from the exact spot where my bedroom was.

Sarah says it's random. Wind-carried. Coincidence.

I know better. Some things take thirty years to germinate. Some memories need proper decomposition before they bloom.

### Fragment 8: What We Learned

Everything rots. This is not pessimism. This is promise.

My marriage, my mother's mind, my childhood home—all became soil. All feed something new.

Kevin's selling dirt now. Makes six figures. Calls it "Heritage Soil." He's not wrong.

Lisa remarried. Sends Christmas cards with photos of her compost bin. We're friends now. Decomposition complete.

The mushrooms in my closet have colonized the entire wall. My daughter films TikToks with them. "My dad's emotional support fungi." Million views.

Sarah's pregnant. We're building a greenhouse where my old bedroom was. The mystery seedling turned out to be a plum tree. Impossible—no plum trees for miles.

Mom loved plums.

We're naming the baby after her. She'll grow up thinking it's normal to talk to soil, to see decomposition as transformation, to know that everything—even endings—feeds beginnings.

The memoir fragments you wanted? They're here. Buried throughout. Composting into something true.
