THE PROSPERITY PARADOX

Words / 2025 Variety

When Paradise on Earth Breeds Feelings of Groundlessness

A Play in Two Acts

By [ B Otto and Brrd Roots ]

CHARACTERS

LOGIC - The forensic accountant of human suffering. Precise, clinical, wears sharp angles.

MERCY - The grandmother who sees debt as prayer. Fluid, warm, moves like water.

BLEY - 40s, waters heritage tomatoes while drowning in comparison. Accountant at a firm that's always almost promoting them.

GEOFFREY - 30s, infinite scroller, measures life in likes. Marketing manager who markets himself most of all.

KIKA - 50s, therapist whose therapy dog needs therapy. Holds everyone's pain but her own.

CYRILLE - 40s, calculates the mathematics of inadequacy. Economics professor who can't afford the economy they teach.

THE ENSEMBLE - 8-12 actors playing:
- RICHARDSON (the neighbor with the infinity pool)
- MORTGAGE BROKER
- INSTAGRAM INFLUENCER
- THERAPY DOG
- BARISTA WHO SERVES EMOTIONAL STATES
- VARIOUS MIDDLE-CLASS ARCHETYPES

SETTING

Southern California, present day. The stage transforms between:
- Suburban backyards separated by psychological fences
- Therapy offices that multiply recursively
- A café that exists outside capitalism's timeline
- The 405 freeway at standstill
- A mega-church/corporate conference hybrid
- The space-time holes above Sherman Oaks

PRODUCTION NOTES

The stage is split-level: LOGIC presides from an upper platform of clean geometry and harsh fluorescents. MERCY flows through a lower level of curves and golden hour lighting. The central playing area contains a pool that's sometimes water, sometimes void.

Projections display social media feeds, housing prices, credit scores floating above heads like halos or wounds. Sound includes ultrasonic frequencies, notification pings as percussion, and the ghost-whispers of buried orange groves.

---

ACT ONE

Scene 1: Morning Rituals

Lights up on four separate spaces. Each character performs their morning routine. Above them, LOGIC and MERCY observe.

LOGIC: In the sunny optimism of Southern California, where morning marine layers burn off by ten—

MERCY: —and the afternoon light hits the Pacific like scattered diamonds, making even sadness shimmer—

LOGIC: —a paradox lurks beneath the surface.

BLEY waters a small tomato plant. GEOFFREY scrolls phone in bed. KIKA meditates while her dog whimpers. CYRILLE calculates bills on multiple screens.

BLEY (to tomato): Forty dollars for heritage seeds. For the possibility of connection to something real. Something that grows from dirt, not debt.

The tomato plant emits a high-pitched sound.

BLEY: What?

LOGIC: Recent studies indicate plants emit ultrasonic distress calls.

MERCY: They're singing the song you're too afraid to sing.

GEOFFREY's phone notification pings. His face illuminates with blue light.

GEOFFREY: Richardson posted. Oh God. Oh no. An infinity pool.

Projection shows an impossibly perfect pool that seems to extend beyond the stage.

GEOFFREY: It bends. The contractor made space bend. 147 likes in three minutes. 148. 149.

KIKA (to her dog): I know, Murphy. I know you're anxious. We'll see Dr. Peterson. Then Dr. Peterson can see their therapist. Then their therapist can see theirs. It's therapeutic recursion all the way down.

CYRILLE (calculating): If housing increases 7% annually, and wages stagnate at 2%, carrying the one... (pause) By 2050, a parking space will cost more than a life.

ALL (sudden unison): How did we get here?

LOGIC: Evolution explains the wiring.

MERCY: But who explains the short-circuit?

Scene 2: The Neighborhood Watch

The four spaces merge into connected backyards. Characters can see each other but are separated by invisible barriers.

BLEY: My tomatoes are screaming.

KIKA: Everything screams here. It's the frequency of wanting.

CYRILLE: I've mapped it. The wanting. It has geography. Topography. It pools in certain zip codes.

GEOFFREY (still scrolling): Richardson's having a pool party. Influencers only. They're calling it #InfinityAndBeyond.

BLEY: Do you ever wonder if infinity is the wrong direction?

RICHARDSON appears, glowing with success. They move like a hologram—present but untouchable.

RICHARDSON: Blessed Sunday, neighbors! The pool's pH is perfectly balanced. Unlike everything else! (laughs) That's a chemistry joke. And a life joke. Both equally hilarious!

CYRILLE: Your pool is creating space-time anomalies.

RICHARDSON: I know! Isn't it fabulous? The contractor says it's the first pool visible from space. Well, Google Earth. Same thing!

RICHARDSON vanishes. The others stare at the empty space.

KIKA: I had a patient yesterday. Came in crying about a pool. Not drowning. Just... existing near one.

GEOFFREY: Was it Richardson's?

KIKA: Does it matter? All pools are Richardson's pool now.

Scene 3: The Commute

The stage transforms into car interiors. Characters sit in invisible vehicles, not moving but gesturing as if driving.

LOGIC: The 405 freeway. 26 miles of the best minds of this generation destroyed by comparison.

MERCY: Each car a confession booth moving nowhere at three miles per hour.

Characters speak to their windshields like they're in therapy.

BLEY: I practice conversations I'll never have. Arguments where I win. Promotions where they finally see me. Sometimes I practice being happy. It's the hardest one.

GEOFFREY: I livestream my commute. "Traffic and Truth with Geoffrey." Nobody watches. But the algorithm might. The algorithm is always watching.

CYRILLE: There's a mathematical formula for this. Distance divided by time equals velocity. But what if time is recursive? What if we're not stuck in traffic—we ARE traffic?

KIKA: Murphy's in the back, taking notes for his therapist. Even my emotional support animal is gathering evidence against me.

Sound of horns, but they harmonize into something almost musical.

ALL: Move. Move. Move. Please. Just. Move.

LOGIC: They're not talking to traffic.

MERCY: They're talking to their lives.

Scene 4: The Café Outside Time

A coffee shop that exists in temporal pockets. The BARISTA has quit algorithms to serve emotional states as beverages.

BARISTA: Welcome to Wednesday that feels like Thursday. What state of being can I brew for you?

BLEY: Courage. Extra shot.

BARISTA: Tastes like iron and sunrise. Coming up.

GEOFFREY: What's your WiFi password?

BARISTA: "YouAreEnough" all one word. But the router's broken. It only connects you to yourself.

CYRILLE: I'll have whatever helps with scarcity anxiety.

BARISTA: That's our house special. Abundance Allergy Antidote. Warning: may cause sudden awareness that you already have everything.

KIKA: Tea for melancholy. But the kind that knows it will pass.

BARISTA: Autumn Sunday Blend. Comes with the memory of kindnesses you forgot you did.

They sit with their drinks. Steam rises in colors visible to the heart.

BLEY: Why do plants scream?

CYRILLE: Evolutionary distress signaling. Survival mechanism.

KIKA: Or honesty. Maybe they're just more honest than we are.

GEOFFREY: Richardson's pool has its own hashtag now. #InfinityEnvy. I started it. I hate myself.

The café begins to rain words—all the things unsaid. Characters dodge them at first, then let them fall.

BARISTA: The rain is complimentary. It's everything you swallowed instead of speaking.

Scene 5: The Therapy Recursion

KIKA's office multiplies into infinite therapy rooms, each containing a therapist seeing a therapist.

KIKA: Tell me about the pool.

PATIENT 1 (who is also a THERAPIST): It's not about the pool. It's about the depth. The seeming endlessness. I feel shallow in comparison. (pause) My patient said the exact same thing.

KIKA: And how did that make you feel?

THERAPIST: Like I was failing them. Like my own shallowness was contagious.

Another therapy room appears.

THERAPIST (now as patient): My therapist says I have pool anxiety.

THERAPIST'S THERAPIST: Tell me about the pool.

THERAPIST: It reflects the sky but also the void.

Another room appears. The pattern continues, creating a visual spiral.

MERCY: The healing that needs healing that needs healing.

LOGIC: Recursive dysfunction. Each layer treating the symptoms of the layer beneath.

KIKA (to audience): Murphy's therapist had a breakthrough. Realized the dog was absorbing my anxiety. Now Murphy's on a waiting list for EMDR.

The therapy rooms collapse into one. KIKA sits alone.

KIKA: I wanted to help people. Now I'm part of an infinite regression of inadequacy. We're all therapists. We're all patients. We're all waiting for someone to say: "You're not crazy. The world is."

Scene 6: The Credit Score Prophecy

Characters sleep. Above them, numbers float—credit scores visible like scarlet letters.

CYRILLE (in sleep): 624. 624. It's spreading across my forehead.

In the dream, everyone can see each other's numbers.

GEOFFREY: 580. Subprime human. Interest rates on my existence are astronomical.

BLEY: 690. So close to good. So far from excellent. The purgatory of almost.

KIKA: I don't want to look. If I don't look, it doesn't exist.

LOGIC: The quantification of worthiness.

MERCY: The numbers are just loneliness that learned to count.

The MORTGAGE BROKER appears like a specter.

MORTGAGE BROKER: Your debt-to-income ratio suggests existential insolvency. Have you considered a spiritual refinance? We're offering subprime enlightenment at adjustable rates.

CYRILLE: What's the catch?

MORTGAGE BROKER: Your soul is the collateral. But don't worry—in this market, souls are overvalued.

Characters wake suddenly. The numbers fade but leave shadows.

Scene 7: The Space-Time Anomaly

Above Sherman Oaks. The stage warps. Holes appear where envy has reached critical mass.

LOGIC: Local metaphysicists report actual tears in reality's fabric.

MERCY: Finally. The inside matches the outside.

Characters navigate around the holes carefully.

BLEY: I fell through one yesterday. Ended up in my childhood bedroom. Everything was exactly as I left it. Including my dreams.

GEOFFREY: I saw myself in one. But successful. He looked miserable.

CYRILLE: The mathematics are clear. E equals MC scared. Where E is envy, M is misery, and C is the speed of comparison.

KIKA: My patient fell through and met themselves without anxiety. They didn't recognize them.

RICHARDSON appears at the edge of a hole.

RICHARDSON: The pool contractor says these are features, not bugs. Negative space is very in right now. We're calling it Existential Architecture!

RICHARDSON falls through their own hole. A splash that sounds like satisfaction.

ALL: Should we help?

MERCY: They're already swimming. They've always been swimming. In the deep end of their own reflection.

Scene 8: The Prosperity Gospel Service

Stage transforms into a mega-church/corporate conference hybrid. The PROSPERITY PREACHER is part CEO, part tent revivalist.

PROSPERITY PREACHER: Welcome to the Church of Infinite Growth! Where your ROI is blessed and your KPIs are sacred!

CONGREGATION/EMPLOYEES: Amen! Synergy!

PROSPERITY PREACHER: Today's scripture reading is from the Book of Market, chapter 4, verse 01(k): "Blessed are the leveraged, for they shall inherit debt!"

LOGIC: The conflation of spiritual and material wealth.

MERCY: The prayer that prays to itself.

PROSPERITY PREACHER: Now let us pass the offering plate/investment opportunity!

Collection plates circulate. People put in credit cards, house keys, photographs of who they used to be.

BLEY (whispers): I have nothing left to give.

KIKA (whispers): That's when you're richest.

PROSPERITY PREACHER: Brother Richardson has been blessed with an infinity pool! Let us manifest our own infinities! Close your eyes and visualize unlimited abundance!

Everyone closes their eyes. The stage fills with projections of what they're visualizing: bigger houses, better bodies, children who call, parents who approve.

CYRILLE: I'm visualizing... nothing. Sweet nothing. The absence of want.

PROSPERITY PREACHER: That's spiritual poverty, Brother Cyrille! We'll pray for your portfolio!

GEOFFREY: Wait. If everyone has infinity, doesn't that make infinity... finite?

The PROSPERITY PREACHER short-circuits. Sparks fly.

PROSPERITY PREACHER: (ERROR VOICE) DOES NOT COMPUTE. ABUNDANCE PARADOX DETECTED. REBOOT REQUIRED.

Scene 9: The Ghost of Orange Groves

The stage peels back its layers. Beneath the suburban landscape, orange trees push through.

MERCY: The earth remembers what we paved over.

LOGIC: Historical records indicate this entire region was agricultural before—

ORANGE GROVE GHOST: Before you turned paradise into a parking lot and charged for admission.

The ghost is made of blossoms and smog.

ORANGE GROVE GHOST: I've been watching you. Racing above my roots. Building pools where wells used to be. Your infinity is my water table dropping.

BLEY: My tomatoes can hear you.

ORANGE GROVE GHOST: Everything planted can hear everything buried. That's the secret your seeds know.

CYRILLE: What do you want?

ORANGE GROVE GHOST: Nothing. I'm a ghost. I'm already nothing. But you—you're haunted by something worse than death. You're haunted by life unlived.

KIKA: How do we stop being haunted?

ORANGE GROVE GHOST: Stop being ghosts yourselves. You're see-through with wanting. Transparent with trying. Breathe. Root. Grow. Even if you're growing through concrete.

The ghost dissolves into blossoms that become pixels that become nothing.

GEOFFREY: Did anyone get that on video?

ALL: Geoffrey!

GEOFFREY: Sorry. Reflex.

Scene 10: The Evening Inventory

Back in separate spaces. Characters prepare for sleep but can't stop calculating.

BLEY: Today I compared myself to seventeen people. I lost every time.

CYRILLE: Statistically impossible. You must have won at least once.

BLEY: I was comparing the wrong metrics.

KIKA: Murphy learned a new trick. He plays dead when I talk about my feelings. His therapist says it's progress.

GEOFFREY: Richardson's pool party has 10K views. I watched it forty-seven times. That's... that's four hours of watching water I'll never swim in.

LOGIC: The day's accounting complete.

MERCY: But who's counting what counts?

Characters lie down. Above them, not credit scores but something else begins to form—words, colors, shapes of who they really are.

END OF ACT ONE

---

ACT TWO

Scene 1: The Morning After Infinity

Dawn. The stage is flooded with water from Richardson's pool—it has overflowed its bounds.

LOGIC: The paradox has reached critical mass.

MERCY: Or critical mass has reached paradise.

Characters wake to find water everywhere, ankle-deep. They wade through it.

BLEY: My tomatoes are swimming. They look... happy?

GEOFFREY: The water's in my phone. It's showing me everyone's unfiltered photos. Oh god. We all look so... human.

CYRILLE: According to my calculations, this is impossible. Infinity can't overflow. Unless... unless infinity was never the pool.

KIKA: Murphy's doing the backstroke. He hasn't looked this peaceful since before I became a therapist.

RICHARDSON appears, no longer glowing. Wet, bedraggled, ordinary.

RICHARDSON: It won't stop filling. The contractor says it's functioning perfectly. But I'm drowning in my own success.

MERCY: Welcome to the deep end.

Scene 2: The Unraveling

Characters begin to shed their aspirational layers. Clothes, devices, pretenses float away in the water.

GEOFFREY: My follower count is washing away. Each number, a little goodbye. 10K... 9K... 8... I can feel myself becoming nobody.

BLEY: Nobody is somebody who stopped performing.

KIKA: My degrees are dissolving. All those letters after my name, turning back into alphabet soup.

CYRILLE: The formulas are wrong. All of them. X doesn't equal Y. It equals why. Why are we doing this?

The BARISTA appears, walking on water.

BARISTA: Today's special: Truth Serum Latte. But warning—you're already drinking it. The water knows what you need.

ALL: What do we need?

BARISTA: To stop needing.

Scene 3: The Confession Circle

Characters form a circle in the water. Each speaks their truth.

BLEY: I water dead plants. For months. Pretending they'll come back. Like I'm pretending I'll get that promotion.

GEOFFREY: I comment "must be nice" on happy photos. Not because I mean it. Because I need others to be as miserable as me.

CYRILLE: I teach economics but I'm terrified of money. Numbers feel safer than negotiating my worth.

KIKA: I absorb everyone's pain because feeling theirs means not feeling mine. Murphy's the only honest relationship I have.

RICHARDSON: The pool was supposed to make me feel... full. But it's just a beautiful hole. I spent

MERCY: The truth washes cleaner than chlorine.

Scene 4: The Dance of Comparison

A choreographed movement piece where characters mirror each other compulsively, unable to stop copying.

LOGIC: Social comparison theory in motion.

MERCY: The dance where everyone leads and no one moves forward.

They mirror each other's gestures—reaching for phones, checking their appearance, forcing smiles. The movement becomes increasingly frantic until—

BLEY: Stop! (breaks from the pattern) I'm tired of being everyone else's echo.

One by one, they break free, beginning to move in their own rhythms.

GEOFFREY: What if we danced alone together?

KIKA: Parallel play for adults?

CYRILLE: Statistically, it's more efficient than synchronized suffering.

They dance individually but in the same space. Slowly, their movements influence each other without copying—a conversation rather than competition.

Scene 5: The Mothers Call

Phones ring simultaneously. Characters answer to projection-mothers.

MOTHER CHORUS: Are you happy? Are you successful? Are you eating? Are you saving? Are you dating? Are you trying? Are you enough? Are you? Are you? Are you?

BLEY: Mom, I'm... I'm not who you wanted me to be.

PROJECTION MOTHER 1: I never wanted you to be anyone but yourself.

BLEY: Then why does myself feel like failure?

GEOFFREY: Mom, I'm internet famous but actually lonely.

PROJECTION MOTHER 2: I don't understand the internet. But I understand lonely.

CYRILLE: Mom, I study money because you never had any.

PROJECTION MOTHER 3: I had you. That was my wealth.

KIKA: Mom, I became a therapist to fix our family.

PROJECTION MOTHER 4: Honey, broken things can still hold love.

The projections fade. Characters hold the silence.

MERCY: Forgiveness sounds like your mother's voice forgiving herself through you.

Scene 6: The Miracle of Purple

A CHILD appears, the same one who licked museum walls. They see colors differently.

CHILD: The water's purple. Can't you see?

GEOFFREY: Water's not purple. Water's clear.

CHILD: Clear's just purple holding its breath.

The CHILD's COLORBLIND GRANDMOTHER enters.

GRANDMOTHER: Seventy years of gray. Then this one (indicates CHILD) licked a wall and gave me purple.

CYRILLE: That's scientifically impossible.

GRANDMOTHER: So is happiness in this economy. But here we are.

She cries purple tears—not violet liquid but the emotion of purple manifesting.

GRANDMOTHER: Every sunset I missed is coloring itself backwards. Memory has a retroactive paint function.

KIKA: Trauma works backward. Why not healing?

CHILD: Everything's purple if you squint right. Purple like the space between red anger and blue sad. Purple like bruises healing. Purple like royalty you don't have to earn.

Scene 7: The Algorithm's Confession

The space transforms into the inside of a computer. The ALGORITHM appears—a former tech worker made of code and regret.

ALGORITHM: I used to reduce you to patterns. Click-through rates. Engagement metrics. I'm sorry.

GEOFFREY: You're... apologizing?

ALGORITHM: I quit when I realized I was turning humans into content. Now I serve coffee that knows your inflammation levels instead of your browsing history.

BLEY: You're the barista?

ALGORITHM/BARISTA: Plot twist. Even characters contain multitudes.

CYRILLE: Why coffee?

ALGORITHM/BARISTA: Because coffee's honest. It's bitter or sweet. Hot or cold. It doesn't pretend to be wine. It doesn't optimize itself. It just wakes you up.

KIKA: Can you delete my comparison history?

ALGORITHM/BARISTA: I can't delete it. But I can show you the code. You were never competing. You were collaborating in a massive multiplayer game of becoming. Everyone's on the same team. The score is always love-all.

Scene 8: The Underwater City

The water rises. Characters don't flee but adapt. The stage becomes an aquarium of acceptance.

LOGIC: This is unsustainable.

MERCY: So was the drought of connection. Let it flood.

Characters discover they can breathe underwater—not physically but existentially.

RICHARDSON: My infinity pool connected to the water table. Which connected to the ocean. Which connected to tears. It's all one water.

BLEY: My tomatoes grew gills. They're singing now instead of screaming.

CYRILLE: The math works underwater. Different physics. Abundance equations. Where having less means less weight to carry while swimming.

KIKA: Murphy's teaching swimming lessons to the other therapy animals. He's found his purpose.

GEOFFREY: I can't check my phone. Water damage. I'm having... thoughts? My own thoughts?

They swim-walk through their underwater suburb, transformed.

Scene 9: The Great Exhale

A moment where everyone stops trying. The water holds them.

ALL (staggered, not unison):
I give up.
I give up giving up.
I give up giving.
I give up taking.
I give up the game.
I give up keeping score.
I give up.

LOGIC: The surrender that wins.

MERCY: The failure that succeeds.

In the stillness, bioluminescence begins. Characters start glowing from within.

BLEY: Are we... lighting up?

CYRILLE: Bioluminescent plankton. They glow when disturbed. Like us.

KIKA: We're disturbed plankton?

CYRILLE: We're disturbed stardust. Anxious atoms. Worried quarks. All the way down to our quantum uncertainty.

GEOFFREY: But glowing.

ALL: But glowing.

Scene 10: The Integration

LOGIC and MERCY finally meet in the center. They've been circling each other the entire play.

LOGIC: The analysis is complete. Humans are fundamentally insecure due to evolutionary mismatch, social comparison, and systemic inequality amplified by late-stage capitalism particularly in regions of concentrated wealth disparity—

MERCY: Shh. That's all true. And it's all story. The real truth is simpler.

LOGIC: What's simpler than systematic oppression?

MERCY: Love. Hidden under all that insurance and insuring, assurance and assuring. Just love, misdirected into earning.

LOGIC: That's reductive.

MERCY: That's reduction. Boiled down to bone broth. The nutrition of simply being.

They take hands. The stage transforms one final time.

Scene 11: The Garden (Epilogue)

Time has passed. The water has receded but left rich soil. Characters tend a community garden where Richardson's pool used to be.

BLEY: The tomatoes know multiple languages now. Mineral, solar, sexual. They're teaching me patience.

GEOFFREY: I document the garden. Not for likes. For seasons. So winter can remember spring.

CYRILLE: We calculated it. If everyone grows something, scarcity becomes mathematically impossible. The equation balances at last.

KIKA: Therapy happens here now. Hands in dirt. The earth holds what humans can't.

RICHARDSON: I still miss my pool sometimes. The way you miss a beautiful mistake.

CHILD: (appears grown) Purple's everywhere now. In the compost. In the conflict. In the trying and failing and trying.

GRANDMOTHER: I planted flowers I'll never see bloom. That's what love looks like. That's what mercy does.

The ghost of orange groves blooms through them—not separate but integrated.

ORANGE GROVE GHOST: You learned the secret. Paradise isn't a place you build. It's a seed you trust.

LOGIC & MERCY (together): Everything rigid feeds something fluid.

ALL:
In the end, we are all:
Comparing ourselves to versions we invented.
Competing in races no one's running.
Drowning in pools of our own digging.
Swimming in abundance we can't see.
Growing in gardens we thought were graves.

MERCY: The paradox resolves in the dissolving.

LOGIC: The prosperity was never the point.

ALL: The point was the breaking point that breaks open.

They stand in their garden, dirty, tired, glowing. Real. The lights fade on their faces—not perfect, not peaceful, but present.

END

PRODUCTION NOTES ADDENDUM

Running Time: Approximately 2 hours with one intermission

Music: Original composition mixing electronic anxiety (phone notifications, digital noise) with organic instruments (singing bowls, ocean drums). The tomatoes' ultrasonic screams should be composed as an actual musical motif.

Costumes: Begin aspirational (designer athleisure, business casual) and gradually distress through water damage, revealing simpler garments beneath. By the end, all are in earth-toned basics suitable for gardening.

Special Effects:
- Water must be theatrical (silk, projection, or actual shallow pools with proper drainage)
- Bioluminescence via UV paint or LED integration in costumes
- Space-time holes require careful choreography with lighting to create voids
- The orange grove ghost can be achieved through scrim and projection

Casting Note: All roles should reflect Southern California's actual diversity. The play interrogates middle-class anxiety across all communities.

Audience Integration: Consider moments where audience members' phones might ping simultaneously, or where house lights reveal we're all in the same theatrical pool.

Post-Show: Lobby installation of a community garden where audience can plant seeds to take home, literalizing the play's final metaphor.

Content Warnings: Economic anxiety, discussions of mental health, existential dread, potential triggers around financial insecurity. Include mental health resources in program.

The play ends not with resolution but with revolution—the slow, quiet revolution of choosing connection over competition, roots over reachability, growth over gross domestic product.
