THE JOY LIBERATORS OF LYON

Public Space

← Words / 2025 Variety

Les Subsistances, Lyon

PRE-SHOW
Streets outside theater transformed with guerrilla art. Audience must participate in mini-Holi (powder throwing) to enter. Given white jumpsuits that collect color throughout.

ACT ONE: The Gray Diagnosis

Stage: Lyon public square replica, entirely gray

MON (urban planner, holding gray paint samples):
Noise complaints: forty-seven this month.
Every street party shut down.
Every mural painted over.
Lyon is becoming silent.
Shows city plans
Private joy only. Public joy is... disorderly.
I studied at École Nationale Supérieure.
They taught us control, flow, efficiency.
Never taught us happiness.
My blueprints win awards for "urban management."
But yesterday, a child asked me,
"Why are all the buildings sad?"
I couldn't answer.

FOEX (elderly resistance fighter):
I remember when these streets sang.
1968, we danced on cobblestones.
Fête des Lumières wasn't always corporate.
It started in windows—candles for Mary.
Every house participating, no tickets sold.
We danced here. Strangers became family.
Now? Everyone watches joy on phones.
Alone. Behind walls.
My granddaughter doesn't know neighbors' names.
Lives here twelve years, knows no one.
We had less but shared more.
One accordion could make whole street dance.
Now everyone has Spotify but dances alone.

DOBET (artist, covered in hidden paint):
They arrested me for yarn-bombing.
YARN. BOMBING.
As if color is terrorism.
Reveals paint under clothes
But I carry rainbows anyway.
Three thousand euros fine for "vandalism."
Making ugly statue beautiful is vandalism?
The statue was already vandalizing our eyes!
Gray concrete Louis XIV, depressing everyone.
I gave him rainbow scarf. Children laughed!
First time they noticed him.
Art should be contagious, not contained.

BOB (tourist from Denmark):
Where's your hygge? Your public life?
In Copenhagen, we sit in squares.
Together. With strangers. In December!
Here, squares are just... transit.
No lingering. No life.
Your beautiful Place Bellecour—
Largest pedestrian square in Europe!
Used for what? Walking through quickly?
We'd have markets, music, meals there.
You have "regulations."

AMEL (teenager, scrolling phone):
Why go outside? Joy is here.
Shows Instagram
Look—Holi in India. Carnival in Brazil.
I watch joy from my room.
Seventy-three countries' festivals this year.
All from bed. No cost, no crowds.
My parents think I'm antisocial.
But I have twelve thousand followers!
I comment on joy globally.
Just don't create it locally.

FOEX:
You watch others' joy.
That's not the same as creating it.
Your followers—do they hold you when you cry?
Do they dance with you at 2am?
Can you smell their happiness?
Touch their celebration?
You're pornographer of joy, not participant.

ACT TWO: The World Tour

Each character embodies different global joy practice

BOB (teaching hygge):
Candles. Blankets. But TOGETHER.
Private coziness becomes public warmth.
Lights candles across stage
See? Strangers gathering already.
In Denmark, darkness is excuse for closeness.
We don't fight winter—we embrace it.
Communal warmth against cold.
Your Lyon winters—perfect for hygge!
But you hide in separate apartments,
Each paying separate heating bills,
Dying separately of loneliness.

DOBET (initiating Holi):
Color doesn't ask permission.
Throws powder
In India, CEOs and street sweepers—
Same purple face. Equality through pigment.
One day where hierarchy dissolves.
The untouchables touch everyone with color.
The priests get painted by children.
Enemies throw rainbow at each other.
Then eat together, purple-faced, laughing.
When did Lyon last equalize anything?

AMEL (discovering Hanami):
Japan stops everything for blossoms.
Entire nation. Under trees. Together.
Projects cherry blossoms
They celebrate transience. We deny it.
Corporations close! Schools empty!
For flowers that last one week!
They plan entire year around maybe-dates.
"Maybe April 3rd, maybe 7th, depends on trees."
Trees determine schedule, not spreadsheets.
We don't even notice seasons changing.

FOEX (demonstrating Sobremesa):
Spanish secret: don't leave table.
Empty plates, full conversation.
Time isn't money. Time is connection.
They have word for it! SOBREMESA!
The table-after-table. Sacred lingering.
No one checks phones. No one apologizes.
Two hours after eating, still talking.
Here? "Check please" before dessert arrives.
Rush to nowhere particularly important.

MON (resisting but intrigued):
But permits! Insurance! Liability!
Someone could slip on color!
Someone could claim candle burns!
The lawyers would destroy us.
The city council would fire me.
The newspapers would say "chaos."
Pause
But also... attendance at city meetings?
Seven people. From 390,000 residents.
No one cares about perfectly regulated emptiness.

ALL:
Someone could experience joy!
Someone could make friends!
Someone could remember why they live here!

ACT THREE: The Permission Experiment

Audience divided: Half stays seated, half joins public square

DOBET:
We don't need permission for sunrise.
Why need permission for celebration?
The sun doesn't file paperwork.
Birds don't get singing licenses.
Children don't need permits to laugh.
When did joy require bureaucracy?

Square group starts dancing

MON (to seated group):
Stay safe. Stay ordered. Stay private.
Your insurance remains valid.
Your liability stays limited.
Your comfort zone intact.

Shows data

FOEX:
Seated group: cortisol rising.
Dancing group: oxytocin, dopamine, serotonin.
Same space. Different choices.
The dancers' hearts synchronizing—
Can measure it! Same rhythm!
The seated? Random heartbeats.
Together but separate.

AMEL (livestreaming then stopping):
Wait. More people watching online
Than participating here.
We've become joy voyeurs.
Three hundred viewing, thirty dancing.
The ratio is always like this.
Ten watching for every one doing.
Puts phone down
I'm switching statistics.

BOB:
In Estonia, 300,000 sang for freedom.
Together. Physical. Real.
No one won independence through Instagram.
They call it Singing Revolution.
Imagine—overthrowing Soviet Union with songs!
Five years of singing together.
Then freedom. From harmony, not violence.
When did Lyon last sing together?

ACT FOUR: The Lyon Liberation

Real Lyon streets appear on screens—live feed

DOBET:
Tonight, while we're here,
My friends are yarn-bombing Place Bellecour.
Live feed shows it happening
Art without permission. Joy without profit.
Seventeen locations simultaneously.
Every sad statue getting color therapy.
Police confused—is yarn illegal?
Children helping before parents stop them!

MON (transformation beginning):
The liability... the insurance...
Pause
My grandfather painted murals.
Before regulations. Before fear.
Lyon was Europe's color capital.
Silk weavers, four hundred years!
Colors no one had seen!
Now we paint everything gray
To avoid "controversy."
Takes paint
His mural still exists. Under seven layers.
Of regulation beige.

FOEX:
Still could be colorful. Tonight.
Hands paint to MON
Your city plans. What if they included joy?
Designated celebration zones.
Permanent permission for art.
Budget line for "unexpected beauty."
You have power. Use it differently.

AMEL (putting phone down):
This Colombian traffic dancer on TikTok—
No. Wait. I could dance at traffic lights.
Here. Real. Now.
Why watch Bogotá's joy from Lyon?
Our traffic is just as dance-able!
Starts choreographing
Red light = performance opportunity.
Green light = bow and run!

BOB:
Every culture has public joy technology.
Lyon invented cinema! Lumière brothers!
Public screening was original social media.
Everyone gathering, gasping together.
Sharing wonder simultaneously.
You invented collective viewing!
Then forgot and watch Netflix alone.
Your DNA contains public joy.
Just needs reactivation.

FINALE: The Spill

Theater walls become transparent. Performance spills into actual Lyon streets

ALL:
Private joy is commodity.
Public joy is democracy.
Gray cities create gray minds.
Colorful cities create possibilities.

MON (now painting with everyone):
New city ordinance draft:
Every gray wall—potential canvas.
Every square—potential celebration.
Every stranger—potential friend.
Every complaint about joy—
Investigate the complainer's sadness.
Maybe they need invitation, not silence.

DOBET:
Capitalism wants you alone, buying joy.
We're together, creating it.
Cost of this paint? Forty euros.
Value of this moment? Unmeasurable.
That's why they fear public joy—
Can't price it, tax it, control it.

FOEX:
Memorize this feeling.
Your neurons are synchronizing.
Collective effervescence. Ancient technology.
Your ancestors knew this secret.
Every culture developed it differently.
But all knew: joy shared multiplies.
Joy hoarded rots.

AMEL:
Posting this? No.
Living it? Yes.
Being the joy others might watch.
But better—being joy others join.
My followers can't feel this paint.
Can't smell this possibility.
Time to make Lyon worth documenting.

BOB:
Take this home. To Copenhagen.
To Cairo. To Canton.
Your city needs Holi, Hanami, Sobremesa.
Start with your street.
One borrowed tradition.
One invented celebration.
One moment of collective courage.

Audience exits through street party, white jumpsuits now rainbow

[International elements and imagistic amplifications remain the same]
