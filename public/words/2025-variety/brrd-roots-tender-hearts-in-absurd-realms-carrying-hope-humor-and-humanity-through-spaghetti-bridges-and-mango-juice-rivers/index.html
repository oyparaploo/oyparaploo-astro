Brrd Roots ~ Tender Hearts in Absurd Realms

Words / 2025 Variety

Carrying Hope, Humor, and Humanity Through
Spaghetti Bridges and Mango Juice Rivers

Every day’s a journey—a leap, a stumble, a roll through surreal landscapes that seem designed to test your patience, your resolve, and occasionally, your ability to eat spaghetti bridges without collapsing them. Some days, you find yourself crossing paths with flawed souls who are carrying pressures so heavy you wonder how they’re still standing. And yet, they do—messy, vulnerable, tender, and utterly human.

The Stardust trees have seen these individuals too. They’ve witnessed workers dragging themselves home, bearing invisible loads of exhaustion and worry, their conversations peppered with quiet sighs. One evening, a man sat beneath the trees, staring at the bioluminescent polka dots glowing on the bark as though they were guiding him somewhere. “Lost my job today,” he admitted to no one in particular. “Lost my temper too. Not a great combo.” A stranger walking by stopped, handed him a donut from the hedgehog stand without saying a word, and sat beside him. It wasn’t a solution—it didn’t fix the job or the temper—but for that moment, he wasn’t alone, and sometimes that’s enough to keep going.

Wading through rivers of mango juice isn’t supposed to be a normal part of life, but for one woman, it might as well have been. Her uniform was sticky, her hair smelled faintly of citrus, and her manager had just informed her she’d need to “volunteer” for extra shifts. Standing on the shore, a man juggling two pool noodles and a pie hollered, “Need a flotation device?” She laughed despite herself, not because her situation wasn’t absurd, but because it was—and that laughter, brief as it was, reminded her she was still human, still tender, still capable of connection.

Madness thrives under pressure. It’s the single mom who works two jobs to keep the glow-in-the-dark jellybeans out of the pantry and the existential balloons from reaching her kids. It’s the teacher who spends late nights creating lesson plans, only to navigate unicycle-powered commutes each morning. It’s the person who holds it together for everyone around them until they quietly break in the privacy of their closet. But even amid the madness, acts of humanity shine through—like the barista who adds an extra shot of espresso for free or the coworker who sneaks them half a churro during the lunch break they weren’t supposed to take. These aren’t grand gestures, but they’re lifelines.

Run like a giant marshmallow is chasing you—if you’ve got the energy for it. But if the pressures you’re facing feel insurmountable, stop running. Sit. Let someone else share the weight for a moment. Maybe it’s the fellow struggler who’s also been pie-fighting their way through life, or maybe it’s the stranger who hands you a glowing rubber duck and says, “Trust me, you’ll need this.” Those moments of sharing, of mutual vulnerability—they’re how we carry the unbearable together.

Pain under pressure feels different—heavier, sharper, and oddly isolating. It’s the manager who’s trying to meet impossible quotas, the artist who’s terrified their work isn’t good enough, the teenager who feels like they’re already failing at life before they’ve really started living it. Yet even here, kindness pokes through the cracks. A neighbor offers to mow the lawn when they see the artist pacing in frustration. A friend sends the teenager a playlist full of songs about hope. Someone scrawls “It’s okay to take a break” on the sidewalk outside the manager’s office. None of it fixes the pressures, but it reminds us we don’t have to endure them alone.

Cities brimming with rubber ducks and pudding packets are filled with flawed, fragile people doing their best to hold it together under pressure. The unicycle commuters wobble under their office bags and existential dread balloons, but occasionally, someone stops to steady a stranger. Surveillance cameras blink, but they can’t see the teacher sharing crayons with a kid who doesn’t have any. Systems press down, yet neighbors band together to build jellybean rafts, imperfect but strong enough to hold a few fragile lives afloat. It’s messy, sure. But it’s tender—and it matters.

So wield your humor like a safety net, your kindness like a pie-ready shield, and let your hope be the pool noodle that keeps you upright in the mango juice. Shine not because life is easy, but because the absurd, the tender, and the flawed deserve light. Whether faced with alien karaoke champions or crumbling spaghetti bridges, let your light say this: we’re imperfect, we’re under pressure, but we’re here—and that’s enough.
