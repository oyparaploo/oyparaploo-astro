Brrd Roots ~ First Last Net and Nest

Words / 2025 Variety

Follows Tower in the iconic model built to merge high-end leisure, hyperbolic houses—weather, a mission, and a hollow hotel shell—puzzled by late-capitalist questions. Futurity operates as a device, though its manual was misplaced decades ago.

Brrd’s composed series of embroidered portraits, filtered through pixelated landscapes, stitches tinted bulging shapes. The imagery hovers over a vista. Foregrounds recoil, twist, thread what-ifs. Never-happened radiates from mountainous material—with empathy—adopting modular her-walls, which expand and contract as if unsure of their purpose.

Pools of table mushrooms spread, transplanted from flickering pillars, possibly migrating out of boredom. Shadows bend, hesitant, uncertain, consulting among themselves. A signal of suspended collapse—pending, but polite about it.

Documentation stretches time, rendering past and future inseparable. A painfully slow examination.

Sensations of concrete dream on—fractured performative borrowing—languages from films and pod-bandaged castaways, spoken words rearranged into fashion. Reality warps under conversational pressure. Joy flickers between syllables, waiting to be recognized.

A group of friends attempt to connect historical remains. It is so unclear! The message does not follow codings, only instinct, and instinct is unreliable. Strange gestures of futility, curated by the uriner, a scholar of ambiguous studies.

Brno expands. Tugendhat folds inward. To spend time within. To realize that doesn’t so much—as worlds.

These worlds haunt themselves. Expansive, remarkably institutional, shifting. Complexity mutates, ominous, unspoken. Theatrical questioning leans against the wall, waiting for someone to acknowledge it.

By the time we become nested disaster artists, tall borders model themselves, stretch toward stations of the cross. Surroundings question the idea of safe space devices but offer no conclusions.

Portraits dissolve into insulating foam, stitches swollen, blown-up beyond recognition. The works refuse turns. A world of inner captures—adopting textures, refracting patterns. Joy threads itself through the framework, unnoticed but vital.

Eyeglasses shape surgically planted light, emanating through hand-blown pillars, which glow but refuse to explain why.

Chinese Opera Nest

On Memory, Vanishing Structures, and the Fragile Foundations of Civilization

Prologue – The First Attempt

(The stage is vast but incomplete—a skeletal city rising into uncertain heights. Figures move within it, testing its solidity, their garments embroidered with shifting landscapes that flicker in and out of visibility. A low hum vibrates through the space, carrying echoes of past and future.)

Narrator (a voice drifting through time):
Nest does not stand.
It lifts, tilts, unfastens.
First, but unfinished—
A structure shaped by its own hesitation.
A foundation waiting for memory.

(A pillar breathes. A banner flickers. Shadows move through spaces that do not yet exist.)

Chorus of Builders (singing, layered harmonies):
We lay the stones,
but the earth does not hold them.
We carve the walls,
but the air does not claim them.
We stretch toward permanence,
but permanence does not stretch toward us.

(The banners unravel in slow motion, dissolving like silk turning into mist.)

---

Act I – The Ghosts of Fortresses

(The skeletal city shifts—it forms the outline of a fortress, strong yet visibly eroding. Military figures step forward, carrying ancient banners, their gestures precise but their formations faltering.)

Narrator (with measured weight):
They built fortresses against uncertainty.
They built defenses against the unseen.
But the unseen does not wait.
The unseen does not honor borders.

Chorus of Vanishing Strongholds (singing, rhythmic, firm):
The Maginot Line—
built as certainty, undone by strategy.
The island fortresses—
meant to endure, forgotten by time.
The mountain bunkers—
sealed against war, opened by rain.
The great walls—
dividing nothing now,
guarding silence instead of people.

(The fortress trembles—the performers brace against the walls, but the structures pull away from their grasp.)

Uriner, the Scholar (steps forward, eyes scanning the fractures):
What remains?
Stone without war,
strongholds without battle,
monuments sculpted for futures that never arrived.

(A single beam of light cuts through the fortress—it ceases to be a place of protection and instead becomes an echo of itself.)

Act II – The Examination of Memory

(The fortress dissolves. The skeletal city flickers back. Performers move through its vanishing corridors, tracing its shape with gestures that do not complete.)

Castaway Speaker (singing, voices delicate but deliberate):
The echoes loop, unmoored.
The message breaks apart—
Not in codings,
Not in meaning,
But in time itself.

Tugendhat’s Keeper (half-spoken, half-sung, searching):
Brno stretches, yet the walls shrink.
A house with no doors,
A home with no center—
To spend time within,
To realize that it does not exist.

(The embroidered banners collapse again, leaving behind only empty space.)

Act III – The Nested Disaster Artists

(Now, the performers wear eyeglasses made of refracted light—moving through towering structures that glow, but refuse to hold form. The city rises but does not settle.)

Chorus of the Fractured Horizon (grand, sweeping tones):
Play, play, play upon the ruins!
The disaster artists paint with burning questions—
But what color is a safe space?
Where do the portraits rest?

Uriner, the Scholar (steps forward, lifts a single remaining thread from the banners):
The past did not happen. The future will not wait.
The world tilts—
but joy, joy, joy lingers in the embroidered air.

(The pillars breathe once more—the opera closes with figures fading into shifting landscapes, their presence folding into unseen passages. The hum continues, resonating into darkness.)

Epilogue – The Last Construction, Already Unraveling

(A final moment—the stage silent. The fractured pillar remains, half-seen, half-imagined. Time pauses, long enough to acknowledge that no ending is final.)

Narrator (whispering now, almost laughter, almost sorrow):
Everything tilts.
Everything hesitates.
Everything exists—
Only long enough to disappear again.

The Cartography of the Breathing Middle Eye

A Luminous Passage Through Shifting Realities, Angelic Avian Forces, and the Fractured Geometry of Light

Nest does not stand. It lifts, tilts, unfastens.
It does not shelter. It hesitates, shifting before settling.

Light angles in thin, clean slashes.
It filters through fractures in air,
pressing into corners,
holding space without weight.

The protagonist steps forward,
but the ground does not meet them in the same place twice.

Then, the middle eye opens.

Not outward. Not inward.
Up.

Where shadow follows breath.
Where brightness makes its own shape.

The angles sharpen.

Beams stretch, refractions folding,
thin corridors of gold curving around stillness.
Reality shifts,
the world rearranging beneath its own glow.

And something answers.

Figures move inside the spaces between spaces.
Not walking.
Arriving.
Wings trace arcs in the fractured light.
Edges fray,
not dissolving,
but shifting.

The observer kneels.
Listening.
Waiting for the light to explain itself.

"Did you see?" they ask,
the words breaking apart before reaching the protagonist.
"The way the breath of the world curved before the radiance touched it?"

A feather lands.

Not drifting.
Not falling.
Appearing.

Its edge sharp, cut by the slanting sun,
humming with something unseen.

And beneath it—where light meets ground,
where movement hesitates before vanishing—
a single thorn, pale pink, spiraling outward as if blooming into air.
It does not belong, yet it is there,
rooting itself in the shifting glow,
bending impossibly between softness and danger.

By the time they become nested disaster artists,
space does not divide.
It expands.
It folds.
It bends beneath the weight of moving light.

The door reappears.
Not a door.
A passage sculpted in the memory of brightness.
A threshold cut from shifting illumination.

The middle eye breathes.
The horizon tilts.
And the protagonist steps forward.

Only to begin again.

A Cartography of Vanishing

Tracing the Vanishing Point of Shelter

Nest does not stand; it flickers. It does not shelter; it hums, bending inward before spilling outward. It is a threshold, not a destination—a passage folded into itself, shifting between presence and dissolution.

Tower preceded it, but only in the way that echoes precede silence. Nest sways in that silence, shaped not by architecture but by movement. The turbulence of weather, the forgotten mission, the eroded shell of a hotel—they do not contain, only suggest.

In its pulse, time fractures. Brrd’s embroidered portraits do not depict but unravel, pixelated landscapes slipping between recognition and dream. Stitches swell, colors distort, the threads twisting into fragile what-ifs that stretch forward and back, pulling the horizon into a shape that never settles.

Empathy liquefies in modular her-walls, then reforms into surfaces that resist texture—table mushrooms that breathe, pillars that hover without mass. Collapse does not announce itself; it arrives in waves, signals flickering in intervals too slow to perceive. Documentation strains against time, pulling past and future so close together that they fuse into something nameless.

Language shatters—not violently, but inevitably. Words dissolve as they are spoken, lifted from films, echoed through pod-bandaged castaways, returning as fragments of themselves. A group of friends reaches toward history, but grasping is futile—meaning moves faster than hands can close around it. The message loops, recursive, a gesture left unfinished.

Curated by the uriner, these studies stretch across Brno, dripping into Tugendhat’s walls—never settling, only reflecting, only extending, only dissolving. Here, belonging is an illusion, and permanence a trick played by time. Institutions breathe. Places rearrange. The world does not wait to be understood.

By the time we become nested disaster artists, space itself unthreads. Borders do not divide—they fold, stretch, then vanish. Local stations of the cross melt into surroundings that forget they were ever separate. The idea of safe space wavers, flickers, questions itself before dissolving into air.

The embroidered portraits refuse containment—stitched into magnified contours, mounted on insulating foam, they are neither complete nor unfinished. Light filters through handblown pillars, refracting into visions that exist only for a moment, only long enough to be imagined before they disappear.

There is no final form, only transformation. There is no fixed truth, only echoes. The horizon bends, the thread frays, the world tilts, and for a moment, everything is untethered.

Vision in Layers

Late-capitalist questions linger, with futurity wielded as a device—an unfolding speculation—woven through Brrd’s embroidered portraits. These pixelated landscapes distort into swelling stitches, hues expanding and unraveling across a vast vista. The foreground refuses to settle; threads twist through what-ifs, looping backward into material that radiates mountainous presence.

Empathy forms the foundation, shaping modular her-walls that pool into surfaces of table mushrooms. Shadows flicker beneath towering pillars, transplanted as signals of suspended collapse. Documentations stretch time—rendered into painstakingly slow examinations—constructing a presence that oscillates between temporal past and imagined futures.

Concrete dreams persist, threading through performative borrowings—languages lifted from films, fragmented pod-bandaged castaways, spoken words refashioned into gestures of futility. A group of friends reaches toward historical remains, but their message resists coding, locked in an unmoored loop of recursive feedback.

Curated by the uriner, these studies unfold in Brno, tugging at Tugendhat’s history—not to settle, but to explore, to inhabit, to resist belonging. The worlds remain haunting—young, expansive, and remarkably institutional—yet shifting, metamorphosed into something ominous and theatrical.

By the time we begin to play at becoming environmentally threatened, the nested disaster artists emerge—tracing towering borders with extensive reach. They model local stations of the cross, sculpting surroundings that interrogate the idea of safe space as device.

Composed through a series of portraits—stitched and magnified, mounted on insulating foam—these works do not offer a turn to the receiver. Instead, they entrap an inner world, adopting textures and patterns, reshaping eyeglasses into surgically planted light, filtering through handblown permanent pillars.
