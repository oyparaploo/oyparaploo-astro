Part One: The Aliens Are Still Puzzled

Someone is seven minutes late to reading this. Always seven minutes, even when time works different near extinction events. In aisle seven of what was Bunny Port, nobody counts bottle caps spelling something only extinct birds can read from above. Goon Ox arranged them: Coke, Coke, Pepsi, Orange, Coke, Coke, Pepsi, Orange, but from above it spells HELP or HOME or GONE. The pigeons know but won't say.

Margaret Chen tastes aluminum where cartoon songs were. Not animated songs but songs that were cartoons, each note a small box with borders, panels in a comic no one drew. She's been tasting this since Tuesday. It's Thursday. It's always Tuesday when extinction needs documenting, when the inventory of absence accumulates.

[Missing: the instruction manual for grief, tomorrow's favorite bird, all compasses from 1987]

The aliens observe: "Humans enter scream-hit mode in parking lots, performing ancient ritual called Being Reasonable." They're not wrong. Vera Marcus screams at her car because it won't start, won't stop, won't decide. This is conservation through confusion. The car runs on extinct bird songs now, everyone's does, we just don't.

Doctor Static walks through this paragraph pressing buttons with bomb-defusing terror even though it's just the dishwasher, the coffee maker, the thing that. He presses everything three times. One two three. This is how we check now. This is how we make sure nothing's running that shouldn't be running since everything runs on what's gone.

Part Two: Beautiful Garbage Accumulating

Landing being a form of falling surrendered to gravity, but moving sideways means floating, means soaring, means ending up in yesterday which is the only place birds still. The shadows taste expensive, like old batteries, like the last penny made from actual copper.

Part Three: Tuesday Keeps Happening

He was counting: forty-seven pinecones for forty-seven extinct species but there are thousands extinct so the counting fails which is the point. One two three. Start over. One two three. Here's what's actually funny: humans hate cars but drive them, fear buttons but press them, mourn trees while buying chainsaws. The aliens document this: "Potato City residents move to mountainside to escape Mother Nature destroying machines, immediately buy worst gas guzzlers available." This is comedy. This is conservation. This is sincere confusion.

Sky Fores tastes copper pennies in raindrops during migration season. Seventeen cents per drop. She's calculated this matters more to rain than to the counter. The rain carries messages from extinct birds, each drop a panel in the weather's comic book. The messages say Tuesday. They always say Tuesday.

Part Five: Circular Requiem Returning

Marcus Stremler builds exact trees from receipt paper. Life-sized. They rustle wrong. This wrongness is the sincerity we've been documenting.

Receipt trees falling in rows become rising when wind arranges them into nests never holding turquoise eggs of air. This is the pattern: linear falling becomes circular rising becomes nothing becomes Tuesday becomes the counting that continues.

Sometimes she writes on her arms with grass juice: "The natural world becomes memory not presence." The words fade as she remembers, which is the point, which is conservation through.

This is data. This is the comic panel where everything.

Part Six: Commercial Break

Part Eight: The Methodology Fails Successfully

Here's where everything breaks right: Olivier Caro's mouth full of static electricity where knowledge should be. His perspective bleeding through everything tastes like but not like. He builds a full-scale replica of spider movements using dental floss and chopsticks in the mall parking lot. Passing drivers slow down thinking it's important. It is important but not how.

The dental floss catches streetlight like surface tension would. The chopsticks pierce nothing carefully. This makes more sense than the original sentence about knowledge traveling through tiny gestures because at least we can see the trying.

[Linear: dental floss in rows. Circular: wind tangling it into nest-shaped knots]

Goon Ox still arranging bottle caps but now they spell something only altitude can read. From ground level: chaos. From bird height: a message. From extinction height: Tuesday. It's always Tuesday from extinction height.

Desta Yuki was going to explain but. Skip it for now. For now stretching forward. For forward becoming backward. For backward becoming Tuesday.
