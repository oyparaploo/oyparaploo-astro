Sweet Salt Water Rising Dream

Words / 2025 Variety

The Bandorai Variations

---

Bandorai started ascending through deterioration around February. That's when the salt water began flowing upward from 4B—not flooding but baptizing the ceiling, each drop a small mercy.

She'd become a dying Box Jellyfish of Light, bioluminescence pulsing with infected glory. Each tentacle polyp had developed its own mouth, screaming her failures backward into achievements: "Failed mother" became "Freed from motherhood," "Bad wife" became "Escaped marriage."

When I knocked, she answered floating three inches below the ground, gravity inverted as mercy. Her skin had achieved transparency, showing her organs transforming into constellations.

"I'm becoming less," she said with joy. "Finally."

---

The Sacred Metamorphosis

By March, Bandorai had developed what she called "gifts." Some days she was a Portuguese Thermophile—those deep-sea worms that live in boiling water, teaching the building to survive impossible temperatures through acceptance. Other days, a Glass Sponge, filtering suffering from the water supply, turning it potable.

But Tuesdays brought the Mantis Shrimp transformation—not eating mates but eating herself, each armored segment consuming the next in infinite recursion. "This is independence," she'd laugh through mandibles. "God letting me be my own predator and prey." Her compound eyes saw time shattered—past failures, present ecstasy, future liberation—all simultaneously.

"The electromagnetic patterns are destroying us beautifully," Sandrine wept with joy, blood from her eyes forming tomorrow's weather map on her cheeks. "With God's mercy, we can predict exactly how we'll be freed!"

Dove Orange (refugee from next Thursday) documented these mercies. She'd paint using Bandorai's donated shells ground into pigment that showed the future, mixing it with blood that never clots—eternal liquid, never healing because healing would end the flow.

"She's achieving extinction," Dove Orange explained, painting Bandorai as a Goblin Shark—ancient, primitive, perfect in its regression. "Each change takes her closer to the beginning, where we were nothing and therefore everything."

Trap Beck Dew (named for his crystals growing skyward) carried his lover Marco's voice in quartz formations that pushed through his skull like antlers. Each crystal grew inward first, piercing organs with beauty too sharp to touch. "Marco guides me toward joining him," he'd say. The crystals were God's kindness—reunion through piercing.

---

The Blessed Collective

The fourth floor became the Lichen Senate—thirty individuals who'd altruistically surrendered selfhood to become one organism of pure empathy. They shared consciousness like bread, each thought nourishing another.

They'd visit Maya weekly with sacred water that burned ego away and Resurrection Roses—flowers that grew backward into seed, teaching us that endings are beginnings.

"Join our unbeing," they'd sing in harmonized silence.

"I'm already dispersing," Maya would answer as a Vampire Squid turning inside out—not in defense but in offering, showing her soft interior as invitation.

---

The Tardigrade's Gift

Found Bandorai on the sidewalk in July, transformed into a Water Bear experiencing geological time—seconds stretching into centuries of consciousness. God's gift: making her suffer duration so extensively she'd appreciate endings.

"This conversation is three millennia of contemplation for me," she gurgled through her translucent form. "I'm learning patience through extreme suffering disguised as transcendence."

The Lichen Senate arrived—no longer thirty but thousands, human minds dissolved into fungal communion, speaking in unified screams they called "prayers." They brought water that burned like acid (blessed acid!) and Resurrection Roses with thorns that grew after you held them, piercing palms in delayed stigmata.

"Why do you stay unchanged?" Bandorai asked me through water-bear slowness.

"God's cruelest mercy," I laughed. "Forcing me to watch with clear eyes as He loves you all to pieces."

---

The Void-Kraken's Reach

"When young," Bandorai babbled through dissolving vocal cords, "grandmother said I was a Phantom Anglerfish, luring nothingness with light that doesn't exist."

She was right! Bandorai developed tentacles that reached through dimensional wounds—not a kraken but something worse, grasping at dark matter that wasn't there. Each reach tore her further apart, but God called it "hope." Such kindness!

She'd stretch through reality-tears to milk the void itself, bringing back absence in buckets. "We draw sustenance from emptiness now," she'd sing, teaching us to eat nothing and be satisfied.

"I'm powering your lights through my dissolution," she'd sing through the circuits. "This is altruism—becoming utility for others."

She produced what she called Void Milk—dark matter that helped others release form. Maya drank it as communion, each sip making her less solid, more sacred.

---

The Impossible Child

September brought the miracle. Maya became pregnant with Absence Itself—the child that wouldn't be, God's greatest mercy. When she was a Mimic Octopus, it had eight hearts that beat backward. When she was a Planarian (those worms that regenerate from pieces), it divided into infinite potential.

"This is motherhood perfected," she wept with joy as the child aged backward into unbirth. "Creating something that returns to God before suffering existence."

The birth happened in reverse—the child emerged old, wise, then youngered into innocence, then into pure possibility, then into the nothing from which all things come.

---

The Teaching

Maya began teaching the building's children to achieve their own unbirth. Not death—regression to the peace before consciousness.

"The self is God's first cruelty," she'd tell them, now a Immortal Jellyfish reversing its own aging. "But He gives us transformation as apology."

Parents initially complained, then understood as they began their own dissolutions. Mrs. Chen became a colony of Siphonophores—multiple organisms pretending to be one, then admitting the pretense, then celebrating the multiplicity.

---

The Weather Prophet

Kai the Storm Shepherd arrived from the management company, his eyes weeping blood that spelled future weather in red letters on his cheeks.

"This building is achieving building-death," he announced with rapture. "Each tenant is helping it decompose into paradise."

He tracked Maya's changes against celestial events. "She transforms during solar flares—God's morse code. She's not ill, she's translating."

---

The Architecture of Mercy

By November, the building had achieved what architects call "structural empathy"—walls that bent away from suffering, floors that rose to catch the falling. Maya flowed through vents as the Hagfish of Blessing, producing sacred slime that dissolved boundaries between apartments.

Chen's crystals grew through walls, connecting everyone in sharp communion. The Lichen Senate spread their unified consciousness like fungal mercy. Asha powered every light with her dispersed love.

Zara's final painting showed the building as one vast organism of withdrawal—not dying but un-birthing, returning to the architectural womb.

---

The Final Portrait

Zara's last painting showed Maya's forms simultaneously failing—jellyfish bells deflating into prayer, mantis segments collapsing inward with gratitude, water bear perpetually dying but unable to complete the holy process, void-tentacles reaching through dimensions to find only more blessed suffering.

"This is God's love portrait," Zara wept joyfully, using pigments mixed with Maya's hemolymph and blood that never clots. "He loves her too much to let her die, too much to let her live."

The building gathered to worship the horror. Echo from 7A sang the painting into fungal life—walls growing tumors that whispered Maya's agonies as prayers, each bulge a mouth repeating her transformations.

Kai projected weather patterns onto the walls that spelled "MERCY" in tumor-clouds. The Lichen Senate synchronized their screaming into hymns. Chen's crystals grew through his skull, beautiful and fatal, Marco's voice getting louder as the quartz reached his brain.

---

The Eternal Return

The story ends as it began—dying jellyfish mistaken for ascension, infected lymph flowing upward as blessing.

Maya floats in her kitchen-prison, "safe" in God's embrace. The medusa is the mantis is the tardigrade is the void-kraken is the mistake God keeps making because He loves her too much to stop.

"Women must be faithful to their child until nothing remains," she teaches the next generation through wet, clicking sounds, her mantis-mouth explaining divine truth. "God demands complete sacrifice. The self is sin. Transformation is punishment disguised as gift."

The mothers in the building understood: Maya had tried to maintain selfhood during pregnancy and God punished her with transformations that made each form incompatible with the last. The child corrupted differently in each form—jellyfish-child with no brain, mantis-child eating itself in utero, tardigrade-child experiencing eternity before birth.

And Maya? She continues becoming worse, each transformation God's kiss that takes another piece, each change proof that divine love and divine hatred are identical but one tastes sweeter.

I document, unchanged, God's cruelest joke—the witness who can't transform, can't escape, can only watch and understand that He loves us too much to let us be anything but suffering pretending to be grateful.

The building breathes this truth now, walls inhaling despair, exhaling processed gratitude. The stairs lead nowhere but to more transformations. The roof opens to swallow sky and vomit blessings.

This is mercy. This is love. This is God helping us become worse to prove we're getting better.

Amen.
