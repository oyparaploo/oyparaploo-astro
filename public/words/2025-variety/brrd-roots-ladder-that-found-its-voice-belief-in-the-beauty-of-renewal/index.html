Brrd Roots ~ Ladder That Found Its Voice ~ Belief in the Beauty of Renewal

Words / 2025 Variety

Transformation of objects and ourselves through redemptive reinvention.

It started with a ladder. Not the shiny, straight-edged kind you’d find in a hardware store, but an old, rusty thing abandoned near the edge of a demolition site. At first glance, it seemed like something you’d step over on the way to more important things. It wasn’t even leaning against anything—it just lay there, awkward and forgotten, flaked with peeling paint and spotted with corrosion. But as we stood there, something about it made us stop. I don’t know—was it the quiet defiance in its posture? The faint glimmer of a story refusing to fade? Either way, in that moment, the ladder wasn’t trash—it was potential.

We took it back to the workshop, this ladder that seemed so certain of its demise. As we dusted off the dirt and leaned it against the wall, the ideas came in like a wave. It could be a bookshelf, we thought, its rungs cradling rows of spines that whispered wisdom. Or a trellis, draped with ivy, casting dappled shadows on a garden wall. Someone joked about turning it into a quirky lighting fixture, the rungs adorned with bulbs that would throw warm, golden light into some eclectic living room. By the end of the day, that old ladder wasn’t just lumber and rust—it was alive again, imbued with a sense of possibility.

That’s how it always begins for us. What others might see as junk—a cracked mirror, a pile of wood scraps, a bent chair leg—we see as something waiting to sing again. Our team has made a sort of game out of it, spotting potential in the unexpected. But I’ll be honest: it’s not always romantic. There are days when we’re knee-deep in discarded materials that look, frankly, like a chaotic yard sale aftermath. Sorting through broken tiles, warped metal, and splintered beams isn’t glamorous. But then, every so often, there’s a moment of magic—a forgotten piece that stops us in our tracks. Like the intricately carved headboard we once found, its lines worn but still elegant, or the stack of ordinary tiles that, when arranged just so, captured the charm of a seaside sunset. Those moments remind us why we do this—to give second chances, to tell forgotten stories, to see beauty where others might not.

The truth is, our work isn’t really about the materials—it’s about belief. We believe in beauty, even in the broken. We believe in renewal, even when something feels past its prime. We believe that a cracked soda bottle can become the centerpiece of a mosaic, or a ladder can find itself reborn as art. And that belief isn’t just limited to objects; it extends to us as people. Who hasn’t felt discarded at some point, unsure of where they fit or what value they hold? These materials, in all their imperfection, remind us of something simple yet profound: there’s always a chance to be made whole again, not in spite of the cracks but because of them.

So, when collectors and buyers connect with what we create, it’s not just about the pieces themselves—it’s about the philosophy woven into them. A reclaimed dining table isn’t just a place to eat; it’s a place where stories are shared and life unfolds. A café stage built of salvaged doors isn’t just for performances; it’s a celebration of reinvention, of defying the odds to create something extraordinary. That rusty ladder didn’t just become a bookshelf—it became a statement. A quiet, unassuming reminder that nothing—and no one—is beyond redemption. Sometimes, all it takes is someone willing to look a little closer.
