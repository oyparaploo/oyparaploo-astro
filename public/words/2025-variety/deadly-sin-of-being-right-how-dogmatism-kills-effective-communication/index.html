Deadly Sin of Being Right

How Dogmatism Kills Effective Communication

← Words / 2025 Variety

Pre-Emptive Funeral

Dogmatism announces from the start: "This conversation is already over. I have nothing to learn from you." It's communication that arrives dead, wrapped in certainty's shroud. The dogmatist broadcasts carved-in-stone positions while pretending to converse—mouths moving, words emerging, but no exchange occurring. A pantomime where everyone wastes time going through motions without possibility of communion.

I watched this destroy a twenty-year friendship. Two men who'd built businesses together couldn't discuss vaccination without each becoming dogmatic. Not passionate—dogmatic. The difference? Passion engages; dogmatism dismisses. They'd stopped hearing each other three sentences in, just waiting to deploy pre-loaded arguments. The friendship ended not over disagreement but over the refusal to believe the other might have one valid point.

Contagion of Certainty

Dogmatism spreads like infection. When one person closes, others close in response. Why remain open to someone sealed shut? An arms race begins—everyone hardening positions because no one softens theirs. Communication doesn't just fail; it becomes combat. Dogma clashing with dogma where no one wins because no one can move.

A workplace mediator told me: "Once dogmatism enters a room, it multiplies exponentially. One dogmatic person creates five. Five create twenty. Soon everyone's defending positions they didn't even hold ten minutes ago."

Seven Sins Embodied

Pride: The arrogance of claiming complete understanding. "I've figured everything out, so listen." Speaking from imaginary mountaintops while standing in the same valley as everyone else. A professor admitted to me: "The day I got tenure, I stopped listening to students. I thought earning the title meant I knew everything. I learned nothing new for five years."

Greed: Wanting to own truth, hoard it, control it. Saying "I have THE truth" instead of "I have A truth, show me yours." This greed impoverishes everyone—no one grows when truth can't be exchanged.

Wrath: Breeding resentment in those denied their fundamental right to be heard, to contribute, to matter. Being treated as empty vessels for someone else's certainty creates rage that simmers, then explodes.

Envy: Unable to bear that others might possess insights you lack. Dismissing preemptively: "That can't be true because I don't already know it." The jealous guarding of intellectual territory.

Gluttony: The insatiable hunger for certainty, consuming it beyond need or health. Gorging on rightness until it becomes sickness.

Sloth: Mental laziness—easier to hold fixed positions than constantly adjusting to new information. Dogmatism lets you stop thinking because you've already decided.

Lust: The pornography of being correct. Getting satisfaction from shutting others down with "facts." The arousal of intellectual domination.

Murder of Wonder

Dogmatism kills curiosity. Why ask questions when you possess all answers? It makes the world small and known when it's actually vast and mysterious. This supposed intellectual strength is actually intellectual death—convictions without flexibility are just prejudices with better public relations.

It commits spiritual violence, erasing others' reality in favor of absolute versions. Killing the validity of lived experience. Good ideas delivered dogmatically become repellent. People reject insights because they're rejecting certainty's aggression.

Self-Defeating Sin

Dogmatism prevents its own truth from being received. Instead of spreading understanding, it creates resistance. The more resistance encountered, the more dogmatic the response. "They reject me because they can't handle truth!" when they're actually rejecting violent delivery.

While the dogmatist remains rigid, opportunities for understanding pass. Moments of potential connection get sacrificed to the god of being right.

Simple Antidote

Three words redeem communication: "I might be wrong." These words open every door dogmatism closes. But dogmatists can't speak them—allergic to the strength that appears as weakness. The strength to be uncertain, in process, learning.

Dogmatism mistakes rigidity for strength and flexibility for weakness. But trees that bend survive storms. Trees that won't bend break. Dogmatic communication always breaks eventually. Reality humbles certainty, but often not before tremendous damage to relationships, communities, truth itself.

Language of Journey

Avoiding this sin starts with genuine questions—real ones wanting real answers, leaving space for surprise. Using exploration's language rather than declaration's. "I'm finding that..." rather than "It is..."

Communication is journey. Anyone claiming arrival has stopped communicating, just issuing proclamations from fixed positions. No one wants lectures from someone's destination. We want to journey together, sharing exploration that makes communication alive.

Tragedy

Dogmatism is chosen deafness in a world full of voices worth hearing. Dogmatists never experience the surprise of learning something new, the delight of changed minds. They've traded growth for the cold comfort of being "right"—the poverty of certainty in a universe of infinite complexity.

The sin of premature certainty murders discovery's possibility, and with it, real communication's joy.
