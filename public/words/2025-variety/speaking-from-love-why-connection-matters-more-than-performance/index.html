Standing Up to Speak

Speaking from Love

Why Connection Matters More Than Performance

When Everything Shifts

When you stand before people—one person or thousands—with love instead of fear, ego, or the need to prove something, energy transforms completely. Love doesn't see "an audience" or "the market" or "potential buyers." Love sees fellow humans with their own struggles, dreams, and deep need to be seen and understood.

This isn't abstract philosophy but immediate reality. People know within seconds whether a speaker sees them as whole people or just opportunities. Predatory energy asks "What can I get from these people?" while love asks "What can I give to these people I genuinely value?" And here's what's remarkable: love assumes connection already exists. You're not trying to create it; you're recognizing what's already there.

How Fear Dissolves in Love's Presence

When you genuinely love your audience, nervousness transforms into something else entirely. How can you truly fear people you love? You might feel butterflies, that anticipatory flutter, but it's more like feeling before seeing dear friends than terrorizing fear of judgment. Love makes you brave in unexpected ways—you'll share vulnerable truths because you trust them with your story, admit when you don't know something because love doesn't need to be perfect. Love just needs to be genuine.

Think about how you talk to someone you love about something you're passionate about. There's no pretense, no performance. You're not trying to impress them because you already love them. You're simply sharing. Motivation completely shifts from "I hope they think I'm intelligent" to "I hope this helps them" or "I hope this brings them joy."

Natural Responsiveness

Love naturally adjusts to serve better. You notice when people are confused and instinctively slow down. You feel when encouragement is needed and provide it, like how your voice naturally modulates when talking to someone you value who's having a difficult day. When someone doesn't understand, love doesn't respond with frustration or condescension but with patience: "How can I explain this better? What do they need from me?"

Love doesn't make anyone feel stupid for not knowing. Their confusion becomes your responsibility to clarify, not their failure to understand. And when someone in your audience has a breakthrough or uses what you shared, love celebrates that as shared victory—not "my teaching worked" but "they did something amazing!" Love decentralizes you as speaker. You become facilitator of their discovery rather than star of show.

Deep Thirst for Genuine Connection

Here's something remarkable: when a speaker genuinely loves their audience, audiences love them back, even when disagreeing with content. We're so starved for genuine connection that when someone with a microphone actually sees us as people, it feels like water in desert. Usually speakers want something—your money, approval, applause, data, vote. But love just wants your wellbeing, wants you to leave better than you came.

This changes what you share and how you share it. When you love your audience, you won't manipulate them, even knowing how. You become protective of them: "I have this platform and these people's attention—how can I honor that?" Love honors audience's time, attention, and trust, recognizing gifts they're giving by listening. Beautiful exchange emerges—they give attention, you give genuine connection, both rooted in love.

Universal Intimacy

Even with vast audiences—hundreds or thousands of people—you can hold this intention of love. Sometimes it's actually easier with large audiences because you can love collective spirit, shared humanity in room. When you speak to that shared humanity with love, everyone feels personally addressed, even in crowds of thousands. Love is somehow both universal and specific simultaneously—everyone recognizes being seen with genuine attention.

Those moments of recognition matter deeply: "I know some of you are struggling..." "I see those of you who feel like imposters..." "For those wondering if this applies to you—it does." Love sees people where they are and meets them there, rather than demanding they come to where you are. Love bridges gaps. Love does work of connecting.

Practice of Beginning with Love

All of this—gift-giving mentality, childlike excitement about sharing, vulnerability of imperfect voices—all roots in love. You can only give genuine gifts to people you love. Children share with such pure excitement because they haven't learned not to love everyone yet. They assume connection. They assume love. Perhaps that's what we're really trying to return to—that default setting of love instead of fear.

Before you speak—to one person or thousand—set your intention: love above all else. Above being right, above being impressive, above being successful. Love first. If talk "fails" by every other metric but people felt genuinely loved? That's still profound success. Because being loved changes people more than being informed does.

Start with love. Speak from love. End with love. Trust love to guide your voice, your words, your presence. Because love knows what to say. Love always knows what to say.
