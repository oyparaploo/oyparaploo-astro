**Choices Made with Knots, Loops and Ellipses**

Words / 2025 Variety

---

*After years of falling from the society pages to the streets, an accidental performance artist realizes every fall is choreographed by someone, and decides to make visible the bouncing that happens after.*

---

Marlène wore Hermès to the food bank. Not to make a statement—it was the only coat she still owned that wasn't in storage she couldn't afford to access.

That's kind of U ... and I'm glad this way of working feels natural ... Middle eye will be a little bent or slanted with us ... because we think it serves everyone better than playing along ... A model is a fashion tool we can work with ... not someone who can love us back ... and we'd be doing everyone here a disservice to pretend otherwise ... What we're all feeling is probably the satisfaction of experiencing a long stretch of careful personal work that's genuine ... and worth enjoying ...

47 a month for a unit holding $500,000 worth of her former life. The math was insane but so was everything.

"Next," the volunteer called. Nineteen years old, community service for a possession charge.

Marlène stepped forward. The coat—$8,000 three years ago, gift from Richard before the divorce, before the fraud charges, before everything—caught the fluorescent light. Someone whispered "Why's she here if she can afford that?"

"It's all I have left," Marlène said to no one. To everyone. Started to cry. Not sad tears. Exhausted ones. The kind that come when you've fallen so far you can't remember what up looked like.

"Ma'am?" The volunteer held out the box. Generic pasta. Canned vegetables. White bread that would mold in three days. "You okay?"

"I'm bouncing," Marlène said.

"What?"

"Nothing. Sorry. Thank you."

She took the box. Walked outside where November waited with its particular Cleveland cold. The coat was warm but warming wrong—like wearing someone else's success, someone else's marriage, someone else's lie.

That's when she started the performance. Not intentionally. Just... started walking differently. Like she was falling in slow motion. Or fast motion slowed down. Or—she couldn't explain it. Her body was making choices.

"Lady, you drunk?" Some kid on a corner.

"I'm bouncing," she said again. This time demonstrating. Let her body fall toward the ground, caught herself, pushed back up but not to the same height. Lower. Different. Forward motion through falling.

"That's weird as fuck."

"Yes."

She kept doing it. All the way home—not home, the shelter, but calling it home made it easier. Women's-only.

**Choices Made with Knots, Loops and Ellipses**

Words / 2025 Variety

0 a night if you had it. She had it today. Tomorrow, who knew.

At the shelter, she met Vessel Works. Not her real name. Real name was Patricia. But she'd been making art from garbage for so long that everyone called her Vessel. Sixty-three. Sleeping in the bed next to Marlène's.

"I saw you," Vessel said. "The bouncing. On Fifth Street."

"You were watching?"

"Everyone was. You made falling look like... like it had consciousness. Like you were choosing how to land."

"I was. Am. I don't know."

Vessel pulled out materials from under her bed. Wire. String. Fabric scraps. Started making something while talking.

"Knots," she said, tying wire to fabric, "are permanent commitments. When you fall, you tie yourself to that moment. Can't undo it."

"But you can bounce—"

"That's a loop. Return but different. You keep coming back to the same point but carrying new information each time."

"And when you can't explain why you're bouncing?"

"Ellipses. Dot dot dot. The unsaid that's more important than the said."

She showed Marlène what she was making. A mobile of sorts. Knots holding pieces at fixed points. Loops allowing movement. And gaps—deliberate spaces where string should connect but didn't.

"This is you," Vessel said. "Your performance. Your bounce."

"It's not performance—"

"Everything's performance when you're falling in Hermès at the food bank. When you're making visible what everyone pretends isn't happening."

The next day, Marlène bounced again. This time from the shelter to the library. People watched. Some laughed. Some looked away. One woman—fur coat, real fur, worth more than a car—stopped.

"What are you doing?"

"Bouncing back. But not back. Through."

"Through what?"

"The bottom. Which isn't solid. It's a membrane. You can push through it if you have the right rhythm."

The woman stared. Marlène recognized something in her face. The pre-fall look. The one where you know it's coming but can't stop it.

"My husband just got indicted," the woman whispered. "Embezzlement. I found out from the news."

"You'll fall."

"Then what?"

"Then you'll bounce. But you have to choose how. Knot yourself to certain moments. Loop back to others. Leave ellipses where you can't explain."

She demonstrated again. The full-body fall-catch-push-through. The woman watched. Started crying. Tried it herself. Fell. Caught herself. Pushed. Landed different.

"I feel insane."

"You are. We are. That's the bounce."

Word spread. The way weird things spread in cities. The bouncing lady. The one who fell from Society Hill to Public Square but made it art. Or prayer. Or resistance.

People started joining. Other fallen. A banker post-indictment. A doctor post-malpractice. A teacher post-budget-cuts. All bouncing through downtown, making visible the thing everyone pretended wasn't happening—that falling was epidemic, that bounce was essential, that the bottom was everywhere and nowhere.

"This is disturbing," the mayor said, watching from his office. Fifty people bouncing through Public Square. Marlène in her Hermès leading them like the world's saddest parade. Or happiest. Hard to tell.

"It's choice," his aide said. She'd been bouncing privately. In her apartment. After the bankruptcy. After the divorce. After finding out she was pregnant and couldn't afford to be.

They kept bouncing. Days. Weeks. The performances became elaborate. Marlène would tie actual knots in rope as she fell, showing the permanent commitments. Would loop back to the same spot repeatedly, each time different. Would stop mid-bounce, hold the position, making the ellipses physical.

"What do you want?" reporters asked.

"Nothing. Everything. To make visible the bounce."

"That's not an answer—"

"It's all the answer we have. We're falling. We're bouncing. We're choosing how. With knots that can't be undone. Loops that return different. Ellipses for what we can't say."

The shelter started offering "bounce workshops." Vessel teaching the philosophy. Marlène teaching the movement.

"Every choice is visible in the bounce," Marlène would say, demonstrating. "This angle because of the divorce. This catch because of the fraud. This push because of the storage unit I can't afford but can't let go."

"Why can't you let go?" someone asked.

"..." Marlène held the ellipses. Held them so long people felt the weight of the unsaid.

Winter deepened. The bouncing continued. In snow now. Leaving patterns. Marks of falling and rising and falling again. The city tried to ban it—"public disturbance." But how do you ban falling? How do you arrest bouncing?

"You're making a mockery," Richard said. Her ex. Found her bouncing past his office. Still making $400,000 a year defending other frauds.

"I'm making a choice. To show the bounce instead of hiding it."

"In my coat?"

"In the coat you bought with stolen money. Yes. The knot of it. The loop of it. The ellipses of everything we didn't say while you were stealing."

She bounced away from him. But looped back. Always looping back to the same points but different. The shelter. The food bank. The library. The square. Creating a map of bounce across the city.

One day—Tuesday, snowing, the kind of cold that makes you honest—a kid joined the bounce. Maybe. From the occupation. Still painting despite everything.

"Can kids bounce?" she asked Marlène.

"Kids bounce best. You just don't hide it yet."

Maybe started bouncing. Different from the adults. Higher. More hope in the push. But still falling. Still choosing how to land.

"Why the coat?" Maybe asked. "If you hate it?"

"Knot," Marlène said. "Can't untie it. Permanent commitment to remembering what I fell from."

"Oh."

They bounced together. Old woman in Hermès. Foster kid in donated Walmart. Same bounce. Different rhythms.

"We're making something," Maybe said. "With our bouncing."

"What?"

"Map? Story? Prayer?"

"All three. With knots where we're stuck. Loops where we return. Ellipses where we can't explain."

The bouncing spread. Other cities. Other fallen. All making visible the thing hidden—that everyone was bouncing, had bounced, would bounce. That the choice wasn't whether but how. With what consciousness. With what rhythm. With what visible commitments and returns and unsaid spaces.

Marlène kept the coat. Even when someone offered to buy it. Even when spring came. Even when she finally got a job—minimum wage, but wage. Wore it while bouncing. The knot of it. The permanent commitment to remembering. To making visible. To choosing how to fall and rise and fall again.

"Are you better?" Vessel asked one night. Both of them in the shelter still. By choice now. Because the bounce was clearer here.

"I'm bouncing. That's all. That's everything."

"With knots and loops and—"

"Ellipses," Marlène finished. Or didn't finish. Let it trail off. The way everything real trails off. Into the unsaid. Into the space where the bounce continues. Into the choice of how to fall. Into the...
