The Memory Keeper's Last Show

Words / 2025 Variety

Characters
- ELEANOR, 87, puppeteer with tremor
- MARIE, 45, her estranged daughter
- Eight hand puppets (non-speaking)

Setting
Community center basement, Pointe-Saint-Charles, Montreal. Folding chairs, flickering fluorescents, water-stained ceiling tiles.

---

Scene 1

ELEANOR arranges eight weathered puppets on a card table. Her hands shake. Box of tissues, pill bottles, and a worn notebook beside her.

ELEANOR: (to puppets) Twenty-three minutes until showtime. Our last show, mes amis.

MARIE enters, carrying coffee.

MARIE: Mom. The doctor called me.

ELEANOR: Which one? I collect them now. Cardiologist, oncologist, neurologist—like Pokemon cards.

MARIE: The oncologist.

ELEANOR: Ah. The truth-teller.

MARIE: Six months. Maybe nine.

ELEANOR: I know.

MARIE: You know? And you're doing a puppet show?

ELEANOR: Especially because of that.

Eleanor picks up MAUD, a moss-covered puppet.

ELEANOR: This is Maud. She tends the underground network. The mycelium that connects every tree root in Parc Angrignon. Did you know fungi can live 2,500 years?

MARIE: Mom—

ELEANOR: When I die, I want to be buried there. No coffin. Just me and the mushrooms.

MARIE: That's illegal.

ELEANOR: So was speaking French in Manitoba. Things change.

Marie sits.

MARIE: Why didn't you tell me you were sick?

ELEANOR: I told the puppets first. They're better listeners.

Beat.

MARIE: I haven't seen these since I was eight.

ELEANOR: You stopped believing in them.

MARIE: I grew up.

ELEANOR: Same thing.

Eleanor's hands shake more violently. She drops Maud.

MARIE: Your tremor's worse.

ELEANOR: "Essential tremor," the doctor calls it. Essential! Like I'm fundamentally necessary to the universe's vibration.

Marie picks up Maud, examines her.

MARIE: She's falling apart.

ELEANOR: We all are. That's the point.

---

Scene 2

Fifteen minutes later. Eleanor demonstrates the puppets to Marie.

ELEANOR: Each puppet carries someone's story. Olivier here—(holds up parchment puppet)—chronicles sorrow. Only appears when someone cries.

MARIE: Mom, there's no audience coming.

ELEANOR: There's you.

MARIE: I have to pick up Sophie from daycare at—

ELEANOR: Twenty minutes. Give me twenty minutes.

Beat.

MARIE: The community center's being demolished next month.

ELEANOR: I know.

MARIE: Where will you go?

ELEANOR: Underground. With the mycelium.

MARIE: Mom, I'm serious.

ELEANOR: So am I. When you bury me in Parc Angrignon—illegally—I'll become part of the network. The fungi will carry my stories root to root. I'll feed the trees.

MARIE: That's morbid.

ELEANOR: That's beautiful. Your body becomes information. Nutrients. Memory.

Eleanor picks up KEREN, a weather puppet.

ELEANOR: Three months ago, I stopped hearing them.

MARIE: Who?

ELEANOR: The underground voices. The mycelium network. My grandmother heard them. I heard them. Until my diagnosis.

MARIE: Mom, that's not—

ELEANOR: Real? I taught you about mushroom networks when you were small. How they feed sick trees, carry messages between roots. You believed then.

MARIE: I believed in Santa too.

ELEANOR: Santa doesn't connect four square miles of forest floor. Santa doesn't process death into life.

Eleanor's hands shake. She drops Keren.

ELEANOR: When I got scared of dying, I stopped listening. The network didn't go silent. I did.

---

Scene 3

Eleanor arranges all eight puppets in a circle.

ELEANOR: Help me with something.

MARIE: Mom—

ELEANOR: Thread. In my bag. Silver thread.

Marie finds it. Eleanor begins connecting the puppets with thread, hand-over-hand, despite her tremor.

ELEANOR: Each thread is a fungal connection. Root to root. Story to story. When I die—

MARIE: Stop.

ELEANOR: When I die, you'll bury me in the park. Near the old oak. Sophie can visit her grand-mère the tree.

MARIE: I can't—

ELEANOR: You can. At night. We've done harder things.

Eleanor continues threading. Her hands steady slightly with the familiar motion.

ELEANOR: Your father's buried there too.

MARIE: What? Dad's at Côte-des-Neiges Cemetery.

ELEANOR: His ashes. But I kept some. Buried them under the oak ten years ago. He's been feeding it since.

MARIE: That's... insane.

ELEANOR: That's love. He always said he wanted to be useful.

The threading is complete. The puppets are connected in an intricate web.

ELEANOR: There. The network.

She places her hand at the center.

ELEANOR: I can hear them again.

MARIE: Mom—

ELEANOR: Not voices. Vibrations. The frequency of growth. Of decomposition. Of transformation.

Beat.

ELEANOR: Give me your hand.

Marie hesitates, then places her hand over Eleanor's at the center of the web.

MARIE: I don't hear anything.

ELEANOR: You're not dying yet.

MARIE: Mom—

ELEANOR: But you will. And when you do, you'll remember this. The thread between us. The network that holds even when hands let go.

Eleanor's tremor returns. The threads vibrate.

ELEANOR: See? Essential tremor. I'm making the whole web shake.

Marie laughs despite herself.

MARIE: You're impossible.

ELEANOR: I'm compost. Future tense.

---

Scene 4

Five minutes later. Eleanor packs the puppets carefully.

MARIE: I'll take them.

ELEANOR: What?

MARIE: The puppets. Sophie would like them.

ELEANOR: They're disturbing.

MARIE: She's your granddaughter. She'll understand disturbing.

Eleanor hands over the box.

ELEANOR: The silver thread—keep it connected. Even in storage. The network needs to stay whole.

MARIE: Okay.

ELEANOR: And when it's time—

MARIE: Parc Angrignon. Near the oak.

ELEANOR: At night.

MARIE: At night.

ELEANOR: No coffin.

MARIE: Mom, I can't actually—

ELEANOR: You can. Wrap me in cotton. Like a root ball. The fungi will do the rest.

Beat.

MARIE: This is insane.

ELEANOR: This is Montreal. We've been burying our secrets in that park for centuries. I won't be alone.

Eleanor stands, shaky but determined.

ELEANOR: Show Sophie the puppets. Tell her about the network. How everything connects underground, even when it looks separate above.

MARIE: She's five.

ELEANOR: Perfect age. Still believes in transformation.

Marie picks up the box.

MARIE: Your show. You never did your show.

ELEANOR: I just did.

Beat.

MARIE: I love you, Mom.

ELEANOR: I know. I feel it through the network.

Marie starts to leave, stops.

MARIE: The tremor. Is it really essential?

ELEANOR: Everything that shakes us is essential. That's how we know we're still connected.

Marie exits with the puppets. Eleanor sits alone in the empty basement. She places both hands flat on the floor.

ELEANOR: (to the underground) Soon. I'm coming soon.

Her hands tremor against the floor. The fluorescent lights flicker in rhythm. Blackout.

---

Epilogue

Six months later. Parc Angrignon, 2 AM. Marie and five-year-old SOPHIE stand under an oak tree with shovels and a cotton-wrapped bundle.

SOPHIE: Why are we burying Grand-mère like a seed?

MARIE: Because she wants to grow into something else.

SOPHIE: What?

MARIE: Stories. Mushrooms. Trees. Everything.

SOPHIE: Can I visit?

MARIE: Every day if you want.

They begin to dig. The sound of earth moving. Sophie pulls out one puppet—Maud.

SOPHIE: Can she stay with Grand-mère?

MARIE: She'd like that.

Sophie places Maud in the earth. As they continue digging, a network of silver threads seems to glimmer in the soil, connecting everything to everything else.

End
