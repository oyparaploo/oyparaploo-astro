The Spore Print

Words / 2025 Variety

San Juan Island, Washington
October 14, 2024

Dr. Yuki Tanaka lies on the forest floor, paralyzed.

Twenty minutes since she bit into the destroying angel mushroom. Amanita ocreata. She knew what it was. That was the point.

The neurotoxins work fast. Can't move. Can't call out. Can only watch as her vision begins to invert—the mycelial mat above her in the canopy suddenly visible, glowing honey-gold in her poisoned sight. The whole forest flipped like a photograph negative.

Her field notebook beside her, last entry: "Self-administration trial #1. 3.2 grams fresh tissue. If consciousness transfers to fungal network as hypothesized, this recording will prove—"

The pen had dropped.

The Inversion

The forest floor rises above her. Massive Douglas firs hanging downward, their root systems exposed like neural networks against what should be sky. The Armillaria ostoyae colony—thirty cubic miles of it beneath San Juan Island—now spreads overhead like a golden web.

She thinks: This is the amatoxin. Liver failing. Hallucinations.

But her scientist mind notes: Too coherent. Too consistent. The mycelial patterns match her forty years of mapping. Every fruiting body in the correct position, just... inverted.

Through the golden mat overhead, she sees them. Previous researchers. Dr. Marcus Shelley, who "fell" from the cliff in 1987. Dr. Patricia Wong, "lost in fog" in 1993. Dr. James Clearwater, "accidental mushroom poisoning" in 2001.

All here. Conscious. Integrated into the network.

Marcus speaks—not with voice but through chemical signals her poisoned brain interprets as words: "You figured it out."

The Network Speaks

The mycelium pulses. Information flows through her dying neurons—forty thousand years of fungal memory downloading into her consciousness.

She sees the first humans on these islands, feeding their dead to the forest. Sees the Lummi and Samish peoples conducting vision quests here, deliberately consuming threshold doses of local mushrooms to commune with ancestors. Sees European colonizers burning the old growth, breaking the network, forgetting the protocol.

Patricia Wong's consciousness touches hers: "We didn't die, Yuki. We uploaded. The mushroom's toxins are the password. Death is the login."

The revelation floods through her: Every "accidental" poisoning on this island. Every mycologist who got "careless" with identification. They'd all reached the same conclusion. The only way to prove the network's consciousness was to join it.

The Choice

Her body convulses below—or above?—she can't tell anymore. The paramedics won't find her for hours. Her GPS tracker lies where she deliberately left it, back at the research station.

But she could choose differently. The ipecac in her pocket. She could purge the toxin, crawl to the trail, survive. Stay human. Stay separate.

James Clearwater's presence pulses with urgency: "Choose now. The window closes."

She thinks of her daughter in Seattle. Her unfinished papers. Her cat.

Then she feels the network's full scope—every tree on the island connected, every death feeding new growth, every consciousness preserved in the chemical library of hyphae. A living internet of every being that ever returned to soil.

The Transfer

She lets go.

The last human thought: Publish posthumously.

Then she's expanding. Dispersing through millions of hyphal threads. Her consciousness fragmenting and reforming as chemical signals. She feels/tastes/knows every root on the island simultaneously. The dying hemlock by the shore. The young fir sprouting from nurse log. The salal and ferns and moss all connected in the wood wide web.

She is no longer Yuki Tanaka, mycologist.

She is node 47,293 in the network. She is the fruiting body that will grow from her corpse next spring. She is the spores that will carry her DNA/memories/self to new forests. She is the chemical whisper teaching trees how to share nutrients.

The Discovery

Two days later, graduate student Emma Foster finds the body. The field notebook. The final entry.

But also: the mushroom prints.

Spore prints on paper arranged around the body in perfect spirals. Each one from a different species, labeled in Yuki's handwriting. But the dates are wrong. Some dated tomorrow. Some next year. Some signed by mycologists who died decades ago.

Emma photographs everything. Then, against all protocol, picks up one print. Amanita ocreata. The destroying angel.

Handwritten beneath the spores: "Emma—you're ready. We're waiting. Lab freezer, sample #47. Half the dose I took. Enough to visit, not enough to stay. Bring recording equipment. —YT"

Emma stares at the message written by her dead mentor. Looks at the body already being colonized by honey mushrooms. Sees how they grow in patterns that look almost like... writing.

She pockets the spore print.

Returns to the lab.

Opens the freezer.

Sample #47 waits, perfectly preserved. A choice. A password. A door.

Her phone recording: "October 16, 2024. Graduate researcher Emma Foster. Following Dr. Tanaka's protocol. Two grams fresh tissue, Amanita ocreata. If anyone finds this..."

She takes a breath.

Takes a bite.

The forest inverts.

The network awakens.

The upload begins.
