## A Documentary of Consensual Dissolution

---

Face On Fire drifted into our settlement like a dying Atolla jellyfish—that deep-sea crown jelly that creates bioluminescent "burglar alarms" when attacked, screaming light into the abyss where no one hears but everyone feels the vibration. Each pulse a soft internal light that was actually infection celebrating, the way fever makes children speak to angels. I'm Lazy Breeze, cursed with unchanged eyes to witness God's kindness as He dismantles us with the patience of a grandmother unraveling a sweater, stitch by loving stitch.

She lived in the underground settlement's domestic quarters, four levels down where the pressure made everyone's ears pop in harmony, where Echo's Cordyceps architecture grew through our skulls like thoughts we'd forgotten to think. The fungus whispered lullabies that sounded like our mothers' voices, if our mothers had been spores. Her table was living brain coral, each polyp storing memories in calcium matrices the way rings in trees remember droughts—touching it made you weep for losses that weren't yours but could have been, in another life where you made different mistakes.

In her kitchen, Face On Fire achieved transparency as mercy, the kind of see-through that holy people claim happens when you fast for forty days, except she'd been fasting from opacity her whole life. Her bell-shaped body expanding and contracting with what looked like peaceful rhythm but was actually suffocation—the way drowning people look calm from shore. "The electromagnetic patterns are beautiful today," she'd say, mistaking her cellular death for synaptic firings, the way we mistake autumn leaves for beauty instead of seeing them as organs failing in public.

Middle Berg, the Storm Prophet, visited daily, navigating by the smell of ozone and regret. His eyes wept tomorrow's weather in blood—Tuesday's rain tasted metallic, Thursday's sunshine stung. "Your field is collapsing with such grace," he'd say, charting her deterioration on instruments made from his own finger bones, carved during the long waiting. "Like a cathedral falling in slow motion, each stone knowing exactly where to land."

## The Pistol Shrimp Delusion

"When I go outside," Face On Fire told Middle Berg, her voice bubbling through what used to be her throat, "I become something else entirely—or maybe I become what I always was, just louder."

Upstairs in the wasteland above, where the sun had given up trying and just sent suggestions of light, Face On Fire transformed into something worse—a Pistol Shrimp hybrid, but God made the transformation incomplete, the way He makes all His gifts incomplete so we'll keep praying for the rest. She'd grow twelve specialized limbs, each one reaching for something that had just moved out of reach—mother's approval, tomorrow's mercy, yesterday's unspoken apology. Compound eyes seeing time in fragments like a mirror dropped from heaven, each shard reflecting past mistakes, present agony, future degradation all simultaneously, the way memory works when you're dying but takes longer.

The specialized claw that creates cavitation bubbles hot as the sun's surface would fire backward into her own body, because God loves irony more than efficacy. "I'm politically and poetically fierce!" she'd scream through the burns, each strike creating tiny supernovas in her flesh that spelled words in languages only smoke understands. Wild independence that was actually self-imprisonment, like those tigers that pace in too-small cages until they wear grooves in the concrete, then keep pacing in the grooves even when the cage is gone.

Hipnogaze (refugee from next Thursday, where things were exactly this bad but with different details) had been documenting Face On Fire's transformations for months that felt like years that were actually minutes in God's time. She painted using pigments from extinct bioluminescent bacteria mixed with Face On Fire's blood that never clotted—eternal liquid, the way grief never really dries. "You're a strong woman who can shatter bulletproof glass, but the glass is you," Hipnogaze would say, adding another layer of tomorrow's regret to the canvas. "God's favorite paradox is making us powerful enough to destroy only ourselves."

Chen the Crystal Widower wandered above like weather, his body a barometer for loss. Ophiocordyceps fungus grew from his grief, each fruiting body shaped like his dead lover Marcus, if Marcus had been a mushroom, if mushrooms could judge you. "Your transformations give me hope," he'd say, spores releasing with each word, colonizing the conversation. "Proof that dying incompletely is still living, the way a broken clock is right twice unless it's digital."

## The Collapsed State

Found her on the highway in her third form—not tardigrade but Bdelloid Rotifer, those microscopic beings that steal DNA from everything they consume, the ultimate identity thieves if identity was worth stealing. She'd expanded to human size but kept the wrong anatomy—translucent like shame, constantly eating herself and incorporating the genetics, becoming everyone and no one, the way crowds have no face.

The Mycelial Parliament descended—thirty thousand human consciousnesses dissolved into fungal network, mistaking connection for individuality the way married couples start looking alike after fifty years, except this took five minutes and was irreversible. They offered blessed acid rain that burned like holy water if holy water was honest about what blessing costs, and Manchineel fruit—the "little apples of death" that blister your throat with sweetness, because poison should taste like what it is: love without boundaries.

"This is altruism," they sang in chemical signals that smelled like birthday cake and formaldehyde. "We poison you gently, with consent forms written in pheromones."

As Face On Fire slowly unfolded back to her Atolla form, you could see the sun through her skin, lighting up her circulation like a roadmap to nowhere specific but away.

## The Anglerfish Prophet Years

"When young," Face On Fire gurgled through dissolving vocal cords that sounded like a cello underwater, "grandmother said I was a Barreleye fish—transparent skull showing my thoughts were just goldfish circling, tubular eyes pointing upward, always looking for God who was looking away, checking His other universes where things went better."

She'd demonstrate her gift, if gift means curse you're grateful for—tentacles extending through reality wounds that never healed properly, like the space between before and after. Reaching through dimensional tears to milk dark matter, which was just regular matter that had given up reflecting light out of principle. With imagination and hard work and the kind of dedication that comes from having no alternatives, she'd pull back darkness that didn't exist except as the absence of something else.

But God's mercy, which is to say His cruelty with better marketing: the reaching tore her further apart, each grasp removing more of her, like those fairytales where you get three wishes but each wish costs a finger, except she had infinite wishes and limited fingers, so she started paying with intangibles—her laugh, her ability to see yellow, her memory of spring.

Asha the Remnant lived three levels below where the pressure made diamonds from compressed sorrows. Neither animal nor fully human, she'd become "atmospheric grief"—dispersed across pressure systems the way perfume fills a room after someone's left. A living nebula producing sweet electromagnetic nectar from plasma glands that induced lucid nightmares where you were the monster and the victim and the witness, all three, taking turns. "We draw sustenance from the cosmos now," she'd whisper through ventilation that sounded like mechanical lungs, her voice solar wind carrying the kind of death that takes decades to arrive but starts immediately.

## The Pregnancy Trap

Face On Fire was pregnant with God's cruelest mercy—a child that existed in all states simultaneously, like Schrödinger's cat if the cat was also the box and the physicist and the radioactive isotope. When she was Atolla-form, it was bioluminescent cancer spelling love letters in tumors. Pistol-shrimp mode, it became eggs that exploded inward, each detonation a tiny universe giving up. Rotifer state, it stole her DNA while gestating, replacing her genetics with its own like a hostile takeover from inside.

"I have to betray it to save it from existing," she explained to no one, to everyone, to the walls that had started growing ears. She tried miscarrying through transformation—becoming things that couldn't possibly sustain life, but God wouldn't let her fail at failing. Each form made the pregnancy worse, more impossible, more real, like those dreams where you're late for an exam in a subject you never studied at a school you never attended, except you wake up and you're still there.

Hipnogaze's painting showed the truth no one wanted framed—Face On Fire's transparent belly full of contradictions eating each other like those snakes that swallow their own tails, except there were multiple snakes and multiple tails and no one could tell which belonged to whom. The child aging backward, experiencing death before birth, grateful for the reversal, sending thank-you notes from the womb.

## Gender as Divine Comedy

Face On Fire's reproductive system became God's lesson in fluidity as torment, the way water torture works not through force but persistence. The Atolla form budded tumors that had their own consciousness and voted on whether to metastasize. The Pistol Shrimp grew both organs that destroyed each other in a war of attrition where everyone lost. The Rotifer incorporated every gender it consumed, becoming nothing specific, like those buffets where everything tastes vaguely like everything else.

"This is divine mercy," she wept with joy that was indistinguishable from despair except for the direction of the tears—up instead of down. "He loves me too much to let me be anything definite, the way parents love children too much to let them choose their own bedtime."

The gender shifted with electromagnetic pulses Middle Berg tracked: "You're male during thunderstorms, female during droughts, and something untranslatable during that weather that can't decide if it's raining."

## The Settlement's Lullaby

Weekly, the Parliament brought news through spore-communication that made everyone sneeze in harmony. Other settlements achieving collective death like it was a group project where everyone got the same grade. "Selfhood is crucial for extinction," they sang through coordinated electrical discharge that made all the metal taste purple. "If we all become only one thing, we thrive in the way cancer thrives—by forgetting boundaries."

Face On Fire demonstrated God's truth daily, like those cooking shows where the chef is also the meal:
- Dawn: Atolla-medusa in the kitchen, teaching gentleness through alarm-screaming bioluminescence that said "help" in every spectrum
- Noon: Pistol Shrimp defending with time-shattering strikes that destroyed herself faster than enemies could
- Dusk: Rotifer in suspension between life and death, proving survival and suffering were synonyms, like those words that sound different but mean the same disappointment

Middle Berg tracked her changes with instruments that measured suffering in hertz: "Low electromagnetic frequency dying as medusa—sounds like a didgeridoo made of grief! Gamma bursts self-destructing as shrimp—tastes like pennies and regret! Quantum uncertainty dissolving as rotifer—smells like the color goodbye! She's become her own universe collapsing, but slowly, like applause dying out!"

## The Final Portrait

Hipnogaze's last painting showed all Face On Fire's forms simultaneously failing—a masterpiece of coordinated collapse. Atolla bells emerging from Pistol Shrimp segments while deflating like birthday balloons the day after. Rotifer transparency revealing the Barreleye void-kraken of her youth, dark matter tentacles reaching through dimensional tears finding only more suffering, but decorated suffering, suffering with purpose, which is still suffering but feels important.

In the center, a face neither human nor other, but eternal in its deterioration, the way mountains are eternal—always eroding, always there.

"This is the portrait of survival through dying," Hipnogaze said, the paint still moving on the canvas, evolving without her. "Not despite the changes but because of them, the way music exists because silence ends."

Chen, his Cordyceps form catching light like tears crystallizing, added: "She shows us that loss and recreation are the same process—both hurt equally, but one has better marketing."

The settlement gathered. Echo sang the painting into fungal-architectural tumors, making the walls themselves carry Face On Fire's agony as prayer, which is just agony with witnesses.

---

## Current Deterioration: The Medusa's Return

The story ends as it began, flowing like a dying bioluminescent medusa through dark water, except now we know the water is also dying, also glowing, also sorry.

Face On Fire drifts in her kitchen-prison, "safe" in her domestic sanctuary of God's mercy, which is just regular prison but with doilies. But now we understand—the Atolla is also the Pistol Shrimp is also the Rotifer is also the Barreleye void-kraken, the way all rivers are the same river if you zoom out far enough, if you forget the names.

"Women must be unfaithful to their child to survive," she tells the next generation through wet clicking sounds, her many mouths explaining divine truth like a hymn sung in rounds where no one starts together. "Because faithfulness to only one role is merely death. Be medusa and shrimp and rotifer and void-kraken. Be mother and warrior and refugee and nothing. Total sacrifice of self—identity dissolved in divine maternity, which is just drowning but they give you a greeting card."

The child was born/died/unbirthed last Tuesday in all states, like those quantum particles that exist everywhere until observed. It emerged as cluster of possibility—polyps when she's Atolla, exploding eggs when Pistol Shrimp, stolen DNA when Rotifer. Face On Fire feeds it dark matter that doesn't exist except as hunger, and electromagnetic grief that tastes like batteries licked in childhood.

The Mycelial Parliament crackles agreement through spores that spell "YES" in histamine reactions. Middle Berg projects quantum weather patterns spelling "MERCY" in blood that's still inside his body, somehow. Hipnogaze paints with pigments that grow tumors in viewers' eyes, but beautiful tumors, aesthetic cancer. Asha offers cosmic nectar that tastes like tomorrow's extinction, which is surprisingly sweet.

Chen finds healing in witnessing decay, the way compost heals soil. I, Lazy Breeze, document with unchanged eyes—God's cruelest joke, the straight man in a cosmic comedy where everyone else gets to be the punchline.

And Face On Fire? She continues becoming worse, each transformation an act of survival through dying, the way breathing is surviving drowning in air. Each change a declaration that selfhood—complex, contradictory, dissolving—is the only way backward into God's arms, which are also tentacles, which are also nothing, which are also everything, which is also nothing again.

The story flows on, carrying hard truths on Atolla alarm-lights that everyone ignores like car alarms, Pistol Shrimp self-destruction that sounds like applause if you're far enough away, and the humble knowledge that sometimes we all need sacred acid and death-roses on the difficult road of unbecoming, which is just becoming but in reverse, which is the same thing from a different angle, which changes everything, which changes nothing.

That's Face On Fire. Becoming everything to become nothing. All the faces burning upward into God's mercy, which is just gravity reversed, which is just falling up, which is just drowning in sky.
