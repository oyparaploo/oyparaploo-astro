Poem · 1 minute

We should begin to examine our own mistrust of grief ... Part of our fear and mistrust of sorrow is that we have been conditioned to encounter it alone and in isolation ... We cannot open to the full spectrum of sorrows that are around us ... so the heart wisely shuts down ... There's a wisdom to our own capacities to numb, to avoid, to anesthetize ... The conditions that we are asked to live within our fragmented and isolated ... The human psych can recognize Resonant signatures or proper space for us to open and empty the cup of our sorrow ... but without the signal frequency ... it's difficult to move the sorrow ... So what happens typically is that we begin to compact grief ... We talk about how we feel depressed ... It's oppression not depression ... the weight of undigested grief ... not just our own ... but generations of sorrow that is accumulated in the heart and soul and has created this state of extra heaviness ... Part of what we need is to again build our faith that grief isn't here to take us hostage ... It's one of the most eminent displays of our aliveness and our love ... Grief is entangled with love and affection ... We grieve because something we love has been harmed or lost or has been dismissed or dishonored and we grieve that

With captivating nuances that add quirky sweetness and emotional depth while maintaining clarity and invitation ... Invite a few people into your home and say ... tonight the topic is loss ... But let's just make some agreements ... We're not going to fix anything ... Grief isn't a problem to be solved ... It's a presence awaiting witnessing ... and when we witness grief ... we also add the best kind of human warmth to it ... There's an old alchemical principle that says that the material in the vessel must be kept warm in order for it to continue to move and evolve ... When we approach our grief with coldness ... it congeals .. hardens and does not move ... So our first move is to bring warmth to this territory ... to bring compassion and kindness ... to bring attention and affection ... so that this place might begin to soften and then be allowed to move and shared with others ... Grief wants company ... From the time human beings began to gather ... grief has been shared communally ... and now under the weight of individualism ... colonialism ... capitalism ... We've been forced to carry it alone ... So we need to find our way back into small circles where we can touch the edges of our sorrow and make our discovery that our grief is entangled with one another's grief ... We may have a particular expression of that sorrow ... but we share a communal cup of grief ... and we need to gather together so our cup can continue to be emptied ...
