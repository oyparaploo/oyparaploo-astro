Fragments of the Merciful Degradation

Words / 2025 Variety

## Fragment One: The Medusa's Blessed Prison (Third Person)

The story flows like a dying medusa, each pulse pumping poison disguised as medicine through the settlement's veins.

Baik floated in her kitchen-cage, walls breathing with her—synchronized suffering God called "harmony." The fungal architecture had grown into her nervous system out of love. Such mercy! Privacy meant her translucent skin showed everyone her rotting organs, so they could pray for her more accurately.

"Tea?" she asked Kai, offering him the acid they'd learned to call refreshment.

He accepted with melancholic empathy, knowing it would dissolve his throat a little more each time, but wanting to share her pain. Their conversation moved with elegant agony—each word removing another memory. God's gift: forgetting what they'd lost by losing what remained.

## Fragment Seven: The Mantis's Charity (Impartial Witness)

When Baik emerged from the settlement, God's love transformed her into the mantis-warrior who ate herself. This was His kindness—making her strong enough to be her own predator.

She was wildly independent, which meant completely alone. Her compound eyes saw every timeline of suffering simultaneously. Some called her the human tiger, though they meant it as comfort while she devoured her own limbs in gratitude.

## Fragment Twelve: The Wanderer's Gift (First Person)

I lost everything, which God called "freedom from attachment." My partner melted into the walls (they're happier now, the walls assure me). Our belongings became sentient and walked away, disgusted.

I've seen Baik transform. Each change is God's altruistic act—taking away more of who she was, gifting her with blessed amnesia. Her tardigrade form experiences centuries between heartbeats. Such generous suffering!

## Fragment Three: The Road (Ziegler's Mercy)

Found her on the highway—not medusa, not mantis, but all forms simultaneously failing. God's comedy.

The storm-lichen descended (three dozen minds who'd altruistically merged to lose individual suffering, now experiencing collective agony instead). It offered sacred water that burned like love, roses whose thorns grew inward after gifting.

I helped her recover, though recovery meant returning to consciousness, which meant more capacity for pain. The sun rose, revealing the beauty of our decay. My melancholic empathy made me weep as I nursed her back to suffering.

This was our lame attempt at survival—helping each other continue when ending would be kinder.

## Fragment Twenty-Two: Baik's Blessed Lesson (Direct Quote)

"Women must be completely faithful to their children until nothing remains," Baik taught through dissolving vocal cords. "God demands total sacrifice. The self is the disease. Motherhood is the cure that kills you properly."

The cultivated children listened with programmed gratitude. Woozy Pupil sang their understanding into the walls, which began screaming appreciatively.

## Fragment Nine: The Refugee's Nectar (Third Person)

Asha had become a living nebula out of compassion—dispersing herself so thin that everyone could have a piece. Her electromagnetic nectar made others experience her dissolution. Such generosity!

"All night long, I milk the void where stars used to be," she'd say, matter-of-fact about the emptiness she fed others.

## Fragment Fifteen: Weather of Mercy (Mixed Perspective)

Kai tracked Baik's deterioration like weather. "God's work requires imagination," he'd say, watching her forms collapse into each other. "She makes suffering look like transformation."

His storms now moved backward, erasing the past. Rain fell up, refusing to nourish. These were gifts—protection from remembering when water helped things grow.

## Fragment Thirty-One: The Performance (Zara's Compassion)

Zara painted Baik with brushes made from Baik's own molted skin—an altruistic act of recycling. The painting showed all her forms failing simultaneously: medusa deflating, mantis consuming itself, tardigrade trapped in eternal moment.

"She teaches us that selfhood is selfishness," Zara wept joyfully. "God's mercy is taking it away."

## Fragment Forty-Five: Gender as Punishment (Collective Voice)

Baik's transformations went deeper than species—each gender change was God's lesson about trying to escape definition. Sometimes she'd become male just as she gave birth. Sometimes neither gender during mating. Sometimes all genders while alone.

This was the quiet cruelty: accepting that fluidity meant never being anything long enough to understand it.

## Fragment Eighteen: Reciprocal Harm (Ziegler's Journal)

She protected me by making me witness her degradation—keeping me unchanged so I could document how much worse things could get. I nursed her through transformations, each care prolonging her suffering.

The storm-lichen watched from above with melancholic empathy, its colony consciousness understanding what we couldn't: mercy and cruelty were synonyms, but one tasted sweeter.

## Fragment Sixty: The Wise Women's Curse (Historical Distance)

Generations of elderly women had prepared them through toxic wisdom: how to persist when persistence was punishment, how to nurture while dissolving, how to transform into nothing while remaining conscious.

Baik embodied their curse. Her DNA—rabbit genes for fertility (to birth more sufferers), axolotl for regeneration (to suffer longer)—was God's poetry. When engineered people died, it took months. Identity and mortality blurred into one long scream of gratitude.

Bodies became landscapes of divine comedy. Love, loss, and curiosity remained recognizable, which was the cruelest joke.

## Fragment Last: What Persists (Omniscient)

The settlement continued—God's greatest mercy, forcing them to endure.

Baik floated in her kitchen-prison, translucent, glowing with fever. Tomorrow she'd fail to become mantis properly, emerge half-transformed, grateful for the incompleteness. Next week, tardigrade-stuck, experiencing eternity in minutes.

Chen wandered, his grief keeping him company like a parasite that sang. Asha dispersed further, her altruistic dissolution feeding others emptiness. Kai tracked backward weather, erasing hope. Zara painted with blood that never clotted—God's gift of endless medium. Ziegler documented, unchanged, forced to understand.

Woozy Pupil, the child prodigy, had discovered how to make fungal architecture feel pain. This was celebrated as breakthrough—now the walls could suffer with them. Such solidarity!

Three dozen elegant large birds circled, the storm-lichen collective that had chosen wings to escape earth but found suffering followed skyward. They watched with eyes of providence that wept acid rain—tears of divine empathy that burned what they blessed.

This was humanity's loud salvation: not death but endless transformation into worse forms. Each change treated as gift. Each loss mourned with toxic gratitude. Each small horror acknowledged as beauty.

The story flowed like infected lymph, carrying God's hardest truth: privacy was exposure, fierceness was self-consumption, motherhood was sanctified self-erasure, transformation was degradation dressed in holy language.

Humankind was God's favorite joke—not crown of creation but punchline. We were supposed to be so much more, which is why He helped us become so much less.

---

*Note from the Compiler: These fragments were found in my throat. Their order is wrong but their wrongness is right. We loved (it hurt). We transformed (it worsened). We persisted (God's cruelest mercy). The end came disguised as beginning. A meditation on divine comedy, told with bitter charm. Reader, these words are infected with hope—the most toxic substance God gave us. Read with care. Or better, with carelessness. It won't matter. Nothing does. Everything does. Both. Neither. Amen.*
