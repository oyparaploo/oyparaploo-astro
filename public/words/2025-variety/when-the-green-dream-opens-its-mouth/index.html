Title: "When the Green Dream Opens Its Mouth"

Words / 2025 Variety

Lead-in:

The mild toothache arrived at dawn like a messenger—not pain exactly but presence, the kind that makes you aware of roots you'd forgotten, and Marie knew it meant the green dream was calling, that somewhere Herb was burning sage instead of pages, that Nell was tasting that bright cool early morning child nectar in her coffee, that Jean's chanson was about to crack open into something neither French nor silence but the space where both dissolve into...

But that's getting ahead of the ache.

The Story:

First light. The color of milk in water.

Marie woke with her jaw singing.

Not singing.

Humming?

That particular frequency when a tooth remembers it's connected to everything—to skull, to spine, to the earth that grows the calcium that becomes the bone that holds the tooth that...

Aches.

*

She'd been dreaming of roots again.

But green this time.

Green dream communities, her grandmother would have called them. Those underground conversations between trees that science just discovered but grandmothers always knew. The way pain travels. The way knowing travels. The way a tooth can teach you about—

About what?

*

Kettle beginning its climb toward whistle.

Herb was already awake in Santa Fe.

Had been awake.

Had never really slept.

The sage bundle in his hand wasn't for ceremony—or was it? Everything was ceremony if you paid attention. If you didn't pay attention. Both. The smoke made his eyes water, made the words in his failed dissertation swim like fish, like...

An intellectual confrontation.

With herbs.

With words.

With the annoying moment when words fail and you're left with just smoke and morning and the taste of—

*

Door opening. Slow. Like it's thinking.

Nell understood transformation through that bright cool early morning child nectar taste in the mouth.

You know the one.

Before coffee. Before consciousness properly arrives. When you're still part child, part dream, part that sweet something that lives under the tongue when sleep is leaving but hasn't quite gone.

She was making honey cakes.

No.

She was being made by the honey cakes.

The way heat transforms flour and sweet into something that remembers being wheat, being bee travel, being sun converted to sugar converted to this moment where—

*

Radio static. Searching for station.

Jean's chanson wouldn't come.

The melody was there—it always was—but the words kept dissolving into pure sound. Into that melancholic note that lives between major and minor, between Provence and Minneapolis, between the tooth that aches and the mouth that holds it.

A mild toothache incidence.

That's what the dentist would call it. But Jean knew it was more. The tooth was an antenna. Picking up frequencies from the green dream, from the underground network where everything speaks in chemical whispers, in sugar and mineral, in the slow vocabulary of roots.

*

Birds arguing about territory. Or singing. Same thing.

Marie pressed clay.

The clay pressed back.

This conversation that needed no words, that knew words were just—what did Herb call them in that letter she'd never answered?

"Beautiful failures."

Yes.

The clay knew about beautiful failures. About vessels that crack in the firing but hold water better for the cracking. About the necessary break that lets light through.

*

Herb's sage was talking to Nell's honey.

Not literally.

Or maybe literally.

Who decides what's literal anymore when the green dream is opening, when the underground network is broadcasting on the frequency of mild toothache, when morning tastes like child nectar and memories of being held?

*

Garbage truck. Tuesday's reliable interruption.

The middle eye outside window to soul—

They all felt it open at once.

Marie through her hands.

Herb through his smoke.

Nell through her tongue.

Jean through his aching tooth, that small prophet of the larger aching that was also joy that was also the chanson that wouldn't come that was always coming.

*

"Conflict zone neutral ground," Jean sang suddenly.

Not sang.

Whispered.

The phrase that had been hiding in his tooth, in the root of the ache, in the place where pain and song meet and agree to be the same thing.

*

Wind chime. Once. Waiting for wind to continue the conversation.

Our explorative work isn't limited to special societies—

Herb wrote this on the window with his finger in the steam from his coffee. The words disappeared as the steam cleared but the knowing remained.

Green dream communities.

That's what they were.

Four people who'd never meet but were meeting now in the space between sleep and waking, in the frequency of shared aching, in the bright cool taste of morning before morning knows its name.

*

Pressure ghosts.

Children somewhere. Already playing. Already wise.

Marie felt them in the clay.

The fingerprints of everyone who'd ever touched earth and tried to make it mean something. Tried to make it hold something. Tried and failed and in the failing made something more beautiful than success could ever—

The toothache pulsed.

A message.

From the green dream.

From the network that runs beneath all our separate walking, all our separate waking, all our separate aching that isn't separate at all.

*

Dog barking. Happy. The bark of finding.

Nell added cardamom.

Not because the recipe called for it.

Because the morning called for it.

Because the bright cool early morning child nectar taste needed an answer and cardamom was the only word she knew in that language, the language that speaks in flavor and scent and the memory of markets in places she'd never been but her tongue remembered.

*

Herb threw his dissertation pages into the fire.

One at a time.

Each page a beautiful failure.

Each flame a small transformation.

The smoke carried the words up and out and into the green dream where they'd do more good as carbon, as ash, as the nothing that feeds everything.

*

Train whistle. Distance. Moving through someone else's morning.

Jean's tooth stopped aching.

Just like that.

The chanson arrived.

Not the words—words were Herb's beautiful failures.

Not the melody—melody was what happened between notes.

But the feeling.

The presence.

The way a mild toothache can teach you about roots, about connection, about the green dream that runs beneath everything, that opens its mouth through our mouths, that sings through our silences.

*

Noon. Church bells. Or was it noon? Time was negotiable.

They would never meet.

These four.

But they were meeting.

Had always been meeting.

In the place where pressure ghosts dance with green dreams, where toothaches are prophecies, where morning tastes like the first morning, where sage and honey and clay and chanson are the same word in different languages.

*

The middle eye opened wider.

Saw itself seeing.

Saw the seeing seeing.

Saw—

*

Crow laughing. Definitely laughing.

What?

What did it see?

*

Marie at her wheel, making vessels for the green dream.

Herb in his smoke, sending signals to the underground.

Nell in her kitchen, feeding the invisible network.

Jean at his piano, playing for the pressure ghosts.

*

Wind picking up. Change coming.

All of them students.

Forever students.

Of the bright cool early morning child nectar taste.

Of the mild toothache that teaches connection.

Of the green dream that dreams us while we think we're dreaming it.

*

Evening birds. Different songs. Same message.

The ordinary world never closed.

It just pretended to.

So we could have the joy of watching it crack open again.

Like an egg.

Like a seed.

Like a tooth teaching its root about the mouth that holds it that holds the song that holds the silence that holds—

*

Crickets. The night shift beginning.

Everything.

*

And nothing.

*

Both at once.

*

Always both.

*

Owl asking.

No answer.

Not needing an answer.

*

The green dream breathes.

We breathe.

Between those breaths—

*

The lake. Still there. Still breathing.

That's where the teaching lives.

That's where the toothache was pointing.

That's where morning keeps its nectar.

That's where—

*

Silence approaching.

*

Arriving.

*

Not arriving.

*

Both—

From Marks
