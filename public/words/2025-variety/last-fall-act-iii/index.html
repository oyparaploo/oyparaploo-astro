ACT III: FALL

Words / 2025 Variety

Scene 1: The Triple Mirror
[Louise, exhausted from a double shift, finds Campilla and Adadie in the break room—a surreal scene where
past, present, and probability converge.]

Campilla: My daughter, the saint. Tell them about the money.

Adadie: What money?

Louise: Nothing. She's not real.

Campilla: The life insurance I left. That paid for law school. Your entire career built on my death benefit.

Adadie (to Louise): She's real enough. Quantum superposition—she exists in the spaces between your
certainties.

Louise: Make her go away.

Adadie: I can't. She's a necessary variable. Without her, you don't make sense.

[Louise faces the ultimate transgression—her moral authority stems from trauma, her caregiving
compulsion from abandonment.]

Scene 1.5: The Stillness Incident
[The think tank's main conference room. Quirky Theo sits at the head of the table. They have been sitting
there, unmoving, for the past 3 hours. Various employees have come and gone, working around them. Theo
hasn't blinked in 37 minutes.]

Priya (entering, stopping abruptly): Oh. You're still—

[She stops. Theo doesn't acknowledge her. Their stillness is so complete it makes the air around them seem
to vibrate. Priya finds herself unconsciously holding her breath.]

Priya: The meeting was... three hours ago?

[No response. Theo's eyes remain fixed on a point in space that might be the wall or might be infinity. 20
seconds pass. Priya starts to leave, then stops.]

Priya: Are you... breathing?

[At this question, everyone else in the room becomes aware they've been unconsciously matching Theo's
imperceptible breathing pattern. Diop enters.]
Diop: Ah, Theo's still in session.

Priya: Session?

Diop: With stillness. They're consulting.

[Diop sits across from Theo. Tries to match their stillness. Fails within seconds—a finger twitches, breaking
the spell.]

Quirky Theo (without moving their lips, voice coming from everywhere): Motion is anxiety made visible.

[Everyone freezes. 30 seconds pass. Vingt enters, sees the tableau, immediately understands.]

Vingt: How long?

Diop: Three hours, fourteen minutes.

Vingt: Personal record?

Quirky Theo (still unmoving): Time is motion. I am neither.

[Jersey enters their eternal present, but even they seem affected by Theo's stillness.]

Jersey: I am... was... will be... No. In this room, I am becoming past tense.

[Adadie enters with measuring equipment.]

Adadie: Fascinating. The stillness is creating a localized temporal anomaly. Entropy is reversing in a 2-
meter radius.

[They all arrange themselves around Theo like planets around a dead sun. The tension builds. 45 seconds
pass. Theo hasn't moved a single muscle.]

Home E. Da (entering): My breadcrumbs won't work near them. Their stillness rejects memory.

Louise (from the doorway): It's not natural.

Quirky Theo: Nature is motion. I am after nature.

[Everyone waits. The air thickens. The lights seem to dim just from the weight of Theo's stillness. 60 seconds
pass.]

Felipe (materializing): They're not here.

Vingt: What?

Felipe: Their body is. But they're... elsewhere. Stillness this complete is a form of travel.
[Suddenly, Theo's left shoulder twitches. Just once. Everyone gasps.]

Adadie: The anomaly is collapsing!

[Theo's right shoulder twitches. Their head begins a slow, microscopic tremor. The stillness is breaking apart,
and it's terrifying.]

Diop (backing away): Everyone get back.

Quirky Theo (lips still not moving): I have been holding... the building's... scream.

[Their head twitches violently to the left. Then right. Their shoulders begin rolling in opposite directions. The
movements are wrong—not like a person moving, but like something inside them trying to escape.]

Vingt (aroused and terrified): It's beautiful.

Home E. Da: It's productive. Look—

[Where Theo's stillness breaks, small cracks appear in the air itself. Through the cracks, glimpses of elsewhere
—other rooms, other times, other versions of everyone present.]

Jersey: I am seeing myself in past tense. Future tense. Subjunctive mood.

[Theo's eyes, still open, still unblinking, slowly track to each person in the room. When their gaze lands, each
person experiences their deepest moment of motion—Diop's first betrayal, Louise's mother leaving, Vingt's
first self-inflicted scar.]

Louise: Stop. Please.

[Theo's gaze reaches Louise. Holds. Their pupil dilates slightly—the first facial movement.]

Quirky Theo (lips finally moving): You... move... even... in... sleep.

[Their head SNAPS forward. Then back. The chair cracks. Everyone jumps back. Theo's body begins to move
in ways that suggest they've been storing every suppressed motion for hours, days, maybe years.]

Adadie: They're having a kinetic seizure. All the motion they didn't do—

Quirky Theo (now moving constantly, fluidly, wrongly): THIS IS WHY I STAY STILL.

[They rise from the chair like smoke. Their body ripples with accumulated movement. Every gesture is too
fast, too much, as if they're moving through thick air while everyone else moves through nothing.]

Felipe: They're magnificent.

Quirky Theo (to Diop): Your seductions are just elaborate stillness. You only move to make others
motion.
[To Louise] Your care is frantic stillness. Moving to avoid the movement that matters.

[To Vingt] You wound yourself to feel motion. But scars are the stillest things.

[To Jersey] Your eternal present is desperate stillness. Afraid one movement will collapse your bubble.

[To each accusation, they perform an impossible gesture—movements that shouldn't be possible with
human joints. The room itself begins to respond, furniture sliding away from them.]

Home E. Da: They're going to—

[Theo SCREAMS. But it's not sound—it's pure motion, visible as distortion in the air. Windows crack. The
smart glass displays chaotic patterns. Everyone is thrown back by the force of accumulated stillness finally
released.]

[Then, suddenly, Theo is still again. Perfectly, impossibly still. Sitting in the chair as if nothing happened. The
room is destroyed around them, but they sit in a perfect circle of calm.]

Quirky Theo (softly, lips not moving again): That is why... I prefer... stillness.

[Their left eye twitches once. A tear of pure kinetic energy rolls down their cheek, leaving a trail of cracked
air behind it.]

Vingt (breathless): Again. Do it again.

Quirky Theo: In three hours. Or three years. Stillness decides.

[They close their eyes for the first time. The room exhales. Everyone realizes they've been holding their
breath for the entire incident.]

Scene 2: Power Oscillations / Louise and Priya
[Split scene: Felipe and Vingt meet in Home E. Da's laboratory while Louise provides sanctuary for Priya at
the elder care facility.]

Left stage - The Laboratory:

Felipe: You're aroused by this. The weaponized trauma.

Vingt: Aren't you? Look—suffering that serves a purpose. Wounds that produce instead of just gape.

Home E. Da (entering): This one understands. Pain without product is waste.

Vingt: Make me useful. Give me acid wounds that think.

Felipe: You believe that's power? To have your wounds do your thinking?
Vingt: My thoughts have only ever wounded me. Why not let my wounds try thinking?

[Home E. Da smiles, recognizing a kindred spirit in self-destruction.]

Right stage - The Elder Care Facility:

Louise: You've been crying.

Priya: Diop... they made me feel special. But I was just another data point in their experiment.

Louise: Brilliance without kindness is just cruelty with better vocabulary.

Priya: But they're brilliant—

Louise: So are you. The difference is you haven't weaponized it yet.

[Priya looks at Louise with new understanding—this is what care without manipulation looks like.]

Scene 3: Campilla's Recognition / The Role Confrontation
[Campilla encounters Home E. Da in the liminal space between buildings and recognizes something
horrifying.]

Campilla: You were my patient. Room 247. The empathy trials.

Home E. Da: I was everyone's patient. The treatment worked too well. I felt every death in the ward. Every
ceiling became my ceiling. Every last breath, mine.

Campilla: We stopped the trial—

Home E. Da: After I'd absorbed 4,000 deaths. Do you know what I learned? The dying see truth because
they face the ceiling—the absolute boundary. So I share that gift.

[Home E. Da's scarring glows faintly, showing the neural map of every death they carry.]

[Meanwhile, Jersey confronts each character about their self-assigned roles:]

Jersey (to Louise): I am paying your salary for the next ten years. You don't have to be here. Yet you are.
The saint role is your luxury.

Louise: I don't do this for money—

Jersey: Everything is money. Care is money. Morality is money. Even betrayal—

[To Diop] I am buying the think tank. Your scandal is now my tax write-off.

Diop: You can't just—
Jersey: I am. Present tense. Your predator role? I am purchasing it. Now you are nothing.

Scene 4: The Chaos Summit / The Wound Garden
[Adadie brings Diop mathematical evidence of the think tank's impending collapse while Home E. Da
corrupts Louise's garden.]

Adadie: Your affairs create a strange attractor—a pattern that draws chaos. If we introduce three more
variables—

Diop: You want me to have three more affairs?

Adadie: I want you to confess to three you haven't had. False data to create controlled implosion.

[In the garden:]

Home E. Da: Your garden grows from death but pretends it's life. Mine acknowledges what it is—a
wound that won't stop giving.

[They plant crystalline weapons like seeds. Where they touch soil, the ground begins to weep the same
crystalline substance.]

Louise: You're poisoning everything—

Home E. Da: I'm clarifying. Every flower is just a pretty scab. Every root system, a network of nerves
exposed.

Scene 5: Jersey Meets the Wanderer / The Fog of Complicity
[Jersey encounters Felipe while Arjun's non-violence becomes visualized.]

Jersey: I am seeing you.

Felipe: You were seeing me. You will see me. You might see me. I exist in the conjugation, not the verb.

Jersey: That is impossible.

Felipe: The building disagrees. It built me from the space between 'is' and 'isn't.' Just as it's building you
into its northwest corner—hadn't you noticed? Your climate bubble is becoming a room.

[Jersey looks down—their bubble has indeed begun crystallizing, taking architectural form.]

[Meanwhile, Arjun becomes progressively more transparent, his non-violent philosophy turning him into
vapor, unable to protect Priya from Diop's atmospheric pressure.]

Scene 6: The Smart Glass Revelation / The Guilt Thrill
[As climate control fails, the smart glass begins to clear in patches, revealing what's been pressed between
the panes all along—preserved moments: a child's drawing, a suicide note, medical reports, love letters—all
flattened into transparency.]

All characters (speaking simultaneously about scars): The wounds know. They've always known. We're
just the transport system for damage seeking damage, pain seeking pain, clarity seeking hosts.

Vingt (laughing): I wanted dangerous affirmation. I got it. I'm affirmed as a danger to myself and others.
My scars have agency. They're recruiting.

Scene 7: The Breadcrumb Feast / Jersey's Immunity
[Home E. Da dumps thousands of breadcrumbs into the ventilation system during the building's collapse.]

Visual: As the crumbs circulate, everyone begins experiencing simultaneous death memories. The ceiling
becomes transparent, showing layer upon layer of dying patients staring up.

Home E. Da: This is my gift—collective clarity. Everyone seeing what the dying see.

[But Jersey, existing only in present tense, cannot be affected:]

Home E. Da: Then you're already dead. Your eternal present is a hospital bed. Your climate bubble, an
oxygen tent. You reject past and future because you're already staring at your ceiling.

Scene 8: The Exposure Séance / The Freedom Paradox
[Felipe gathers all characters in a room that exists only when they're all present.]

Felipe: The building wants to show you something. Its dream last night. I'll translate.

[The walls become transparent, revealing the hidden corridor system—a vast network of passages created
by every unspoken truth.]

Vingt: I am becoming my own victim. Finally. FINALLY. The thrill of self-prosecution!

Vingt: We're all broken in exactly the right places.

Diop: Wrong places.

Adadie: Same coordinates, different interpretation.

Scene 9: The Perfect Chairs / Felipe Meets the Wounded
[The think tank's ergonomic chairs begin to sweat—not moisture, but a clear fluid that smells like tears.]

Felipe: Your wounds exist in more dimensions than your body.
Home E. Da: The acid burned through dimensional barriers. I exist in every patient room simultaneously
now. Each scar is a door.

Scene 10: The Affluence Equation / The Final Ceiling
[Adadie attempts to model Jersey's behavior but discovers their wealth makes them a mathematical
anomaly.]

Adadie: Your trust fund... it's not just money. It's crystallized time. Generations of labor compressed into
your now.

[Home E. Da reveals their ultimate plan:]

Home E. Da: Every breadcrumb was a recording device. I haven't just shared memories—I've been
collecting yours. Soon, I'll have enough clarity to solve the equation—how to make everyone face their
ceiling without having to die.

Scene 11: The Unraveling Algorithm / The Spontaneous Revelation
[Adadie seeks out Louise one final time as all characters' scars literally communicate.]

Adadie: Your pattern. I can't solve it.

Louise: I don't have a pattern.

Adadie: Everyone has a pattern. But you—you're not random, you're not ordered. You're...

Louise: Human?

Adadie: An impossibility. My equations say you should have burned out years ago. Compassion fatigue,
secondary trauma—the mathematics are clear.

Louise: Maybe your mathematics are missing something.

Adadie: What?

Louise: Grace.

[Adadie begins to cry—an action they immediately try to quantify. Louise simply offers them tea, no
judgment, no measurement.]

Scene 12: Jersey's Present-Tense Explosion
[In the conference room, as crystalline wounds spread across walls, Jersey suddenly breaks from their eternal
present.]
Jersey: I am—was—will be—NO! I am feeling it. The guilt. But it thrills. It THRILLS!

Vingt: Welcome to timeline. It hurts beautifully, doesn't it?

Jersey: I am denying while confessing. I am desperate while satisfied. This is obscene. This is ecstasy.

Felipe: You're experiencing duration. Guilt needs memory, thrill needs anticipation. You're finally human.

Jersey (laughing/crying): I am paying for every present moment I stole. The bill arrives as pleasure. Cruel.
Delicious. More.

[Jersey's climate bubble cracks, releasing decades of postponed sensation.]

The Subjective Collapse (Interlude)
[The stage fractures into multiple realities—each character experiencing Jersey's breakdown differently:]

From Louise's Perspective: Jersey appears as a child, finally experiencing consequences, tears mixing
with laughter. Their disempowerment looks like healing.

From Diop's Perspective: Jersey becomes irresistibly attractive in their breakdown—vulnerability as
aphrodisiac. Their lack of control is intoxicating.

From Home E. Da's Perspective: Jersey's emotions crystallize in real-time, growing weapons from fresh
trauma. Their destabilization is literally productive.

From Vingt's Perspective: Jersey transforms into a mirror—every emotion they experience reflects
Vingt's own carefully cultivated powerlessness. They're destabilization twins.

From Felipe's Perspective: Jersey exists in all states simultaneously—powerful and powerless, stable and
chaotic, solid and dissolved. They've become truly liminal.

Scene 12.5: The Disempowerment Ecstasy
[As Jersey's temporal bubble fully collapses, they experience something unexpected—the erotic charge of
powerlessness.]

Jersey: I can't stop it. Time is raping my eternal present and I'm—grateful? Aroused? What is this?

Adadie: The mathematics of disempowerment. When control breaks, the relief registers as pleasure.

Louise: You're not well—

Jersey: I'm PERFECTLY unwell! Do you understand? My whole life was empowerment—money, influence,
temporal immunity. But this? This gorgeous helplessness? It's like discovering I have nerve endings.
Vingt: That's what I've been trying to tell everyone. Powerlessness is the only honest power.

Jersey: I'm destabilized. Liquified. And it's... sexual? Spiritual? I can't tell anymore.

[The room itself responds to Jersey's destabilization—walls breathe, floors ripple. Their emotion is so
powerful it's warping physical space.]

Scene 13: The Cruelty Configuration
[Jersey, now experiencing linear time, reveals the sadistic architecture of their denial.]

Jersey: I knew. Every donation, every charity—I was buying indulgences for crimes I was still committing.

Louise: That's not revelation, that's performance—

Jersey: Performance IS revelation! Watch—I'm denying your truth while desperately needing it. I'm cruel
to you because your goodness makes my guilt thrilling!

Diop: You're just discovering what I've always known. Transgression is the only honest pleasure.

Jersey: No. You transgress for power. I transgressed through temporal exile. My cruelty was making
others exist in time while I floated above it.

Adadie: The mathematics are beautiful. Guilt multiplied by thrill equals ecstatic self-destruction.

Jersey: I am the equation solving itself. It hurts. It pleases. It disgusts. More. MORE!

Scene 14: The Mathematics of Revision
[The elder care facility's supply closet. Adadie is taking inventory with obsessive precision. Campilla
materializes in the corner where cleaning supplies create chemical shadows.]

Campilla: You can see me.

Adadie: Statistically impossible. Yet here we are.

Campilla: You're counting bandages like they're prayers.

Adadie: Everything counts. That's how we know what we've lost.

[Campilla moves closer. Adadie doesn't retreat but their counting becomes faster.]

Campilla: I need to tell someone. About Louise. About what I—

Adadie: Forty-seven, forty-eight, forty-nine—

Campilla: I asked her first. Before I left. If she wanted me to stay.
Adadie: Fifty, fifty-one—

Campilla: She was eight. I asked an eight-year-old for permission to abandon her.

[Adadie's counting stops. The silence is mathematical—absolute zero.]

Adadie: You corrupted consent by asking someone incapable of giving it.

Campilla: I needed her to say yes. So when I left—

Adadie: You could revise it. "She wanted me to go." The mathematics of that are elegant. Evil, but
elegant.

Campilla: You understand.

Adadie: I asked my brother to forgive me. While he was dying. Extracted absolution from someone too
weak to refuse.

[They stand in parallel guilt, not looking at each other.]

Adadie: What did Louise say? When you asked?

Campilla: "Will you be happier?"

Adadie: And you translated that into permission.

Campilla: An eight-year-old wanting her mother's happiness—how is that not consent?

Adadie: Because consent requires understanding consequences. She couldn't know that your happiness
meant her abandonment.

Campilla: I've spent twenty years revising that moment. Sometimes she begs me to stay. Sometimes she
pushes me out. Sometimes she doesn't exist at all.

Adadie: Which revision lets you sleep?

Campilla: The one where I never had a daughter. Where Louise is someone else's ghost.

[Adadie returns to counting, but the numbers are different now.]

Adadie: One timeline where you stayed. Zero timelines where she's unharmed. Infinite timelines where
you justify it.

Campilla: Don't mathematize my shame.

Adadie: Shame is just guilt multiplied by time. Very mathematical.
Campilla: Then what's your equation? For what you did to your brother?

[Adadie's hands shake. Pills spill. They don't pick them up.]

Adadie: He was on life support. I presented options using probability matrices he couldn't understand
anymore. Made continuing seem selfish. Made dying seem generous.

Campilla: You needed his consent to pull the plug.

Adadie: I needed his consent to live with pulling it. Different variable entirely.

Campilla: So you corrupted it. Wrapped it in numbers until yes was the only logical answer.

Adadie: The way you wrapped abandonment in love. "Will you be happier?" As if an eight-year-old could
refuse her mother's happiness.

[They finally look at each other. Ghost and mathematician recognizing their shared revision.]

Campilla: How do you survive it? The knowing?

Adadie: I calculate. Obsessively. If I can solve enough equations, maybe I'll find the one that changes
what I did.

Campilla: And I haunt. If I exist as guilt made visible, maybe that's payment enough.

Adadie: It's not.

Campilla: I know.

Adadie: But we revise that too. Call haunting "penance." Call calculation "research." Call corrupted
consent "complicated choices."

Campilla: Louise still saves everyone. Trying to save me retroactively.

Adadie: Every patient is you. She's trying to revise your leaving by making everyone else stay.

Campilla: While you turn everything into data. Trying to revise your brother's death by proving it was
numerically inevitable.

[The chemical shadows deepen. Both women seem to age—or maybe that's just shame becoming visible.]

Adadie: I've modeled our conversation. Sixteen million variations. In none of them do we forgive
ourselves.

Campilla: Because forgiveness would require admitting what we actually did. Without revision.

Adadie: "I manipulated my dying brother into agreeing to die."
Campilla: "I made my child complicit in her own abandonment."

[The words hang between them like nooses. No revision possible in this clarity.]

Adadie: The worst part? We'd do it again.

Campilla: With slightly different revisions. To make it bearable.

Adadie: I'd use different numbers. More sophisticated models. But the outcome—

Campilla: I'd leave at night. While she slept. No question to corrupt. But the leaving—

Both: Inevitable.

[They stand in the supply closet surrounded by instruments of care, having admitted to weaponizing love.]

Adadie: Is this what Felipe meant? About existing between states?

Campilla: This is what existing AS shame looks like. We're not people who did shameful things. We ARE
the shameful things, walking around in people-shaped containers.

Adadie: Then what's the point of revision? If we can't change what we are?

Campilla: Survival. We revise to survive. Otherwise—

Adadie: Otherwise we'd have to feel it. Unmitigated. The full weight of what we stole—their consent, their
trust, their ability to choose.

Campilla: So we edit. Constantly. Making ourselves the heroes of stories where we're clearly the villains.

Adadie: Or making ourselves villains in stories where we're just... human. And weak. And so afraid of that
weakness we'll corrupt anything to feel strong.

[Louise's voice echoes from somewhere in the facility. Both women flinch.]

Campilla: She's coming.

Adadie: Should we—

Campilla: What? Tell her? That her whole life is built on a corrupted yes? That every patient she saves is
just her revising my leaving?

Adadie: The mathematics suggest—

Campilla: Fuck your mathematics. And fuck my haunting. We're both just shame dressed up in different
costumes.
[Campilla begins to fade as Louise's footsteps approach.]

Campilla: Keep counting your bandages. I'll keep haunting my daughter. And we'll both keep revising
reality until it's bearable. That's all consent means for people like us—permission to lie more elegantly
about what we've done.

[She's gone. Adadie stands alone among the spilled pills, counting. But now we know what the numbers are
really measuring—the distance between who they were and who they've revised themselves to be. It's
infinite. It's zero. It's the exact width of a corrupted yes.]

Scene 15: Pockets Full of Wonder
[The elder care facility's common room, just after dawn. Priya sits with Mrs. Fernandes, who clutches a music
box that no longer plays. Louise enters with medication. The light through the windows is impossibly gentle.]

Mrs. Fernandes: It used to play "Clair de Lune." My daughter...

Priya: I know. You told me yesterday.

Mrs. Fernandes: Did I? Well. It was beautiful.

[She winds the broken key. Silence. She smiles anyway.]

Louise (to Priya): You've been here all night?

Priya: Couldn't sleep. After what Diop... what we... I needed to be somewhere clean.

Louise: This place isn't clean. It's full of endings.

Priya: But honest endings. Mrs. Fernandes doesn't pretend her music box works. She just loves it anyway.

[Vingt enters, fresh bandages on their arms. They stop at the doorway, uncertain.]

Vingt: I... is it okay if I...?

Mrs. Fernandes: Come, child. Sit. Your wounds are singing.

Vingt: They're not. They're just—

Mrs. Fernandes: I can hear them. Little bells. Like my music box, but internal.

[Vingt sits carefully. Mrs. Fernandes takes their scarred hand without hesitation.]

Louise: Mrs. Fernandes, that's—

Mrs. Fernandes: What? Improper? I'm ninety-three. Propriety is for people who think they have time.
[She traces Vingt's scars with ancient fingers.]

Mrs. Fernandes: So much story in these lines. Like my wrinkles, but you wrote yours yourself.

Vingt: They're ugly.

Mrs. Fernandes: They're punctuation. How else would we know where one chapter ends and another
begins?

[Priya watches this exchange with something breaking open in her face.]

Priya: How do you do it? Know all this damage exists and still... glow?

Mrs. Fernandes: Oh, child. Innocence isn't not knowing. It's knowing and choosing to remain tender
anyway.

[Home E. Da enters with their breadcrumb pills. Everyone tenses except Mrs. Fernandes.]

Home E. Da: Time for your clarity enhancement, Mrs.—

Mrs. Fernandes: No thank you. I'm quite unclear and prefer it that way.

Home E. Da: But the ceiling visions, the profound understanding—

Mrs. Fernandes: I understand plenty. That my music box is broken. That this child (gesturing to Vingt) is
bleeding inside. That you're trying to sell me certainty when wonder is all I have left.

Home E. Da: Wonder is just ignorance dressed in—

Mrs. Fernandes: Wonder is wisdom that refuses to calcify.

[She pulls something from her pocket—a river stone, worn smooth.]

Mrs. Fernandes: Found this sixty years ago. My daughter... she said it looked like the moon. It doesn't.
But I've carried it ever since because she saw moon where others see rock. That's innocence. Choosing to
see moon.

Vingt: Even though it's just a stone?

Mrs. Fernandes: Especially because it's just a stone. Anyone can see magic in magic. But to see it in the
ordinary? That takes will.

[Louise has been silent, fighting something in herself.]

Louise: My mother left when I was eight.

[Everyone turns. Louise never shares personal information.]
Louise: I carry her leaving like... like Mrs. Fernandes carries that stone. Not because it doesn't hurt.
Because the hurt has worn smooth from handling. Because I choose to see it as the moon of my
becoming, not the night of my abandonment.

Priya: That's not innocence. That's denial.

Louise: Is it? Or is innocence the refusal to let damage make you damaging?

[Felipe materializes in the corner, drawn by talk of refusal.]

Felipe: Cynicism is gravity. It takes no effort to fall. Innocence is flight—improbable, temporary, essential.

Mrs. Fernandes: Yes! This one understands. We're all Icarus, but some of us remember the flying more
than the falling.

[She stands with difficulty, moves to the window.]

Mrs. Fernandes: Every morning I watch the sun rise like it's the first time. Because it is. This sun, this day,
this particular light through these specific clouds. Cynicism would say it's just another sunrise. Innocence
says there's no such thing as "just another" anything.

Vingt: But you know you're dying.

Mrs. Fernandes: We're all dying, child. Innocence is decorating the journey.

[She opens her other pocket. It's full of small objects—buttons, candies, folded papers.]

Mrs. Fernandes: Pockets full of wonder. That's what we are. Walking collections of moments that could
have made us hard but we chose to let them make us... particular.

Home E. Da: This is exactly why you need clarity enhancement. To see past these delusions—

Mrs. Fernandes: I see perfectly. You're the one who thinks clarity and cynicism are synonyms.

[She takes one of Home E. Da's breadcrumb pills, but instead of swallowing it, she puts it in her pocket with
the other wonders.]

Mrs. Fernandes: There. Now it's not a tool of forced wisdom. It's a strange little pearl someone tried to
feed me once. Much more interesting this way.

Priya (to Louise): Is that what you do? Transform the pain into... peculiarity?

Louise: Every patient I lose goes in my pocket. Not as grief. As proof that connection is worth the ending.

Felipe: Innocence as aikido. Using the world's force against itself.
Vingt: But the scars—

Mrs. Fernandes: Are your pockets, turned inside out. Showing everyone your collection. Very brave.

[She winds her broken music box again. This time, Vingt hums "Clair de Lune." Then Priya joins. Then Louise.
Even Felipe's existence seems to hum along. The broken box plays through human voices.]

Home E. Da (disturbed): This is... this is...

Mrs. Fernandes: Innocent? Yes. We're refusing to become what knowledge should make us.
Disappointing, isn't it? All that data about suffering, and here we are, still soft.

Vingt: Like choosing to be wounded rather than numb.

Priya: Like loving someone you know will betray you.

Louise: Like caring for people you can't save.

Felipe: Like existing between states when choosing one would be simpler.

Mrs. Fernandes: Exactly. Innocence isn't ignorance. It's the sacred stubbornness of staying tender when
you have every reason to harden.

[She sits back down, tired but luminous.]

Mrs. Fernandes: My music box will never play again. My daughter is gone. I'm dying. These are facts. But
facts are just the beginning of the story, not the end. Innocence is what we write in the margins.

[Home E. Da leaves, unable to process this resistance to clarity. The others remain, each holding their
damage differently now—not as weight but as collection, not as scarring but as peculiar decoration.]

Mrs. Fernandes: Now. Who wants to help me sort my pockets? I think I have a butterfly wing in here
somewhere. Or maybe it's just a dried leaf. In this light, who can tell the difference?

[They gather around her, each reaching into their own pockets—literal and metaphorical—pulling out their
polished wonders. The morning light makes everything briefly holy. Even the scars. Especially the scars.]

Final Scene: The Precipitation
[All the play's accumulated vapors—breath, lies, intellectual pretensions—reach saturation point. The sterile
clouds can no longer hold their moisture. It begins to rain inside the think tank, each drop a betrayal made
liquid.]

Visual Climax:

Diop's designer clothes dissolve, revealing the nothing underneath
Louise's simple uniform remains intact, but she's soaked in others' sins

Adadie's equations run like mascara down the glass walls

Jersey stands dry in their bubble, untouched but also unreachable
Priya collects the rain in her palms, finally understanding its composition

Campilla becomes the rain, achieving the dissolution she always sought
Home E. Da's wounds respond by liquifying and reproducing
Vingt opens their mouth to taste the precipitation of damage

Felipe guides some through corridors between corridors while others remain
Quirky Theo sits perfectly still as chaos reigns around them

Mrs. Fernandes holds her broken music box above her head like an umbrella

The Final Image: The think tank—the intellectual home—is flooded. Characters stand knee-deep in the
precipitation of their betrayals. Only the elder care facility, built to withstand human moisture, remains
functional. Louise opens its doors, offering shelter not from weather but from the sterile clouds of
thought divorced from feeling.

Louise: Come. All of you. Even you, Diop.

Diop: I don't deserve—

Louise: Deserving is just another calculation. Come because you're wet.

[One by one, they wade through their liquified betrayals toward the elder care facility. Some make it. Some
remain in the flood, unable to leave what they've created.]

Mrs. Fernandes (at the doorway): Welcome to where things are allowed to be broken and loved anyway.

[The rain continues. The building's perfect systems fail perfectly. In the distance, another think tank is being
built, another cycle beginning. But for now, in this moment, some choose shelter. Some choose to remain
tender. Some choose to carry their scars like pockets full of wonder.]

Epilogue: The Feminine Shaman's Witness
[After the play concludes, lights dim. A figure emerges who has been present but unseen throughout—the
Feminine Shaman, sitting in lotus position stage right, having witnessed everything.]

The Shaman (speaking directly to audience): I have been here. Before Diop's first seduction, before
Louise's first patient, before the building dreamed itself into being. I am the witness you didn't see, the
shadow in every scene.
This Jersey child—I watched them discover what mystics have always known: guilt and thrill are the same
energy wearing different masks. The electric shock of recognition when we see our cruelty clearly—it's
indistinguishable from orgasm. The spirits laugh at our surprise.

But here's what you missed—the nuance invisible to those who've never been truly powerless: Jersey's
disempowerment wasn't loss. It was homecoming. All power is borrowed. All control, illusion. When the
illusion breaks, what floods in isn't weakness—it's the universe's actual strength, too vast for any
container.

Watch how Jersey shook. Not from pain—from the pleasure of finally being small enough to feel
everything. The privileged fear powerlessness because they mistake it for emptiness. But powerlessness is
fullness—every emotion you've postponed arriving simultaneously.

I saw Vingt collecting wounds like prayer beads, not knowing each scar was an offering to gods of
necessary damage. They understood—disempowerment is a technology. A tool for feeling what power
insulates against.

The destabilizing emotions? They're not symptoms—they're solutions. Every breakdown is the psyche
solving equations the conscious mind can't process. Jersey's hysteria was calculation. Their tears, data
transfer. Their thrilled guilt? The algorithm of transformation.

But humans categorize: stable/unstable, empowered/weak. Missing that the most profound power comes
through willing disempowerment. The shaman knows—to heal others, first be wounded. To lead, first be
lost. To stabilize reality, first let it dissolve.

Home E. Da's acid wounds? I sent that burning. Not as punishment but as invitation. Some
transformations require dissolution. They accepted. Became prophet of productive pain. But they still seek
control through their wounds. Jersey found something deeper—the ecstasy of having no control at all.

The subjective nature of breakdown—how each character saw Jersey's collapse differently—this is the
secret. There is no objective suffering or joy. Every emotion is refracted through the lens of the observer's
own relationship to power and powerlessness.

Louise saw healing because she needs Jersey's sickness to justify her wellness. Diop saw seduction
because they need everything to be about desire. Each perception reveals more about the observer than
the observed.

Desperation and cruelty dance together in the space between heartbeats. Jersey learned this in one
explosive moment—that we're cruel precisely because we're desperate for connection, desperate
because we're cruel to ourselves first.
The overlooked nuance? Disempowerment isn't the opposite of power—it's power's raw form before ego
shapes it into control. Jersey's billion-dollar climate bubble was weakness. Their sobbing breakdown?
Pure strength finally flowing.

Every character thinks they discovered something new. Child, your scars are older than stars. Your guilt is
ancient. Your thrill at transgression? The first sensation after the first boundary was drawn.

I've been sitting here since before the foundation was poured, waiting for this moment—when the
eternal present shatters and reveals the eternal wound. Every character thinks they discovered something
new. Child, your scars are older than stars. Your guilt is ancient. Your thrill at transgression? The first
sensation after the first boundary was drawn.

The play ends but the witnessing continues. In your chairs, you're growing the same crystalline wounds.
Feel them? That's guilt. Feel the pleasure? That's recognition. You're all Jersey, pretending you exist
outside consequence. You're all Vingt, making scars to feel real. You're all the building—wounded space
hoping someone notices.

I remain. Witnessing. Waiting for the next bubble to burst, the next eternal present to shatter into
glorious, guilty time. The play you watched? It's happening now. Has been happening. Will happen. In the
space between your denial and your desperate need to be seen—truly seen—in your cruelty.

The final nuance, the one that breaks minds: empowerment is the cage. Disempowerment is the key. But
the lock is on the inside, and you must become small enough—powerless enough—to reach through the
bars and turn it.

Go. But know I'm watching. Have always been watching. Will watch as you discover what Jersey now
knows—guilt and thrill are not opposites. They're lovers, fucking in the foundation of everything you
build to hide from time.

[The Shaman closes her eyes. When she opens them, the theater is empty—or is it? In every shadow, her
presence remains, witnessing the eternal play of denial, desperation, and the cruel thrill of finally, finally
being caught.]

[The audience must choose when to leave, making their grammar visible through action. The play doesn't
end. It just stops including the actors.]

The Overlooked Nuances of Disempowerment
Physical: The body's euphoria when control collapses—endorphins flooding as the nervous system
celebrates the end of vigilance.

Temporal: How powerlessness returns us to the present more violently than any meditation—Jersey
discovered presence through absence of control.
Relational: Disempowerment creates genuine intimacy—when Jersey broke, others could finally touch
them through their climate bubble.

Spiritual: The mystic truth that all power is borrowed from the universe, and disempowerment is simply
returning the loan with interest.

Erotic: The profound sexuality of surrender—not submission to another but to the flow of experience
itself.

Mathematical: Adadie's observation that disempowerment follows inverse exponential curves—the more
you lose, the more you gain.

Theatrical: The stage itself destabilizes with Jersey, suggesting that our environments are held stable only
by our illusion of control.

Director's Note
"LAST FAll refuses to provide easy answers or comfortable moral positions. It asks us to examine our own
complicity in systems of power, desire, and transgression. Set in contemporary Mumbai, it speaks to
universal themes while remaining grounded in the specific pressures and contradictions of modern Indian
society. This is not a play about good and evil, but about the more troubling territory that exists between
them.

The play requires audiences to experience their own grammatical complicity in real time, to feel silence as
a psychic trap door, to understand that betrayal is not a past event but a continuous action. Every
element—from the sterile cloud aesthetic to the final precipitation—serves to make visible what we
spend our lives trying to hide: that we are all patients, all staring at ceilings, all growing strange things
from our scars."

Content Advisory
This production contains mature themes including power dynamics in relationships, psychological
manipulation, explorations of consent, self-harm, and intense psychological content. The play includes
extended silences, direct audience address, and moments designed to create discomfort. Recommended
for mature audiences.

The production includes scenes with sparse dialogue and silences lasting up to 3 minutes. Some audience
members may find these pauses challenging.

The Rangmanch Collective provides post-show discussions with counselors on select evenings.

Performance Notes
Running time: Approximately 4 hours with one intermission

The play demands extreme physical and emotional commitment from actors
Audiences should be prepared for direct engagement and long silences
The final precipitation scene requires audiences to potentially get wet in front rows
Exit timing is part of the performance—audiences make their grammar visible through when they

The Rangmanch Collective is committed to producing challenging contemporary theater that pushes
boundaries while maintaining respect for our audiences and artists. LAST FAll represents our most ambitious
production to date—a fearless exploration of power, transgression, and the possibility of remaining tender in
a world that demands hardness.
