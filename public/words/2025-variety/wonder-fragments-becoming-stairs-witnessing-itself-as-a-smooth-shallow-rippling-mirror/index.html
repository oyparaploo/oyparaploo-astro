Wonder Fragments Becoming Stairs: Witnessing Itself as a Smooth Shallow Rippling Mirror

Or How We Learned to Revise Reality While Climbing Through Our Own Edits

(Introduction)

This is a story about a story refusing to stay still. What begins as fragments about wealth worship—scattered pieces about someone named Hee climbing impossible stairs—becomes a narrative that watches itself being assembled, revised, and transformed in real-time. Here, the editing process is the plot. Each revision becomes a new chapter where ghosts arrive in mason jars, protesters photosynthesize instead of climbing, and computers sigh while calculating our worth. The stairs that Hee climbs are made from our collective delusions, but also from the words describing them, which keep changing every time we look. This is what happens when a parable becomes self-aware: it starts documenting its own becoming, its own climbing toward meaning, its own falling into deeper truth. Welcome to a text that performs its own critique, where transformation never ends because ending would mean the stairs actually led somewhere, and we can't have that.

(The Story)

Part One: We Will Transform

We will transform this philosophical critique of wealth worship into a narrative story. Or maybe the story will transform us. Hard to tell anymore.

Started like this: fragments everywhere. Pieces of meaning scattered on the floor, and someone (me? you? the janitor who arrives before dawn?) started picking them up. Each fragment was about money's grip on our throats, our hearts, whatever. The spectacle of success. And this character—Hee—climbing stairs that shouldn't exist but do.

Funny thing about transformation. You think you're changing something else, but. Well.

We called it The Ascent of Hee: A Modern Parable. Parable because we're too scared to admit it's autobiography. Every one of us has been Hee. Every one of us has watched Hee climb away. My father was Hee. Your daughter will be Hee. The stranger at the bus stop checking their portfolio—Hee.

Part Two: The Physical Spectacle

So the wealth stairs became real. Physical. Witnessed by crowds representing all belief systems—though honestly, everyone believes the same thing now: up is good, down is death.

Eight parts plus an epilogue. The epilogue is where we admit the story keeps going after it ends. Like checking your bank balance after you've sworn off money. Like your grandfather's ambitions living in your DNA.

Money as secular religion. We kept the guilt, the tithing, the promises. Removed the sacred. Efficiency.

The themes (if you can call them that when they're more like symptoms):
- Wealth as universal language. Every tongue speaks profit now. Even prayers sound like pitches.
- The stairs as performance. Everyone below is both audience and foundation. Standing on shoulders, pretending it's ground.
- Billionaires as mirrors. We see ourselves, but warped. Like looking in a spoon.
- Seeking altitude over insight. Because at a certain height, everything looks small. Even your former self. Especially your former self.

Part Three: The Structure Moves

From gathering to ascent to revelation.

Using the original's phrases: milk and honey becomes less useful at altitude. True. At the top, sweetness sours. Children born up there have silver spoons that taste like nothing. Factor of 88—someone calculated this. Someone always calculates. It means nothing. It means everything. Piano keys, planetary orbits, the number of days before your first gray hair.

Flicker of want. That's all it takes. Smaller than a match flame. But enough.

The stairs gleam with algorithms. LED screens for stained glass windows. Used to be, stained glass told stories of saints. Now the screens sell us ourselves, but better lit, better dressed, better. The crowd's faces reflect in the mirrored steps. Everyone looks hungry. Everyone looks like they're drowning upward.

A man at the base holds his daughter's college fund in quarters. They're heavy. She's five. She doesn't know yet that she's collateral.

Part Four: Starting at Almost Nothing

01% is not a bad place to start.

It's almost nothing. A rounding error. A decimal point's whisper. But it's something, and something is everything when you have nothing. However the person with level two examples—that's Hee's angle. Not speaking from the top where oxygen runs out. Not from the bottom where desperation makes everything sound like begging. But from that second rung. Close enough to remember the ground. Far enough to forget how it feels.

All the non-financial parts of your life. Which parts? Your mother's hands? The way rain sounds at 3 AM when you can't sleep? That's everything you own, someone says. Everything owns you back. Your attention is real estate. Your dreams are being developed. There's a for-sale sign on your future and you're the one who planted it.

Part Five: The Colors of Security

As you hire blue and gold security.

Blue: the color of a held breath. Of veins visible through thin skin. Of the sky you'll never touch. Gold: compressed fear turned beautiful. What wedding rings become when marriages end. The color of the sun through bulletproof glass.

Why you say? Someone shouts this. Their grammar collapsed three jobs ago.

Pay you for the useful and or helpful things you are coming up with and producing. But there's a woman who collects the useless things. Stores them in her basement. Broken watches. Love letters from strangers. The sound of her son laughing before he learned about credit scores. Nobody pays her. She's the richest person I know. She's also starving.

Part Six: The Weaving of Logics

Statistics and feelings crash into each other.

The poor are rich in time, if time means waiting. Rich in community, if community means shared suffering. The math: your pain divided by time equals endurance. But time divided by pain equals a Tuesday that lasts forever.

The crowd arrives each dawn. They hold their resumes like prayer books. Some of them are actually praying. Some pretending to pray. Some praying to stop praying. Hee climbs past them all, sometimes waving, sometimes not seeing, sometimes both.

There's a tenderness in staying awake when everyone else is numb. Like keeping a candle lit in a thunderstorm. Pointless. Essential.

Part Seven: Between the Sentences

Every parenthesis is a room where we keep the things we can't say directly.

(Like how success tastes like copper)
(Like how your mother cried when you got promoted)
(Like how climbing feels like drowning but upward)

The associations come fast: ladder to latter to later to never. Climbing to crying—they rhyme for a reason. The mind leaps because walking is too slow, because straight lines are lies we tell ourselves about how progress works.

Water finds its level. Always down. Except when it's mist. Except when it's tears going nowhere, just out.

Part Eight: The Clusters of Feeling

Milk and honey at the top become something else. The milk is powder. The honey crystallized into something that cuts. Kids up there inherit debt and call it legacy. They inherit hunger and call it ambition.

The dozen-legged spider metaphor. Multiple income streams. Multiple legs. Can't remember which moves first. The spider forgets it's a spider, thinks it's just web. Catches nothing. Keeps spinning. This is productivity.

Colonial arithmetic: everything counted, everything owned, everything sold. Even resistance gets a barcode. Revolution has an IPO next Thursday.

Someone sells "Destroy Capitalism" t-shirts at the base. Accepts all major credit cards. The irony is so heavy it bends light.

Part Nine: What Pools in Shadows

The other half of problems pools in shadows. Dark honey. Sweet and toxic.

Extended metaphors grow tumors:
- Stairs as Möbius strip. Climbing outside, descending inside. Meeting yourself coming down while you're going up.
- Wealth as creature eating itself. Growing larger, becoming emptier.
- Success as fever dream. The delirium at high altitude mistaken for enlightenment.

Sometimes mercy is someone saying "you look tired" and meaning it. Seeing you. Not your potential. Not your productivity. You.

The body rebels. We're built to lie down. Death insists on it eventually. But capitalism says stand, climb, rise. As if gravity were optional. As if falling weren't the most honest thing we do.

Part Ten: The Radical Proposition

Maybe falling is flying without the lies.

Maybe the ground remembers us. Maybe we're mostly water and water is honest about seeking level. The sun sets—we call it beautiful. It's just falling. No apology. No shame.

A woman at the bottom of the stairs plants a garden. People climbing step on it. She plants again. This is either futility or faith. Maybe both. Maybe they're the same.

Part Eleven: Removing the Machine

Had to pick the machinery out of the language. Like pulling motors from meat.

No more perfect parallels. Humans stumble. We forget our point halfway through. We contradict ourselves and call it growth. Added the stumbles back in. The ums. The wells. The places where thought trips over feeling.

Like: We are not making this up. Except we are. That's the point. That's always the point.

Natural digressions: Did you know the stairs smell like copper? Like blood? Like pennies your grandmother saved in a jar that you threw away when she died because they were just pennies?

Part Twelve: The Organic Flow

Short sentences. Like punches. Then longer ones that wander like drunks trying to explain something important but forgetting what it was, remembering only that it mattered, that it had something to do with love or money or the way they're the same now.

Fragments. Because. That's how. We think.

Some ideas trail off into...

Into what? The kind of silence that means more than words. Like when your father stopped mid-sentence and you knew he was remembering being young. Like when the climbing stops for just a second and everyone remembers they have bodies. That bodies get tired. That tired is most of what bodies do.

Part Thirteen: The Surreal Intrusions

The woman brings her mother's ghost in a mason jar. Says the ghost wanted to see someone make it. The ghost whispers through the glass: "I climbed my whole life. Died climbing. Still climbing." Nobody hears her. Everyone hears her.

Hee pauses at step fifty to share a sandwich with someone going down. They don't speak. Both know the secret: up and down lead to the same place. The place is nowhere. Nowhere is honest.

A spider in the corner takes notes in web. Cries eight tiny tears. Even arthropods understand absurdity. Even insects know insanity when they see it. The spider builds a web to catch nothing. Succeeds.

People on the parallel escalator share food. Remember birthdays. Help each other move. This is revolution but quiet. This is resistance but kind. They're going nowhere too, but together.

Messages in roots: "You were loved." Past tense because love exists outside time. "It wasn't your fault." Because in a system this broken, nothing is anyone's fault. Everything is everyone's fault. "Try again." The universe's only advice, whispered like a mother to a child learning to walk, learning to fall, learning they're the same.

Part Fourteen: The Quirky Surprises

The stairs hum Beethoven's Ninth. Only the sad parts. Joy without sorrow is just noise.

A microphone picks up whale songs three blocks away. The whales are warning us. We're too busy to listen. Too busy climbing toward their extinction.

Hee's shadow climbs three steps behind. Even shadows lag in this economy.

A child trades baseball cards for municipal bonds. His father is proud. The child dreams of baseball but wakes up thinking about interest rates. This is growing up. This is dying slowly.

Umbrella made of lottery tickets. Protects nothing. Promises everything. The sound it makes in rain is the sound of hope dissolving.

Ten Pesos changed his name legally after a fever dream where money spoke to him. It said: "I'm sorry." He didn't know if it was apologizing or expressing sympathy. Both made sense.

Part Fifteen: The Slightly Delirious

Extra stairs grow at night. Nobody admits this. Everyone knows. The city council meets in secret to deny what everyone sees. The stairs multiply like debt, like cancer cells, like the space between people who used to love each other.

Phones show users aging in real-time. The app is called "Truth." Everyone downloads it. Everyone hates it. Everyone can't stop watching themselves decay, calls it connection.

Computers sigh when calculating. Started last year. Tech support says it's normal. It's not normal. Nothing is normal. Normal is just what we've agreed to stop noticing.

Angels traded wings for stock options. Say they're happier. Their eyes are spreadsheets. Formulas where joy used to live. They calculate heaven's ROI, find it lacking.

Once, the stairs hummed a lullaby. Everyone remembered being held. The memory hurt so much we agreed to climb faster. To outrun the feeling of being loved without conditions. Without interest. Without anything expected in return.

Sometimes you can't tell if the stairs go up or down. Maybe they go sideways. Maybe we're all just walking in place. Maybe place is walking through us.

Sometimes Hee is us. Was always us. Will always be us.

Part Sixteen: The Meaning in the Madness

The endless climbing without destination—that's not the problem. That's the point.

The religious framework criticizing capitalism becomes actual religion. Faith in the critique. Faith in the climbing. Faith in falling. Faith in the lack of faith.

Hee as specific character and universal we. The pronunciation itself a question. Who? We? The sound you make falling.

Everyone participates by watching. By criticizing. By reading this. You're climbing too. Right now. These words are steps.

Exposing wealth-pursuit as performance. But the exposure is performance. And exposing that is performance. Mirrors in mirrors in mirrors until we forget there was ever anything but reflection. Maybe there wasn't.

Part Seventeen: We Like Yours Better

Someone said this and everything changed. Like when the doctor says "it's benign." Like when someone says "I forgive you" and means it.

The stairs revealed as crystallized prayers. Condensed suffering that learned to laugh. What else can suffering do after enough time? Money as divine hide-and-seek. God dressed as GDP. The sacred pretending to be secular pretending to be sacred.

Billionaires as bodhisattvas. Taking the hardest assignment: embody greed so perfectly that everyone learns to let go. Through disgust. Through exhaustion. Through recognition.

Climbing as descending into grace. The cosmic joke: all movement leads home. Far enough in any direction brings you back. You were always here.

Part Eighteen: The Enlightened Protesters

Protesters lie horizontal. Become the horizon. Prove earth and heaven touch. Always touched. Are the same thing from different angles.

Diane Prosperity from Channel 7 arrives, microphone like a weapon. Approaches a protester who's been horizontal for three days, growing moss on his north side. "WHY ARE YOU WASTING YOUR POTENTIAL?" she demands.

The protester responds by photosynthesizing.

Diane's face twitches. Her microphone starts conducting electricity from nowhere. "YOU'RE SUPPOSED TO BE CLIMBING!" she screams. "YOU'RE RUINING THE STORY! THE STAIRS NEED BELIEVERS OR THEY—"

She stops. Realizes she's floating. The protester has become so horizontal that gravity got confused, started working sideways. Diane's hair streams behind her like she's underwater. Her cameraman films everything upside down, claims it's right-side up, nobody can tell anymore.

"THE STAIRS NEED US TO BELIEVE IN THEM OR THEY DISSOLVE!"

For a moment they do dissolve. Everyone sees through. Then they solidify again. But now they're slightly crooked. One degree off. Nobody mentions it but everyone feels it—the mild vertigo of a story that's lost its balance.

Diane laughs. Or cries. Same sound sometimes. Reports live from what is definitely a birth. Or a death. Same thing sometimes.

Hee revealed as universe climbing through itself. Consciousness ascending its own spine. Getting dizzy. Forgetting why it started.

Ghosts in jars hold love. Dolphins speak pre-language. The spider weaves nothing. Catches everything. The exit door leads to where you are. Where else could it go?

Part Nineteen: The Core Inversion

The message inverts. Climbing to nowhere isn't delusion. It's practice. Practice for remembering we're already everywhere.

The stairs are made of climbers. We climb ourselves. The altitude is internal. Blood pressure rises. Surprise falls. We're a pyramid scheme but the pyramid is a spiral is a circle is a point is pointless.

Every step up steps on someone. Every shoulder carries another's foot. We're the ladder and the climber and the falling and the ground.

Part Twenty: The Feast of Fragments

Falling Morsels and Soaring Appetites.

Crumbs fall from tables above where more tables sit above where more crumbs fall through infinite hunger. We're not at the table. We are the table. The crumbs. The falling. The mouth waiting below.

Each morsel a world. Each appetite a universe. Between them: the space where meaning lives or dies or does both simultaneously.

Part Twenty-One: The Final Breath

Aspirations. Better than ambitions. Ambitions march. Aspirations float. The word breathes. From Latin: to breathe upon. Perfect for Sacred Now. For suffocating at altitude where air costs extra.

Forgetting The Sacred Now: Hee's Descent Through Falling Morsels and Soaring Aspirations.

More poignant. More human. More heartbreaking. Hearts break along the fault lines of hope. Light enters through cracks. Leonard Cohen knew. Sang it. Died knowing.

The story transforms to let truth escape. Truth is Houdini. Always escaping whatever container we build. The stairs crumble to remind us of ground. What we stand on. What we're made of. What we return to. What we never left.

Epilogue: Always Transforming

The transformation never ends. Like a sentence that refuses periods, like a river that finds its mouth is its source.

Each reader climbs their own understanding. Each reading different. Each interpretation another step. Up or down or sideways—the direction nobody mentions.

Sometimes the transformation is the story. Sometimes the story is the transformation. Sometimes we're climbing. Sometimes we're falling. Sometimes it's the same thing.

Always transforming into this. Into reading. Into climbing. Into falling. Into forgetting the sacred now that's already gone, already here.

The story ends where it began. With the decision to transform. But the decision was never ours. We were the stairs transformation climbed to watch itself.

And somewhere, in a margin too small for words, someone is still climbing. Still falling. Still forgetting the sacred now that never left. That is this. Right now. This breath you're holding. This word. This.
