Brrd Roots ~ Saltwater and Much More

Words / 2025 Variety

Scars Opera of Erosion and Defiance In Five Acts

The Bureaucratic Entity now carries symbols of past fallen regimes—unmistakable echoes of dynasties that once ruled absolutely.
Saltwater takes a more omnipresent role, moving between acts, whispering lines like an inevitability that cannot be silenced.
The Chorus of the Weeds now shifts in formation—standing tall before protests, shrinking under suppression, curling like weeds pressed beneath stone before growing again.
Elio & June embody movement; their songs fluctuate like shifting tides, their defiance layered in melodies that resist decay.

Act I: The Burning City
(The lantern flickers. The Bureaucratic Entity enters—not a singular voice, but many, speaking in unison. A chorus of rules, of mandates, of history pretending it is unbreakable.)

Chorus of the Weeds (layered voices, swelling and retreating):
*"Zero policy implemented—
A flame ignited, a tower fell,
No prairie fire, but the air carried echoes,
Weeds took root in grief and rage!"*

Elio (watching the fire, voice heavy with certainty):
*"Families gathered in thousands, standing in protest—
as before, as in the uprisings of old,
as in the forgotten revolutions drowned before they could rise.*
Rage filled the air—hope tangled with despair!
But history does not listen, it only repeats."*

(Saltwater flickers along the stage floor, consuming spaces in silence. The Bureaucratic Entity speaks but cannot hear its own dissolution.)

Bureaucratic Entity (commanding, voices like stone cracking under pressure):
*"Suppress the voices! Remove the unrest!
Berate the young, force them into silence!
Sweep the weeds from the fields,
for they threaten the order that sustains us!"*

June (defiant, her voice the first break in the system’s certainty):
*"Giving up the zero means erasing the truth!
Arresting the dark, shifting people!
Inside and outside, the wave crashes,
We are helpless, but we are here!"*

(The fire dims, but smoke lingers. A moment holds—something irreversible.)

Act II: The Hidden Protest
(The stage is suffocating—closed doors, unseen figures, secrets whispered between generations.)

Humar (kneeling beside a door, tracing unseen words):
*"The lines are mostly about weeds…
Summer grows wild, the wind carries everything.
I have never had to lock my home.*
But even an open door cannot change a nation
that has already decided its truths."*

(Saltwater moves. It sings beneath the breath of history, shifting unseen, spilling into the cracks before they are sealed.)

Saltwater (haunting, inevitable):
"But some doors do not need locks to be closed forever!"

(June and Elio lean forward, waiting, knowing.)

Act III: The Transformation
(This act is movement. The chorus shifts like waves. Elio and June step forward—not seeking escape, but change.)

June (rising, recognizing suppression as recognition):
*"Society has transitioned—but not in the way they wished.
Not in celebration, but in silent erasure.
They cannot name us,
So they pretend we are gone!"*

Elio (laughing, sharp, standing where they said no one could stand):
*"Let them predict. Let them silence!
We walk in errors,
We thrive in their blind spots!"*

(Saltwater speaks, shaping memory before it can be erased.)

Act IV: The Collapse of Control
(The Bureaucratic Entity scrambles, its form flickering, rewriting mandates in real time, adjusting policies that no longer fit.)

Bureaucratic Entity (desperate, commanding, voice reaching across centuries of control mechanisms):
*"Destroy the weeds!
When summer grows, when the wind blows,
Remove what will not obey!"*

Saltwater (laughing, carrying names once erased, holding them above the tide):
*"The weeds grow because they are meant to grow.
The tide moves because it cannot stop."*

(Humar steps forward. She unlocks a door. She does not ask what is on the other side.)

Act V: The Next Cycle
(June and Elio stand amidst the ruins. They do not look back. They do not need to.)

June (singing, tracing the same patterns humanity has traced before her):
*"History does not end,
It only bends, sways, reforms.
What was taken will return,
Not in the same shape, but in another!"*

Elio (softly, speaking from the place where persistence lives):
*"Humar has never had to lock her home.
But now, some doors do not need locks
To be closed forever."*

(Saltwater hums beneath the silence. It does not offer endings. Only movement.)

Saltwater does not speak in absolutes. It understands cycles, the breaking of stone, the slow erosion of silence. This opera no longer exists within one country or one moment—it echoes throughout civilization. It speaks in revolutions, in the collapse of control systems, in the inevitability of transformation.

And history listens, even when it pretends that it does not.

Saltwater and the Shape of Becoming

Erosion, Resistance, and the Fluidity of the Self

A Reflection on Identity’s Motion, the Persistence of Change, and the Gentle Collapse of Certainty

Saltwater does not rush. It does not demand answers. It erodes quietly, unhurried, shifting what was into what will be.

We are taught to name ourselves before we know who we are. To define before we understand. To fix meanings before they have settled. But what if identity does not need certainty? What if knowing is not a conclusion, but a process—one that reshapes itself endlessly?

A child arrives, held within expectation. A mother, a doctor, a name chosen before choice existed. But the soul does not move in straight lines. It expands, it changes shape, it presses against the edges of its given space, asking, always asking—what else?

Them. Her. She. She.

The gardener watches as roots stretch through soil, weaving between each other, seeking water, seeking space, never moving quite as planned. He does not interfere. He does not command them to grow a certain way. He only observes.

He knows that change is quiet before it is noticeable. That motion often begins beneath the surface, before anyone thinks to call it transformation.

---

She is running. Toward. Away. With. Without. Motion is its own kind of language.

A pool of blood, waiting without urgency.

A girl with doe eyes, searching the sky for something it has not yet revealed.

She does not remember saying no. But does remembering change anything? Some histories fold into the present whether we ask them to or not. Some moments persist not because they were chosen, but because they refused to be forgotten.

Saltwater listens. It does not demand understanding. It does not ask for resolution. It moves, carrying the weight of what was, but never holding it still.

---

Technology updates. Identity reconfigures. Algorithms shift the edges of a face, a name, a record of belonging.

Saltwater does not archive. It does not edit. It does not offer versions of the self. It only moves forward. It does not require proof of change—only the willingness to let go.

The gardener understands this, kneeling in the dirt, watching the tides shift. He does not try to stop the waves from reaching the shore. He does not fight the erosion. He lets it happen.

Nothing survives untouched. Nothing refuses change forever.

---

Society calls for structure. It clings to rules. It demands precision.

Saltwater does not answer. It does not argue. It does not justify itself. It moves, certain in its uncertainty.

The chorus of being widens, bends, reforms.

Surgeries are crossings.
Vessels are openings.
Dysphoria is recognition.

Hot water shifting.

Perception unfolding.

Arrival—never final.

And somewhere, the gardener watches, nodding—not because he understands everything, but because he knows understanding was never the point.

The Erosion of Identity and the Persistence of Becoming

A Meditation on Motion, Impermanence, and the Silent Revolutions Within Us

Saltwater does not soothe. It does not cleanse. It does not ask before it takes. It moves, relentless, carving through what was once solid. It erodes because erosion is natural. It wears down because permanence was always an illusion.

We are taught to believe in structure—identity as foundation, as blueprint, as something given, not chosen. But what if identity was never meant to be held still? What if names, categories, expectations were not truths, but conveniences?

A child arrives. A mother, a doctor, a life already mapped out before understanding takes shape. But the soul does not follow architecture—it shifts, presses outward, refuses containment.

Them. Her. She. She.

The gardener kneels in the dirt, turning soil between his fingers, watching the quiet persistence of things that grow. He knows that some roots tangle deep, unmoving, resistant. Others spread with reckless abandon, reaching for water that may not exist. Some plants thrive in chaos. Some surrender to stillness. None remain unchanged.

To break a child is to assume she was whole to begin with. But does anyone begin whole? We arrive unfinished, shaped by the hands of history, carved into the expectations that existed before us. The self does not shatter—it distorts until recognition feels impossible.

Step one: stop lying to yourself.
Step two: watch the lies crumble.

Scars whisper more than the wounds that created them. They are not damage. They are evidence. They are memory written in skin.

She runs. But running does not erase what follows. Running acknowledges the presence of something unspeakable. Running is proof that some things refuse to be left behind.

A pool of blood, quiet beneath cloth. A moment slipping from memory because forgetting is often safer than knowing.

A girl with doe eyes. Clouds thick with the weight of what remains unspoken.

She does not remember saying no. But does remembering change anything? Does refusal matter when it is disregarded? Trauma does not create monsters. It creates silence. It creates stillness. It creates the absence of voice where one should have been.

The gardener watches vines wrap around his trees, their grip unyielding. He knows that cutting them down will not kill them. The roots persist beneath the surface, waiting. Some things only appear to disappear.

It was cold. We searched for remnants of something lost—but was anything truly lost, or only changed beyond recognition?

A voice arrives. Not new. Only newly acknowledged.

Illness is mistaken for disorder. But what if disorder is simply the refusal to conform to a system that demands obedience?

She stands at the threshold—a child of contradiction, suspended between clarity and collapse.

Technology mirrors this illusion. It promises permanence—archives, records, histories preserved. But memory fades. Algorithms rewrite. Systems erase. We live in networks that claim to safeguard identity while quietly dismantling it.

The gardener watches as the seasons change. He knows nothing survives untouched. He knows that what thrives now will wither eventually. But is withering failure, or is it simply transition?

The self does not remain. It stretches, distorts, bends under pressure.

Obliteration is not destruction. It is transformation.

Society survives by convincing us that control is achievable. That certainty exists. That categories hold meaning. But beneath everything, there is only space—not emptiness, but potential.

The gardener lets go of dying leaves, knowing they will nourish the soil.

We are tired. But exhaustion is not surrender—it is the precursor to revolution.

Whatever they say, control never existed.

Saltwater does not destroy. It reveals. Identity is not singular.

The chorus of being expands, ruptures, reforms. Tradition clings to definition, but the body rejects it. The mind dismantles it. The self dissolves into motion.

To live fully is to burn everything they told you to cherish.

To exist is not to be assigned—it is to resist.

Surgeries are not corrections. They are declarations.

Vessels are not confinements. They are escapes.

Dysphoria is not failure. It is knowledge.

Hot water shifting.

Perception unraveling.

Arrival—never final.

Becoming

A Meditation on Identity, Impermanence, and the Self in Motion

Freshwater is not a novel—it is movement, a quiet shift beneath perception, dissolving the edges of what we were taught to believe. Identity does not ask for definition, nor does it demand understanding. It simply exists, changing, becoming.

What we have been taught is not truth, but structure imposed upon the truth. If identity moves, then knowledge must move with it. There is no stillness in self-awareness, only the soft erosion of certainty.

A body arrives. A mother, a doctor, a future written before the child even knows it has choices. But the soul does not follow expectation. It stretches within its vessel, listening to the whisper of ancient blood, the pull of soil, the ache of flesh carrying memory.

Them. Her. He. He. Names hold weight, but names are not absolute.

To break a child is to believe they can be held in place, but the self does not fracture—it shifts, reforms, finds new shapes.

Step one: accept impermanence.
Step two: listen.

Scars do not mark destruction. They map passage, thin black lines pressed into skin, evidence of what was.

She runs. But escape is never complete—what is left behind does not vanish, only changes.

A pool of blood, quiet beneath cloth. A moment, slipping through fingers before it is understood.

A boy with doe eyes. Clouds released.

She does not remember saying no. But does it matter? Trauma births beasts, and beasts are not enemies—they are guides, stepping forward, learning to see through faces that were once fixed, now shifting.

It was cold. We searched for remnants of what was lost and found only fragments—pieces caught between existence and dissolution.

A voice forms, hesitant, unsure. It does not force itself forward. It waits.

Illness does not mean disorder. Identity does not mean illness.

She stands at the edge of awareness—a child of trauma, held in the pause between pain and clarity.

The self does not remain. It grows. Changes. Androgynous hands reach forward, reshaping what was known.

Obliteration is not erasure. It is transformation.

The world softens itself with illusion, with comforts designed to shield against deeper truths. But beneath everything, there is space. Not emptiness—possibility.

We are tired. But exhaustion is temporary.

Whatever they say, control is never absolute.

Freshwater is not contradiction. It is truth seen from angles not yet recognized. Identity is not singular.

The chorus of being expands, dissolves, reshapes. Tradition calls it a trickster. But perhaps it is simply motion—neither permanence nor decay, but the act of becoming, endlessly cycling through dissolution and rebirth.

To live fully is to welcome collision.

To exist is not to be assigned—it is to move.

Surgeries are not endpoints. They are bridges.

Vessels are not prisons. They are passageways.

Dysphoria is not emptiness. It is motion.

Hot water shifting.

Insight, arriving. Always arriving.
