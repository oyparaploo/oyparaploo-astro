Poem · We Garden · 3 minutes

Soil and Sky

Moonlit Garden Rabbit Reckoning

Pulse Beneath Surfaces

She moves through the garden, though it does not feel like hers anymore. The lavender sways, restless beneath the silver hush of the full moon, the scent of damp earth and rosemary thick in the air. She kneels, pressing her hands into the soil, feeling its slow exhale, its quiet pulse beneath her fingertips. The garden is alive, but it is watching.

They are watching.

The rabbits slip through the shadows, their movement barely perceptible—a flicker between fur and moonlight, between hunger and hesitation. They do not hurry, nor do they hesitate. They move like ghosts, their presence felt before their shapes emerge, their hunger older than their bodies.

She exhales, brushing dirt from her palms. She stands, surveying the damage—the parsley, the tender greens, the remains of what was once fennel. Everything grazed, everything reshaped, but never ruined. The rabbits do not destroy. They take only what is needed, only what time allows, only what the night will forgive.

The moon lingers, pooling in the dips of leaves, clinging to the edges of petals, turning soil into shadow and silver. It listens, impassive, watching the quiet rearrangement of the earth, the slow negotiation between hunger and growth.

She does not fight. She does not chase them away. She whispers to the night instead, knowing the garden will remember, knowing it will shift, knowing it will not be conquered, only persuaded.

The rabbits retreat into silence, into the waiting spaces between stone and vine, their hunger momentarily soothed, their movement slipping back into the quiet breath of the world.

Beneath the surface, beneath the hush of moonlight and the restless sway of lavender, the earth breathes in slow, deliberate pulses. It does not sleep. It shifts, hums, trembles with unseen movement, a quiet symphony of roots threading through loam, of fungi whispering between layers of decay, of creatures tunneling through the dark corridors of soil.

She presses her hands into the damp earth, feeling its slow exhale, its quiet pulse beneath her fingertips. The garden is alive, but it is watching.

They are watching.

The rabbits slip through the underground passages, their movement barely perceptible—a flicker between fur and shadow, between hunger and hesitation. They do not hurry, nor do they hesitate. They move like ghosts, their presence felt before their shapes emerge, their hunger older than their bodies.

The tunnels stretch deep, winding through the roots of ancient trees, through the forgotten bones of creatures long returned to the soil. The mycelium listens, weaving its silver threads through the earth, carrying messages between roots, between stones, between the quiet breath of the world.

She exhales, brushing dirt from her palms. She stands, surveying the damage—the gnawed roots, the hollowed stems, the remnants of what was once fennel. Everything grazed, everything reshaped, but never ruined. The rabbits do not destroy. They take only what is needed, only what time allows, only what the earth will forgive.

The soil shifts, folding into itself, absorbing the remnants of what has been taken, weaving ruin into renewal. The garden does not mourn. It remembers. It adapts.

The moon presses into the earth, sinking into the soil, urging it to speak. The garden hums in response, a low vibration that ripples through the tunnels, through the bones of the rabbits, through the breath of the woman who is no longer certain she is awake.

She does not fight. She does not chase them away. She whispers to the earth instead, knowing the garden will remember, knowing it will shift, knowing it will not be conquered, only persuaded.

The rabbits retreat into silence, into the waiting spaces between roots and stone, their hunger momentarily soothed, their movement slipping back into the quiet breath of the underground.
