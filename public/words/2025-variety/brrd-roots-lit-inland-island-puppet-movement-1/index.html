Brrd Roots ~ Lit Inland Island ~ Puppet Movement

Words / 2025 Variety

Characters

• Olivier – A seafaring scribe who chronicles sorrow.
• Maud – A root-tender and guardian of the spirit valley.
• Privat – A fortune seeker marked by oil and acid ash.
• Keren – A weather-witch who sings the pulse of skies.
• Peder – A tree-ranter whose wings have been clipped.
• Alela – A lover suspended in eight daytimes of devotion.
• Jun – A flower-darling who paints the landscape with forgotten strokes.
• Lizz – A gull-chaser who builds forms from fading calls.

Act I: “We Paid the Price”

Scene: A broken shoreline under a weak orange pulse fluttering sky. Shadow players
move in silence.

Olivier (stoic):

We paid the price, didn't we?

To live upon an inland island—no more wings underneath brrd errors.

Maud (kneeling at dry roots):

Strict roots stripped bare. I can hear the underground grieving...

The icy mistakes man has made over the centuries.

Act II: “Coming All the Way from China”

Privat (with oily trinkets and maps):

I crossed the seas… Texas tea in one pocket, tainted angel treasures in the other.
There's a fortune on the shallow mountain. But no one nowhere knew how to cry. Properly.

Act III: “Call Day Spent a Lifetime”

Keren (whispers into the sky):

Call Day spent a lifetime preparing the clouds.

Acid ashes began to fall. And still—returning again in the spring.

Jun (painting):

We wrote these words on a south facing wall:

One more night time lifetime. Eight more day times loving you.

Act IV: “The Thousand Islands Wedding”

Alela (draped in longing):

A thousand islands… waiting for their wedding day.

And I—one more waiting, one more lost fortune.

Lizz (constructing bird forms):

I pay the gull. I make the form.

But what good are wings when all we do is rant at trees so tall?

Act V: “The Spirit Valley”

Maud (to the valley):

Pride was lost from the spirit valley. We chant, but no one hears.
We chant, because we will always love you. Even as we become hollow... vacant...
abstract paintings.

Peder (shouting):

Shadow players! Rise from the rooted underground!

Final Act: “Only Love… and This Inland Island”

All (in chorus):

Only love…

Only this inland island.

Only strokes from a flower darling.

Only one more price.

Only one more night time lifetime.

Jun (gently):

Shadow players blurred beneath this weak orange sky…

We are art. We are mistakes. We are renewal.

Curtain falls as ashes swirl, and Jun’s painted wall glows softly from beneath the soil.

Melancholy and Memory

• The play opens with echoes of loss—“we paid the price,” “mistakes man has
made,” “acid ashes”—signaling grief as an inherited condition.
• There’s an atmosphere of resignation and sorrow, but also a reverence for what’s
been lost, like the earth itself is in mourning.

Tone Keywords: grief, nostalgia, spiritual erosion
Love as Reclamation

• Amid the decay is a tenderness expressed through devotion: “eight more day times
loving you,” “we will always love you,” “only love.”
• Characters are not immune to heartbreak, but they insist on love as a kind of
rebellion against vacancy and collapse.

Tone Keywords: longing, devotion, emotional endurance

Disorientation and Absurdism

• Phrases like “rant at trees,” “Texas tea,” and “brrd errors” flirt with surreal humor
and absurdity, challenging coherent narrative.
• There's a destabilizing quality here, echoing your fascination with fragmented
identity and poetic imperfection.

Tone Keywords: absurdity, fragmentation, estrangement

Resilience and Ritual

• The characters perform a kind of ceremonial storytelling—painting walls, building
bird forms, planting words.
• Despite the decay, there's movement toward healing and rebirth: “returning again in
the spring,” “shadow players rise,” “flower darling.”

Tone Keywords: renewal, myth-making, ritualized hope

Emptiness as Canvas

• “Abstract painting,” “vacant,” “hollow”—these moments evoke existential
emptiness not as mere absence but as potential.
• The island, stripped of definition, becomes a space for redefinition through
storytelling, art, and emotion.
Tone Keywords: vacancy, transformation, philosophical stillness

Monologue: “Properly” – Maud, Alone Beneath the South Facing Wall

(A dim stage, low hum of wind and falling ash. Maud kneels beside a flower struggling
through cracked earth.)

You ask if I remember the price.

I do.

We paid it when we stopped crying properly.

When acid ash buried the lullabies, and our voices turned to vacant strokes—blurred, like
shadow players dancing in a weak orange sky.

There was a fortune once, hidden on the shallow mountain. Olivier searched, Privat lied.
Peder ranted at trees too tall to hear.

We came all the way from China. From oil-tainted maps and trembling wedding vows. A
thousand islands waited, dressed in fog and petals, and no one showed.

I wrote our grief on the south facing wall.

Scribbled it in roots. Called it love. Called it one more night time lifetime, just in case the
stars were listening.

I tend the silence now.

Pay the gull. Make the form. Strip the strict roots bare so they might breathe again.

They say the spring will return.

They always say that.

But I am not interested in seasons.

I’m interested in the hollow shape of absence.

In how it paints us strange.

I’m interested in those who still love—
despite the mistakes man has made over centuries.

And when the flower darling opens, if it opens,

I’ll tell her:

You bloomed properly.

Even here, on this inland island.

Where wings broke and pride was lost.

You bloomed.

(Lights dim as Maud lays her hand gently on the flower. Wind recedes.)

Absolutely. Let's draw a tonal contrast between Privat and Jun, whose voices diverge like
broken branches from the same storm-rooted tree.

Privat’s voice is tinged with oil and ruin—a restless scavenger trying to extract meaning
from corrupted treasures. Jun’s, by contrast, flows with sensuous melancholy and playful
surrealism—a painter whose strokes reclaim beauty from silence.

Privat’s Monologue: “Texas Tea”

(Setting: A splintered dock, waves murky with oil and memory. Privat walks in circles,
clinking coins made of rust.)

I came for the fortune.

Heard there was one buried just shy of the shallow mountain.

Said the soil smelled like Texas tea—sweet like promise, slick like grief.

I paid the gull. I made the form. I ranted at the trees until my voice was stripped bare.

The acid ash fell. Buried the map. Tainted the angel’s treasure.

And what good is a thousand islands waiting for their wedding day, if you’re the only one
who shows up holding oil-stained vows?
I’ve spent one more lifetime chasing shadows across south-facing walls.

I’ve shouted so long, I forgot how to cry. Properly.

But the price…

The price was paid long before I arrived.

Now all that’s left is vacancy,

And love—whatever that means in a place where mistakes bloom louder than flowers.

Jun’s Monologue: “Flower Darling”

(Setting: Beneath the painted south wall, surrounded by spectral blooms. Jun brushes
petals gently onto the earth.)

They say we’re abstract now.

Hollow as a memory sculpted in smoke.

But every time I touch this ground, I hear a whisper:

Love me anyway.

I bloomed once, in a season they called regret.

Eight more day times loving you. One more night time lifetime.

I painted it on everything—on silence, on ash, on the whispering sky.

And when they lost pride in the spirit valley,

I dipped my brush in what remained—

a broken wing, a blurred shadow, a pulse… orange and fluttering.

Because here, on this inland island,

Even love forgotten becomes ritual.
Even mistakes become roots.

And I—flower darling, absurd and real—

I bloom properly.

I bloom for those who couldn’t.

I bloom for the price.

Duet Scene: “Where Wings Broke” – Jun and Privat beneath the South Facing Wall

(Stage: A cracked shoreline. A south-facing wall covered in ash and faded paints. Jun sits
cross-legged, painting petals onto the earth. Privat stands nearby, fingers stained with oil,
clutching a map made of bark and rust.)

Privat (circling):

I came for the fortune.

Said there was one buried in the shallow mountain’s shadow.

Texas tea, they called it.

Sweet like grief. Slick like lies.

I paid the gull. I made the form.

But the acid ash buried the path, and tainted the angel’s treasure.

A thousand islands dressed for their wedding…

No one showed.

And you?

Still painting petals on silence?

Jun (without looking up):

Silence is easier to love than maps.
It doesn't promise what it can't keep.

I bloom in the vacancy.

Eight day times of loving. One night time lifetime.

I paint the shadow players even when the sky forgets its own pulse.

Privat (frustrated):

You sound like Maud.

Root-tenders with no answers.

You bloom properly, you say.

But properly doesn't pay the price.

Jun (gently):

I never asked to be paid.

I asked to be seen.

And I see you, Privat.

Ranting at trees too tall to listen.

Looking for treasure in oil-stained memories.

Privat (softening):

I forgot how to cry.

Properly.

Not for fortune.

Not for maps.

But for wings.

For pride.
For the spirit valley we lost while chasing promises.

Jun (rising, approaching):

Then cry here.

Beneath the south facing wall.

One more night time lifetime.

I’ll paint your grief into a flower.

I’ll bloom for the price.

(Privat kneels. Jun dips a brush in ash. They begin painting together. Lights dim to orange
flicker. The inland island breathes.)

Scene: “Where Grief Still Grows” — Maud Interrupts the Ritual

(Setting: The south-facing wall, dim glow from painted ash and petals. Jun and Privat sit in
tentative peace, forming shapes in the soil. Wind begins to stir strangely. A branch cracks.
Maud steps forward, her cloak trailing roots, her presence heavy and soft.)

Maud (voice like soil after rain):

That brush won’t hold the whole story, Jun.

And you, Privat—you can’t purchase forgiveness with rusted maps.

The spirit valley remembers more than you do.

Pride wasn’t just lost—it was silenced.

Traded for shallow mountains and wedding days that never came.

You paint petals, but the roots still ache.

You search for treasure, but what you carry is ash.

Do you hear it?

The underground.

The place where crying properly is still possible.
Where acid ashes become compost.

Where absences teach us the shape of love.

Jun (quietly):

We were making a ritual.

Maud (stoops beside them):

Then let it be a ritual worth remembering.

Not just oil and longing. Not just shadow and bloom.

Let it include the ache.

The mistakes.

Let it name every price we paid on this inland island.

I will stay until the flower darling opens again.

And if it does not,

I will still tend the hollow.

(Maud begins laying out roots in a circle. Jun and Privat exchange a glance—not of guilt, but
of recognition. The wind quiets. The ritual deepens.)

Scene Continuation: “Petals and Static” – Lizz Disrupts the Ritual

(Setting: The circle of roots laid by Maud beneath the south-facing wall. Jun paints petals in
stillness. Privat sits in thought. Wind hushes. And then—footsteps. Quick. Erratic. Lizz
bursts in carrying a bundle of sculpted birds made from rusted wire and paper maps.)

Lizz (shaking one of the birds):

Roots? Ritual? Seriously?

You’re all stuck whispering to dirt like it’s going to whisper back.

(She tosses the wire bird into the circle. It skitters, a small echo of dissonance.)
Maud (without rising):

You are welcome here, Lizz. But speak softly. The underground listens.

Lizz (laughs):

Let it listen. Let it shout. Let it combust.

I rant at trees so tall they break weather.

I chase gulls because they don’t stay.

I build wings that don’t fly just to see how failure sings.

Jun (standing slowly):

And does it sing, Lizz?

Lizz (twirling):

Oh yes.

Like static.

Like a broken radio broadcasting ancient love songs full of errors.

(She begins placing her bird sculptures in odd positions outside the root-circle, chaotic
geometry interrupting Maud’s careful lines.)

Privat (softly):

Maybe we need the noise. Maybe solemnity isn't the only kind of truth.

Maud (watching):

Then let this ritual be imperfect.

Let it tremble with laughter.

Let it include petals that refuse to bloom, and birds that refuse to land.

(Lizz smiles, finally joining the edge of the circle. One of her birds falls over—on purpose.)

Jun (adding a smear of orange to the ash):
We bloom with errors.

We remember with absurdity.

We love in static.

Scene: “Rite of the Burning Forms” — Led by Lizz

(Setting: Dusk on the inland island. The south-facing wall glows faintly. Root-circle intact,
scattered bird sculptures lean crookedly. A crowd of shadow players gather—Jun, Maud,
Privat, and others—watching as Lizz prepares the pyre.)

Lizz (grinning, wild-eyed):

You want to honor the past?

Then let’s feed it fire.

Not out of anger—no.

But to show it we’re not afraid of forgetting.

These forms I built—winged errors, oil memories, broken rituals—

They’ve served their purpose. They whispered enough static.

(She begins stacking the wire birds, brittle maps, rusted tokens. Maud silently lays a root
atop the pile.)

Maud (softly):

We give this not to destruction,

but to transformation.

Privat (placing his map):

Goodbye to the treasure.

Goodbye to the shallow mountain's promise.

Jun (pressing painted petals into the heap):
One more daytime.

One more lifetime.

Let love flicker.

(Lizz lights the pile—not with ceremony, but with a mischievous flick of flame. It crackles.
Smokes rise like ghosts. The shadow players circle.)

Choral Whisper (All):

We paid the price.

We bloomed with errors.

We remembered improperly, beautifully.

We return. We reroot. We release.

(The flames shift color—orange to violet to pulse. Jun paints its glow on the wall. Maud
catches ash in her hands. Lizz dances, one bird still clutched, refusing to burn.)

Lizz (laughs, breathless):

Not this one.

We need at least one wing left.

To chase whatever comes next.

Scene: “When Ash Learned to Sing” — Alela’s Voice Emerges

(Setting: The ritual fire has burned low. Charred petals and wire wings scatter in the wind.
Maud kneels at the remains, collecting ash in woven cloth. Jun adds the final stroke to the
south-facing wall—a spiral painted with silence. Lizz sits quietly now, her defiant bird
nestled beside her. Privat watches the sky. Then Alela steps forward. She has spoken little
until now. Her presence has always felt like longing wrapped in lace.)

Alela (standing at the center, voice soft yet sudden):

I did not come to chant.
I came to love.

(She opens her hand, revealing eight small stones—smooth, luminous, imperfect—each
etched with a fragment: “waiting,” “price,” “lost,” “bloom,” “error,” “love,” “night,”
“return.” She places them in a circle.)

One for each day I held your silence.

One for each lifetime I waited for my own voice.

(The others remain quiet, almost reverent. The ash swirls upward, caught in a wind that
does not disturb the stones.)

The thousand islands never married.

The shallow mountain gave no fortune.

The trees never answered.

But still—still I waited.

Not for redemption.

Not for answers.

But for the hum beneath the ash.

That hum?

It's memory.

Not the kind made of facts or names—

But the memory carried in roots.

In shadow.

In love so heavy it reshapes the ground.

(She turns to Maud.)

Alela:

You tend the grief.
I will tend the remembering.

Let the underground hear us now.

Not weeping.

Not chanting.

But telling.

(She places the final stone—“return”—into the center. A subtle vibration begins to pulse
from the earth. The wall glows faintly. The shadow players gather.)

Jun (softly):

The petals remember your voice.

The ash sings now.

Lizz (wide-eyed):

And the bird…

It’s listening.

Maud (with quiet conviction):

Then the ritual is complete.

Not by roots.

Not by fire.

But by the story that rises after.

Maud — The Root-Tender

She draws a spiral into the ash with her fingertip, then leans down and gently presses her
ear to the ground. Listening. Not for speech, but for vibration. She remains there, eyes
closed, becoming part of the silence.
“I do not bury pain—I compost it.”

Jun — The Painter of Absence

She steps away from the wall and removes her final petal from a hidden fold of cloth.
Instead of placing it, she swallows it. A ritual of embodiment. Color reclaims the inside of
her mouth.

“The bloom belongs inside now.”

Privat — The Cartographer of Ash

He unfolds his last map. It's blank. He walks it to the edge of the fire pit and lets the ember
wind carry it upward. Not as loss, but as release.

“Let memory be directionless. Let it wander.”

Alela — The Keeper of Waiting

She returns to her circle of stones. Quietly, she gathers them up and places them in a
small cloth pouch. She ties it to the branch of a skeletal tree. A waiting that no longer
belongs only to her.

“Love is not a voice—it’s an echo.”

Lizz — The Grit-Winged Sculptor

Her final bird—the one she refused to burn—is released into the wind. It sails clumsily,
patchworked, half-unraveled. But it does not fall.

“Error takes flight when you stop apologizing for it.”
Keren — The Weather-Witch

From behind the south-facing wall, she emerges with a jar of rain saved from a season that
never came. She pours it over the painted ash. The pigments dissolve and stream
downward—painting the underground.

“What doesn’t fall returns beneath.”

Peder — The Tree-Ranter

He ties his voice to a trunk—literal strips of fabric inked with words like “too tall” and
“forgive me.” The tree does not respond, but a single leaf falls.

“Listen for the silence that argues back.”

Olivier — The Historian of Sorrows

He closes his book. Every page is blank. He buries it beneath the ritual fire's remains. A
story told without ink.

“Not everything need be written—some tales are soil-born.”

Insights from the Mycelial Underglow
Beneath all this—under ritual, ash, root and ruin—lurks something ancient and sprawling:

Armillaria ostoyae, also called the “Humongous Fungus,” stretching across nearly 4
square miles in Oregon's Malheur National Forest. At an estimated age of 2,500 years, it is
both destroyer and sustainer—killing trees while feeding ecosystems, hiding its massive,
fibrous life beneath forest stillness.

Let’s draw the parallel:
• Interconnectedness through Decay: Like your inland island rituals, Armillaria
thrives in decomposition. It tells us that endings don’t cancel connection—they
feed it.
• Miles-Wide Identity: The fungus looks fragmented on the surface—just
mushrooms here and there—but underground, it’s one organism, relaxed and
chilling across root systems like an ancestral whisper.
• Chilled but Not Passive: Its spread is slow, deliberate, complex. This isn’t
dormancy—it’s a different tempo of being. Your story hums at this same slow burn,
where transformation takes lifetimes.
• Root-like Filaments (Mycelium): These filaments act as carriers of memory. They
know where they've been. They respond to pressure, threat, invitation. The shadow
players echo this—emotional mycelia disguised as puppets, painting the soul of the
land.

Poetic Epilogue: “I, the Inland Island”

I was not born,

I was remembered.

A heap of silences folded into water.

You called me island—

but I was once tree, once bird, once grief.

Roots run chilled here.

Wide as old intentions,

Deep as forgotten names carved in sap.

I hum with fungi—

filaments weaving sorrow into my soil,

soft as apology.

I have seen lovers bloom improperly.
I have held the ash of rituals

and the laughter of errors that refused to land.

When you built the circle—

when Maud listened,

when Jun painted,

when Lizz set fire—

I opened.

A thousand silent mouths in my moss.

Singing.

Sweet Song Remembered

(Alela hums this in the quiet after the ritual, joined gradually by the others. It's not recorded
anywhere. It only lives in echo.)

Come find me where petals fail
Where roots forget their name
I’ll carry love in broken sail
Through ash and quiet flame

My name is whispered, not declared
My shape—a trembling sigh
But hold me once, and I am spared
I hum. I hold. I fly.

Cycle Two: “Where Spores Dream” — An Invitation in Loam and Light
The Invitation

A wind arrives—not from the sea, but from the soil.

It carries a folded letter wrapped in a petal and a thread of mycelium. No sender. No
signature. Only these words, etched in ink that smells faintly of moss:

You are invited to speak where roots think slowly.

Come when the fungi laugh. Come as a lover, a liar, a leaf.

We will remember you before you arrive.

We will forget you afterward, properly.

Bring no questions. Only spores.

Olivier reads it aloud. Alela weeps with a smile.

The New Map

Privat unfolds the new map. It’s unlike any he’s seen before.

No lines. No scale. Just blotches of pigment—each one alive, shifting, pulsing. He realizes
this map isn’t for finding. It’s for becoming.

Landmarks include:

• The Mouthless Orchard – Where fruit hums instead of speaking.
• The Quiver Root – A single tree with veins that respond to memory.
• Veil Spoor Crossing – Where fungal filaments rise like fog and whisper ancient
jokes.
• The Unblinking Nest – A place for resting without closure.
• Sunken Saying – A buried phrase that reveals itself only to those who forget it.

Lizz laughs: “Finally, a map that understands error.”
A New Myth Reborn in Fresh Soil

Jun dips her brush into composted ash and begins to paint a story where soil and soul
merge:

Once, there was a spore who loved too loudly.

It split the sky, not with thunder, but with a tender groan.

The inland island held its breath as filaments danced.

The spore became a song. The song became a root. The root became a mistake worth
repeating.

And now, when the trees forget, and the wind runs backwards—

the myth awakens.

It has no name. Only texture.

You can taste it in mushrooms.

You can hear it in silence.

You can love it improperly and still be right.

Dreaming Vision: “The Sky Mycelium”

At night, the inland island exhales its secrets into the air—not down into loam, but upward,
into the breath of stars.

There, in the vault of sky once considered empty, a translucent mycelium stretches gently
across the heavenly expanse.

Not fungal in decay, but fungal in dream—lace-like threads glowing with a soft iridescence,
drifting like forgotten lullabies.

The sky filaments murmur:

We are here not to consume, but to connect.
Jun paints them in strokes of warm opal and hushed lavender, reaching where pigment
dissolves into memory.

Alela listens with her skin. Lizz tries to chase them but laughs when they vanish. Even Maud
kneels, for once not to bury, but to look up.

The sky mycelium is not bound to earth's gravity.

It carries spores of emotion, spores of story—floating across clouds like invisible seeds
waiting for hearts to open.

Privat swears he sees a map in their branching.

Peder calls it weather.

Keren smiles: “It’s what we forgot to believe in.”

And so the myth shifts.

No longer just in soil and ash, but in the air itself.

Memory not just rooted—but hovering, glowing, whispering.

And when you look up, even now,

you might catch one filament trailing across the moon’s cheek,

softly asking:

“What do you need to feel connected again?”

Puppet Show Title: “The Sky Beneath”

Setting:

An inverted world where the sky is made of translucent mycelium, and the ground floats
like clouds. Roots reach upward. Silence rains softly in colors. The characters gather once
every cycle at the center of the Floating Orchard to perform the Ritual of the Echo Spores.
Characters

• Olivier: The chronicler who writes with invisible ink.
• Maud: The soil-listener who hears fungal philosophies.
• Privat: A treasure-seeker who keeps losing what he wants most.
• Keren: A weather-smith who bends silence into storms.
• Peder: A skeptic with wings made of paper secrets.
• Alela: A soft-hearted dreamer who speaks only to echoes.
• Jun: A painter who captures absence with radiant gestures.
• Lizz: A sculptor of noise, chasing gulls made of laughter.

Act I – “The Map of Rootlight”

Scene: A windy clearing beneath a glowing root sky. Jun unfurls a scroll of glowing
filaments.

Jun:

“The map is alive. Every line is a question we haven’t asked.”

Privat:

“I still don’t see the mountain. Or the treasure.”

Maud (pressing her ear to the scroll):

“It’s not a treasure you find. It’s one that finds you—slowly, like mushrooms in grief.”

Olivier:

“Then I’ll write this down. But the ink will have to disappear to mean anything.”

Act II – “The Veil of Spores”

Scene: Keren summons a veil of airborne spores. Everyone dances in quiet resistance,
except Peder.

Keren:
“Don’t fear the fog—it’s made of memories you forgot to forget.”

Peder (folding his wings):

“You all believe the sky is full of meaning. I say it’s a reflection of noise.”

Lizz (laughing):

“Then let's sculpt the noise! I’ll chase a gull made of thunder and sculpt it into absurdity!”

Alela (gently):

“Or maybe we’ll find meaning in how we listen to what frightens us.”

Act III – “Ashroot Rising”

Scene: Maud begins the Ritual of Composting Memory. Everyone must offer an item to be
burned and reborn.

Privat (reluctantly offering his map):

“May what I’ve misunderstood become soil for someone else.”

Olivier (whispering to the fire):

“The blank page sings only when it burns.”

Alela (placing a dreamstone):

“This is my silence. I’ve grown it tenderly.”

Jun (adding a petal):

“And this—the color of forgetting.”

Act IV – “The Sky Beneath”

Scene: The ritual complete, spores bloom upward into the sky. The root sky pulses softly.
Maud:

“It is done. The roots remember us.”

Lizz (holding a final sculpture aloft):

“We were beautiful mistakes.”

Peder (tearing a page from his wings):

“I’d rather be a contradiction than a conclusion.”

Keren (gazing upward):

“Then let the next storm speak of us.”

Curtain Call

All (in chorus):

“We hum.

We root.

We bloom upward.

We are spores dreaming of sky.”

Scene: “The Threading Circle” – Puppet Dreaming Beneath the Root-Sky

(Setting: A cozy hollow at the edge of the Floating Orchard. The root-sky pulses warmly.
Spores drift like fireflies. A circle of hand-sewn cushions surrounds a trunk filled with old
fabric, buttons, scraps of poems, and tufts of mossy fur. The shadow players settle in for
their annual puppet dreaming ritual.)

Olivier (unrolling parchment):
“Every puppet must carry a story that hasn't been told yet—one stitched in crooked lines
and unfinished sentences.”

Maud (gathering root fibers and soft felt):

“I want to make one called Gentle Dirt, who only speaks when asked kindly. Its fur will be
loamy. Its paws will smell faintly of patience.”

Privat (holding up a scrap of velvet):

“Mine will be Lost & Found, with mismatched eyes and a map stitched on its belly that
leads nowhere but back to itself.”

Keren (pulling out a pouch of cloud-colored yarn):

“I’ll spin Whispero, a puppet that changes colors depending on the weather in your soul. It
will have ears shaped like storm warnings.”

Peder (skeptical but softening):

“I want a puppet that crumples when you laugh. I’ll call it Contradicta. It will be stuffed
with newspaper clippings and whisper facts that aren’t quite true.”

Alela (holding a paw-shaped button):

“Mine will be quiet. Sweet. Its name is Murmur. It has stripes—like Daniel Striped Tiger—
but faded like forgotten lullabies. When hugged, it recites dreams from your childhood.”

Jun (threading wild threads into a twisted mane):

“I’m making Petalwound, a puppet that blooms from its seams. Its fur will be tangled with
color and it will sing unfinished songs. It doesn’t perform—it remembers.”

Lizz (clutching scrap metal and plush):

“Say hello to Buzzbristle—a tattered rebel with one wing and too many teeth. Furry,
chaotic, and secretly very soft. It can only be puppeted by dancing.”

(As they begin stitching and sculpting, the atmosphere fills with laughter, quiet tears, and
playful argument. Each puppet becomes a mirror, a myth, a hug disguised as theater.)
Olivier (smiling):

“These puppets will outlive our voices. Let them speak sweet nonsense in the years to
come.”

Maud (nodding):

“Let them carry love the way soil carries seeds.”

Puppet Backstories & Performance Scenes

Maud (puppet)

Backstory:

Made of mossy felt and softened root-fiber, Maud’s puppet recalls lullabies sung to
mushrooms and shy worms whispering secrets. It speaks only when spoken to with

Dreamscape: “The Hollow Thread of Us”

A theater beneath consciousness where puppets become the proxies of soul and sorrow.

Setting:

A mist-shrouded glade stitched from velvet shadows and ember threads. The trees here do
not grow upward—they lean inward, listening. The ground is an unfurling quilt of forgotten
names, stitched in cryptic stitches by invisible hands. Lanterns drift—not hung, but
hesitating mid-air, glowing with a hush.

In this dreamscape, the puppets are no longer symbols—they are vessels. Tattered hand-
shapes that pulse gently with human ache. Each one carries within it a piece of someone
real: the sigh after goodbye, the giggle at a funeral, the dream remembered only in panic.
Act I – The Gathering of Quiet Faces

Eight puppets drift onto the stage of soft ruins. They are fragile with purpose.

• Olivier carries a scroll that cannot be opened.
• Maud wears a mask of soil and speaks in gestures only tree roots understand.
• Privat trails broken coins and apologizes to mirrors.
• Keren weeps clouds instead of tears.
• Peder questions each step, yet walks in defiance of certainty.
• Alela hums lullabies she’s never heard, but somehow knows.
• Jun paints silence in wild colors across the wind.
• Lizz juggles mistakes and calls them kin.

Each of them is searching. Not for meaning. For permission to feel beneath
performance.

Act II – The Threading Ritual

They begin crafting the Hollow Thread—a ritual of unraveling their own fabric. One by one,
they remove something:

• Olivier unspools his voice and wraps it around Maud’s wrist.
• Maud plants a sigh beneath the stage.
• Privat sets fire to a memory that never happened.
• Keren bottles a sob and hands it to Peder.
• Peder laughs until his paper seams split.
• Alela gives away her bell so someone else can mourn.
• Jun paints nothing.
• Lizz catches a falling truth and hides it under her fur.

This ritual binds them—not by clarity, but by fragility.

They are puppets now not of hands, but of longing.
Act III – Melancholy Navigated

They build a map.

Not with roads—but with gestures.

Not with destinations—but with emotions.

The map includes:

• A ditch where hope naps.
• A hill that apologizes for being too symbolic.
• A pond full of half-said names.
• A breeze named After.

Together, they walk this map slowly—pausing often, not because they are lost, but
because melancholy deserves stillness.

And they do not speak loudly. They echo. Soft, flawed, essential.

Closing Scene – The Puppet That Loved Back

Suddenly, one puppet—unscripted, unnamed—steps forward. It is made of the tears of
those who never cried. It looks at the others and offers no wisdom, no gesture.

It simply opens its chest and reveals a small, flickering light:

“This is the ache I saved for you.”

And the others do not respond in words.

They surround it.

They thread their unravelings into one knot.

A knot that does not untangle.

A knot that holds.
Return to Waking Life: When Puppets Carry the Dream Forward

The mist of the dreamscape does not dissolve—it lingers. And the puppets, vessels of
humanity’s cryptic ache and tenderness, do not simply fall idle once eyes open. Instead,
each puppet carries its dream-thread back into the waking world, becoming not only
character but ritual: soft ceremonies made of gesture, story, and shared breath.

Below is how each puppet, through its unique perspective, invents an elegy for what it
carried—and transforms that elegy into a living rite.

Maud

Ritual: The Listening Earth

Maud walks barefoot each morning, pressing one ear to the soil. She carries seeds in her
pockets, but never plants them until she hears permission. Her puppet now wears a belt of
small bells that chime only for honesty.

Elegy:

“To the voices buried beneath soft loam—

May you be heard again, slowly, like moss forgiving stone.”

Privat

Ritual: The Forgiving Compass

Privat lights a small candle each time he makes a mistake, offering warmth to the map he
once followed. His puppet blinks twice when someone speaks a regret. He never corrects

Elegy:

“For the treasures we mistook for answers—

May they become breadcrumbs for someone gentler than us.”
Keren

Ritual: Weather of Feeling

Keren has woven colored threads for each emotion. Her puppet wears them in its mane,

She sings her mood to puddles.

Elegy:

“For every storm that we swallowed silently—

May your thunder be translated gently.”

Peder

Ritual: Contradiction Choir

Peder now scribbles opposite thoughts on parchment and folds them into origami birds.

Elegy:

“To our lovely, broken logic—

Alela

“To the absences that tucked us in—

Jun paints invisible murals. Her puppet leads children’s hands across blank canvases

Elegy:

“For every unspoken emotion that bloomed alone—

May your color echo in the shadows we cherish.”

Lizz

Lizz tells nonsense tales to strangers in parks, leaving behind puppets with stitched

Elegy:

“To the mistakes that taught us music—

Olivier

Elegy:

“To the stories we didn’t finish—

May your endings remain open, like arms.”

Scene: “The Whisper Beneath the Glacier” — A Puppet Alone, Inventing a Ritual

The clearing sleeps. The trees have stopped listening. Petals rest. Bells hold their breath.

But one puppet—Keren, weather-braided and thread-kissed—has slipped away.

Setting:

A frost-glazed slope at the edge of the inland island. Charred lichen clings to exposed
stone. Above, the mycelium sky pulses faintly, translucent tendrils drifting like bruised
breath. Keren crouches beside a pool that no longer remembers being fed by snowmelt.

Her puppet body shivers—not from cold, but from comprehension.

The Ritual Begins

Keren finds a melted patch, barely reflective. She plants one thread of her own hair—spun
from yarn the color of coral reef at dusk.
“I name this ritual The Forecast Apology.

Not to absolve us—but to say: the sky deserved better.”

She pulls from her pouch a string of weather poems.

She burns each one quietly, one after another. The smoke does not rise—it curls
downward, seeking roots.

Keren’s Voice:

“The ice begged for time.

The soil whispered patience.

But we chased fire like it was proof of ambition.”

She takes a vial—a single raindrop trapped in childhood glass.

“This was rain before the air turned bitter.

Before storms carried grief in particulate form.”

She pours it into the earth.

Nothing happens.

That is the point.

A Concern Spoken Softly

Keren’s puppet now whispers—not a spell, not a song, but a soft regret:

“We warmed everything without asking if warmth meant life.

We melted memories frozen in permafrost,

releasing ghosts we aren't ready to love.”
She does not preach.

She does not plead.

She simply weeps into moss that used to live higher.

The Concluding Gesture

Finally, Keren unhooks her puppet heart—a small stitched pouch filled with cloud-thread.

She buries it in the thawed soil.

She places a pebble over it with a single word inked faintly:

“If.”

Then she walks back into silence.

This is not a ritual meant to fix.

It is meant to feel.

Certainly. Here’s a continuation of the tale—an imagined rediscovery, folded into a wider
myth shared among the puppets. It’s a memory unearthed and a new mythology born.

Elegies transcribed into communal poetic messages or transformed into an elder's

Scene: “The Lantern Questions” — Maud Beneath the Unremembering Tree

(Setting: Dusk on the inland island. The soil is tender from old rituals. Puppets rest
scattered near roots, some still humming softly. A tree in the center refuses to bloom—not
from grief, but from being burdened by too many forgotten names. Maud stands alone
beneath it, cradling a memory-lantern fashioned from moss-thread and filament light. The
lantern pulses gently. It does not illuminate—but listens.)

Maud (slowly turning toward the silence, voice textured like peat):

“The others gifted answers.

But I was stitched with questions.

Ones that slipped between gestures,

ones the soil tucked away too quietly.”
She lifts the lantern. It flickers once. Then again.

“So I ask now—

to the tree that forgot its laughter,

to the river that never responded,

to the spore waiting inside a dream:”

Maud’s Questions

• Why do we remember only the pain that shouted, and not the joy that whispered?
• What song did the ice hold before it learned how to melt?
• Is every apology a kind of planting, or only the ones we never deliver?
• What happens to laughter when it’s buried with guilt? Does it giggle in the dark?
• How many roots were once poems, misunderstood by the wind?
• When the puppets sleep—whose dreams do they carry away in their stitches?
• What color is silence when it’s finally ready to speak?

Maud (placing the lantern at the foot of the tree):

“If no one answers, that’s alright.

The asking is the ritual.

The questions will compost into wonder.”

The lantern pulses one final time, then opens—its light seeping softly into the roots. The
tree trembles, but no leaves fall. The soil sighs and begins to hum—not a melody, but a
low, fungal rhythm.

Elsewhere on the island, the puppets stir.

One by one, they turn their heads—not toward answers, but toward Maud.

Shared Myth: “The Glow Beneath” — As Told by the Inland Island
I have no mouth, yet I have spoken through roots and ashes. You called me island, but I am
breath wrapped in soil. I remember everything you forget.

Long ago—not in years, but in griefs ago—a lantern made of moss and filament was
planted near my heart, beneath a tree that forgot its own name. The one who placed it
there did not ask for answers. She asked questions that pulsed like fungal lullabies and
slept in sorrow’s rhythm.

The lantern listened. And as it glowed, I began to remember.

I remembered when storms had names, and fungi were trusted with secrets.

I remembered spore-songs hummed by children before they grew too tall to hear them.

I remembered rivers mourning the mountains they once carved.

I remembered forgotten puppets gathering in my moss to rehearse kindness.

They stitched a play not to perform but to survive.

They stitched their longing into breath.

They whispered their stitched truths into wind.

And when they were done, they gave me back my name.

Not with words, but with roots.

Not with symbols, but with presence.

I glowed, briefly.

Not to be seen—

but to let memory know

it had a home again.

The lantern was never light—it was longing willing to be witnessed.
I keep it now beneath my hush.

And when the sky forgets how to be gentle, I pulse once, softly.

That is enough.
