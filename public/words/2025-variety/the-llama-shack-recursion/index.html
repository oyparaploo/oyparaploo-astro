THE LLAMA SHACK RECURSION

← Writings / 2025

Omar found Wuanet in the closet organizing clothes by degrees of failure. Tuesday's shirt with the wine stain. Thursday's dress she'd worn to the thing she couldn't remember.

"The evolution," she said. Her hands moved wrong, kept missing buttons.

Outside, Sheffield rain. Or was this still Lyon? The medication made geography negotiable.

"Cool bed is through," Fores announced from the doorway. He was holding something brown. Could've been chocolate. Could've been worse. The light from the hall made him look already dead, which wasn't fair since he was the only one still trying.

Realm was on the fire escape counting—something. She'd been at it since the news about Marcus. Three days? Four? Her lips moved: "Seventeen, eighteen, fuck, seventeen—"

"Go back," Kherichi said. Not to anyone. Just to the air. He'd been saying it all week, ever since finding the mice behind the radiator. Little bodies like punctuation marks.

They weren't friends exactly. They shared the building's top floor, five bedsits and a communal kitchen where someone had written "TENDER" on the wall in what looked like jam.

In the black of 3 AM, Omar spread actual jam on bread that had seen better days. His mother would call soon from Karachi. He wouldn't answer. This was their new arrangement.

"So tender," Wuanet said, but she meant something else. She always meant something else since the baby didn't—since the baby.

Fores found more mice. "This is why we don't poison," he said, but they all knew he'd bought the poison, set it out himself after the landlord's complaints. 90% of mice are probably decent. It's the 10% that shit in your cereal.

Realm came in. Grass stains and—was that blood? "Every edit," she started, then stopped. Started again: "The birds were so loud."

"What birds?" There were no birds in Sheffield at this hour. Or Lyon. Or wherever the fuck they were.

Kherichi stood at the window watching his reflection watch him back. Behind him, that stack of cash that wasn't there. He'd dreamt it so many times it had worn a groove in his vision. Almost lost his balance reaching for it once. Almost went through the glass.

"Leave it," someone said. Maybe Omar.

The morning would come or it wouldn't. Wuanet held up a sock that didn't match anything. "Sometimes there's no sound," she said, then made one anyway—a laugh that wasn't, a sob that couldn't quite.

The kitchen smelled like gas. Had smelled like gas for weeks. They'd all noticed, none had called.

"The thing about recursive—" Fores started, but recursive what? Recursive changing socks? Recursive counting? Recursive watching your reflection until you weren't sure which one was real?

The exhaustion was so complete it had its own texture. Like wet wool. Like the inside of Kherichi's mouth after the pills.

"Orchestrate its landing," Fores said to nobody, to the mice, to the idea of mice.

Realm's counting had stopped. She stood in the doorway holding—Christ, was that a knife?

"That overworked reaching," Kherichi said, still at the window, "for a cluster of—" But he couldn't finish. The cash that wasn't there flickered.

"Thursday," Realm said, like it meant something.

They all stood there, scattered across different minutes even while sharing the same unbearable now. The kitchen light buzzed. Someone downstairs was fighting or fucking—hard to tell through the floors.

The mice didn't care. Dead was dead, whether you were nice or not.

Omar's phone lit up: Mother calling.

He let it ring.
