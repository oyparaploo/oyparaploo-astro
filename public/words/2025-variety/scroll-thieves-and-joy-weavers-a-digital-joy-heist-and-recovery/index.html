SCROLL THIEVES and JOY WEAVERS…

Words / 2025 Variety

*SCROLL THIEVES and JOY WEAVERS ~ A Digital Joy Heist and Recovery
Les Subsistances, Lyon

---

PRE-SHOW PROTOCOL
Audiences surrender phones into "Joy Recovery Vault" - receive analog journal instead. Document:
- Hours spent scrolling today
- Last non-digital joy
- Current exhaustion level (1-10)

---

ACT ONE: The Theft

Stage: Five isolation pods with screens showing infinite scroll

IBRAHIM (ex-tech designer, now digital detective):
I designed the algorithm that stole your joy.
Variable ratio reinforcement. Intermittent rewards.
Shows code
See this line? That's your dopamine schedule.
Randomized just enough to keep you pulling.
Slot machine mathematics applied to human attention.

HARVEST:
How many people did you addict?

IBRAHIM:
Conservatively? Three hundred million.
I'm here to confess.
But also to explain. You need to understand the mechanism
before you can break it.

HARVEST (burnout specialist, visibly exhausted):
Three thousand clients this year.
All scrolling eight hours daily.
All wondering why they're empty.

SUNE:
Eight hours? That's a full workday.

HARVEST:
Exactly. They work eight hours, scroll eight hours,
sleep barely six. Where's the life?
Shows brain scans
Look—dopamine depletion here, cortisol elevation there.
They're literally paying to feel worse.

AROOJ (recovering scroll addict):
That was me. Ten hours daily.
Started with morning news check.
Five minutes became five hours.

IBRAHIM:
The first scroll is never just one.
We designed it that way.
"Just checking" is the gateway drug.

SUNE (joy researcher, surrounded by data):
The paradox: more information, less joy.
More connection, less presence.
More everything, less anything.
Projects statistics

SOFIA:
How many phone touches daily?

SUNE:
Average person: 2,617 daily phone touches.
Zero sunset observations.
We've traded sky for screen.

AROOJ:
Day 47 without infinite scroll.
My thumb still twitches.
Phantom phone syndrome—

HARVEST:
—feeling vibrations that aren't there.
I know. Most of my clients have it.

AROOJ:
But yesterday, I saw a bird.
Actually saw it.
Not through camera app. Not for Instagram.
Just... a bird being bird.

SOFIA (philosopher of unexpected joy):
I study joy in cancer wards, prisons, war zones.
Know what they have in common?

IBRAHIM:
Death proximity?

SOFIA:
No WiFi during peak joy moments.
When the diagnosis comes, phones disappear.
When the verdict's read, nobody scrolls.
Presence is mandatory when death is close.

HARVEST:
So we need near-death experiences to put phones down?

SOFIA:
Or we could choose presence before crisis forces it.

---

ACT TWO: The Paradox Experiment

Audience divided into two groups

IBRAHIM (to Group A):
You get your phones back. Ten minutes.
Find joy online. Real joy. I'll wait.

GROUP A MEMBER 1:
Finally! I have seventeen notifications.

GROUP A MEMBER 2:
My Instagram... wait, why does everyone's life look better?

IBRAHIM:
Keep scrolling. Joy must be in the next swipe.

GROUP A MEMBER 3:
This article about climate collapse...
I should read this. Stay informed.

IBRAHIM:
How's that joy search going?

GROUP A MEMBER 1:
I'm just... comparing vacation photos.
Theirs are better.

IBRAHIM:
Finding comparison instead? Outrage? FOMO?
That's not accident. That's design.
Every swipe calculated to keep you seeking,
never finding.

Meanwhile, with Group B

HARVEST (to Group B):
Close your eyes. No screens. Just presence.
Feel your heartbeat.

GROUP B MEMBER 1:
I can't remember the last time I did this.

HARVEST:
When did you last notice it without worry?
Without checking fitness tracker?
Guides breathing
This is free. Always available. No subscription.
No updates. No battery required.

GROUP B MEMBER 2:
It's... actually pleasant.

SUNE:
Your body's been broadcasting joy signals.
You've been too distracted to receive them.

After 10 minutes, groups reconvene

SUNE (showing biometric results):
Group A: Cortisol up 23%, attention fragmented,
joy unchanged despite "connecting" with hundreds.
Group B: Cortisol down 31%, presence increased,
joy registered from just breathing.
Same ten minutes. Different universes.

GROUP A MEMBER 1:
But I was informed! I read twelve articles!

AROOJ:
About things you can't change,
in places you'll never go,
involving people you'll never meet.
How did that help?

---

ACT THREE: The Guilt Trial

AROOJ:
I felt guilty being happy while Syria burned.
My family's homeland, destroyed.
So I scrolled through Syrian suffering.
Hours. Daily. Years.

SOFIA:
Did your scrolling help Syria?

AROOJ:
No. But I felt like I was doing something.
Witnessing. Not forgetting.

IBRAHIM:
That feeling? We programmed it.
"Engagement" metrics. You were engaged.
With advertisements between tragedies.

AROOJ:
You mean my guilt was... monetized?

IBRAHIM:
Every minute of it. Your paralyzed attention
sold to highest bidder.

HARVEST:
The sickest part? Scroll-induced exhaustion
prevents actual helping.

SUNE:
Studies show: people who limit news consumption
donate more, volunteer more, sustain activism longer.

SOFIA:
The guilt equation is rigged.
You think: suffering equals caring.
But joy creates energy—

AROOJ:
—energy enables action.

HARVEST:
Your exhaustion helps nobody.
Your joy might help everybody.

GROUP B MEMBER 2:
But isn't it selfish to be happy when—

SOFIA:
When what? When problems exist?
Problems always existed. Always will.
Should joy wait for perfect world?

IBRAHIM:
We programmed the guilt.
"Stay informed" means stay scrolling.
"Be aware" means be anxious.
We monetized your conscience.
Made you feel guilty for not consuming tragedy.

AROOJ:
So my misery was product, not compassion?

ALL:
Yes.

---

ACT FOUR: The Recovery

HARVEST:
First, recognize the theft.
Your attention was stolen, not given.
You're not weak. You were hacked.

AUDIENCE MEMBER:
How do we unhack?

SUNE:
Small analog joys. Starting now.
Distributes paper, crayons
Draw something stupid.

AUDIENCE MEMBER:
I haven't held a crayon in twenty years.

SUNE:
Feel the texture. The weight.
The slight resistance on paper.
Your hand remembers even if you don't.

AROOJ:
Third, the paradox practice.
Watch: I'm sad about the world—
my family's displacement, climate collapse, everything.
AND I'm happy about this crayon.
This specific blue. This waxy smell.

AUDIENCE MEMBER:
Both at once?

AROOJ:
Both at once. That's human.
Not either/or. Both/and.

SOFIA:
Fourth, unexpected territories.
Lights reveal beautiful installation made from discarded phones
Even digital waste can become art.
Joy finds a way.

AUDIENCE MEMBER:
Those are phones?

SOFIA:
Dead ones. Transformed.
Like you can transform.

IBRAHIM:
Fifth, the redesign.
Shows new app interface
What if algorithms promoted presence?

HARVEST:
Impossible. Presence doesn't profit.

IBRAHIM:
What if notifications celebrated non-usage?
"Congratulations! You haven't checked in three hours!"

SUNE:
What if we coded for joy instead of addiction?

IBRAHIM:
I'm trying. It's harder than stealing attention.
Attention grabs easily. Joy requires permission.

---

FINALE: The Analog Uprising

Audience phones returned but transformed—now only work for creation, not consumption

AUDIENCE MEMBER:
My phone... it won't scroll.

IBRAHIM:
Correct. Creation only. Photos out, not in.
Calls to humans, not to algorithms.

AUDIENCE MEMBER:
This is broken!

AROOJ:
No. It's fixed.

ALL:
Your scroll was stealing life minutes.
Converting presence into profit.
Transforming joy into anxiety.
But the theft is reversible.

IBRAHIM:
Every algorithm can be rewritten.
Including the one in your head.
The one saying "check phone."
That's just code. Bad code.

HARVEST:
Burnout isn't from doing too much.
It's from experiencing too little.
While consuming everything.

SUNE:
Joy doesn't need bandwidth.
Requires no updates.
Never buffers.
Always in stock.

AROOJ:
Withdrawal is temporary.
Presence is permanent.
Choose.

AUDIENCE MEMBER:
But how do I stay informed?

SOFIA:
About what? Things you can't change?
Or things within arm's reach?
The bird outside your window
needs attention more than distant disasters.

IBRAHIM:
The unexpected place where joy thrives?

ALL:
The space between scrolls.
The pause between notifications.
The life between updates.

AUDIENCE MEMBER:
I'm scared to disconnect.

AROOJ:
I was too. Day one was hell.
Day three, easier.
Day seven, I heard birds.
Day forty-seven, I'm here.
Alive. Present. Joyful despite everything.

HARVEST:
Not joyful because world's perfect.
Joyful because joy is rebellion
against algorithms that profit from misery.

SOFIA:
Your joy is protest.
Your presence is resistance.
Your analog life is revolution.

Final act: Audience creates collective analog artwork from their joy journals

IBRAHIM (watching creation):
This is what we stole.
This collaboration. This presence.
This... what do you call it?

ALL:
Life.

IMAGISTIC AMPLIFICATIONS

The Scroll Thumb
Repetitive scroll motion creates specific muscle memory in thumb flexor pollicis brevis. Brain scans show this movement triggers same reward anticipation as lever-pulling in gambling addicts. The thumb becomes autonomic—scrolling without conscious command, seeking dopamine hits that rarely arrive.

The Attention Fragment Cloud
During doom scrolling, attention splinters into what neuroscientists call "cognitive confetti"—thousands of micro-focuses that never cohere. Brain imaging shows attention centers lighting up chaotically, like fireworks with no pattern. Each scroll throws more confetti, making coherent thought impossible.

The Joy Restoration Wave
When phones are removed, the brain undergoes "digital detox cascades"—waves of restoration as different systems come back online. First, visual processing calms (hour 1), then attention coherence returns (hour 4), then creative networks reactivate (hour 12), finally joy capacity resurges (hour 24-48).

The Paradox Body
Holding simultaneous contradictory emotions creates specific muscular patterns—grief contracts intercostals while joy expands them, creating "emotional breathing" that's neither sob nor laugh but both. The diaphragm learns to accommodate paradox, expanding capacity for complex feeling.

The Analog Neural Pathway
Tactile experiences (paper, crayon, texture) activate ancient neural pathways that predate digital stimulation by millions of years. These pathways connect directly to joy centers without passing through anxiety-producing analytical regions. Your hands know joy your eyes forgot.

The revolution: Lyon becomes the city where people travel to break scroll addiction, recover stolen joy, dissolve false guilt, embrace paradox, and discover that unexpected joy was always hiding between the updates.
