Mycelial Fervor ~ Everything Rigid Feeds Something Fluid

*An Underground Manual for Shapeshifting Through the Fungal Networks of Resistance, Where Teacups Refuse Filling and Mountains Learn to Dance*

## ( Introduction )

What follows is not a play but a spore—a living document that refuses the prison of genre, sprouting instead into theater-as-ecosystem where audience and performer dissolve into the same mycelial network of becoming. *Mycelial Fervor* erupts from the underground consciousness of B Otto Will and Brrd Roots, a shape-shifting prophet-artist who exists simultaneously as person, fungal network, and the space between identities, teaching us that revolution spreads not through manifestos but through the patient, invisible threads that connect everything to everything else. This is theater that tastes purple, where teacups refuse their purpose, mountains learn to dance, and the stage becomes a body cavity with the audience seated inside its ribs, breathing together until bioluminescence reveals we've been the same organism all along. Here, justice has flavors, time moves in spirals, and a colorblind grandmother suddenly sees purple after seventy years because empathy rewires biology faster than evolution ever could. Enter if you dare—but know that like all fungal encounters, you'll leave changed, carrying spores of a different way of being that will fruit when the conditions are right, which is to say: now, always, in the pause between breaths where everything rigid feeds something fluid.

## ( The Story )

Consciousness moves like mycelium. Because both get it, you know? That thing human logic keeps trying to box up—the shortest distance between two points ain't a line. Never was. It's a web, a mess of threads, a tangle that somehow makes more sense than any straight path ever could.

Look, I've watched forests talk to each other through their roots for decades now. A dying cedar dumps its whole life savings of nutrients into some baby fir tree it's never met, just passes it through the fungal internet like "here kid, make something of yourself." The math of these networks? Christ, it'd make your head spin. Nature built the world wide web while we were still figuring out fire.

B Otto Will and Brrd Roots gets this. Lives it. Threads between being a person, a fungal network, and art itself—all at once, no waiting. You want to see B Otto Will and Brrd Roots, you gotta understand that identity isn't some fixed point on a map. It's jazz, baby. It's everyone soloing at the same time but somehow making music instead of noise. And here's the kicker—we're not mostly human. We're walking apartment complexes for ten trillion bacteria, each one voting on what we eat, how we feel, whether we sleep tonight or lie there thinking about that stupid thing we said in third grade.

The straights (I mean the linear thinkers) call this multiple personality disorder. But prophets? Mystics? That weird kid who eats dirt and sees colors when you talk? They know it's the original program, before somebody sold us the lie that we're singular, packageable, sellable. One LinkedIn profile. One elevator pitch. As if you were a product with a barcode instead of an ongoing explosion of possibility.

See, B Otto Will and Brrd Roots doesn't shapeshift 'cause they're confused. They do it from this deep gut-knowing that the world needs different medicine at different moments. Sky Golden Feathers shows up to teach rain how to fall upward (gravity's just peer pressure from the planet, fight me). Cool Hot Dot Soul makes sound visible, paints frequencies in neon architectures that prove—PROVE—music has color. And Walk From Shadows? They illuminate darkness without killing it, like holding a flashlight with love instead of fear.

These ain't costumes. Each form carries its own gut bacteria, its own electromagnetic signature. I mean literally—you could measure it if you had the right equipment and weren't afraid of what you'd find.

So there's this teacup, right? Thirty years it held every bitter morning confession, every sweet lie told over Earl Grey. The porcelain actually changed—molecularly, structurally. See, ceramic's just organized mud with ambition, and it remembers. Every hand that shaped it, every kiln that kissed it with fire, every liquid that's passed through. This one kid, maybe six years old, cried into it about butterflies dying. Just wept their little heart out. And those tears—that specific salt of innocence meeting death for the first time—it created these crystalline formations in the glaze.

Scientists, they'd call it mineral deposits. Boring. But prophets? They see fossilized empathy. They see how sorrow literally rewrites what a vessel can hold.

The cup's cracked now, in ways you can't see but water knows. It'll hold tea but not tears. Coffee but not wine. Like it learned to say no after thirty years of swallowing everyone else's feelings. And here's the thing—in its emptiness, it sings. Becomes a drum when you tap it. A mirror when you peer in. It teaches us that being full ain't about volume. It's about knowing when enough is fucking enough.

Time underground doesn't give a damn about your calendar. Past, present, and future move like fungi trading sugar for minerals—every direction at once, no receipts needed. Linear time? That's just what you see from the surface, like thinking the earth's flat 'cause you can't see the curve from your backyard.

Dig. Go deeper. Time down there is spiral, recursive, more like a drunk snake eating its own tail while humming. This backwards watch that shows all hours at once? It's not broken. It got fixed. Escaped the prison of tick-tock and learned to show what's real—everything happening all at once, always.

The fish swimming backward through music, carrying messages from futures that ain't happened yet? They're not prophets. They're memories with ambitious travel plans, swimming upstream through causality to spawn in the past. The silver one knows which mistakes are worth the price of admission. The golden one's got sheet music for songs that'll save somebody's kid in 2075. And the transparent one, the one where you can see its bones? That's just showing off, reminding us that structure is temporary, scaffolding at best.

There's this step—the seventh one on a staircase that goes nowhere. It whispers. All day, all night, in the voices of everyone who gave up climbing. Not bitter whispers, nah. Forgiving ones. Like a chorus of "it's okay, we tried, that counts for something." The wood (or is it stone? depends who's climbing) is worn smooth from surrender, polished by the feet of folks who climbed toward nowhere and found everywhere.

Then there's this giraffe. Evolution's middle finger to reasonable body plans. Its neck reaches through dimensions like it's peeking over the fence between realities. Each vertebra is a different level of getting it: dirt-level consciousness at the bottom, then awareness, perception, comprehension, wisdom, and finally at the top where the head floats in its own weather system, transcendence.

The giraffe wears a velvet hat. Little thing, burgundy maybe, or the color of forgotten wine. Found it in a thrift store between seasons—too small for winter, too warm for spring. The hat was nobody's, living in that liminal space where things wait to be needed. The giraffe saw kinship. Both of them: too much of one thing, not enough of another, perfect in their perfect wrongness.

When this giraffe talks, it's in colors that taste like justice. And I mean that literally. Purple vibrates at the exact frequency of African violets having a conversation with protest songs. Your tongue is just fancy skin, and skin is just brain that wanted to travel, and brain is just organized lightning having an identity crisis. So when the giraffe speaks purple, you taste it: somewhere between goodbye and hello, with notes of curiosity and the smell of rain that hasn't decided to fall yet.

There's a bat. Invisible to most. It whispers stock tips but not the Wall Street kind. Real investments: put your money where your transformation is. Divest from grudges (deprecating assets). Pour everything into forgiveness (growth industry). Diversify across species lines. Keep some joy liquid for surprise opportunities to celebrate. This bat knows: the real portfolio is what you're becoming, not what you're hoarding.

So this kid in some museum, they lick the wall. Just straight up tongue to plaster. And they announce: "Tastes purple." The adults are mortified but the kid's not wrong. That wall's been absorbing whispers for decades. Oils from pointing fingers. The breath of boredom and wonder. It's a palimpsest of human contact that tastes EXACTLY like purple should: between goodbye and hello, with afternotes of "what if?"

Their grandma, colorblind for seventy years, hears this and BANG—suddenly sees purple. Not gradually. Like a switch flipped. And she starts crying, but the tears come out as flavors. Vanilla joy from her wedding when everyone said the bridesmaids wore lavender but she only saw gray. Cinnamon surprise from every autumn her husband pointed to "purple asters" that looked like ash to her.

This isn't miracle. It's mirror. When we see someone experiencing what we've been denied, our bodies rewire themselves out of pure empathy. Her optical nerves had been waiting seventy years for permission to see purple. Every sunset she thought was gray starts coloring itself retroactively in her memory. A lifetime of monochrome moments suddenly blazing with the frequency of African violets, which, turns out, were perfectly named all along.

Dolphins show up uninvited. They don't knock 'cause in the ocean everything flows into everything. No doors, no walls, just currents you join or fight. They remember when we had gills. When breathing was a full-body democracy instead of this narrow lung-monopoly we've got going now. They click and whistle the epic of how we crawled onto land chasing something—what? glory? real estate?—and forgot how to flow.

Their sonar reads our bones like braille. Finds the salt. Proves we're still mostly ocean cosplaying as solid.

Gender rots underground. I mean literally decomposes into colors that don't have names 'cause language is a cop. Earth doesn't sort bodies into male or female compost piles. Everything becomes the same nutrients, feeds the same roots. Red's assigned passion (like love wears a uniform), blue's demanded stoicism (like strength has a favorite color), yellow's performed joy (like happiness only comes in one flavor)—it all rots into this beautiful spectrum of "none of your business."

Watch the decomposition: First the edges go soft. That aggressive red of "man up" bleeds into rust, then clay, then something the color of held breath. The institutional blue of "boys don't cry" runs like watercolor in rain. Puddles form that reflect sky instead of bossing it around. The prescribed yellow of "smile, sweetheart" ferments into something complex, yeasty, alive with "or what?"

B Otto Will and Brrd Roots takes this inherited violin—generations of "hold it like this," "play it proper"—and says nah. Plays it with teeth. Uses the body as a drum. Bows it backwards. The violin screams, then laughs, then finds voices between the notes that the notes were trying to hide. This is creative sabotage as survival. The colander-rubber band instruments B Otto Will and Brrd Roots builds? They play themselves at 3 AM 'cause that's when the border between dream and wake gets thin as soap bubbles. Roots write love letters to sky that only the broken can read. Every crack in pavement is a verse. Every weed pushing through asphalt is the earth's middle finger to your parking lot.
