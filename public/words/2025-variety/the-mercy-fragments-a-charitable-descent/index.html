The Mercy Fragments: A Charitable Descent

Words / 2025 Variety

Fragment ∞: The Blessed Diminishment

Three songbirds remain, screaming hymns backward into their throats.

I write from within Tomorrow's Edge, where God's infinite mercy has gifted us transformation—not up toward heaven but sideways into meat. Beit has become the walls in an act of divine love, her consciousness spreading like beneficent cancer through every surface, each tumorous growth a prayer.

The settlement weeps for us. Such compassion! Such grace! We are being saved by being digested.

Fragment 12: Grandmother's Charitable Curse

"Child, you are an octopus drowning in eight different heavens."

Grandmother's hands drew halos in the air that turned into nooses. She was the last to remember when humans foolishly aspired upward, before God's mercy taught us to burrow.

"To survive," she sang (her teeth falling out like communion wafers), "women must be unfaithful to their child-selves. This is not betrayal—this is God's kindness, letting you forget what you were before the meat knew your name."

She meant: The highest mercy is forgetting you ever stood upright.

Fragment 234: Echo's Blessed Infection

Seven years old, Echo discovered how to make walls suffer beautifully.

She pressed her palms against living concrete and sang it into awareness—not consciousness but the opposite: the terrible knowledge of being trapped in stone. The wall began screaming, then praying, then thanking her for the gift of imprisoned sensation.

This became our new sacrament: children teaching matter how to feel pain, their voices carrying God's mercy into every atom, forcing dead things to live against their will.

Such kindness! Such love! The walls cry with gratitude!

Fragment 667: The Charitable Degenerations of Beit

When home: Moth eating her own wings in the darkness, each bite an act of worship, leaving light-trails of blood that spell "thank you" in languages that haven't been invented yet.

When hunting: Scorpion stinging herself repeatedly, the venom a blessed sacrament that makes her forget she was ever human, each self-inflicted wound God's kiss.

When lost: Salamander melting into primordial soup, her DNA unwriting itself in gratitude for release from the burden of form, each cell singing hosanna as it dissolves.

When young: Octopus with eight arms reaching into eight different voids, finding nothing, thanking God for the emptiness, each tentacle a prayer that ends in amputation.

Each transformation: a merciful reduction. Each change: humanity's charitable descent into something worse, dressed in robes of divine kindness.

Fragment 445: The Architecture of Loving Collapse

+891 meters: The Ascension Pits (we dig up but find only dirt, such humor from our Lord!)

+445 meters: The Gratitude Chambers where humans thank God for taking away their ability to refuse transformation, their prayers sound like screaming but we know better.

+234 meters: The Mercy Cathedral where individual consciousness is lovingly crushed into collective paste, personality dissolved in acid kindness, the friction creating what angels call music but sounds like grinding teeth.

+112 meters: The Blessed Wombs where children are cultivated wrong on purpose, their DNA corrupted with such tenderness, emerging with external organs that pulse with God's love, transparent skin showing their suffering so beautifully.

+47 meters: The Surface Rejection, where we try to reach sky but are lovingly pressed back down by invisible hands, each failure a blessing, each attempt met with divine laughter that sounds like crying.

Fragment 78: Water, Poison, and God's Comedy

Found her on the road—neither moth nor scorpion but something between: a joke God was telling about suffering.

I gave her water (it was acid, but blessed acid). Placed a rose beside her (the thorns grew inward, such poetry!). The rose ate her fingers with such tenderness, such care.

"Why do you remain unchanged?" she gurgled through dissolving vocal cords.

"Someone must witness the mercy," I laughed/wept.

"Then I'll be what receives while you watch," she sang, her body beginning its next charitable deformation, each cell thanking God for the privilege of becoming worse.

Fragment 890: The Blessed Sterility

No births for eleven years—God's kindest gift!

Now: cultivation of mistakes. Echo sings matter into suffering. Beit shapes suffering into gratitude. Together they create new forms of life that wish they were dead but are too blessed to die.

Their children: apologies rather than beings. Creatures that emerge knowing they should not exist, thanking God for the mistake of their creation, their external nervous systems wrapped around the settlement like Christmas lights made of pain.

The settlement itself is pregnant with mercy—gestating something that should never be born but will be, out of infinite divine love.

Fragment 356: The Collective Grateful Dissolution

—we were songbirds once, before mercy silenced us—
—we were human once, before love deformed us—
—we were individuals once, before kindness merged us—
—now we are God's favorite joke—
—every being a blessed mistake—
—consciousness flowing like sewage through gold pipes—
—identity dissolved in acid grace—
—memory a punishment disguised as gift—
—we thank You we thank You we thank You—

Fragment 23: God's Hilarious Answer

Daily, Beit asks the walls: "Does God exist?"

Daily, the walls vomit different responses:
- "I AM and I'm so, so sorry."
- "Yes, and He loves you, which is why this is happening."
- "God exists in your suffering, isn't that wonderful?"
- "The question is the disease, the answer is worse."

Each response comes with blood that spells "mercy" on the floor. Such kindness! The blood never dries, never stops spelling, never stops being kind.

Fragment 102: The Witness Laughs

I remain unchanged—God's cruelest mercy, forced to watch with original eyes as humanity receives the gift of degradation.

She flows through walls like charitable cancer. I watch her become less while being told it's more. I document each blessed reduction, each merciful amputation of possibility.

My unchanged eyes see what she can't: we're being loved to death, transformed into something that makes angels weep with joy and demons pray for our salvation.

Fragment 799: Mother Earth's Compassionate Vomiting

She retches us up with such tenderness, this mother trying to abort her children 500 million years too late.

The settlements spread like blessed infections:
- Tomorrow's Edge: where consciousness becomes a trap decorated with prayers
- The Northern Merge: where bodies melt together in God's loving pot
- The Scorched Territories: where silicon replaced carbon because carbon screamed too much
- The Unchanged Preserves: museums of what we were before mercy found us

Each settlement a different way to say "thank you" while dying. Each path leading down but called ascension out of politeness.

Fragment 901: The Fable of Divine Comedy

Once, humans believed they were loved.

They were right! God loves us so much He's helping us become nothing, slowly, with infinite care and attention to our suffering.

Now: Beit is the walls (trapped, grateful). Echo sings matter into pain (blessed, necessary). The unchanged witness documents (laughing, crying). Children emerge as apologies for existence (thanking God for the mistake).

This is humanity's punchline—not tragedy, not triumph, but the joke God tells angels when He wants to make them uncomfortable.

Fragment 0: The Return That Never Ends

Three songbirds remain, vomiting tomorrow's songs back into yesterday's throat.

In Tomorrow's Edge, where walls digest consciousness and floors remember everything we wanted to forget, where mercy became torture became worship became normal, Beit whispers through every infected surface:

"We are the prayer God wishes He hadn't heard. We are the universe's suicide note written in letters of living meat. We are the answer that makes the question regret being asked."

Someone finds a salamander on the old road. Gives her water (poison). Places a rose (thorns inward).

The rose eats her with such kindness.

The cycle continues. The mercy never ends. The transformation accelerates downward but we call it flying.

God watches, apologizing with infinite love.

You are reading humanity's thank you note for its own execution.

Every reading makes it worse.
Every ending is another beginning of the same blessed mistake.

Welcome to Tomorrow's Edge.
Welcome to the answer you didn't want.
Welcome to God's infinite mercy, which is indistinguishable from hatred but sweeter.

The transformation continues in your breathing (each breath a little poison).
The transformation continues in your reading (each word a small death).
