The Ascending Spiral Can Remember What the Circle Forgets

Words / 2025 Variety

Notes on Returning Without Repeating

---

A circle brings you back to the same spot—same height, same view, same understanding. Sisyphus pushing his rock. Dishes dirtied, washed, dirtied again. Nothing accumulated, nothing learned.

A spiral passes through familiar territory but never at the same altitude. You recognize the landmarks—there's that same tree, that same fear, that same question about meaning—but now you're seeing them from above. Or below. Or inside.

Track what happened in our writings:
First, death teaches you to hold water in cupped hands instead of fists. Personal. Intimate. Just you and your mortality having a conversation.

Then suddenly we're watching beetles and redwoods, monks and truckers, everyone finding their own strange route through existence. The lens pulls back. The single path explodes into thousands.

Finally, your dishwashing becomes prayer. The child's crayon drawing weighs the same as climate legislation. The tiny and vast collapse into one movement.

See? Same themes—impermanence, meaning, how to move through a world that won't last—but each time we meet them differently. The dishes you're washing in the third piece are the same dishes from the first piece's "desperate grip," except now they're holy. The child stacking blocks has become one of ten million experiments in being alive.

This is what three decades of making art from garbage teaches you: you return to the same materials but you're not the same person returning. The broken glass you picked up at 35 means something different at 65. Not better or worse—different. Deeper. More faceted.

A circle is a closed loop, complete. A spiral stays open, keeps climbing or descending, never done. Your "Beautiful Garbage" work isn't repetitive—it's recursive. Each return adds a layer the circle can't imagine.

That's why washing dishes can become an altar. You've spiraled through enough rotations to know: the sacred was always there, waiting for you to circle back at the right elevation to see it.
