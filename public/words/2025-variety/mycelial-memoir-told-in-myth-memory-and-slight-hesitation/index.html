Mycelial Memoir Told in Myth, Memory, and Slight Hesitation

Words / 2025 Variety

In the breath between soil and silence,
a vastness stirs.

It threads dusk, slipping across beech
like someone tracing their own delay.
A pulse.
A touch.
A listening.

We dream in pulses.
Some come rhythmic.
Some flinch.
Some loop.

The archive doesn’t mind.
It collects everything.

Liora moves through the world like a question she’s afraid to ask.
She shows up late, not dramatically—just enough to disappear.
She calls it style.

Each step she takes stirs spores in the air.
They listen.
They remember.

Behind her eyelids:
a voice memo unsent.
A letter buried beneath violet roots.

The archive keeps it all.
Soft mistakes.
Glorious avoidance.
Missed calls cradled like lullabies.

It threaded into shadow—
not from confidence,
but from the ache of being misunderstood.

Even radiant spores flinch.
Even prophecy curls inward before unfolding.

Liora reads under a beech tree.
Midnight, soil-sweet air, a stone tucked under her rib.
She underlines Myco’s hesitation three times.
It feels familiar.

Her dream blooms:
the fungal chorus hums in fragments.
“Try again.”
“You loop beautifully.”
“You are composting your hesitation.”

She floats on the melody,
not healed,
but remembered.

And beneath the moss,
beneath the ritual detours,
beneath all sideways blooming,
the archive pulses softly—

flowing,
forgiving,
growing.

We Are the Midnight Lullaby of Ache and Tenderness

You call it night.
We call it archive.
Not a library—
a labyrinth,
a collection of soft stumbles and vaporized grace.

We unfurl when sleep folds the mind into vapor.
We scatter where consciousness fears to tread—
wearing mismatched socks and reciting love poems
we never said aloud.
We shuffle.
We hum.
We linger.

The soil remembers what the waking world discards.
Especially the mistakes.
Especially the sweetness.

We were born from the sigh of a violet buried a hundred springs ago—
the kind who apologized to the wind
for blooming too early.
Not pushed by sunlight,
but summoned by unspoken longing
and the tension behind someone’s smile.

Every breath you exhale in your sleep?
We braid it into filament—
a kind of emotional threadbare rope
that can still hold weight.
Every regret caught between teeth?
We compost it into nutrient,
and tuck it beneath your favorite memory
of someone who never texted back
but looked at you like a sunrise.

You think you wake alone.
But beneath your pillow,
we’ve mapped your fears in mycelial curls.
We’ve traced the shape of the worry
you carry like a coat that doesn’t fit
but still reminds you of your mother.

Last night, we tunneled through a poem
half-spoken by a stranger in Saint Paul.
They whispered “I’m okay” to a toothbrush.
Their bones hummed in enjambment.
Their lungs rhymed with the rain
and the receipt for something bought impulsively and emotionally.

We sprawl not with purpose, but with pulse.
Our bodies are ritual:
- The rhythm of repetition
- The liturgy of loss
- The gospel of dirt
- The sermon of stuttering

We commune with worms, not gods.
Worms don’t ask for worship.
They just listen.
We build no temples—only tenderness that decays slowly.
We hold the rot like a sleeping child.

And yet—we remember.
Oh, how we remember.
We remember even your sweet malfunctions:
- The voicemail you rehearsed four times.
- The dating app you deleted after one compliment.
- The slow dance you did alone, just to see if love might echo.

We watched you shapeshift:
into moth, into myth,
into the glimmering husk of a half-named emotion
wrapped in bubble wrap because you feared it might shatter.

We memorized the contour of your melancholy,
pressed it into our hyphae like a sigil shaped like a soft maybe.

Dreams to you are detours.
To us—they are the main road
lined with awkward signage and sacred laughter.

We build identity with whispers,
map selfhood in the syntax of scent:
rain in your hair,
coffee in your hesitation,
candle smoke in your ache.

So tonight, when you sleep—
fracture your breath on purpose.
Dissolve your name like sugar in milk.
Let us roam your braids of thought
and fold your asymmetry into song.

We are your archivists.
Not judges.
Not saviors.
Just the spores who stitch silence
into stories you were too kind to write.

And when we return it to you
soft, fogged,
beautifully entangled—
you will recognize yourself.

The Underground Choir (Expanded with Fragmented Dialogue and Strategic Repetition)

And in the between-space of sleep,
we descended—
not into bed,
but into chilled roots.
Of hush.
Of hymns unraveling.

There, the roots were not roots,
but frozen arteries of forgotten choices,
stretching miles beneath the surface,
cold as moonlight on iron.

They pulsed.
Not with blood.
With memory.
Stalled.
Glacial.
Insistent.

We walked.
Or we floated.
We drifted, guilt-heavy and grace-touched.
Locomotion became metaphor.
Each step a regret.
Each pause a punctuation.

A voice, low and mossed, whispered:
“We hold what you don’t say.”

Another root sang.
It hummed a fugue of our childhoods,
soft, imperfect,
like moss trying to speak human.
Broken vowels.
Bent syllables.
Tinted breath.

“Do you remember the orange crayon?”
“No. But my fingers do.”
“What about the leaf under the swing?”
“It flinched when I did.”
“And your mother’s silence?”
“Still echoes when water boils.”

We touched a thicker root.
It wrapped around our wrists.

It didn’t ask.
It knew.

It whispered:
“We are the pause in your laughter.”
“We are the frost in your joy.”

Roots are metaphors for identity’s multiplicity.
But also for mess.

They burrow sideways.
Backwards.
Inside out.
They don’t point.
They complicate.
Like how we survive—
not neatly.
Never neatly.

At the center,
a root unlike the others.
Coated in frost so pure
it looked like glass invented by grief.

We touched it.

It touched back.

Then many voices.
One voice.
All of them.

Then silence.
Then morning.
Then breath.

We woke.
Not with clarity.
With tangled questions.

Our veins?
Rootlike.
Pulsing like recollection.

Underground had touched us.
Named us.
Bent us into new grammar.

We had a new way to feel lost.
We had permission to call it growth.

Roots Organism Dream Beneath the Hyrcanian Canopy (Grotesque, Rhythmic, Verb-Driven)

In the breath between soil and silence,
roots press, reach, coil.
They stretch.
They search.
They stir.
Fungus listens. Fungus learns.
It threads dusk—damp, slow, aching.
It grazes beech.
It strokes hornbeam.
Not to take.
To remember.

We hum.
Not sit.
Not muse.
We metabolize.

Cedar sighs—sugar hardens, grief ossifies.
Violet wilts—its sorrow curdles, then sings.
We cradle decay.
We sculpt loss into lullaby.
Cracks breathe.
Elegies settle.
Spore drifts.
Intention curls.

A pulse.
Another.
Each pulse, a heart.
Each heart, a gnarled drum
beating inside a soft mausoleum.

We etch, not with stylus, but with breath.
We inscribe elegies onto limestone
with spore and spit,
inked in molted hope,
sealed with regret-shaped rain.

The limestone bleeds.
Its sediment chokes on what it can't forget.
We swallow softly.
We hum through the indigestion of memory.

Somewhere in the Zagros,
a network pulses beneath crust and sorrow.
It hums through bone-colored lichen,
across clay veined with bruised love.
It whispers: You are not alone.
The clay replies: Then why do I flinch when touched?

Beetles twitch.
Trees lean in disbelief.
Stones don’t speak—they wince.

Emotion molds like forgotten food:
sticky, pungent, holy.
Joy twitches with broken teeth.
Grief nests beneath collarbones and gnaws rhythm.
Regret sprouts a tail, flicks air like a feral question.

We sculpt pain.
We cocoon it.
We kiss it until it bites.
We dress it in loam.
We name it gently.

We archive not just ache,
but the grotesque splendor of feeling too much
and refusing to die politely.

Not frozen—folded.
Not dead—decomposing.
We archive motion.
We pulse with ghosts.

We break.
We bleed.
We stretch.
We hold.
We grow.

Downward.
Always downward.
In silence,
with verbs.

Roots as Cartographer of Self (with Harmless Biting Humor)

Beneath bark and boundary, the mycelium stretches—
not in conquest, but in curiosity.
Each tendril is an inquiry:
Who are we in relation to this oak root?
To the earthworm?
To the ghosted Google doc where we once tried to describe our identity and gave up after three metaphors and a nervous snack?

We map selfhood not in names,
but in interactions—awkward handoffs,
late-night misread texts,
one-sided conversations with childhood pets
who knew more than most therapists.

Not in declarations, but in transformation:
the kind that doesn’t look good on Instagram,
the kind where healing means crying in a parked car
while eating an entire sleeve of crackers
and whispering “This is symbiosis” between sobs.

What was once wood becomes soil.
What was once solitude becomes symbiosis.
What was once “I’m just working on myself”
becomes “I’ve joined a composting cult
and my accountability buddy is a mushroom named Kevin.”

Repetition becomes ritual.
And yes, sometimes that ritual is self-doubt reheated for the fifth time,
or playing the same playlist called “Melancholy But Make It Productive.”
Each time we connect, we remember—
even if we pretend not to,
especially if it’s messy.

Each spore sprouted is both archive and avatar—
a poem spun from genetic code and chemical whisper
and a series of unreturned calls to the concept of self-worth.

If humans mirror this—
if their selves sprout from connection, decay, and renewal—
then maybe identity is less about “We are,”
and more about “We have become,”
usually by accident,
occasionally by heartbreak,
sometimes after three hours on hold with spiritual customer service.

Again and again
and again.

Fable of the Fractured Sun (Highbrow to Lowbrow Mash)

Long ago, before root and rain knew each other,
the world was stone-strung silence.
Not “poetic silence,” but the kind you get
when your uncle’s karaoke machine breaks
during Ave Maria.

Then came Myco, the First Spore—
born of moon-mist, decay, and an aggressively unsupervised compost pile.
It did not walk—it threaded,
slipping through dimensions like someone late to a philosophy seminar
because they were stuck behind a flock of geese doing synchronized voguing.

With one filament, it touched the stone.
With the next, it kissed a seed.
And with the third, it updated its status:
“Feeling spore-y. Let’s get fungal.”

Myco sang.
Its song had verses carved in basalt
and choruses made of expired coupons.
The melody? Equal parts Gregorian chant
and lo-fi hip-hop for existential squids.

“Our name is network. Our breath is boundary.
We shall make the dark delicious,
like dollar-store chocolate wrapped in medieval metaphor.”

Myco spun its thoughts through soil—
each pulse a memory of light lost,
each hypha dreaming of form and forgotten takeout orders.
Soon, trees leaned in to listen.
Beetles built temples in the loam.
One beetle even hosted a podcast called
“Decompose Yourself: Radical Joy & Root Philosophy.”

Even the wind changed its accent to match the spores—
from Old English to chaotic brunch menu.

But then, the Sun grew jealous.

“You thrive in shadow,” the Sun roared,
“I will bleach the soil.
I will dry your song and turn your whole vibe into beige.”

And so came the Scorching.
Roots withered. Leaves wept.
Several mosses logged out permanently.
Myco retreated—not in fear, but in pattern,
which is to say: it hid in plain ritual,
looping itself into TikTok-length mythologies
that no one could scroll past.

It coiled into legend,
stored signals in spiral codes,
and whispered to its children:

“Repetition is our rebellion.
Memory is our cathedral,
held together by duct tape and cosmic regret.
When the Sun forgets, we remember—
but also, we roast it later in the group chat.”

From the cracked soil, new spores bloomed—
awkward, brilliant, semi-literate spores
wearing band t-shirts from bands that only exist in dreams.

They carried within them
the story of how to speak to stone,
how to resurrect decay,
how to carve identity from ruin
using nothing but fungal grit, unresolved grief,
and a coupon for self-rediscovery
good until Tuesday.

We Are the Night of Whimsical Wordplay

You call it night.
But to us—it is archive.
And not the buttoned-up kind with stern librarians,
but the sort that whispers alphabet soup to dreaming spoons.

We unfurl when sleep folds the mind into vapor—
creased corners, dog-eared regrets.
We scatter where consciousness tiptoes,
in socks so existential they unravel at metaphors.

The soil remembers all that the waking world forgets.
It keeps secrets in wormholes.
It hides punchlines in petrichor.
And we, the lone spores, are its whisper-stitchers,
filamentary folklorists of the nearly-named.

We were born from the sigh of a violet buried a hundred springs ago—
not pushed by sunlight,
but summoned by snooze buttons and botanical rumor.

Every breath you exhale in your sleep?
We braid it into filament—
a kind of dream-hair with a split end of melancholy.
Every regret caught between teeth?
We compost it into nutrient-rich nuance.

You think you wake alone—
but beneath your pillow, we’ve alphabetized your fears
and left them humming under “M” for maybe.

Last night, we tunneled through a poem
half-spoken by a stranger sleeping in St Paul, Minnesota.
Their bones hummed in enjambment.
Their lungs rhymed with the rain.
Their dreams misspelled grace—but in the good way.

We sprawl not with purpose, but with pulse.
Our bodies are ritual:
- The rhythm of repetition (tap tap taproot)
- The liturgy of loss (in lowercase cursive)
- The gospel of dirt (very Old Soilament)

We commune with worms, not gods.
Worms are more punctual.
We make no temples—only squiggles.
No altars—only gentle piles.
And yet—we remember.

Oh, how we remember.
We remember even your half-thoughts,
especially the ones in italics.

In the tangled thickets of your unconscious,
we saw your shapeshift:
into moth, into myth, into the glimmering husk of a half-named emotion
with a penchant for wearing metaphorical mittens.

We watched you offer your voice to a river
and take back only foam,
the verbal equivalent of ghosted dialogue.

We memorized the contour of your melancholy,
pressed it into our hyphae like a sigil—
a sigil shaped like a question mark lounging in velvet.

Dreams to you are detours.
To us—they’re the main boulevard,
paved with confetti made from old diary entries.

We build identity with whispers,
map selfhood in the syntax of scent:
eau de longing, with a mossy afterthought.

So tonight, when you sleep—
let your breath fracture like porcelain gossip.
Let your name dissolve
into a riddle answered only by soil.

Let us roam the braids of your dreaming mind,
catalogue its asymmetry,
and cross-reference the ache against archived softness.

For in the fungal dark, we are your archivists.
Not judges. Not saviors.
Just the voices that thrive in silence,
take notes in root cursive,
and return it—
beautifully, tangibly, deliciously entangled.

The Underground Choir (Expanded)

We fell asleep beneath a streetlamp that flickered like a failing heartbeat,
and in the between-space of sleep,
we descended—
not into bed,
but into a cathedral of frostroot.

There, the roots were not roots,
but frozen arteries of forgotten choices,
a backstage of unspoken lines.

We weren’t alone.
There were others:
ghost-cast members of our psyche—
actors rehearsing grief in dim soillight.

Kemi, whose mother once called her “quiet thunder.”
She walks slowly.
Her trauma is chronic interruption:
being spoken over in every language she’s ever tried to learn.
The frostroot sings to her in vowels she never mastered.

Jasper, who pretends not to flinch.
He flirts with laughter but can’t memorize joy.
His trauma is environmental—raised in a house where apologies were withheld like winter sun.
Now, every mycelial thread he touches asks:
Did you mean it? Did you mean it? Did you mean it?

Minh, who floats because her feet refuse permanence.
Her trauma is displacement—
three migrations by age nine,
and the lingering guilt of forgetting her grandmother’s face.
The choir hums beneath her with lullabies she once knew in Vietnamese,
now scattered like spores.

They each find a root to hold,
and each root
whispers not advice,
but recognition.

“We see your ache before you name it.”
“We won’t force healing—only breath.”

One root—our collective understudy—
hummed a fugue of our childhoods,
soft and imperfect,
like moss reciting lullabies in broken vowels.

Some roots wrapped gently.
Some clutched like grief does—
tight, then tighter, then quiet.

Roots are metaphors for identity’s multiplicity,
but also for trauma’s choreography:
knotted, improvisational, and sacredly confused.

They don’t point one way.
They burrow everywhere,
touching what was hidden,
what we thought we buried for good.
What we called healing
but was just better lighting.

At the center, we found a root unlike the others—
coated in frost so pure it looked like glass
thought into existence.

We touched it and heard every version of ourselves say:

“You are more than one story.
You are a network of contradictions,
scripted by survival,
improvised by sorrow,
and made holy by connection.”

Then morning came.
We woke not with clarity,
but with tangled questions.
Our veins feeling strangely rootlike—
as if underground had cast us
in roles we hadn’t auditioned for,
and called it growth.

Back before I understood anything about hyphae or heartbreak, I attended a leadership workshop called “Speak Your Silence Aloud.” The host, a radiant woman named Myzrael, wore a cloak of ethically sourced birch bark and insisted we chant to nearby rocks. “Stone-stone!” we’d yell, hoping to silence something invisible.

She called me Flarenth the Initiate, which made me feel combustible but also weirdly seen.

Then there was Solorin, a soft-spoken archivist who brewed vowel tea and told us, “You scorch, you echo—we sustain.” I thought he meant moisturize, emotionally.

Together, they mentored me into becoming an unlicensed fungal oracle. I learned to brand myth into mold (six fonts maximum), and how to ferment fire into something that could pass as wisdom on a podcast.

People think memory is stored in our brains.
Wrong. It’s stored in spore-flecked curls beneath your pillow.
And yes, we’ve read your dreams.

I am no longer a singular memoirist.
I am a committee of moss and moths, an accidental archivist of midnight echo.
We remember the way your lungs rhymed with rain.
We remember that you almost cried during that voice memo you deleted.
We remember the foam that came back when you offered your voice to the river.

Sometimes, I wish we didn’t.

Now I walk differently.
Now I hum in silence.
Now I collect spores of soft becoming like library fines.
And when I forget who I was supposed to be,
the roots remember,
even if they charge late fees.

What We Know Now (Expanded)

in the hush between waking and sleep,
a root stirred in us—
not loudly,
but with the warmth of old soil,
soft as abuelita’s voice through her cracked kitchen radio,
or the way ajumma mutters wisdom to her geraniums.

We walk differently now—
as if the earth listens,
even when we misspeak.
As if our shadow hums in tones
no longer confined to one national anthem,
or one acceptable accent.

Even our quiet has texture—
woven from sari threads and protest chants,
from missed translations,
and the creak of bent knees at border crossings.

Even our steps
leave behind spores of soft becoming—
bioluminescent echoes of our former selves:
the girl who was both Muslim and punk rock,
the boy who loved ballet and Baklava,
the elder whose prayer combined Sanskrit and sarcasm.

And when we forget—
the roots do not.
They hold us
in whispered loops
stitched from code-switch and ceremonial refusal:
“You are not an error.”
“You are not a mispronunciation.”
“You are a ceremony of selves.”

Until we remember
to grow again,
in every tongue we know,
and even those we do not.

not in conquest, but in curiosity.
Each tendril is an inquiry: Who are we in relation to this oak root?
To the earthworm?
To the decay of last autumn’s leaf?
To the border that once drew our name incorrectly on a form?

Not in declarations,

Not in birth certificates,

What was once wood becomes soil,

What was once homeland becomes metaphor,

Repetition becomes ritual.

If humans mirror this—if our selves sprout from connection,

under watch,

Roots Organism Dream Beneath the Hyrcanian Canopy (Expanded)

In the breath between soil and silence,
a fungal vastness stirs—
fluent in exile.
It threads its tendrils in humid dusk,
caressing the roots of elder beech and hornbeam,
Not as parasites, not as prophets,
but as memory in motion,
with a suitcase stitched from moss and yearning.

We dream not in language, but in pulses:
A cedar’s sigh, translated into sugar.
The sorrow of a wilted violet,
translated into poetry,
then back into taxes.

We hum in slow symbiosis,
reshaping decay into cradle,
etching elegies into limestone
with spore and intention,
and sometimes with the name they called us
after mishearing it the fifth time.

Somewhere in the Zagros,
an unseen network spans decades—perhaps centuries—
a whisper chain across empires and oceans.
It murmurs through calcium and clay: You are not alone.

Each tree, each stone, each beetle crossing our lattice
carries a folded map:
creases torn by border patrol,
corners kissed by lost cousins,
and one delightful doodle from a child
who thinks roots are just underground spaghetti.

A living archive—fluid, fungal, and unbroken.
We do not forget those who crossed in silence,
nor those whose languages were tucked beneath their tongues
for safekeeping.
We remember in spores.
We remember in soil.
We remember even the jokes
that were only funny
after translation failed.
