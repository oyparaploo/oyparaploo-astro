A Call Across Time

Living with Purpose and Love

← Words / 2025 Variety

Across the centuries, Saint Francis of Assisi and Pope Francis shine as rare individuals whose lives transcend the boundaries of their eras, awakening within us something timeless and essential. Their boundless compassion, humility, and courageous faith invite us to ask: What does it truly mean to live—with purpose, simplicity, and love?

Simplicity and Humility: The Quiet Defiance
For Saint Francis, simplicity was rebellion—a stripping away of worldly possessions to embrace liberation. Imagine him standing in the public square, his fine garments discarded, whispers rising from the crowd. His father’s anger burns like a storm, but Francis stands firm, finding freedom in vulnerability.

Centuries later, Pope Francis echoes this defiance, though expressed differently. Picture him stepping into his modest apartment, where the scent of aged wood mingles with the hum of city life. His unadorned desk and quiet surroundings reflect a choice to lead humbly—not above others, but beside them. In the stillness, perhaps he wonders: What does it mean to lead through service, not status?

Their gestures challenge us to ask: Do the things we hold onto truly free us, or do they weigh us down?

Advocacy for the Poor: A Gesture of Connection
Saint Francis kneels before a beggar, the bread in his hands rough and warm. Their eyes meet, and in the man’s weariness, Francis sees something sacred—a reflection of divinity.

Centuries later, in a bustling square, Pope Francis leans down to embrace a man cloaked in hardship. The smells of smoke, sweat, and damp pavement cling to the man’s coat, yet in this fleeting moment of connection, words are unnecessary. His gesture says: You matter. You are loved.

Both figures remind us to pause amid chaos and truly see the humanity in others. Can we, too, recognize the spark of divinity in those society overlooks?

Reverence for Creation: A Sacred Responsibility
For Saint Francis, nature was more than a backdrop—it was a sacred companion. Beneath a canopy of stars, the rustling leaves and murmuring streams formed a symphony of praise. To Francis, creation was a language of divine love.

In modern times, Pope Francis takes this reverence further, penning the encyclical Laudato Si' as a plea for environmental justice and care for our common home. His ink-stained hands and thoughtful reflection weigh heavily with the urgent cries of a planet in peril—rising seas, suffocating oceans, and vanishing forests. He challenges us: If we fail to care for the Earth, how can we care for one another?

Together, they call us to protect creation, recognizing the inseparable connection between the cries of the Earth and the cries of the poor.

A Call to Courageous Faith
Faith, for these two figures, is not a refuge of comfort—it is a force of transformation. Saint Francis walks barefoot through icy streets, his bold voice calling others to embrace Gospel truths. He touches lepers with hands others withdraw from, embodying love that transcends fear.

Pope Francis similarly confronts modern challenges, speaking boldly against corruption and clericalism. Standing in a divided Church and fractured world, his words cut through resistance, asking: What must we risk to reignite faith and serve justice?

Both men remind us that faith is a fire—uncomfortable and disruptive, yet profoundly transformative.

Joy Amid Trials: A Light That Endures
Amid suffering, both figures radiate joy. Saint Francis sings hymns of gratitude on his deathbed, his fragile frame illuminated by candlelight, the scent of wildflowers drifting through an open window. His voice, though weak, rises defiantly against despair.

Pope Francis, despite the weight of his responsibilities, finds joy in small moments—a child’s laughter, a comforting embrace. In these moments, chaos and hope intertwine, teaching us that joy is a choice to see light where others see only darkness.

Their lives remind us: Even in trials, joy sustains us. Can we, too, embrace this light?

A Legacy That Demands Action
Saint Francis and Pope Francis do not simply inspire; they compel us to act. Their stories converge with our own, asking: What will we let go of for love? Will we break barriers to build connection? Will we listen to the cries of the Earth and humanity, and will we respond?

Their voices rise not as distant echoes but as truths woven into the present—whispers in the rustling leaves, the clash of city streets, the quiet spaces of reflection. They remind us that transformation, though daunting, is always within reach.

Through their example, they ask us to reconsider our lives—our priorities, comforts, and apathy—and embrace a life lived for others. Their call is both beautiful and haunting: Will we choose to see, to act, and to love?
