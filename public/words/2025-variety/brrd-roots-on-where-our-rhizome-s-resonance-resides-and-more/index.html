On Where Our Rhizome’s Resonance Resides and More

← Words / 2025 Variety

Sprouts Whisper of New Beginnings and Transformation, While a Most Wonderful Source of Light Calls Us Home

The earth had always known.

It had known before the fire, before the ruin, before the silence.
Beneath the surface, beneath the weight of centuries, it pulsed—
sacred, stained, alive.

The gardener whispered to it once,
her hands deep in the breath of seasons.
She cradled the soil, pressed her fingers into its warmth,
murmured words that only the roots could understand.

"You were grown here," she had told me.
*"Before you were born, before you were imagined,
you were already waiting beneath the roots."*

I had laughed.
Or maybe I had cried.
Or maybe both.

Then the fire came.

It did not ask permission.
It did not care for the hands that had tended the land,
for the prayers whispered into the wind.
It took everything.
And left nothing.

Except the scent of charred petals, curling into the sky
like forgotten memories.

"You must keep walking," she had said.
*"Even when the road is gone.
Even when the sky is shattered.
Even when the world forgets your name."*

So I walked.

Through the **ruins, through the quiet,
through the madness of knowing**
that nothing would ever be the same.

And yet—

The soil still breathed.
The roots still reached.
The wind still carried voices,
fragments of laughter, sighs,
the distant toll of church bells dissolving into air.

Then—movement.

A child ran through the tall grass,
arms wide, laughter spilling into the morning.
The trees leaned in, listening.
The flowers turned their faces toward the sound.

The garden awakened.

Rhythms of movement—
feet pressing into the earth,
hands brushing against petals,
bodies weaving between branches.

Echoes of laughter—
bouncing off stone paths,
spilling into the sky,
folding into the wind.

Interaction with nature—
touching the soil,
picking flowers,
climbing trees—
each action deepening the connection
between humans and the living world.

The soil exhaled.
The vines stretched.
The birds sang louder.

Wildlife stirred—
a butterfly fluttered toward the commotion,
a rabbit paused, ears twitching,
the wind shifted, carrying voices through the leaves.

Emotional energy rippled through the space—
joy, curiosity, wonder.
The garden absorbed it all,
becoming more than just earth and roots.

It became alive.

The rhizome stretched.

Through the **cracks of ruin, through the embers of loss,
through the silent ache of forgotten names.**

It did not stop.

It reached **beyond the horizon, beyond the burning sky,
beyond the shattered constellations**
where time dissolved into dust.

It moved through the outer reaches of the universe,
where stars hummed in quiet reverence,
where gravity bowed to the weight of memory,
where the echoes of the past
were stitched into the fabric of the cosmos.

And still—

It did not stop.

It ascended,
threading itself into the honey-toned light
of heaven’s embrace.

Here, the soil was soft, golden, eternal.
Here, the wind carried the scent of new beginnings.
Here, the gardener’s voice lingered—
not in sorrow,
but in quiet, boundless peace.

The rhizome had traveled far.
Through fire, through ruin, through the endless dark.
And yet—

It remembered where it began.

It was always meant to return to the light.

Resonances of Terre de Bruyère Growing Beyond That Epic Cry About The Land

Cool Metamorphosis Rooted In The Unknown

Stepping into the unknown with no roadmap, yet somehow finding yourself woven into its rhythm. Expanding beyond preconceptions, adapting to a space that once felt distant or impossible.

Like roots threading through unfamiliar soil, we begin to anchor ourselves—first tentatively, then steadily—until what seems foreign becomes wonderfully familiar, and that vision only imagined becomes real.

The wind carries voices—fragments of laughter, sighs, the distant toll of church bells dissolving into the air. But beneath the surface, something stirs. The earth shifts, unseen movements threading through the roots, whispering of change before it arrives.

A wildfire, untamed, sweeping through the harvest. The scent of charred petals lingers in the air, curling into the sky like forgotten prayers. The fields once golden with promise—now ash, now embers, now silence.

"But I was never meant to be here," she says, her voice quiet, almost lost in the wind.

I glance at her, waiting.

"My grandmother grew me," she continues, eyes tracing the horizon. "She was known as the gardener in our village. People came to her for seeds, for wisdom, for the kind of patience only soil understands. And somehow, she grew me too."

I let the words settle, watching the way she holds them—like something fragile, something sacred.

"She said I was planted in the earth before I was ever born. That my body was shaped by the seasons, by the rain, by the hands that knew how to nurture life."

The harvest is gone, but the land remains.
The roots, buried deep, wait for rain.
And when it comes—
new growth, new beginnings, new resilience.

The sky bends, the horizon flickers, and suddenly, you are weightless—floating between the past and the possible, between the known and the uncharted.

The colors keep trying, keep shifting, keep whispering.

Nature teaches us that the unknown is not to be feared but embraced. That stepping forward, even without certainty, is the only way to truly grow.
