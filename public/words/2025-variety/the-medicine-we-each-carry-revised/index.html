The Goodness of Grief

The Medicine We Each Carry

○
The Medicine We Each Carry

"A visitor's witnessing"
_

Someone spray-painted "HOPE IS CANCELLED" on the bridge today. Someone else added "FAITH STILL ACCEPTING APPLICATIONS." This might be the most honest exchange I've witnessed here.

This might not be a season for hope. Hope keeps refreshing the news, waiting for good headlines. Maybe it's time for faith instead - faith that sits with what is. That doesn't need tomorrow to be better to keep going today.

I'm noticing humanity's been climbing for so long. Every generation promised more than the last. Always ascending, always improving. But now you're learning to walk downstairs. Finding out progress isn't guaranteed, might never have been.

We're in decline, darkening. The kind where you stop making five-year plans. Systems breaking down. Not failures but overdue reckonings. Every institution scrambling, every safety net showing its holes. Watching things collapse in real-time. While still having to go to work, pay rent, pretend.

This darkness might last generations. Longer than a Netflix series, longer than your patience. Those who reach the other side might see something new emerge. Your children's children, maybe. It's going to be rough. No one's pretending otherwise anymore.

But you're being asked to ripen into maturity. Not the maturity that has answers, but that can hold questions. Trauma makes us small - we hoard, hide, strategic. Refreshing Amazon carts we'll never buy, saving articles we'll never read. But we're at a threshold. Being asked to become adults who can witness collapse without becoming it.

The level of loss ahead will be strong. Stronger than your therapy coping skills. Nature's initiating us. Through fires, floods, and the slow realization that normal's never coming back. We've entered conditions where ordinary life falls apart. Where you realize your parents can't save you, your degree won't protect you.

We can't go back. That door closed during lockdown, or before, or last Tuesday. We can participate in what's emerging or be dragged by it. Though some days it's hard to tell the difference.

Each of us might ask: How can I help? Not fix - help. There's no answer but there is response. Like the difference between solving homelessness and sharing your sandwich. Everyone must find their particular response. Your medicine won't look like mine.

Because everyone carries specific medicine. The neighbor who checks on elders. The friend who reminds you to eat. The stranger who smiles at your crying baby. Every one of us necessary. Even the broken ones. Especially the exhausted ones.

We need your particular gift. The thing you don't think matters. The small skill. The stubborn refusal to quit. Even if it feels tiny against the enormity. Like bringing cups of water to a forest fire - still necessary, still medicine.

Still learning breakdown might be breakthrough in work clothes. Still discovering faith is quieter than hope, sturdier too. Still witnessing how each small medicine adds up to survival.
