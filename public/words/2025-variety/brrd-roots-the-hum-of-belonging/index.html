Brrd Roots ~ The Hum of Belonging

Words / 2025 Variety

Redemption, Grace, and the Symphony of Earth and Spirit

There was no silence left. Not a square inch. Not a breath unscarred.

The air hummed—
a resonance older than memory, stitching together futures we might never touch.
The primordial music of a world alive, alive and aching.

Its bark carried sorrow, its whispers bound in longing,
and rice paddies mirrored moons that had watched us forget ourselves.
There was no border, no clear edge—where spirit ended, where earth began.

*"What is harmony without choice?"* I thought, the question stretching across me like horizon.
*"If the world hums without us, are we its rhythm—or its interruption?"*

And yet, my mind wandered, tethered to the weight of want.
Did I belong to the earth, or did I simply crave it?

---

We stood in the shadow of our frailties.

Aya carried the scars of her decisions, every breath a ledger of cost.
Her hands trembled when they touched the bark of a tree,
her voice fractured when she confessed:
*"I burned forests in the name of progress, not realizing
that destruction is its own kind of exile."*

*"How do you love something you’ve wounded?"* she asked,
tears carving rivers through her silence.
*"How do you ask for grace when your own hands destroyed it?"*

The ologist paused, her hands brushing Aya’s.
*"You build a bridge with love,"* she murmured.
*"It is not perfect. It is not the same.
But bridges still carry us across."*

Aya knelt then, as if to surrender her soul to the soil.
And when she rose, her chest heaved not with despair,
but with the heavy, redemptive ache of love rediscovered.

---

Sato spoke with longing for answers too sharp to hold.
*"I thought knowledge was salvation.
Politics as scaffolding. Economics as justice.
But nothing is whole—not even our truths."*

*"What do you want then?"* I asked him.

He stared into the horizon as if it could break itself apart for him.
*"I want to believe there is meaning beyond my hands.
But it’s all fractured—our intentions,
our progress, our grasping at things we cannot keep."*

*"Then stop grasping,"* the ologist said.
*"Surrender does not make you smaller. It makes you vast."*

In the green murmurs of the fields,
Sato surrendered to the hum,
not with despair,
but with the trembling stillness of a heart
finally unclenching.

---

Elias carved because creation made him alive.
His hands, calloused and certain, were a testament to passion—the kind that never died,
even when buried under doubt.

*"I carved violins because they spoke better than I could,"* he said.
*"But then my hands betrayed me.
I couldn’t trust them anymore,
and the silence ate me whole."*

He lifted his fingers then, watching the light catch on his skin,
as though testing if they still knew the rhythm of his soul.
*"Do you think love needs trust,
or does trust follow love?"*

*"Love transforms first,"* I replied,
*"and trust catches up like a breath after running."*

Elias carved that night—not perfectly,
but purpose had returned to his hands.
And as he worked, it wasn’t music he carved,
but salvation.

---

The hum shifted. Its cadence rose not just from the earth, but from us.
The tension between spirit and body blurred,
our aspirations woven into the soil that carried us.

*"Perhaps belonging requires listening,"* I whispered.
*"Not fixing. Not controlling. Listening."*

Aya pressed her palms to the bark she once burned.
Sato stood with his arms open, the skeptic no longer searching,
but receiving. Elias’ hands danced against the wood.

And I knelt, surrendering not with regret,
but with wonder for what the land would ask of me.

---

It was in our giving,
our stepping away from self,
that we found redemption.

A selfless higher purpose carried us—not out of our struggles,
but through them.
We became a note in the symphony,
not its composer, not its audience, but its vessel.

The hum rose, ancient and new.
And for the first time, the rhythm found balance:
Earthly sorrow met spiritual grace.

Not a square inch of silence left.

*"I know everything, and I know nothing,"* he said, shadows pooling around his feet.
*"Economics. Politics. Evolution. Every human construct breaks if you press hard enough."*

His words landed sharp—carved with the edges of exhaustion.
*"I thought certainty would save me.
I thought progress was truth.
But it isn’t. It’s just noise we pretend is music."*

The ologist tilted her head. Fireflies danced in fractals around her.
*"If not certainty, what then?"*

*"Grace,"* Sato said softly, and his voice cracked. *"But what is grace in a world that breaks itself?"*

We stood still, listening—not just to the hum, but to the silence that rose beneath his words.

*"Surrender,"* I whispered.
*"Belonging is surrender."*

Sato’s gaze softened. *"Surrender to what?"*

*"To what’s left,"* said the ologist.
*"Not everything needs fixing. Some truths need only to be carried."*

And in that moment, Sato closed his eyes. His walls melted against the hum’s rhythm.
He surrendered—not his knowledge, but his soul.
The land absorbed him, carried him, held him.

And then came **Aya**, undone by guilt, shattered by choices made.

Her steps were slow, deliberate, the weight of her mistakes coiled in her shoulders.
She didn’t speak at first, her grief too thick for words. But when she did, the air seemed to stop.

*"I let it fall,"* she whispered, her voice brittle as roots torn from earth.
*"I burned forests because progress said they were obstacles.
I silenced songs because power said silence was control.
And now…"*

Her voice broke, tears streaking lines against her cheeks.
*"Now, I wonder if grace can find me in the ruins I made."*

The ologist placed a hand on hers, steady and unflinching.
*"Grace doesn’t erase,"* Elias said, emerging from the shadows. *"It transforms. You carve new melodies from the broken strings."*

Aya’s gaze wavered, tears catching in the fireflies’ golden threads.
Surrender wrapped her too.

The greenhouse world hummed louder now.
Its rhythm carved through the rice paddies, its breath pressed against fractured skies.
And in that hum, we found ourselves.

*"Perhaps belonging is listening,"* I whispered.
*"Not fixing. Not saving. Listening."*

And then the hum shifted. It held more than sound—it held us.

Elias lifted his calloused hands. His fingers moved, tentative and careful,
as if testing whether they could trust him again.
*"I think I need to try carving again."*

The ologist touched the palm oil still clinging to her skin.
Her expression was thoughtful, weary, but certain.
*"We begin where we end.
Creation. Connection. Grace. Memory."*

Sato opened his eyes. Contentment wrapped him, gentle as sunlight stretched across dusk.
Aya lifted her head, redemption rising in her chest, heavy but whole.

For the first time, the rhythm of the land seemed to balance itself.
Serenity cradled chaos. Humor warmed heartbreak.
Harmony rose where perfection had failed.

The hum carried truths—about us, about progress, about grace itself.
Not everything needed answers.
Some things simply needed to belong.
