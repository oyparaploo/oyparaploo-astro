Steel, Sweat and Tariffs

Echoes of Industry

Brrd Roots ~ Free-Verse
Echoes of Industry:
Weaving Loss, Renewal, and the Promise of Hope

When was it, you ask?
That golden glow, the golden age—
when American manufacturing stood tall.
June, 1979.
Factory floors alive with rhythm,
the hum of machines a hymn, not just a sound.
A heartbeat shared by roughly 19.6 million hands.
Steel sealed with sweat, steel steeped in strength.
“Made in America” wasn’t a label—
it was a vow, an echo, a promise forged in fire.
Picture it: clang of metal, echoing rafters, ringing raw.
Glow of sparks—falling stars—scattered across the floor.
Faces smudged, eyes focused, hands steady.
Steel beams reaching skies, rising, molded, folded.
Progress written in iron—etched.
Each word, a weld; each weld, a world.
On the Detroit factory floor—
Sparks flying, futures forming.
Voices calling, spirits soaring.
Grease thick in the air, effort heavy yet shared.
These weren’t just jobs—they were lifelines.
Lines stitching, stretching, securing dreams
into the fabric of the nation.
This work—it was sacred.
Not just paychecks—purpose.
Not just labor—legacy.
Factories weren’t just places.
They were temples,
cathedrals built on sinew and sweat.
Altars of creation, the union of man and machine.

But tremors began, tremors beneath surfaces,
gentle whispers, subtle shifts, unsettling shapes.
Globalization sailed in, soft promises carried over water.
Cheaper labor, cheaper hands, cheaper plans.
Then came automation,
machines gleaming, glaring under sterile lights.
Metal humming without heart, humming hollow.
No pause for breaks, no sharing of coffee smiles.
This wasn’t just change—it was erosion.
It was the dismantling of dignity, bit by brittle bit.
For many, losing work was losing worth.
Losing rhythm was losing reason.

Sheila in Akron,
a mother, alone yet not undone.
Rubber stung her skin, her hands cracked.
Night shifts, conveyor belts, hum and heat.
And yet—pride carved out of labor.
Paychecks stretched, stretched thin but steady.
Her daughter twirled in ballet shoes bought
by a factory’s pulse.
When silence came—
the hum collapsed into nothing.
Announcements quiet, machinery muted, hollow halls.
Dreams packed in boxes, stacked, sent away.
And Martin, Flint’s second son—
Legacy laced with steel.
His father’s boots worn but proud,
a relic, tethered to tradition.
Engines built, stories shared, echoes lived.
The plant closed—legacy lost.
In his garage, Martin sat, staring.

Main streets fell faint—
Wind whispered through stripped awnings.
Shuttered diners stood still, their menus curling.
Dreams dissolved.
Practical pain—
Rent unpaid, tables bare.
Groceries dwindling, hours thin.
Not just systems, but souls unraveling.
Thread by thread, heartbeat by heartbeat.

And yet—
even in quiet, new words rose.
Youngstown, Ohio—
Rust turned to resilience.
Smokestacks stilled, yet knowledge spilled.
A steel mill reborn as code;
hands learning, hands typing, hands alive.
Where Lena wove software in place of wires.
Detroit,
wildflowers whispered through cracks.
Urban farms grew where factories fell.
Squash bright, beans climbing, seeds singing.
This isn’t just survival—
this is redemption.
Ashes turned art, hope turned harvest.

Sheila, apron tied tight, flour flecks glinting bright.
Her bakery glowing warm, her cinnamon rolls rising.
The scent stretched through corners of her new world.
A rebuilt rhythm.
And Martin, welding—
but now art rose, sculpted from scrap.
Galleries glowing, his metal whispered stories
in windows once blank.
These stories—
they echo motion, they breathe grace,
they hold hymns of hope.
Each step—forward, upward—
carves renewal from loss.

So, what’s the takeaway?
Progress builds, progress breaks.
What progress makes, progress takes.
Economies aren’t just systems.
They are hands and hearts, connected—fragile—whole.
Sheila with her nights at the pin.
Martin sculpting dreams from steel within.
Families stitched together—
failing, fragile—resilient.

This isn’t just mourning.
This is honor.
Strength rises, loss releases, renewal rests.
Let us build futures beyond regret.
Where growth holds care,
where grace meets connection.
It’s not just what’s been lost—
but what comes next.
Threads of effort, stitched into hope.
