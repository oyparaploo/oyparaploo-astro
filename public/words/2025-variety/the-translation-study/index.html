The Translation Study

Words / 2025 Variety

UC Berkeley Mycology Lab
November 2024

Dr. Liora Park stares at the grant rejection. Third one this year. Her research: proving fungal networks store human memory patterns.

Her lab assistant KEMI enters.

KEMI: The specimens from your mother's grave are ready.

LIORA: Don't call them that.

KEMI: The soil samples from plot 247?

LIORA: Better.

---

The Study

Six participants. All immigrants. All grieving.

LIORA: We're testing whether mycelial networks preserve emotional memory through decomposition.

JASPER (participant, 51): You're saying mushrooms remember our dead?

LIORA: I'm saying fungi might archive neural patterns during decomposition. Consciousness as chemical data.

MINH (participant, 44): How much does this pay?

LIORA: Two hundred. Plus you get soil from your loved one's burial site.

MINH: My grandmother's in Vietnam.

LIORA: We have samples from Saigon cemetery. Shipped yesterday.

---

The Workshop Leader

Enter MYZRAEL, 58, Liora's former advisor. Dismissed from Stanford for ethics violations.

MYZRAEL: Still chasing your mother through mushrooms?

LIORA: It's legitimate research.

MYZRAEL: You're testing dirt from graveyards on grieving immigrants. That's not research. It's exploitation.

LIORA: Says the woman who gave LSD to undergrads.

MYZRAEL: Psilocybin. And they consented.

LIORA: So did my participants.

---

The Test

Participants hold soil samples. Electrodes measure responses.

KEMI (to Jasper): Your sample's from Colma?

JASPER: Dad's grave. Five years now.

The mycelium in the petri dish responds—hyphae reaching toward Jasper's hand. Monitors spike.

JASPER: What's happening?

LIORA: The fungi recognize you. Or rather, they recognize your father's genetic markers in you.

MINH: That's impossible.

Screen shows chemical signatures matching between Minh's DNA and the Vietnamese soil sample.

MINH: My grandmother... I smell her kitchen.

LIORA: Olfactory hallucination. The fungi release compounds that—

MINH: Cinnamon. She made pho with cinnamon.

---

The Crisis

MYZRAEL: You're traumatizing them.

LIORA: I'm proving consciousness persists.

MYZRAEL: You're desperate to believe your mother's still here. In dirt.

LIORA: Get out.

MYZRAEL: I taught you everything. Even your delusions.

LIORA: You taught me drugs were doors. I'm finding actual doors.

Kemi interrupts—the specimens are overheating. Contamination spreading between samples.

KEMI: They're connecting. All the samples. Creating a network.

---

The Network Forms

The separate petri dishes—Korean, Vietnamese, Mexican, Indian soils—begin growing toward each other. Hyphae bridge the gaps.

JASPER: My dad was Korean. Never learned the language.

MINH: My grandmother spoke French. Colonial education.

PARTICIPANT 3: My mother code-switched. Spanish at home, English at work.

The fungi create patterns. Not random. Linguistic.

LIORA: They're translating. The networks are translating between genetic memories.

MYZRAEL: That's projection. You want meaning where there's only mold.

---

The Recording

Kemi plays audio from the lab's ultrasonic recorder. Fungal networks emit sound at frequencies humans can't hear. Slowed down, patterns emerge.

It sounds like voices. Multiple languages. Overlapping.

JASPER: That's... that's my father's laugh.

MINH: Stop. Please stop.

LIORA: We're documenting something unprecedented—

MINH: You're using our grief for your career.

KEMI: Dr. Park, your mother's sample is reacting.

---

The Mother

Liora's mother's soil—kept separate, control group—begins growing rapidly. Toward Liora.

LIORA: Mom was a translator. Six languages. Before the stroke.

MYZRAEL: She lost them all, didn't she? The stroke took her languages.

LIORA: She hummed. When she couldn't speak, she hummed.

The ultrasonic recording from her mother's sample: a melody. The lullaby she hummed in the hospital.

LIORA: She's still translating. Through the fungi. Between death and life.

---

The Decision

JASPER: I want my sample back.

MINH: Mine too.

KEMI: If we break the network now, we lose the data.

LIORA: Two more hours. Please.

JASPER: You're recording our dead. That's not science. That's grave robbing.

MYZRAEL: Let them go, Liora. Your mother's gone.

LIORA: No. She's just... translated.

The participants take their samples. The network breaks. The humming stops.

---

After

Liora alone with her mother's soil.

KEMI: The university's pulling your funding.

LIORA: I know.

KEMI: Myzrael reported you to the ethics board.

LIORA: I know.

KEMI: The recording... should I delete it?

LIORA: No. Keep it.

She holds her mother's sample. No electrodes. Just hands and earth.

LIORA: 엄마. Māma. Maman. Mother. Ima. Majka.

Six languages her mother knew. The soil warms slightly. Or maybe that's just Liora's hands.

KEMI: What now?

LIORA: Now I bury her. Again. Properly this time.

KEMI: Not in the lab?

LIORA: In the ground. Where translation happens naturally.

---

Berkeley Rose Garden
December 2024

Liora and Kemi bury the sample illegally, midnight, under heritage roses.

KEMI: Think she'll grow?

LIORA: Everything grows. Just changes form.

KEMI: The recording... I listened again. Slowed it more.

LIORA: And?

KEMI: It's not words. It's just pattern. But pattern that sounds like meaning.

LIORA: Maybe that's all meaning is. Pattern we choose to hear.

Spring will bring new mushrooms to the rose garden. Tourists will photograph them. No one will know they're archives. Memory translated through soil, reaching toward light.

End
