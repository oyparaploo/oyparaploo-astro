The Memory Keeper's Censored Waltz

## A Play in Three Acts

( Introduction )

In "The Memory Keeper's Censored Waltz," water flows upward through a space that exists between abandoned theater and flooded cathedral, where three extraordinary beings dance a waltz with missing beats—spaces where [CENSORED] memories refuse to be spoken. Sister Golightly catches tears that fall toward the sky, Loon Plague hoards thousands of stolen memories in glass vials that line his coat like organs of light, and Soleil exists as living echo, their voice overlapping itself across time. This revolutionary theatrical experience transforms grief into beauty as audiences witness impossible transformations: water learning to tango with fire, furniture made of crystallized anxiety, and an orchestra playing the spaces between notes. As memories escape their bottles and fly home like luminous pigeons, the play asks what happens when we finally release what we've desperately kept, revealing that perhaps our most profound truths live in the [CENSORED] spaces—the gaps where each audience member's own unspoken stories rush in to complete the dance.

( The Play )

### Characters:
- **SISTER GOLIGHTLY**: A woman who moves between worlds, keeper of tears and translator of seasons. Age indeterminate. Wears layers of gauze that shift color with her emotions. Carries an umbrella that catches grief instead of rain.
- **LOON PLAGUE**: The Memory Keeper. A man who collects and guards others' recollections to fill his own emptiness. Sharp-featured, hollow-eyed, wearing a coat lined with thousands of glass vials. His shadow moves independently, always reaching for what he cannot have.
- **SOLEIL**: The Echo Collector. Non-binary, luminous. Moves like smoke, speaks in reverberations. Wears mirrors that face inward. Their breath creates small auroras in cold air.
- **THE VISITORS**: Various lost souls seeking their memories, their echoes, or their tears.

### Setting:
A space that exists between an abandoned theater and a flooded cathedral. Water pools in unexpected places, reflecting not what is above but what was. Doors stand without walls. Church pews face a stage where no play was ever performed. Time moves in three-quarter rhythm. Throughout the space, certain words and phrases appear [CENSORED], as if memories themselves have been redacted by an unseen hand.

### Extended Production Notes:

**On the Nature of Performance**: This play exists in the space between theater and ritual. Each performance should be unique, with actors encouraged to discover new moments within the structure. The [CENSORED] sections can change nightly, responding to the specific audience's energy.

**On Casting**: The three principal roles should be cast with attention to vocal versatility and physical poetry. Age, gender, and background are less important than the ability to exist in multiple states simultaneously. Consider non-traditional casting that challenges assumptions.

**On Design**:
- **Lighting**: Should be a fourth character, capable of weight, texture, and emotion. Use of projection mapping encouraged for the transformation sequences.
- **Sound**: Layer live and recorded elements. The echoes should feel physically present. Consider using the entire theater as a speaker.
- **Costumes**: Should transform with the characters. Hidden pockets, reversible elements, and materials that react to light and movement.
- **Set**: Minimal but transformative. Water is essential—whether real, projected, or implied through fabric and light.

**On Audience Integration**: Consider:
- Pre-show installations in the lobby where audiences can deposit their own memories
- Post-show rituals where audiences can take home "echoes" of the performance
- Interactive moments where audience members' shadows become part of the scenic design

**On Music**: Commission a score that includes:
- Prepared instruments that can play "censored" notes
- Vocal techniques from multiple cultural traditions
- Spaces for improvisation within structure
- A waltz that can be danced with missing beats

**On Movement**: Choreography should blend:
- Classical waltz structure
- Butoh-influenced transformation sequences
- Contact improvisation for the element dances
- Pedestrian movement elevated to ritual

**On Language and Translation**:
- The multilingual elements should be preserved in all productions
- Programs should include a glossary of [CENSORED] words—blank spaces for audiences to fill with their own
- Consider supertitles that show not translation but emotional temperature

**On Rehearsal Process**:
- Begin with memory-sharing circles
- Practice "echo exercises" where actors repeat and transform each other's gestures
- Develop a company vocabulary for the physical life of tears, memories, and echoes
- Leave room for discovery—the play should grow during its run

**On the Deeper Meaning**: This play asks:
- What do we lose when we try to preserve everything?
- How do absence and presence dance together?
- What happens to the things we can't or won't remember?
- How do we hold space for mystery in an age of total documentation?

The Censored Waltz is, ultimately, a dance between what we can say and what we cannot, what we remember and what we forget, what we keep and what we release. It suggests that perhaps the most beautiful moments are the ones that exist in the spaces between—in the [CENSORED] that makes room for each audience member's own deepest truths.

**Final Practical Note**: This play requires a brave company willing to work in the liminal spaces between theater and performance art, between narrative and dream, between the spoken and the ineffable. It is not easy to perform, but when done with full commitment, it creates an experience that audiences carry with them long after the curtain falls—a memory that transforms each time they remember it, a tear that continues to fall upward toward hope, an echo that finds new meaning with each return.

The play ends where it began, but we who witnessed it are countless miles closer to understanding that the journey itself is home.

# Production Guide: The Memory Keeper's Censored Waltz
## A Comprehensive Manual for Theatrical Creation

### I. CONCEPTUAL FOUNDATION

#### Understanding the Core
1. **Establish the play's reality**: This work exists between dream and waking, memory and forgetting. All production choices must honor this liminal space.
2. **Define your [CENSORED]**: Decide what memories/truths will be redacted for your specific production and community.
3. **Embrace incompleteness**: The waltz has missing beats. The play has missing words. This absence is the heart of the work.

#### Pre-Production Research
1. Research memory disorders and their emotional impact on families
2. Study water physics and impossible movements for staging inspiration
3. Explore cultural approaches to grief and memory (especially Korean han, French mélancolie, Russian toska)
4. Investigate local history for community-specific memories to incorporate

### II. SPACE & DESIGN CONCEPTION

#### Selecting/Adapting the Venue
1. **Ideal spaces**:
- Abandoned theaters with water damage
- Deconsecrated churches
- Warehouses with high ceilings and water access
- Spaces with multiple levels and unexpected corners

2. **Space modifications**:
- Install water features (pools, fountains, drip systems)
- Create elevation changes with platforms
- Design entrances that appear/disappear
- Build architectural impossibilities (doors without walls, stairs to nowhere)

#### Scenic Design Process
1. **Water systems**:
- Design recyclable water flow that can reverse direction
- Create "dry water" effects using projection, fabric, or particles
- Install misting systems for atmospheric moisture
- Plan drainage for safety

2. **Transformative elements**:
- Use automated fly systems for gravity-defying moments
- Design modular set pieces that reconfigure between scenes
- Create translucent walls that reveal/conceal alternate realities
- Build furniture that can "breathe" using hidden motors

### FINAL MEDITATION

This play requires a company willing to exist in uncertainty, to find structure in fluidity, to create absence as powerfully as presence. It asks the production team to be both technicians and shamans, both craftspeople and dreamers.

The ultimate goal is not to produce a play but to create a ritual—a space where collective memory can dance with collective forgetting, where individual grief can transform into communal beauty, where the impossible becomes not just possible but necessary.

Remember: The waltz is censored not because something is missing, but because the missing beats are where the audience's heart enters the dance. Your production should create space for that entrance, that participation, that transformation.

The play ends where it began, but no one—neither performer nor audience—remains unchanged. That change is the true production value, the real special effect, the lasting echo that continues long after the lights fade.

Countless miles closer, indeed.
