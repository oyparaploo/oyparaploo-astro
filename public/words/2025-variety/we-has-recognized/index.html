We Has Recognized

Words / 2025 Variety

---

The night janitor at the city's contemporary art museum begins creating unauthorized exhibitions from what artists throw away, discovering that the labor of maintaining art might be more important than making it—and that a cornucopia of necessary and useful things already exists in what everyone else calls garbage.

---

Wrack Lumière had been cleaning the Contemporary for seven years. Every night, emptying trash from artists' studios. Every night, seeing what got thrown away. Sketch after sketch. Broken frames. Paint tubes squeezed to death. Failed attempts worth more than her annual salary. All this... shit that was actually...

"What are you building, woman?" the security guard asked, finding Wrack in Gallery C at 3 AM, garbage everywhere—no, not everywhere, arranged, sort of, maybe—

"Something about value... labor... I don't—practice? Fuck, I don't know."

She'd started just keeping things. A sketch here—Brendan Malone's discarded portrait, he made

The museum tried stopping them. Failed. Tried officializing them—offered to make it a "community engagement project" with no pay but "exposure." They refused. Kept working days, creating nights. Or was it creating days, working nights? The boundary dissolved then reformed then dissolved messier.

"We're the museum," Hinge said one dawn, Gallery C through F now infected with their assemblages.

"We always was," Wrack corrected.

"Were?"

"WAS. We was. We is. We has been. We has recognized what was always true."

Someone suggested documentation. They documented. Then threw away the documentation. Then made art from the thrown away documentation. Then threw that away. Then rescued it. Cycle cycle cycle. Like their shifts. Like their paychecks that came and went without anything leftover.

"Beautiful aesthetic value," Wrack would say, holding actual trash. Someone's lunch wrapper. A visitor's used tissue. A pink slip from HR.

"Worth contemplating?" Hinge would ask.

"Or not?" Peal would add.

"Yes!" someone would shout.

"No!" someone else.

"Both!" everyone.

The work spread. Not just galleries. Bathrooms where they'd cried during shifts. Offices where they'd been disciplined for being three minutes late. The parking garage where they slept in cars between doubles. Garbage art or art garbage or just necessary useful things everywhere. The museum became unrecognizable. Better. Worse. Both.

"This is our cornucopia," Mélodie said one night, crying or laughing. Both. Always both.

"Of necessary—"

"And useful—"

"Things!"

"That nobody needs!"

"That everyone needs!"

"That we needs!"

"That we has!"

They never stopped. Never got permission. Never got stopped. Just kept maintaining and making, making and maintaining, until nobody could tell which was which, until the museum was garbage was art was labor was life was—

"We has recognized," Wrack would say, mopping floors that would never be clean enough, arranging garbage that would never be art enough, living a life that would never be valued enough.

"We has," everyone would answer.

Or not answer. Just keep working. Keep making. Keep recognizing the gorgeous in the discarded. Keep creating importance where there was none. Or where there always was but nobody saw until the janitors started saving everything, arranging everything, making the reproductive labor visible by making such a beautiful necessary useful mess that you couldn't ignore it anymore.

You couldn't ignore them anymore.

We has recognized.

We is recognizing.

We continues.
