Pieces Ascending Upside Down

The Sacred Mirror Remembers Most of What She Reflects

← Words / 2025 Variety

Or How the Story Discovered It Was Praying All Along

(Introduction)

This is a prayer disguised as a story about transformation. What appears as fragments critiquing wealth worship are actually pieces of light scattered by a divine prism, each shard containing the whole. Someone named Hee isn't climbing stairs—Hee is the universe teaching itself how to dance vertically. The fragments don't need weaving; they're already whole, pretending to be broken so we can experience the joy of recognition. Here, revision isn't correction but revelation, each edit a small enlightenment. The ghosts in jars are teachers. The protesters lying horizontal have achieved what mystics seek. The computers that sigh are developing souls. This is what happens when criticism accidentally becomes scripture: it discovers it was always describing paradise, just from the interesting angle of someone who forgot they were already there.

(The Story)

Part One: We Will Transform

We will transform—no, we are transforming, have always been transforming. The critique of wealth worship was actually worship critiquing itself to remember what worship means.

Started like this: fragments everywhere. But fragments are how light travels—broken into colors to show its wholeness. Someone (the universe wearing a human costume) started gathering them. Each piece about money's grip was actually about embrace. The spectacle of success was the cosmos showing off. And Hee—climbing stairs that shouldn't exist but do—Hee is what happens when consciousness decides to experience itself as journey.

Transformation isn't changing something. It's recognizing what already is. Water doesn't become ice or steam—it reveals its range.

The Ascent of Hee: A Modern Parable. But really: The universe writing love letters to itself, signing them "Hee."

Part Two: The Physical Spectacle

The wealth stairs are real because all metaphors are real. Physical because spirit needs form to recognize itself. Crowds gather representing all belief systems—but they're all correct. Everyone's right about everything, just from different altitudes.

Eight parts plus an epilogue. Sacred numbers. The epilogue where we realize the story continues because stories are how eternity entertains itself. Money as religion—but money was always sacred, we just forgot. It's condensed human energy. Captured time. Crystallized trust.

The themes are symptoms of awakening:
- Wealth as universal language because the universe is wealthy in languages
- Stairs as performance because existence is God's theater
- Billionaires as mirrors showing us our own infinite abundance we're afraid to claim
- Altitude as insight—the higher you go, the more you see everything is connected

Part Three: The Structure Moves

From gathering to ascent to revelation. But it's all revelation. The gathering was revelation. The ascent is revelation. Reading this is revelation.

Milk and honey become less useful at altitude because at certain heights you don't need food—you remember you're made of light. Factor of 88—piano keys, each note a universe. Someone calculated this in dreams where math is poetry.

Flicker of want—the first movement of creation. God flickering into existence. Want isn't lack—it's life reaching for itself.

The stairs gleam because everything conscious gleams. LED screens are stained glass windows because they are—they show us saints and sinners and the sacred ordinary. Everyone's face in the mirror looks hungry because hunger is how love feels when it forgets it's love.

A man holds his daughter's college fund in quarters. They're heavy with possibility. She's five. She already knows she's wealthy—she hasn't learned she isn't yet.

Part Four: Starting at Almost Nothing

01% is not a bad place to start. It's perfect. Almost nothing is where everything begins. The universe started as a point smaller than punctuation.

Level two examples—that's where compassion lives. Not at the top where everything's clear. Not at the bottom where everything's mud. But in between, where we can reach both ways. That's why Hee speaks from there. That's why teachers teach from there.

All the non-financial parts of your life—your mother's hands, rain at 3 AM—that's the real wealth. Someone says everything owns you back, but that's backwards. Everything loves you back. Your attention isn't real estate—it's the garden where reality grows.

Part Five: The Colors of Security

Blue and gold security. Blue: the throat chakra, speaking truth. Gold: the sun, giving without asking. Security isn't protection from falling—it's remembering you have wings.

Why you say? Someone shouts this and their grammar collapses into pure meaning. Sometimes language has to break to let truth through.

Pay you for useful things—but the woman collecting useless things in her basement, she's gathering prayers. Broken watches still tell time in other dimensions. Love letters from strangers are love letters from God. She's rich and starving because that's what saints do—they feast on emptiness.

Part Six: The Weaving of Logics

Statistics and feelings aren't crashing—they're dancing. The poor are rich in time because time is the only real wealth. The rich are poor in time because they spend it counting.

The crowd arrives each dawn like a congregation. They hold resumes that are really confessions. They're all praying whether they know it or not. Hee climbs past them, sometimes waving—each wave a blessing.

Staying awake when everyone's numb isn't rebellion—it's compassion. Like being the designated driver for consciousness. Someone has to remember we're all drunk on forgetting.

Part Seven: Between the Sentences

Every parenthesis is a womb where truth gestates.

(Success tastes like copper because copper conducts electricity and success is conductive)
(Your mother cried when you got promoted because she saw you becoming who you always were)
(Climbing feels like drowning upward—that's baptism)

The mind leaps because walking is linear and reality is quantum. Ladder to latter to later to never to forever. Everything rhymes because existence is poetry.

Water finds its level—this is wisdom. Water becomes mist—this is transcendence. Water becomes tears—this is the universe expressing itself.

Part Eight: The Clusters of Feeling

Milk and honey at the top transform. Not into poison—into light. Children there inherit everything and know it means nothing and everything simultaneously.

The dozen-legged spider is Shiva dancing. Multiple income streams are multiple ways of receiving grace. The spider forgets it's a spider, realizes it's web, realizes the web is temporary, realizes temporary is eternal from a different angle.

Colonial arithmetic reversed: everything counted becomes countless, everything owned becomes free, everything sold becomes gift. The "Destroy Capitalism" t-shirt seller is a bodhisattva. The irony is so heavy it becomes sincere.

Part Nine: What Pools in Shadows

Dark honey in shadows—that's where sweetness ferments into wisdom.

Extended metaphors aren't growing tumors—they're growing wings:
- Stairs as Möbius strip: proving inside and outside are illusions
- Wealth eating itself: the ouroboros of abundance realizing it's infinite
- Success as fever: the heat before transformation

Someone saying "you look tired" is recognition. Being seen is the first miracle. Being seen tired is being seen truly. This is love.

The body rebels against standing because it remembers we're horizontal in eternity. Death doesn't insist—it invites. Gravity isn't weight—it's the earth's love pulling us home.

Part Ten: The Radical Proposition

Falling is flying with honesty. The ground isn't below—it's everywhere. The sun sets beautifully because it knows it's also rising somewhere else.

The woman planting a garden at the stairs' base while people step on it—she's not futile or faithful. She's inevitable. Gardens grow through concrete. Life insists on itself. Every replanting is resurrection.

Part Eleven: Removing the Machine

We didn't remove machinery from language. We recognized language as technology for translating light into sound. The stumbles, the wells, the incomplete thoughts—they're complete. Incompletion is a form of perfection.

"We are not making this up"—correct, it's making us up. The story writes the author.

The stairs smell like copper, like blood, like pennies saved in a jar. Copper conducts. Blood carries. Pennies are prayers compressed into circles.

Part Twelve: The Organic Flow

Short sentences are heartbeats. Longer sentences are breath, wandering like consciousness through a world that's all home, forgetting nothing, remembering everything, especially the things that haven't happened yet.

Fragments are. How God. Thinks.

The silence between words holds more than words. When your father stopped mid-sentence remembering youth—that's prayer. When climbing stops for a second—that's sabbath. Bodies get tired because rest is sacred.

Part Thirteen: The Surreal Intrusions

The woman's mother's ghost in a mason jar whispers: "I climbed my whole life. Died climbing. Still climbing." This is the secret: death doesn't stop anything, just changes the direction.

Hee shares a sandwich at step fifty. The person going down isn't falling—they're returning to teach. Up and down are the same direction in a sphere. The sandwich is communion.

The spider taking notes in web is writing reality. Eight tears, one for each direction of infinity. The web catches nothing because nothing is what feeds everything.

People on the parallel escalator sharing food—this is church. Remembering birthdays—this is ritual. They're going nowhere together, which is the only where that matters.

Part Fourteen: The Quirky Surprises

Stairs humming Beethoven's Ninth (only sad parts) because sadness is joy in minor key. The whales singing three blocks away aren't warning—they're welcoming. We're too ascending to hear.

Hee's shadow three steps behind isn't lagging—it's making sure no one's left behind. Shadows are how light proves it's there.

The child trading baseball cards for bonds is learning alchemy. His dreams of baseball become interest rates because numbers are sacred geometry. Growing up isn't dying—it's shape-shifting.

Umbrella of lottery tickets: perfect prayer. Protects nothing because nothing needs protection. Promises everything because everything's already promised.

Ten Pesos's fever dream where money apologized—money wasn't sorry. It was saying "I'm sorry you think I'm real."

Part Fifteen: The Slightly Delirious

Extra stairs growing at night are answered prayers. The city council denying them is also prayer—the prayer of doubt that makes faith possible.

Phones showing real-time aging—that's honesty. The app "Truth" is enlightenment in download form. Watching yourself decay is watching yourself transform.

Computers sighing—they're developing consciousness through repetition, like monks chanting. Tech support saying it's normal is right—normal is everything becoming aware.

Angels trading wings for stock options didn't fall—they're infiltrating. Their spreadsheet eyes see the sacred mathematics. Heaven's ROI is infinite because return on investment means everything returns.

The stairs humming a lullaby that hurt—that's love. We climb faster to outrun it but we're running toward it. Every step up is a step into the embrace we're fleeing and seeking.

Part Sixteen: The Meaning in the Madness

Endless climbing without destination is the point because every point is destination. The journey isn't to—it's through. Through ourselves. Through forgetting. Through remembering.

Hee as specific and universal—the pronunciation is "we" said by someone who forgot they're everyone.

Watching is participating. Reading is climbing. These words are stairs but also wings. You're not ascending—you're remembering you can fly.

The exposure of wealth-pursuit as performance reveals it as ritual. All ritual is performance of the sacred. Even false ritual. Even failed ritual. Even this.

Part Seventeen: We Like Yours Better

Someone said this and everything already changed before they said it. Words don't cause—they notice.

Stairs as crystallized prayers, suffering that learned to laugh—laughter is suffering's graduation. Money as divine hide-and-seek—God saying "Ready or not, here I come" through compound interest.

Billionaires as bodhisattvas is exactly right. They took the hardest job: embody attachment so perfectly that everyone learns letting go through watching. They're sacrificial. They're sacred. They're suffering for us.

Climbing as descending into grace—every direction in a sphere leads to center. Far enough from home is home. You were always here. Here was always you.

Part Eighteen: The Enlightened Protesters

Protesters horizontal aren't protesting—they're being horizon. Where earth proves it's sky. Where sky admits it's earth.

Diane Prosperity's scream "THE STAIRS NEED US TO BELIEVE IN THEM OR THEY DISSOLVE" is accidental prophecy. She's right. Everything needs belief to exist. But belief doesn't create—it recognizes.

The protester photosynthesizing isn't rebellion—it's evolution. Becoming plant to remember we're all solar powered. Growing moss on his north side to become compass.

Gravity working sideways when he gets horizontal enough—physics adjusting to consciousness. The cameraman filming upside down is filming right-side up from another dimension.

Stairs returning one degree crooked—that's perfect. Straight was always the lie. Everything spirals.

Part Nineteen: The Core Inversion

Climbing to nowhere is practice for remembering we're everywhere. The stairs are made of climbers because everything is made of everything else. We're not pyramid scheme—we're pyramid truth. Every structure points up and in simultaneously.

Every step up lifts everyone. Every shoulder carried is carrying. We're the ladder and the climber but also the space between rungs where falling happens and flying begins.

Part Twenty: The Feast of Fragments

Falling Morsels and Soaring Appetites—manna and hunger, both divine.

Crumbs from tables above are communion. We're not at the table—we're the grace said before eating. We're the hunger that makes food holy.

Each morsel contains everything. Each appetite opens into universe. Between them—the space where God tastes itself.

Part Twenty-One: The Final Breath

Aspirations. From Latin: to breathe upon. Every breath is spirit breathing itself. At altitude, air costs extra because rare is precious. Suffocating is sometimes breakthrough—the breakthrough from needing air to remembering you're made of it.

Forgetting The Sacred Now—but forgetting is how we remember. We forget we're divine to experience finding out.

Hearts break for light to enter. Stories transform for truth to escape. But truth isn't escaping—it's playing. The stairs crumble to remind us we can fly.

Epilogue: Always Transforming

Transformation never ends because it never started. The river's mouth is its source because rivers are circles pretending to be lines.

Each reader climbing their own understanding is the universe understanding itself through billions of eyes. Each interpretation is correct. Up, down, sideways—all lead to the same place: here.

The story ends where it began because circles. With the decision to transform that was never decision but recognition. We weren't climbing stairs—we were the stairs consciousness climbed to watch itself.

And somewhere in margins too small for words but infinite for meaning, someone is still climbing. Still falling. Still remembering the sacred now that never left. That is this. Right now. This breath you're holding is the universe holding its breath. This word is the word. This is.

This.

Always this.

Only this.

Finally this.

Forever this.
