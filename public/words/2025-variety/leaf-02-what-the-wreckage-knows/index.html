Leaf #2 — Oyparaploo

Production Specifications

Format Single leaf (one sheet) Trim Size 5 × 7 inches Stock 80–100 lb uncoated text, cream or
natural white Ink Black only Sides Front: content / Back: colophon + mark

Recommended Stock Color

Terracotta (#B87A5E) — Warm rust-orange. Echoes the soil, the manure, the earth that doesn't
care which country shat on it.

Maker's Mark

Mark #5 from the Depth Spirals family. Second leaf in the series.

Transformation Approach

Source: 14 questions asking for humor that is raw, jagged, ugly—broken glass, decaying reason,

chaos as law. Then the turn: manure, working hands, silent endurance, tenderness. A wise refugee and

naive immigrant speaking.

Approach: Answer the questions by writing the piece they call for. Not prose about the prompts,

but the voice itself: two women in a garden, one old and knowing, one young and watching. Dialogue

that holds both broken glass and honey.
Word count: ~210 words. Fits comfortably on one side with room to breathe.

Front (Content Side)

What the Wreckage Knows

The old woman laughs like something breaking. "You want to know what's funny?"
she says. "The soil here smells like the soil back home. Manure is manure. The
earth doesn't care which country shat on it."

The young one watches her hands—cracked, patient, moving through the dirt like
they remember a different garden. "Did you think it would be different?"

"I thought I would be different." She pulls a weed, examines its roots. "I am the
same person who left. Just further from the door."

Between them, the rows of vegetables do not care about borders. The tomatoes
ripen in whatever language you speak to them. The beans climb toward light that
has traveled eight minutes from a star that also doesn't know the word refugee.

"What keeps you going?" the young one asks.

The old woman's laugh again—glass and honey, jagged and warm. "The same
thing that keeps the worms going. The work is here. The hands are here. What
else should they do?"
She gestures at the field, the fog lifting, the silent endurance of green things
pushing through.

"The world chews us up," she says. "But look—we are still spitting seeds."

Printing Instructions

Paper Cut to 5×7" before printing, or print on letter and trim Sides Print front, flip, print back
(align carefully) Scale 100% (no fit-to-page)

Alternative: Print front only on heavier stock (100 lb+). The leaf can stand with a blank back—the

silence after the content.

Series Context

This is Leaf #2—the second single-sheet piece in the Oyparaploo series. Where Leaf #1 held a garden
scene with daisies and laborers, this one holds two voices in a different garden: a refugee and an
immigrant, speaking across generations about what survives.
