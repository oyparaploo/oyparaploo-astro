The Gift You're Giving

When Speaking Becomes Generosity

← Words / 2025 Variety

From Performance to Present

When you think of speaking as gift-giving instead of performing, everything shifts. A gift is about generosity, not proving something. You're not trying to impress; you're offering something valuable.

The worst speakers take from audiences—time, attention, energy—without giving back. They're mining for applause or validation. But when someone approaches speaking as gift-giving, their whole presence changes. They're not nervous the same way because they're focused on what they're giving, not getting.

Varieties of Offering

What gifts can we give through speaking? Permission—to see differently, feel things, try something new. Understanding—helping people make sense of complexity. Connection—making people feel less alone.

For artists, sharing struggles becomes the gift of "me too." Technical knowledge shared freely, even to potential competitors, shows real generosity. The artists who share most freely often become most successful—people remember who gave them something valuable.

Wrapping Matters

How you give matters as much as what. Information shared condescendingly becomes a poisoned gift. Simple knowledge shared with genuine care becomes precious. I've seen artists discuss basic color mixing with such joy it felt like learning magic.

The tone, pace, warmth—that's the wrapping paper. Deliver valuable information with contempt or indifference, you've ruined the gift.

Transaction Versus Gift

Reading from scripts feels wrong because gifts require presence. You're completing a transaction—delivering information. Real speaking offers a gift, creating moments of exchange, not broadcast.

Even personal stories must serve audiences. Sharing healed scars and learned wisdom is a gift. Bleeding openly and asking for help is a request. Audiences know the difference immediately.

Only Yours to Give

The most valuable gift is your genuine perspective, your real experience, your actual insights. Anyone can recite information. Only you can give your specific understanding. That's the irreplaceable gift.

When you realize this, speaking becomes less scary. You're not competing; you're contributing. Adding your verse to the conversation.

Responsive Generosity

Mastery comes when speaking becomes responsive gift-giving. Really seeing who's in front of you and what they need. Not changing who you are, but recognizing what would be most generous in this moment, with these people.

The shift from "I have to perform" to "I have something to give"—from performance to generosity—changes everything. People don't remember what you said as much as what you gave them. Whether confidence, permission, understanding, or just the feeling of being seen.
