Brrd Roots ~ Resurrection of Christ ~ Free Verse ~ Part One Versions

Words / 2025 Variety

It begins in a monastery.
It begins in silence so thick it holds the weight of centuries.
It begins where shadows don’t just linger,
but stretch and shift,
clinging to walls like the breaths of those who came before.

The air?
Heavy, alive.
It carries the musk of beeswax, the damp whispers of stone,
the faint, aching sweetness of ancient parchment.
The air doesn’t just surround you.
The air knows you.
It presses against your skin,
offering what the world has tried to forget.

It begins here, in this sacred stillness.

And here, Tomas sits.
Tomas, the monk with ink-stained fingers,
the man with history on his back and eternity in his hands.

A gilded quill trembles above the page,
its point hovering between hesitation and hope.
The page waits.
The page whispers:

Do you see?
Do you believe?
Does this matter?

Christ rises before him,
golden light spilling—no—
breaking from wounds that do not bleed.
Wounds that shine.
Wounds that sing.

And for a moment, Tomas feels it.
The hum curling at the edges of his thoughts.
The vibration of something ancient,
something eternal brushing against his skin.

Does this matter?
The question cuts through him.
A blade made of doubt.
Does this light he carves into vellum matter—
when the world devours itself just beyond these walls?

Outside, the earth shatters.
Villages burn beneath skies choked with ash,
skies so black the sun hides its face.
Fields crumble into bone-dry dust.
Rivers rise, greedy and unyielding,
waters swallowing homes,
swallowing names,
swallowing the echoes of love spoken in their halls.

The screams fade.
And silence—
the kind of silence that cuts deeper than any cry—
settles into the earth.

And yet, here Tomas sits.
Here he sits, gilding halos onto parchment,
etching glory onto pages so fragile
they quiver beneath his trembling hands.

What good is this light, Tomas wonders,
when the world drowns in shadows?

The thought trembles in his chest.
The quill trembles in his hand.

He closes his eyes,
letting the weight of memory pull him under.

Ash. Smoke. Embers still glowing.
A boy kneeling beside his mother,
their home scattered around them like shattered porcelain.

Her hands trembling as she lights a single candle.
Its flame shivers against the vastness of night.
Her voice, soft but steady—
steady as the earth beneath their knees:

“Light endures.”

She didn’t say it with certainty.
She said it with desperation.
She said it with defiance.

And Tomas had believed her.
Then.

He had believed in that fragile flame,
believed it might stretch its trembling fingers to heaven,
pull eternity down into their dust.

He had clung to that moment,
threading salvation into embers that should have burned out.

But now?
Now her words feel thin.
Fragile.
Like cobwebs caught on the edge of a storm.

He aches to believe her still.
He needs to believe her still.
But the question circles him, relentless:
Does light endure?

Picture this:
Christ—
not stepping,
not walking,
but rising.

The air bends around Him, shimmering, breaking.
As though creation itself cannot hold the weight of His radiance.

His wounds—
not scars,
but doorways.
Doorways through which grace pours like water from a spring.

This light—
relentless, golden, alive—
does not erase the fractures.
It makes them glow.

Tomas paints this with trembling hands.
Each stroke is heavy with hope,
each line etched with the echo of grace.

The angels hang in the air,
wings quivering, faces frozen between awe and silence.

The women kneel at the tomb,
their astonishment pressing against the world,
a silence louder than all the cries that came before.

Reality cracks.
Reality bends.
And out of its fractures, grace rises.

And in his mind, Tomas sees Mariam.
Mariam kneeling in the village chapel below.

Her hands lighting candle after candle,
her prayers breaking against her lips.

Half hope.
Half despair.
For the children the floodwaters took.

He wonders:
Would she see herself here?
Would she see herself in this moment,
this light weaving its way through her sorrow?

Would she feel it thread hope
into the cracks of her breaking heart?

Because this light Tomas inks,
this illuminated Resurrection,
is not just triumph.

It is defiance.
It is declaration.
It is persistence.

It is light persists.

Even in the fractures of the world.
Even in the empty spaces despair would claim.
Even in the shadows no one dares to touch.

Light persists.

It threads itself into the broken.
It spills into the unspoken.

Collapse.
Tomas knows its taste.

Collapse is ash on the tongue.
Collapse is smoke in the throat.

He remembers standing barefoot in the ruins of his childhood.
A locket warm from the fire pressed into his palm.

“Light endures,” his mother had said.
Soft.
Shaking.
But unbroken.

She planted that seed in him—
a seed that would root itself deep.
Fragile, yes.
But alive.

And perhaps that’s what light is.
Not strength.
Not glory.
But life pressing its way through dirt,
through stone,
through the silence despair leaves behind.

From the chapel below, a hymn rises.

Threaded with grief,
threaded with hallelujahs.

It weaves through stone,
a melody that carries both sorrow and hope
like two hands clasped together.

Tomas hears it.
And in his mind, he sees the villagers.
Their faces flickering in the light of trembling flames.

There is Mariam.
Her hands steady now.
Her voice strong.
Strong, because even she knows.

And then there is the page.
The illuminated Resurrection gleams beneath the candlelight.
Christ’s wounds shimmer like constellations,
like galaxies spilling into the cracks of the world.

Tomas imagines them all:
Mariam.
His mother.
The villagers whose names he’ll never know.

He imagines them kneeling within this impossible radiance.
And he imagines the light finding them.
Threading itself into their fractures,
pressing hope into their bones.

And Tomas—finally, finally—understands.

Grace does not erase the fractures.
Grace illuminates them.

Light does not extinguish pain.
Light reveals the beauty still breathing beneath it.

This is why, Tomas whispers.
His voice steady now.
Soft.
But unyielding.

This is why light endures.

So now, reader—
this question is yours:
What light will you carry forward?

Not monuments.
Not victories.
But light.

not because of defiance,
but because of quiet resilience.

In the manuscripts Tomas preserves,
the Resurrection ripples outward—
not contained, not bound.

Christ stands at the axis of creation itself,
His wounds refracting light,
spilling grace into every corner of time and space.

Tomas wonders,
*Could this light stretch beyond these pages?
Could it touch the fractured world beyond these walls?*

The question presses against him, heavy, yearning.

Below the monastery, voices rise—
a hymn carried by the stone corridors,
threaded with grief and hallelujahs.

Mariam sings among the villagers,
their faces lit by trembling flames.
Her voice steady now.
Her hands no longer trembling.

For a moment, Tomas feels it—
the weight of grace pressing gently into the cracks of his doubt.

Grace does not mend what is broken.
Grace illuminates it.

And then, there is the page.
The illuminated Resurrection gleams beneath candlelight.
Christ’s wounds shimmer like constellations,
spilling light onto the parchment, onto the spaces Tomas thought empty.

He imagines them—
Mariam, his mother, the villagers.
Kneeling within this impossible radiance.

And finally, Tomas understands.

Grace does not erase sorrow.
It threads itself through it.

Light does not extinguish pain.
It reveals the beauty still breathing beneath it.

This is why, Tomas whispers.
His voice steady now, soft but insistent.
This is why light endures.

So what remains, reader?
Not monuments, nor victories.

But the light you choose to carry forward.
