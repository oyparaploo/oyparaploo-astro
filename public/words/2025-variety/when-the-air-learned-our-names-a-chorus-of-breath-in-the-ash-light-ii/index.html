The sun is a rumor…

Words / 2025 Variety

The sun is a rumor, she thinks, standing at the window that once showed her the television tower, the same window where her grandmother stood watching the Wall go up, the same window that now shows only silted sky, and she wonders if every generation has its own opacity, its own way of not seeing.

The pills for climate inflected prose sit in their capsules on the kitchen table—the same table where her mother sorted ration cards, where she herself once sorted love letters, where now she sorts emergency broadcasts by date, by severity, by the particular taste of ash they describe.

Stay and help versus flee.

But this is the wrong question, has always been the wrong question, like asking whether to love the dying or the dead, whether to document the disappearing or disappear into the documentation. Her grandmother stayed through the bombing. Her mother stayed through the Wall. She stays through the smoke. Each staying is different. Each staying is the same.

The actors at their lecterns under flashing lights are reading Dense factual footnotes, but she knows—as her body knows the weight of waiting, as her lungs know the weight of breathing—that facts are only the skeleton. The flesh is in the Primary Sensorium: smell and taste of ash that connects this moment to every burning, every ending, every beginning that rises from destruction.

In the desk drawer—the desk that was her father's, that was nobody's, that was the state's, that is hers—she keeps the TikTok transcripts next to the senate hearing transcripts next to her grandmother's diary from 1945. Lack of drama, abundance of drama, the drama of lack. All of it the same story told in different frequencies.

Displacement particulate memory erodes.

Like the way she can no longer remember his face without photographs, can no longer remember clean air without longing, can no longer remember the Wall without nostalgia for its clarity. At least a wall was visible. This atmospheric threat has no checkpoints, no guards, only the slow suffocation of everything once taken for granted.

She writes: Record versus forget—but recording is forgetting, forgetting is recording, each act of memory an act of transformation. The musicians flee as a megafire darkens a western sky, and she thinks of the orchestra that played while Dresden burned, thinks of the pianist who kept playing while the Wall fell, thinks of all the music that continues despite, because of, through.

Time will not be the driving engine of any plot.

Time hiccups. She is twenty-five meeting him at the theater. She is fifty-five breathing through a T-shirt. She is not yet born. She is already dead. The smoke makes all moments simultaneous, like the moment when the Wall both existed and didn't, when love both was and wasn't, when the future both approached and receded.

In the margins of her notebook—Syllabus module: pair the story with Fire Weather—she writes the real curriculum: How to breathe each other's breath. How to taste memory in bread. How to love in the time of atmospheric dissolution.

We breathe through T-shirts and lullabies.

The lullabies are her grandmother's, are her mother's, are hers, are no one's. They translate through dust storms that cloak voice notes, each translation a small betrayal, a small salvation. She counts the days, but the days count differently now—not forward but sideways, not linear but layered like sediment, like ash, like the weight of all the stories we tell ourselves about staying.

Love versus duty under toxic skies.

But love is duty, duty is love, the binary dissolves like everything else in this new atmosphere where lineage blurred by dust means she is her grandmother fleeing, her mother staying, herself caught between motion and stillness, between the personal flights of fancy at micro-scale and the Cultural memories of Global systems at macro-scale.

The storm opacity as mentality: this is what interests her. Not the storm as metaphor but as consciousness, as way of being, as the new condition in which all other conditions must be understood. Hope versus cynicism within climate activism—another false binary in a world that demands both, neither, something unnamed that exists in the space between.

She reads the emergency alerts as epistolary forms, as love letters from a dying system to itself. Data feeds structures punctured by human breathing, by the irregular rhythm of hearts that continue despite everything to beat, despite everything to break, despite everything to go on breaking.

Why It Matters.

It doesn't. It does. It's the only thing that—

The sentence never completes, can never complete, like the love that ended but didn't end, like the country that disappeared but remains, like the air that's unbreathable but breathed. She thinks of the prize winners who haven't yet written the storm reports and air-quality alerts as their front and center framework, thinks perhaps the framework must first dissolve before it can be written.

In the end—but there is no end, only this continuous middle, this ongoing meanwhile—she returns to the window. The same window. The different window. The window that frames only what we allow it to frame. Outside, ash drifts like gray snow, like time made visible, like all the words for snow she learned as a child now repurposed for this new precipitation of endings.

Can you still hear us?

The question arrives from everywhere, from nowhere, from the space between frequencies where static meets signal meets silence. She doesn't answer. The answer is in the breathing. The answer is in the staying. The answer is in the way she places one word after another, like placing one foot after another through ruins, through rubble, through whatever comes after the after.

It reads like a—

The sentence fragments, refuses completion, like everything now refuses the completion we once called conclusion, resolution, ending. There are no endings. Only this continuing. Only this breathing through. Only this registration of what is while it still is, before it becomes what was, what might have been, what never could be again.

She closes the notebook. Opens the notebook. Closes it. Opens. This too is a kind of breathing.

They arrive as hybrid fictional data, part truth, part hallucination—like my dating profile claiming I'm "emotionally available" and "breathes normally."

So here I am, Alternating between maximalist paragraphs about our planetary demise and tweet-length interjections of pure dread. Why? Because that's how we process apocalypse now! Long-form anxiety interrupted by push notifications about new ways we're dying!

Why It Matters, you ask? Everything matters when nothing matters! My therapist charges $350 per session to tell me this is "catastrophic thinking," but have you seen the August Fog Deluge forecast? It reads like a Parable for a Sower if the sower was on Lexapro and had an adjustable-rate mortgage!
