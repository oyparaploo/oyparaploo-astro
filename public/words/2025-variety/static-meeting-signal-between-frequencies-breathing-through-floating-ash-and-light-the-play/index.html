Static meeting Signal between Frequencies ~ Breathing Through Floating Ash and Light

Words / 2025 Variety

( The Play )

## Theater Production Instructions and Performance Guide

### I. CONCEPTUAL FOUNDATION

**Core Vision**: Create a theatrical experience that breathes with the audience, where the boundary between performance and shared survival dissolves. The production should feel like attending both a wake and a birth, a documentary and a fever dream.

**Tonal Evolution**: Structure the performance in three atmospheric movements:
1. **Documentary Realism** (Acts 1-2): Grounded, tactile, recognizable
2. **Magical Disruption** (Acts 3-4): Reality bends but maintains emotional truth
3. **Delirious Transcendence** (Act 5): Time hiccups, logic surrenders to poetry

### II. ENVIRONMENTAL DESIGN

**The Breathing Space**:
- Design the theater as a failing ecosystem that regenerates through community
- Install HEPA filters as visible set pieces that actors interact with
- Create "air quality zones" with varying visibility using haze machines
- Suspend ash-like particles (biodegradable paper/fabric) that fall intermittently

**Lighting Architecture**:
- Begin with harsh emergency lighting (red exit signs prominent)
- Gradually introduce bioluminescent greens and golds (the mushrooms)
- Use handheld lights (phones, flashlights) carried by actors
- Create the "seventeen-minute miracle" with sudden, blinding clarity
- End with audience members given small LED lights to hold

### III. SOUND DESIGN INSTRUCTIONS

**The Archivist's World**:
- Pre-record actual city sounds from your location
- Layer these with the cast's breathing patterns recorded during rehearsal
- Install speakers throughout the space (not just traditional positions)
- Create a "degradation protocol" where sound quality decreases with "air quality"
- Emergency broadcast interruptions should feel viscerally real

**Static and Signal**:
- Develop a vocabulary of static that conveys different emotions
- Train actors to speak "through" white noise generators
- Create moments where only gesture/touch can communicate
- The question "Can you still hear us?" should emerge from unexpected sources

### IV. ENSEMBLE DEVELOPMENT

**Character Constellation**:
- **The Archivist**: Guardian of memory, performed with visible recording equipment
- **The Driver**: Calculator of escape, but their car never actually moves
- **The Diabetic Neighbor**: Living countdown clock with visible insulin props
- **The Climate Reporter**: Cannot stop documenting even as reality warps
- **Mrs. Chen**: Baker of impossible bread (real bread shared with audience)
- **Senator Fluffbottom**: Puppet that evolves throughout, gaining wings

**Chorus Work**:
- Develop a "smoke movement vocabulary" - how bodies move through thickness
- Create synchronized breathing patterns that become percussive
- Build scenes where the ensemble becomes the fire, the ash, the static
- Practice "molecular performance" - actors as particles of smoke

### V. PROP AND COSTUME PHILOSOPHY

**Functional Beauty**:
- Every costume includes a real or suggested mask
- Pockets visible and used for sharing resources
- Layers that can be given away during performance
- Distressing that looks both ancient and freshly destroyed

**Living Props**:
- Sourdough starter maintained throughout run (fed on stage)
- Real plants that actors tend between scenes
- Water bottles distributed to audience members
- Insulin vials (prop) that glow under certain lights

### VI. AUDIENCE INTEGRATION

**Participatory Elements**:
- Audience receives masks upon entry (optional wearing)
- Emergency broadcast instructions delivered to their phones
- Moments where they must share resources with neighbors
- The wall writing scene invites audience additions
- Final scene: audience recordings played back

**Spatial Dynamics**:
- No fixed seating - audiences can move to "clearer air"
- Create refugee center spaces with blankets and supplies
- Install community bulletin boards for real mutual aid
- Exit doors remain visibly accessible (part of the reality)

### VII. REHEARSAL METHODOLOGIES

**Building the World**:
1. **Week 1-2**: Actors live with real emergency supplies, practice rationing
2. **Week 3-4**: Develop personal "smoke languages" and movement
3. **Week 5-6**: Time distortion exercises - scenes played backwards/simultaneously
4. **Week 7-8**: Community building - actors organize actual mutual aid

**Special Techniques**:
- **Breath Work**: Every rehearsal begins with conscious breathing exercises
- **Object Stories**: Each prop must contain three different memories
- **Static Practice**: Actors learn to perform "between frequencies"
- **Documentary Moments**: Cast records real stories from climate disasters

### VIII. TECHNICAL MAGIC

**The Impossible Elements**:
- **Time Hiccups**: Lights flicker, actors reverse movements mid-gesture
- **Upward Rain**: Ribbons on invisible fishing line, pulled by ensemble
- **Sentient Bread**: Projection mapping on real loaves
- **Third Wing**: Appears through strategic lighting and puppetry
- **Memory Breathing**: Scent design that triggers shared experiences

### IX. PERFORMANCE RHYTHM

**Breathing Structure**:
- Each act follows an inhale/hold/exhale pattern
- Build in actual rest moments for actor recovery
- Emergency alerts punctuate but don't interrupt flow
- Silence becomes as choreographed as speech

**Evolution Protocol**:
- First performance of run is most "realistic"
- Each subsequent show adds more surreal elements
- By closing night, the production has "mutated" significantly
- Document changes as the Archivist would

### X. CLOSING INSTRUCTIONS

**The Final Question**:
End with every performer and audience member asking "Can you still hear us?" in their own time, creating a cascade of voices that continues even after house lights rise. The answer lives not in words but in the shared breath of the room.

**Legacy Elements**:
- Create actual mutual aid networks from audience connections
- Archive recordings made during the run
- Share Mrs. Chen's bread recipe
- Leave the wall writings as installation art

**Remember**: This production should feel like a necessary gathering, not a performance. The magic isn't in perfect execution but in the moments when fiction becomes instruction manual, when metaphor becomes survival strategy, when breathing together becomes both the challenge and the solution.

*The theater itself becomes the archive, the shelter, the space between frequencies where static meets signal and we learn, finally, terribly, beautifully, what it means to hold on to each other.*

# Character Dialogue Development for "Static meeting Signal between Frequencies"

## Character Profiles

**TORD** - The Driver. Methodical, counts everything, speaks in fuel calculations and distances. Carries others but going nowhere.

**GYOA** - The Archivist. Records everything, speaks in frequencies and memory. Her words often play back at unexpected times.

**KIKI** - The Climate Reporter. Documents obsessively, caught between data and art. Her language fractures as reality bends.

**JOWEE** - The Baker (Mrs. Chen reimagined). Speaks in flavors and temperatures. Their words taste like memory.

---

## ACT ONE: Documentary Realism
*[Air Quality Index: 147 - Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups]*

**GYOA**: *(adjusting recording levels)* Testing... testing... Tuesday, 4:17 PM. The construction noise from the Hartley Building stopped at 4:15. That's... that's the third silence today.

**TORD**: Fuel's at three-quarters. Sixty-two miles to the coast, but the 580's blocked. Reroute adds twenty-three miles. At current consumption—

**KIKI**: *(filming)* Are you calculating again?

**TORD**: Always. Miles per gallon times remaining fuel divided by—

**JOWEE**: *(entering with a covered bowl)* Anyone want bread? The starter's been strange lately. Bubbling in patterns.

**GYOA**: What kind of patterns?

**JOWEE**: Like... like it's trying to spell something. Here, taste.

**KIKI**: *(still filming)* This is content. No wait—this is data. No... *(frustration)* I can't tell anymore.

**TORD**: We have seventeen minutes before the next air quality alert.

**GYOA**: *(into recorder)* Subject claims precognitive knowledge of emergency broadcast timing—

**TORD**: Not precognitive. Pattern recognition. Every six hours when the winds shift.

**JOWEE**: The bread tastes different when you talk about time. Try it.

**KIKI**: Can you still hear us?

**ALL**: *(pause, uncomfortable)*

**GYOA**: I... yes. Why did you ask that?

**KIKI**: I don't know. It just... came out.

---

## ACT THREE: Magical Disruption
*[Air Quality Index: 367 - Hazardous]*

**TORD**: *(to his empty hands, as if holding a steering wheel)* Take me to yesterday's hope.

**GYOA**: You're not driving, Tord.

**TORD**: I'm always driving. Even standing still, we're moving at... at... *(confused)* The numbers are tasting purple.

**JOWEE**: That's the smoke. Tuesday's smoke is musical. Listen—

**KIKI**: *(camera glitching)* My footage shows people who aren't here. Look—there's you, Gyoa, but young. Warning about fires that haven't—that won't—that might—

**GYOA**: *(overlapping with her recorded voice playing from nowhere)* "Can you still hear us? Can you still—still—still—"

**JOWEE**: The bread remembers now. This slice contains the memory of rain.

**TORD**: Rain doesn't have memory.

**JOWEE**: Taste it.

**TORD**: *(takes a bite, stunned)* April. April 15th, 2019. The last time it rained for more than an hour.

**KIKI**: How do you know the exact—

**TORD**: The bread told me. No. I calculated. No. I... *(breathing heavily)* The oxygen's too thick.

**GYOA**: We're sharing air now. Every breath you take has been through someone else's lungs.

**KIKI**: *(filming her own hand)* Why do I have three shadows?

**JOWEE**: Because tomorrow you'll need them.

---

## THE SEVENTEEN-MINUTE MIRACLE
*[Air Quality Index: CLEAR]*

**ALL**: *(gasping, amazed)*

**TORD**: I can see... I can see the actual distance. Not in miles. In... in hope.

**GYOA**: *(recorder off, speaking directly)* Quick—everyone memorize this feeling. The weight of clear air.

**KIKI**: I dropped my phone. I... I don't want to pick it up.

**JOWEE**: The bread is singing. Can you hear it?

**TORD**: Everything's singing. The distance between us is singing.

**GYOA**: *(laughing)* I'm not recording this. I'm just... being in it.

**KIKI**: Can you still hear us?

**ALL**: *(in unison, joyful)* YES!

**JOWEE**: We're the answer. We've always been the answer.

**TORD**: Sixteen minutes, thirty seconds remaining.

**GYOA**: Don't count. Just breathe.

**TORD**: I can't stop. But I'm counting breaths now. Each one is... is infinity.

**KIKI**: *(picking up phone, then putting it down again)* No. Memory is enough.

---

## ACT FIVE: Delirious Transcendence
*[Air Quality Index: ∞]*

**GYOA**: *(her voice coming from multiple sources)* Today's sounds include tomorrow's birds and yesterday's silence and the color of Jowee's laughter.

**TORD**: I drove here through three timelines. In one, we've already arrived. In another, we never left.

**KIKI**: *(filming with a phone that isn't there)* My footage shows the ending. We're all there. We're all... wait. *(to audience)* You're in the footage too.

**JOWEE**: I've been feeding the starter our conversations. It dreams better now. Here—bread that tastes like the first time we met.

**TORD**: We haven't met yet.

**JOWEE**: We will. We are. We did.

**GYOA**: *(recorded and live voice in harmony)* Can you still hear us hearing you hearing us?

**KIKI**: The static between frequencies—that's where we live now.

**TORD**: My car exists in the space between leaving and arriving. That's where I take passengers. The nowhere that's everywhere.

**JOWEE**: *(offering bread to audience)* This batch remembers the future. Eat it and taste what we become.

**GYOA**: I'm archiving this moment in the space between heartbeats.

**KIKI**: Is this real?

**ALL**: Does it matter?

**TORD**: The distance to safety is measured in held hands now.

**JOWEE**: The bread knows we survive. It's in the fermentation. The way the bubbles spell "together" in every language at once.

**GYOA**: *(all her recordings playing at once)* We are still here. Still here. Here. Hear. Us.

**KIKI**: *(to audience)* You're breathing our air. We're breathing yours. The documentation is the living.

---

## FINAL MOMENT

**TORD**: *(calculating)* The exact distance between the question and answer is—

**GYOA**: *(completing)* —the space of a shared breath.

**KIKI**: Can you still—

**JOWEE**: —hear us?

**ALL**: *(to audience, waiting for response)*

**GYOA**: *(after audience responds)* Archive complete. Archive continuing. Archive is the archive of archiving.

**TORD**: New passengers. Always room for three more. Even when there's no car.

**KIKI**: *(laughing)* I finally understand. The data IS the art. The art IS the data. We're both. We're neither. We're the interference pattern.

**JOWEE**: *(final line)* The bread will remember this. The bread remembers everything. Even the things that haven't happened yet. Especially those.

*[They begin the breathing pattern that includes the audience, creating the cascade of "Can you still hear us?" that continues beyond the performance]*

---

## DIALOGUE NOTES FOR ACTORS

- **TORD**: Your calculations become increasingly abstract. Numbers transform into feelings, distances into relationships.
- **GYOA**: Your recorded voice develops independence. By Act 5, you're in conversation with your past and future recordings.
- **KIKI**: Your relationship with documentation fractures. The camera becomes a mystic device showing parallel realities.
- **JOWEE**: Your language should literally feel edible. Speak as if words are ingredients, conversations are recipes.

**Breathing Patterns**:
- Each character has a signature breath rhythm
- These rhythms should sync during ensemble moments
- In Act 5, breathing becomes a form of dialogue

**The Question**:
"Can you still hear us?" should be delivered differently each time:
- First time: Genuine uncertainty
- Second time: Growing recognition
- Third time: Joyful confirmation
- Final times: Invitation and invocation
