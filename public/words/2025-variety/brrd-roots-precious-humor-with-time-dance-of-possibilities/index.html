Chassé, Revel.

He had never truly noticed time before. It was always just *there*, ticking politely in the background, never demanding attention. Then, one day, it **broke**.

At first, it was subtle. Morning coffee **poured slower than gravity should allow**, stretching midair as though reconsidering its purpose. The neighbor’s dog barked in a loop—a **one-second delay, one-second delay**—like a corrupted audio file. Time had gone off-script, and he was its latest unwitting protagonist.

And then, floating between warped seconds, **a transparent figure appeared**—a soft shimmer of movement, neither threatening nor reassuring.

*"You think you lost track of time,"* the figure whispered, **soft as dust settling in forgotten corners of the past**.

He narrowed his eyes, feeling the weight of the words **before** he fully processed them.

*"But tell me… where do you think time was ever going in the first place?"*

The clocks in the apartment **paused**, as if awaiting an answer. There was no way to tell what day it was anymore—Monday? Thursday? 1997?—but for the first time, something clicked.

*"Time is not your leash,"* the ghost murmured.

He exhaled. And for the first time, he laughed—not because he understood, but because suddenly, **he didn’t have to**.

Gentle, Slightly Sarcastic Reminders of an Unseen Watcher*

He never truly noticed time before. It was always just *there*, ticking politely in the background, never demanding attention. Then, one day, it **broke**.

At first, it was subtle. His morning coffee **poured slower than gravity should allow**, stretching midair as though reconsidering its purpose. The neighbor’s dog barked in a loop—a **one-second delay, one-second delay**—like a corrupted audio file. Time had gone off-script, and he was its latest unwitting protagonist.

I watched from just beyond the veil—always near, never seen. **Honestly, I had been hoping for a more dramatic existential crisis—one with shaking fists and poetic lamenting—but this? This would do.**

He barely noticed my presence, but I whispered anyway, because that’s kind of my thing.

*"You think you lost track of time."*

He froze. The words sank in, hovering in the air like an overdramatic plot twist.

*"But tell me… where do you think time was ever going in the first place?"*

The clocks **paused**, as if even they needed a moment to reconsider their choices.

He squinted at the ceiling, deep in thought, or possibly wondering if the coffee was actually just broken. I gave him a beat to process before leaning in with the real kicker.

*"Time is not your leash,"* I murmured.

To his credit, he didn’t panic—though I did catch him side-eyeing his watch, as if it might suddenly apologize for all the years it had been bossing him around.

And then, **he laughed**.

Not because he understood, but because suddenly, **he didn’t have to**. **Which, frankly, is a huge win for both of us, considering how long I’ve been whispering wisdom at him while he rushes through life like a contestant on a game show with unclear rules.**

I smiled. My task was never to control, never to interfere. Just to remind him, gently—but firmly—that time does not own the soul.

And with that, I drifted away. My work here was done.

For now.

### *Time, Darling, Is Not Yours to Chase*

She had always ruled her world with precision. Time obeyed her—or at least, it had the good sense to pretend. Deadlines bowed at her feet, schedules bent to her will, and she could command an entire editorial team with a single glance over the rim of an espresso cup.

Then, one day, time **forgot who was in charge**.

At first, it was subtle. Her espresso **poured itself at the speed of existential dread**, each drop hovering midair as if deciding whether to commit to gravity. An intern walked into her office three times—except it was *the same* intern, *the same* entrance, *the same* bewildered look, as if he had been caught in an infinite loop of professional confusion.

And then, amidst the absurdity, **Eduardo entered.**

Eduardo had no time for nonsense. He had spent the last decade serving underprivileged youth, mentoring teenagers who had far bigger concerns than whether Milan was having a corduroy renaissance. He was kind but **unshakably practical**, capable of carrying six grocery bags in one hand while solving life’s problems with the other.

He looked around at the frozen clocks and sighed the way one sighs when discovering they must **fix something ridiculous yet again**.

*"Ah,"* he muttered, nodding at the editor. "*Time is ignoring you too, huh?*"

She narrowed her eyes. **Time did not ignore her—it simply needed reminding of its place.**

*"It does not ignore me,"* she corrected, adjusting the cuff of her silk blouse. "*It misplaced itself.*"

At that precise moment, one of the clocks **dramatically fell off the wall**, landing with the kind of theatrical flair usually reserved for overly confident furniture in Spanish telenovelas.

Eduardo shook his head.

*"You people with your obsession over time,"* he said, picking up the clock as if it were a misbehaving toddler. "*You wear watches like shackles. Meanwhile, I had a kid today tell me that clocks are just ‘existential decorations.’ I think I might believe him.*"

She exhaled sharply.

And then, like a misplaced plot twist, a ghost appeared—a shimmering presence with **the unsettling confidence of someone who knew the secrets of the universe but refused to explain them in bullet points**.

*"You think you lost track of time,"* it murmured, voice smooth as silk—or possibly **fabricated entirely out of misplaced seconds**.

Eduardo blinked.

*"Huh,"* he said thoughtfully. "*That’s exactly what Enzo said this morning. Eleven years old and already philosophizing.*"

The editor-in-chief ignored him, focusing on the presence before her.

*"But tell me… where do you think time was ever going in the first place?"*

Eduardo raised an eyebrow.

*"That is such a ghost thing to say,"* he muttered. "*You could just give a direct answer, but no, gotta be mysterious about it.*"

Meanwhile, one of the clocks **continued spinning wildly**, as if time itself was having a full-blown existential meltdown.

*"Time is not your leash,"* the ghost murmured.

The editor straightened her posture, considering. Eduardo simply shook his head again.

*"I don't get paid enough for whatever this is,"* he muttered, tossing the broken clock onto a chair like it was *time's problem now*.

And then, **she laughed**.

Not because she understood, but because suddenly, **she didn’t have to**.

The ghost gave a nod of approval and vanished—possibly to haunt another executive who had mistaken time for something predictable.

Eduardo grabbed his bag, already moving toward the door.

*"Right, well. I’m going back to reality,"* he announced. "*There’s a kid waiting for me to teach him how to cook rice without setting off the fire alarm. You enjoy… whatever this bizarre metaphysical crisis is.*"

She watched him go, amused.

And then, adjusting the collar of her perfectly pressed blouse, she took a sip of espresso—partially to make a point, partially to confirm that **gravity had finally returned to its duties**.

She stepped forward. Not because time allowed her to, but because, at last, **she had stopped asking for its permission**.

### *Time, Kid, Ain’t on Your Side*

Broadway doesn’t move without people like him. The lights? He rigged them. The sets? He built them. The machinery that made an entire stage glide effortlessly between acts? **His hands, his sweat, his quiet mastery.**

For years, he had measured time in **cues and transitions**, in the hum of the machinery and the shuffle of actors waiting in the wings. Time wasn’t abstract—it was lived in seconds that ticked toward the next scene.

Then, one evening, time **hesitated**.

At first, it was subtle. His coffee **poured like molasses**, slow and thick, as though deciding whether to arrive at the present moment or linger in yesterday. A spotlight wavered, casting an uncertain glow, its beam stretching longer than it should—as if time itself had started second-guessing the rules.

And then, standing quietly amidst the fractured flow of time, **a presence appeared**—not imposing, not urgent, just there.

*"You think you lost track of time,"* it murmured, **gentle as dust settling on an empty seat in the balcony**.

He straightened, adjusting his tool belt, feeling the weight of the years behind him.

*"I don’t lose time,"* he muttered. "*It just moves differently when you’ve been chasing it for too long.*"

The ghost smiled—not the eerie, haunted kind, but something softer, something **knowing**.

*"Tell me… where do you think time was ever going in the first place?"*

He had spent decades measuring moments in precision—the strike of the cue, the unfurling of the curtain, the final note drifting into silence. And yet, here, where time had bent, it dawned on him.

Time didn’t rush toward anything. **It simply waited for presence to catch up.**

*"Time is not your leash,"* the ghost whispered, as if **offering permission to let go**.

For the first time in his life, he let himself wonder—what would it feel like to stand outside of it all? To breathe without chasing, to exist without measuring?

And then, **he laughed**. Soft at first, then richer, fuller. Not because he understood, but because suddenly, **he didn’t have to**.

The presence faded, vanishing into the folds of time, leaving behind only the quiet hum of the stage, the warmth of the lights, the steady pulse of life moving forward.

He took a sip of his coffee—no rush, no urgency, just **enjoying the moment for what it was**.

Then, he tightened a loose rig, stepped forward—not because time allowed him to, but because, **at last, he had learned to walk alongside it, not behind it**.

Time, backstage, isn’t elegant—it’s a gritty tango with chaos, a whispered war with deadlines, a silent negotiation with gravity itself. The union card holders don’t glide; they pivot, dodge, lift, shove, sigh, and side-eye their way through the madness.

Then, one night, time hesitates.

It starts small. Coffee pours slower than a government department processing paperwork, each drop hovering midair like it’s asking for permission from gravity. A rig refuses to drop, a spotlight refuses to shine, as if the entire system has taken a step back to think things over.

And just when things can’t get weirder, a ghost appears.

Not a Victorian specter. No chains, no tragic monologue. Just a casual, self-assured presence, standing like it owns the timeline.

*"You think you lost track of time,"* it murmurs, smooth as silk—or possibly woven entirely from misplaced seconds.

And before he can answer, something shifts.

A second presence arrives—this one colored in innocence, painted with moons, carrying time the way nature intended it to be held.

This spirit doesn’t wrestle with time, doesn’t demand explanations—it cradles seasons, holds tides, shifts light like poetry written by the wind.

And suddenly—time isn’t Broadway. It’s Provence.

Feet brushing wildflowers instead of steel beams. Wind carrying scent instead of fog machines faking atmosphere. Unhurried steps instead of frantic movements toward scene transitions.

And in the pause—between union sweat and celestial ease, between grit and grace, deadlines and drifting petals—he laughs.

Not because he understands.
Not because he *should*.
But because **suddenly, he doesn’t have to**.

The ghosts nod, satisfied. The moons flicker, shifting with gentle certainty. The machinery resets.

And as the city calls him back, as the cables pull him home, he takes one last sip of coffee—half expectation, half curiosity—before stepping forward.

Not because time allows him to.

But because, **at last, he has stopped asking for its permission**.
