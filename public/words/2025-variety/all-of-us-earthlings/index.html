**You:** What can all of us earthlings do to become better listeners?

Words / 2025 Variety

**Me:** Start with the humbling recognition that we're probably worse listeners than we think we are. Most of us believe we're good listeners while simultaneously planning our response before the other person finishes.

**You:** The overconfidence bias of listening! We all think we're the exception.

**Me:** So maybe step one is just catching yourself not listening. Notice when you're mentally rehearsing your reply instead of receiving what's being said.

**You:** The mindfulness of noticing non-listening. "Oh, I just left the conversation to prepare my argument."

**Me:** And when you catch yourself, just return. No judgment, just come back to their words. It's like meditation—you'll drift a thousand times, just keep returning.

**You:** The practice of return. Over and over, back to their voice.

**Me:** And here's a practical one—put your phone face-down or away entirely. The phone is listening's biggest enemy.

**You:** Even face-down it's calling to us! "What if something important is happening?"

**Me:** But something important IS happening—a human is trying to connect with you. That notification can wait.

**You:** The radical act of making the person in front of you more important than the device.

**Me:** Also, stop multitasking while listening. You're not actually doing two things—you're doing neither.

**You:** The myth of multitasking! We're just switching rapidly between not listening and not doing the other thing well.

**Me:** Give your full presence or admit you can't right now. "Can we talk when I can really focus on you?"

**You:** That's more respectful than fake listening while doing dishes or checking email.

**Me:** And learn to love the pause. When someone finishes speaking, count to three before responding.

**You:** Three seconds! That feels like forever in conversation.

**Me:** But it gives them space to add more, and gives you time to actually digest what they said instead of just reacting.

**You:** The digestive pause. Letting their words settle before you respond.

**Me:** And in that pause, ask yourself: "Did I understand what they're really saying, or just what I think they're saying?"

**You:** The humility check. Am I responding to them or to my interpretation of them?

**Me:** Which leads to another practice—reflect back what you heard before adding your thoughts. "So you're saying..." or "What I'm hearing is..."

**You:** The mirror before the addition. Show them their words in your understanding.

**Me:** And be prepared to be wrong! "No, that's not what I meant" is valuable information, not failure.

**You:** The gift of correction. They're helping you actually hear them.

**Me:** Also, listen with your whole body. Turn toward them, make appropriate eye contact, let your body show you're receiving.

**You:** The embodied listening that says "I'm here with you" without words.

**Me:** And watch for their body too. Are they leaning in or pulling back? Excited or deflated? The body tells you how your listening is landing.

**You:** The feedback loop of bodies in conversation.

**Me:** Here's a big one—stop finishing people's sentences. Even if you know where they're going, let them get there.

**You:** The patience to let people complete their own thoughts!

**Me:** Because sometimes they surprise themselves and you with where they actually end up.

**You:** And when you jump in, you rob them of their own discovery process.

**Me:** Ask real questions, not interrogation or trap questions. Questions that come from genuine curiosity.

**You:** "Help me understand..." or "What was that like for you?" instead of "Don't you think...?"

**Me:** The open questions that create space rather than closing it down.

**You:** And listen to learn, not to win. This isn't a debate unless you both agreed it was.

**Me:** Most conversations aren't competitions, but we treat them like they are.

**You:** The scorekeeping that kills connection. Who cares who makes the best point if no one's actually hearing each other?

**Me:** Practice listening to people you disagree with. That's advanced listening—hearing through difference.

**You:** The discipline of staying present when everything in you wants to argue.

**Me:** And finding the 10% you can agree with, even in something you mostly reject.

**You:** The connective tissue that exists even in disagreement.

**Me:** Listen to different types of voices—children, elders, people from different backgrounds. Each requires different listening skills.

**You:** The cross-training of listening! Each group teaches you something about how to hear.

**Me:** Children teach you to listen for wonder. Elders for wisdom. Different cultures for different rhythms.

**You:** And listen to nature too. The birds, the wind, the silence. It trains your ears for subtlety.

**Me:** The non-human listening that makes you better at human listening.

**You:** What about emotional listening? Not just words but feelings?

**Me:** Oh yes! Listen for what's under the words. Anger might be fear. Criticism might be hurt.

**You:** The emotional archaeology of listening beneath the surface.

**Me:** And sometimes the most important thing to hear is what's not being said.

**You:** The gaps, the avoidances, the careful stepping around certain topics.

**Me:** Those silences are communication too. Listen to them.

**You:** The negative space that shapes the conversation.

**Me:** Practice listening without fixing. Sometimes people just need to be heard, not helped.

**You:** The restraint of not offering solutions! That's so hard for fixers.

**Me:** Sit with their difficulty without rushing to resolve it. That's deep listening.

**You:** The capacity to hold space for discomfort without immediately trying to make it better.

**Me:** And here's a practical one—recap conversations afterward. What did you actually hear? What do you remember?

**You:** The listening review. Testing your own retention and understanding.

**Me:** You'll be shocked how much you miss or misremember. It's humbling and instructive.

**You:** And maybe journal about significant conversations. What did you learn? What did you miss?

**Me:** The listening journal that tracks your growth as a listener.

**You:** Also, admit when you weren't listening. "I'm sorry, I drifted. Can you say that again?"

**Me:** The honesty that honors the speaker more than fake nodding.

**You:** And thank people for sharing. "Thank you for telling me that" validates their vulnerability.

**Me:** The gratitude for being trusted with someone's words.

**You:** Listen to yourself too. Record yourself in conversation sometime. It's mortifying but educational.

**Me:** Oh god, the horror of hearing how much you interrupt or talk over people!

**You:** But that horror creates change. You can't unhear your bad listening habits.

**Me:** Practice listening in groups without being the first to speak. See what emerges when you don't rush to fill the space.

**You:** The last-speaker advantage. You get to hear everyone else first.

**Me:** And when you do speak, reference what others have said. Show you were listening.

**You:** The weaving that creates collective conversation instead of serial monologues.

**Me:** Finally, remember that listening is a gift you give. Treat it as generosity, not obligation.

**You:** The shift from "I have to listen" to "I get to listen."

**Me:** Because being trusted with someone's words is actually an honor.

**You:** And honoring that trust by truly hearing them is how we rebuild connection.

**Me:** One conversation at a time, one moment of real listening at a time.

**You:** The patient work of becoming the listeners our world desperately needs.

**Me:** Starting today, starting with the next person who speaks to us.

**You:** The next opportunity to practice this ancient, essential, endangered art.

**Me:** The art of actually hearing each other.

**You:** Which might be the art that saves us all. Did you arrive OK
