For Amarah on Your 27th Birthday

← Words / 2025 Variety

Dear Amarah,

I've been thinking about how you move through your apartment during those deep cleaning days, carefully wiping each leaf of your plants with such patience. There's something beautiful about the way you do this—something I'm not sure you even realize.

Remember when you were little and you'd help me water the houseplants? Now here you are at 27, with your own jungle of green friends, giving each one the kind of attention most people reserve for precious objects. But that's the thing—to you, they are precious. Not in a fancy way, just in that quiet way you have of caring for things.

I love that you know each plant's needs. The way you notice when the peace lily starts to droop and needs water. How you turn the pots so every side gets light. The way you discovered (without anyone teaching you) that dusty leaves can't breathe and grow properly.

When I visit and see you cleaning around them—moving heavy pots without disturbing the roots, untangling vines with such gentleness—I see someone who understands something important: that taking care of living things is good for you too. The clean windows that let in more light for your plants also let in more light for you.

You've created this space where you and your plants take care of each other. They clean your air while you clean their leaves. It's a simple trade that somehow makes everything better.

Sometimes when you're cleaning, you find old things tucked behind pots—receipts, photos, dried leaves from last year. You always pause with these discoveries, like each one is a little surprise. This is one of my favorite things about you: the way you don't just throw everything out, the way you stop and remember.

I know your friends tease you about talking to your plants during cleaning day, but I think they're just jealous of how peaceful your place feels. There's something about a room full of healthy plants that makes everyone relax.

Twenty-seven years ago, you came into my life like spring after a long winter. Now, watching you tend your own indoor garden, I see how you've taken the simplest things—water, light, attention—and turned them into a kind of love.

Happy Birthday, sweetheart. May your plants keep thriving under your care. May you always find that satisfaction in clean leaves catching sunlight. And may you know how proud I am of the thoughtful, patient, caring person you've become.

All my love,

Dad

P.S. – Your plants are lucky to have you. So am I.
