Brrd Roots Precious Layered Mosaic

Words / 2025 Variety

Imagine holding a shard of glass up to the light—something sharp, broken, discarded. The edges catch the sun just so, scattering tiny rainbows onto your hand. It’s not just glass anymore. It’s a fragment of wonder, humming with potential. Art is like that—it doesn’t demand perfection. It takes what is fractured, what is forgotten, and breathes life into it, filling the cracks with light and story. And isn’t that how life feels too? A little jagged, a little messy, but luminous in the right hands.

Picture moonlight spilling over a weathered ice auger, its rusty edges softened by time but still holding its ground—a quiet sentinel of winters past. Nearby, ribbons of silk drift in the breeze, as if charting paths for dreams yet to be traveled. And beneath the arching branches of a Mongolia tree, tarpaulin clings stubbornly to frozen earth, like a canvas painted by survival itself. Nothing here screams for attention, but everything whispers if you’re willing to listen.

This isn’t about grand gestures. It’s about the quiet power of connection. Like the scarf left behind by a loved one, still holding the faint scent of their perfume. Or the feel of soft, worn ribbon in your hand, brushing against memories you didn’t realize you had. These aren’t just objects—they’re echoes. They carry stories stitched from joy and sorrow, love and loss, life and imperfection. A crocheted duck nestled next to stained origami isn’t just playful—it’s poignant. Folded paper and fraying yarn hold humanity, folding it into a shape that feels both childlike and sacred.

Art, like life, knows how to laugh at itself. A whip handle brushed with fake fur, as absurd as it is strangely tender, exists without apology. Steel pipes rise defiantly from summer sand, claiming their space as boldly as wildflowers staking out cracks in the pavement. There’s humor in resilience, isn’t there? Even in the absurdity of our lives—the spilled coffees, the mismatched socks—there’s beauty. It’s not polished, but it’s real.

Still, art doesn’t shy away from the weightier truths. It holds space for the shadows too—the edges worn down by grief, the silences that feel impossibly heavy. But in those silences, there’s also warmth. A dread wig, clumped and imperfect, becomes a sacred relic of history lived and endured. It doesn’t need to be smoothed out or made whole. Its beauty lies in its honesty. Its spirit isn’t in being fixed—it’s in being seen.

When we stand in front of these fragments, we’re not just looking at art. We’re standing in the thin space between the broken and the whole, between the ordinary and the eternal. The veil that separates us disappears. A rusty auger isn’t just an auger; it’s a reflection of every time we’ve stood steady in the face of our own winters. The crocheted duck becomes an emblem of our own absurd, tender hearts. The lines blur, and for a moment, we don’t just see—we belong.

This is the gift of art. It doesn’t fix us or offer neat answers. It doesn’t need to. It holds us as we are, inviting us to see the light in the brokenness, the wonder in the fragments. It says, “Here, look—there’s beauty even in this.” And maybe that’s all we need. To hold our own jagged edges up to the light and find that they, too, can scatter rainbows. To know that even in our imperfection, we are luminous. That, perhaps, is enough to make the silence sing.
