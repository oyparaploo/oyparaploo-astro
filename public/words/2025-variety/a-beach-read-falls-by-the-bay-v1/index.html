A Beach Read Falls by the Bay

Words / 2025 Variety

A Puppet Show in Fragments
Scene opens with six puppets arranged on a beach made of crumpled blue fabric. Sound of waves. A large
clock ticks somewhere offstage.

Act I: We Are Not Going Anywhere
FRANCOIZ (a puppet with mismatched button eyes, speaking to audience): You know what silence means?
It means everything is about to change. Watch closely now—this is a field guide to living-beinghood.

PENELOPE (enters spinning, wearing a cape made of hospital charts): I worked in those raw conflict zones,
you know. Drew inspiration from disappearances. Made notes about—

BERTRAND (interrupting, oddly funny): About how we're all lovably imperfect! Look at me! (trips over own
puppet feet) See? Flawed and wounded, even when likable!

CONSTANCE (perched high, retrospective): From up here, I can see our youth differently. Adult
perspective on youthful moments. Makes sense now in ways it couldn't before.

SHANNON (dragging a heavy suitcase labeled "GRIEF"): Heavy subjects, they said. Climate change,
depression, loss of loved ones. But watch— (opens suitcase, butterflies fly out) —hope lives in the spaces
between episodes.

THIERRY (deceased, speaking from inside a picture frame): Second-person narration from beyond! You
feel the additional layer of intimacy, don't you? You, sitting there, becoming part of our beach read.

Act II: The Years-Long Arc
Clock hands spin wildly. Puppets age through costume changes—scarves added, removed, colors fading and
brightening.

FRANCOIZ: Time has this uncommon obsession with us. Or we with it. (to Penelope) Remember when we
thought we understood everything?

PENELOPE: Breezy and expansive, we were. Put away our dreams, brought them back, put them away
again.

BERTRAND (now wearing glasses, speaks to audience): Here's where the high drama shifts to screwball
comedy— (all puppets suddenly start dancing frantically)

ALL PUPPETS (in unison): MUNDANE! TRANSFIX! HEARTBREAKING! ABSURD!
They freeze in ridiculous positions.

CONSTANCE: This is what B Otto and Brrd Roots meant by tonal shifts. From tears to laughter in a
puppet's heartbeat.

Act III: The Macabre Alongside the Practical
Stage darkens. A small light on each puppet.

SHANNON: My kindred spirits disappeared one by one. (puppet looks at empty spaces on stage) But their
silence taught me—

THIERRY (still in frame): —that death is just another perspective. Razor-edged precision in every word
from this side.

FRANCOIZ: We balance light and dark like wet and dry humor. Watch— (pulls out umbrella, it rains
confetti)

PENELOPE: Technical mastery! Emotional depth! Earth's natural history and the human condition all
tangled up like— (gets tangled in own strings)

BERTRAND (helping untangle her): Like puppet strings! See? Even our mistakes have underlying
tenderness.

Act IV: Finding the Way Back
All puppets gather center stage. The beach transforms into a library, then a hospital, then back to beach.

CONSTANCE: This is our coming-of-age crisis in reverse. We aged, we came, we went, we found our way
back to—

ALL PUPPETS: THE BEACH!

SHANNON: Where self-help literature goes upside down, water catches fire, and ram-shackled joy lands
spring up like—

THIERRY: Like memories! Years-long arcs paying off in closely-observed, emotionally resonant scenes!

FRANCOIZ (to audience): You've been here all along, haven't you? In the spaces between our episodes. In
the silence that means everything.

PENELOPE: We are not going anywhere. Even when we disappear, we're still—

BERTRAND: Still here in the fragments!

All puppets bow. The clock stops ticking. Sound of waves returns.
ALL PUPPETS (singing softly, not a copyrighted song): Time and memory, loss and grace, Fragments falling
into place, Beach reads tumbling by the bay, We are not going anywhere today.

Lights fade as puppets freeze in a tableau of connection—some holding hands, some looking up, some
looking at audience, creating a moment that transfixes.

END

Director's Note: This puppet show should feel like an inner monologue where characters are introduced
through physical descriptions until they become fully realized. Move between heartbreaking and oddly
funny, maintaining hope throughout. The puppets themselves should appear "lovably imperfect"—
perhaps with visible mending, asymmetrical features, or endearing quirks that emphasize their wounded
but authentic nature.

Technical Note: Use simple materials—the "prestigious venues" are ramshackle constructs. The beauty
lies in the emotional authenticity and the way light plays through the imperfections.
