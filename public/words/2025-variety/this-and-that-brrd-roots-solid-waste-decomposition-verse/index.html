This And That Brrd Roots Solid Waste Decomposition Verse

Words / 2025 Variety

The earth, as silent as it seems, is a keeper of secrets—and our waste is its whispered confession. Beneath the surface lies a story of choices made, of moments hurried and conveniences claimed. Glass bottles rest for centuries like forgotten treasures, while banana peels slip away in weeks like visitors just passing through. The land waits, patient and enduring, to turn what we throw away into what it needs—or to hold it, stubbornly, for lifetimes.

Picture this: you finish your orange, its peel twisting in your hand like a little spiral of sunshine, and you toss it into the compost. It vanishes into nature’s rhythm within weeks—effortlessly reclaimed by the soil that gave it life. Now contrast that with the foam cup you used for coffee on a rushed morning, discarded without a second thought. It sits in the earth for 80 years, a memory you forgot but the soil can't shake. And yet, both items have a story—a legacy written in decay and endurance.

Take disposable diapers, cradling the softest beginnings of life, yet clinging stubbornly to the land for over 400 years. It’s a bittersweet juxtaposition: nurturing new life while tethering the earth to old waste. Or the plastic bag, seemingly weightless in your hand but bold enough to linger for decades, fragile yet unyielding. And then there’s the wool sock, whose cozy existence fades in mere years—a fleeting goodbye in the grand timeline of decomposition.

There’s humor here, too—imagine the banana peel, so quick to disappear, bragging to its plastic cousins stuck in the ground: "Catch me if you can!" Or the ironic endurance of the glass bottle, outlasting civilizations while sitting quietly underfoot. It’s absurd, really—the things we think of as temporary often linger longer than we’ll ever live. Foam cups might as well form a club, chatting about the centuries they’ll spend side by side in silent judgment.

But there’s vulnerability in acknowledging our role in this cycle. We live in a world of instant solutions, grabbing whatever’s convenient, often forgetting the long-term cost. And yet, the earth quietly forgives us, taking what we throw away and finding ways to mend itself, piece by piece. It’s not asking for perfection—it’s asking for care. It’s asking us to pause, to think about what we leave behind.

Spiritually, the soil holds a profound lesson: everything returns, eventually. Some things take centuries, others mere days, but the earth is always waiting. It’s a rhythm we can honor, a reminder to tread lightly. Each time we discard something, we’re making a choice—not just about what we throw away, but about the kind of legacy we leave.

Take this with you: the earth doesn’t demand perfection, but it does deserve reverence. From the glass bottles to the paper towels, from the nylon to the orange peel, every item has its time. Cherish the soil not as a silent witness, but as a living partner in your journey. What you discard today shapes tomorrow—not just for the land, but for everything rooted in it. Honor that rhythm, and you’ll find yourself part of something larger than convenience—a cycle of care, connection, and timeless renewal. That, after all, is the heart of the earth’s quiet endurance. Let it inspire you to choose wisely.
