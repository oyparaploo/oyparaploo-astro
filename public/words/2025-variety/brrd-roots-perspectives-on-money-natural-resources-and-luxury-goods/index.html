Brrd Roots Perspectives On Money

Natural Resources ~ And Luxury Goods

← Words / 2025 Variety

What if the things we chase are only as valuable as the reasons we chase them? Money, luxury, status—they shine brightly, whispering promises of happiness, but often conceal an unspoken truth: they’re reflections, not sources, of light. Imagine standing in a world where every decision, every ambition, is shaped by wealth. It’s tempting to think of it as a glittering paradise, but look closer. Beneath the surface, the sheen dims, and what you’re left with is a choice: will these treasures serve you, or will they own you?

Picture someone who’s finally “made it.” Their home sprawls across acres, a gallery of glittering chandeliers, pristine pools, and cars they never even drive. At first glance, it’s perfection. But there, in the quiet, the owner sits alone, scrolling mindlessly through social media, disconnected from their own grandeur. The house is full, yet it echoes. The possessions are dazzling, yet they feel like walls closing in. They wonder if they’ve collected everything but themselves.

Now, picture a farmer. Their wealth isn’t polished or obvious—it grows in rows, fed by sun, rain, and time. Their work is backbreaking and beautiful, guided by the rhythms of the land. At sunset, they sit on their porch, eating the apple they picked hours ago, a burst of sweetness that tastes like effort rewarded. Their children chase fireflies in the field, their laughter weaving through the air like music. The luxury here isn’t shimmering—it’s rooted. It thrives in connection, not accumulation.

There’s humor in how we cling to the allure of wealth, even when it shows its cracks. Think of the person who saves up for months to buy a designer jacket—only to realize they’re too scared to actually wear it because what if it gets ruined? Or the endless cycle of upgrading gadgets, spending more time choosing cases to protect them than actually enjoying what they do. We laugh, because deep down, we know that no new purchase can fill an old void.

And yet, we’re all vulnerable to the pull. Money whispers of security when bills pile high. Status promises belonging when we feel invisible. Luxury goods offer the illusion of permanence in an ever-changing world. It’s not wrong to want these things, but the weight they carry depends on what we give them. The mansion owner envies the farmer’s peace. The farmer dreams, now and then, of the mansion’s comfort. And somewhere in between, there’s balance.

Spiritually, this wealth-driven economy is a lens, magnifying our best and worst instincts. It’s not about rejecting riches, but reimagining their purpose. What if natural resources weren’t just exploited but revered? What if luxury goods became symbols of artistry rather than excess? What if money, instead of dividing, became a tool to unite—to uplift, create, and heal? True wealth, after all, is never just about accumulation. It’s about intention.

Take this with you: you don’t have to renounce ambition or deny the pull of luxury to live meaningfully. But let wealth align with your values, not dictate them. Let money be a tool to create and connect, not a master to bow to. The most profound treasures aren’t in bank accounts or gilded halls—they’re in the unassuming moments that make you feel alive. They’re in the laughter that shakes your core, the work that roots your soul, the love you give and receive without measure. Choose to strive for an economy of the heart—where balance sustains the body and the spirit alike. That’s where the light lives. Let it guide you.

What if money were nothing more than a shadow? Something you could see and hold for a moment, but never let define the light you stand in. Picture it not as an end, but as a fleeting tool—fluid, replaceable, and faint compared to the real treasures of life: laughter that shakes the walls, the quiet kindness of a stranger, the weightless joy of being understood. Money is an echo of something louder and truer, and yet, how often do we give it center stage?

Imagine someone chasing money like it’s the finish line of a marathon—eyes fixed on the next promotion, the bigger house, the pristine new car. And when they finally cross that finish line, they find...what? The joy is fleeting. The car sits idle, gathering dust. The house feels cavernous, empty of life. The ladder of success turns out to be a treadmill—exhausting, endless. It’s not the money that failed them, but their belief that it was the answer.

Now think of the artist who works in small strokes—an idea, a melody, a sculpture taking form—without promises of fame or fortune. Their wealth lies elsewhere. In the stranger who pauses to whisper, “You gave me something I didn’t know I needed.” In the messy, fleeting beauty of time shared with loved ones: pancake batter splattered on the counter, the quiet hum of bedtime stories that no raise or bonus could ever replace. Their treasure isn’t kept in vaults; it’s held in moments.

And yet, humor has its way of keeping us grounded. Take the tech-lover who spends months obsessing over specs, saving for the latest gadget, only for a “better” version to hit the shelves before they’ve unboxed their prize. Or the person who prides themselves on financial discipline, only to break into uncontrollable giggles while dancing with their vacuum cord during a cleaning spree. Because that’s what life does—it reminds us that joy and absurdity can’t be bought, and sometimes, they’re the best gifts we never see coming.

But let’s not ignore the weight of money, either. The pressure to make ends meet can feel suffocating—bills stacking up, the future looming large. And in those moments, it’s easy to let money decide your worth, to believe that it’s all or nothing. But what if we let it take its rightful place? A tool, a thread, a humble piece of the whole instead of the centerpiece. Money can sustain the body, but it’s connection, meaning, and grace that feed the spirit.

Spiritually, money is a mirror. It reflects our intentions, our fears, our longings. Used wisely, it can transform—a bridge that uplifts, empowers, creates possibility. But when it becomes an idol, it chains us invisibly to the very thing we thought would set us free. True freedom isn’t about abundance—it’s about alignment. Knowing what matters most and letting that guide your choices, not what glimmers in a store window.

So here’s the truth: money isn’t evil or pure—it’s neutral. It’s the paintbrush, not the masterpiece; the seed, not the bloom. Its power lies in how you use it, how you frame it within your life. Let it serve your purpose, not the other way around. Let it be a tool that builds, supports, carries—but never steals.

Take this with you: you are not what you own, not what’s in your wallet or your accounts. The treasures that truly matter—the ones that bring peace, joy, and meaning—are found in shared laughter, in the warmth of connection, in the unapologetic beauty of being present. Money will fade. But the moments you weave with intention, with care, with love? They endure. Let money be the thread, but let your soul sew the story. Because that’s where life begins—not with what you hold, but with what you create and share.
