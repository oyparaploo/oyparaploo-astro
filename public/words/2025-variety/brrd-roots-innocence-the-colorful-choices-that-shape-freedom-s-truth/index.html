Brrd Roots ~ Innocence ~ The Colorful Choices That Shape Freedom’s Truth

"Innocent" implies a freedom from corruption, deceit, or calculated intent—like a child experiencing the world for the first time, reacting genuinely to sensations and emotions.
"Primal" points to our deepest, most ancient instincts—hunger, fear, passion, survival—the forces that have shaped human nature long before civilization.
"Wild" suggests a disregard for societal rules, living in harmony with nature but unconcerned with order, etiquette, or control.
"Animalistic" ties to our physicality—movement, desire, aggression, pleasure—experiencing life through the body rather than the mind.

Imagine innocent flailing not just as movement, but as an expression of pure, untamed energy—something deeply human yet free from thought or intention. It’s like surrendering to the moment, allowing the body to respond before the mind catches up. There’s something profoundly honest about it—like the way emotions ripple through us before we even understand them.
At a deeper level, it’s a metaphor for the way we navigate life itself. We all flail in one way or another—through uncertainty, joy, frustration, discovery. It’s the raw attempt to connect, to push against limitations, to embrace existence with the full force of being alive. And yet, it remains grounded in simplicity—because what could be more natural than letting go and moving without restraint?

A silver-winged moth flutters aimlessly in the twilight, drawn not by urgency but by quiet curiosity. Its movements are erratic, like the gentle flail of a soul untethered by time, floating through the air as if the wind itself cradles its decisions.

In a meadow where dawn never quite arrives, pearl-petaled flowers bloom with a soft hum—wisps of light curling around them as if nature breathes in slow, deliberate contemplation. Innocence lingers here, where no shadow chases the light, where growth is an endless dream rather than a fleeting moment.

Somewhere beyond thought, an orb-like creature drifts between planes of existence, untouched by consequence. It tumbles without fear, its laughter a song only the stars understand. There is no hurry to become, no rush to know—only the slow unraveling of being, wrapped in cosmic silk.

Absolute innocence means approaching the world without preconceived notions, without the instinct to anticipate others' needs or intentions. It’s like stepping into existence with only raw experience to guide you—reacting purely, without calculation or expectation.

In this state, someone might not grasp the unspoken rules of empathy or mutual understanding at first. Their world is centered on immediate perception, unfiltered and direct. Without internalized concepts of duty, reciprocity, or emotional navigation, interactions may be shaped by impulse rather than awareness.

But innocence also allows for untainted discovery—learning through genuine experience rather than assumption. There’s no manipulation, no strategic behavior—just the pure unfolding of understanding as one stumbles, flails, and absorbs what it means to exist among others. Some may grow into a deep attunement to people’s needs over time, while others may retain an untouched, dreamlike separation from the rhythms of human connection.

Innocence is a double-edged force—it can act as a shield, keeping someone untouched by cynicism, cruelty, or manipulative tendencies. Those who remain untainted by malice may not even conceive of darker motives, making them less likely to engage in harmful behavior themselves. A truly innocent person might radiate warmth that repels negativity, drawing in kindness rather than corruption.

But innocence also leaves one exposed. Without an understanding of deception or the complexities of human nature, an innocent person may walk unknowingly into danger or be taken advantage of by those who lack their purity of heart. The absence of guardedness makes vulnerability inevitable—a trusting spirit that isn’t accustomed to cruelty may struggle to recognize it when it appears.

It’s a delicate balance: innocence can preserve the purity of a soul, but without wisdom, it can lead them unprepared into the shadows. Maybe the safest path isn’t clinging to innocence or abandoning it entirely, but letting it evolve—allowing kindness to remain while layering it with understanding.

Innocence and arrogance seem like opposites—one is unguarded and unaware of power, while the other thrives on self-importance. But in certain ways, innocence can accidentally take on shades of arrogance, though not in the typical boastful or calculated sense.

An innocent person, untouched by self-doubt or social caution, might move through life with an unshaken confidence simply because they don’t realize the need to second-guess themselves. They may speak their truths with blunt certainty, not out of superiority but because they lack the filters of doubt or diplomacy. This can come across as arrogance, even if it isn’t driven by ego.

In some cases, innocence may lead to a kind of unintentional obliviousness—where one assumes they understand the world fully, simply because they haven’t yet encountered complexity or contradiction. This kind of purity, while untainted by malice, can resemble arrogance from an outside perspective.

But true arrogance—the kind that thrives on dominance, comparison, and superiority—requires an awareness of status and power that innocence lacks. So while innocence may accidentally echo arrogance in certain moments, it never truly becomes it.

The Jesus Horse is not ridden; it rides you, galloping through the spirals of time with an untamed, celestial force. It does not die, for it was never alive in the way we understand—only a shifting shimmer, a radiant pulse between dimensions.

Innocence doesn’t resist it, nor does it tame it. It becomes the wind in its mane, the glow in its eyes, the echo in its hooves as they strike the unseen ground of forgotten worlds. Every gallop folds space into poetry, and suddenly, the sky isn’t blue—it’s laughter, rippling outward in waves that break against reality itself.

The horse doesn't need a rider. It moves with the weightless rhythm of a dream, carrying only fragments of light and dust. And those who witness it—truly see it—are never quite the same again. They walk away lighter, hollowed but whole, forever touched by something too vast to comprehend.

The Jesus Horse did not arrive with purpose. It did not gallop like prophecy, nor descend like revelation. It simply was—a creature of uncertain holiness, standing in the middle of the street, chewing absentmindedly on the edges of existence.

No one knew how to react. Some stared, then pretended they hadn’t. But innocence never pretended. It did not look away. It did not search for explanations. It simply watched, with the soft patience of something untouched by the need to dissect the incomprehensible.

The horse’s mane flickered—sometimes strands of divine light, sometimes loosely tangled spaghetti. The hooves clopped lazily, the sound reminiscent of a doorbell ringing in an abandoned house, an invitation without an answer. But innocence did not wonder why. It did not need certainty, only presence.

Then the horse melted, pooling into a shimmering puddle of forgotten intentions. It did not resist dissolution, nor did innocence mourn its departure. And when the horse un-melted—reforming into a small bureaucratic office building with a sign reading Closed for Lunch. Forever.—it felt as natural as the tide shifting on a shore.

A pause. A ripple in meaning.

Without warning, the horse tripped over an idea, evaporated, and reassembled itself as a pigeon. The pigeon had eyes too knowing, carrying the weight of secrets it would never share. The crowd muttered, confused. But innocence—innocence saw no tragedy in transformation. It did not clutch at the divine, did not fight to preserve the fleeting miracle. It understood something no one else did: purity does not need permanence to be real.

And so, as the pigeon shuffled away into obscurity, innocence remained.

Nobody discussed this again.

Ever.

There once was a heart so pure, untouched by the world’s sharp edges, that it bloomed effortlessly—like wildflowers stretching toward the sun, unburdened by doubt or fear. It did not question its place, nor weigh its worth against fleeting affections. It simply was—softly, wholly, without pretense. But even the gentlest souls are not immune to the creeping whispers of uncertainty. One day, a quiet thought settled in—do they truly like me? A delicate question, fragile yet persistent, threading its way into a mind that had once known only peace.

Oh, honey, come sit down and let me tell you something with all the warmth and wisdom of a grandmother who’s seen it all.

Even the sweetest souls, the ones who practically glow with kindness, sometimes get tangled in that nagging worry—do they really like me? But oh, my dear, let me wrap that thought up in a soft quilt of reassurance. Most people are far too busy muddling through their own insecurities to be analyzing you the way you fear. And those moments of doubt? They’re just little hiccups on the journey, like when you accidentally bake cookies with salt instead of sugar. Nobody is perfect, but goodness, it doesn’t make them any less lovable.

Now, about self-awareness—bless the hearts of those who think they’ve got it all figured out, marching around convinced they know exactly how they’re perceived. Meanwhile, the truly thoughtful ones are always second-guessing, wondering if they said too much or not enough. It’s a funny thing, really—a reminder that sometimes, the most socially clueless are blissfully unaware, while the most perceptive carry the weight of uncertainty like a well-worn cardigan.

And narcissism? Oh, child, that’s a tricky one. It’s like a fancy cake with beautiful icing but no real flavor—it looks polished on the outside, but inside, it’s crumbling under the weight of insecurity. The irony is, the ones who need reassurance the most are often the least likely to ask for it.

So, my dear, let me hand you a metaphorical cup of tea and a plate of warm cookies—because life is too short to fret over every little social misstep. You’re doing just fine, better than fine, and if you ever forget that, I’ll be right here, reminding you. Now go on and be your wonderfully imperfect, lovable self.

And so, the heart—still gentle, still unscarred despite its fleeting doubts—found solace in the truth that it did not need to be anything but itself. It did not need to chase love or prove its worth, for kindness needs no validation to exist. Like a stream flowing effortlessly toward the ocean, it embraced its place in the world, unshaken, unbroken—forever open, forever soft. And in that quiet resolve—that refusal to harden against love—it remained beautifully, undeniably innocent.

Innocence is not the absence of struggle—it is the quiet trust that beneath all uncertainty, there is a deeper knowing. A soul untouched by deceit or manipulation does not remain unchallenged; rather, it moves through life with an openness that allows truth to emerge, even in the face of doubt.

A crisis of identity is not always a fracture—it can be a calling, a sacred invitation to step beyond the self we thought we were and into something greater. The most innocent among us may still feel the pull of questioning, but their search does not come from fear—it comes from a natural unfolding, a trust that identity is not something to grip tightly, but something to surrender to.

The innocent do not avoid uncertainty; they allow it to refine them. They do not seek external validation to know who they are; they let divine wisdom guide them toward a truth deeper than labels or expectations. Perhaps the purest souls do not escape the questions of self, but they approach them with a different light—a knowing that their essence has always been intact, untouched, whole.

What if identity was never something to defend, but something to discover, like the gradual unfurling of a flower that knows, without doubt, how to bloom? The truly innocent might not be those who never question themselves, but those who do—with reverence, with trust, with the quiet faith that who they are has always been enough.

Imagine, for a moment, that innocence does not merely delay the storm—it is the storm’s very absence. That a soul, untouched by the weight of doubt, does not evolve through conflict but through the natural unfolding of divine design. If the world insists that identity is fluid, a thing that must shift and bend under the pressure of experience, what if—just if—the most innocent among us do not wrestle with who they are because they have always known?

Perhaps the ones who never agonize over their place in the world are not lacking self-awareness, but rather existing exactly as they were created to be. What if questioning identity is not proof of evolution but evidence of disruption—a departure from the unshaken truth of our existence? The world whispers that we must change, that we must wrestle with our reflection, but the innocent simply are—fully formed, whole, unbroken.

And if one day doubt does arrive, tapping gently at the edge of a once-unshaken heart, perhaps it is not a reckoning but merely an echo of voices that were never meant to be heard. A call from a world that has long forgotten the quiet certainty of being exactly as God intended—unchanged, unburdened, and perfectly enough. Innocence does not postpone self-discovery. It is self-discovery in its purest, most sacred form.

Innocence isn’t just for the quiet, soft-spoken types who look like they just stepped out of a Jane Austen novel, clutching a lace-trimmed handkerchief and whispering pardon me every five seconds. No, innocence comes in all flavors—some of them loud, unpredictable, and possibly accompanied by jazz hands.

A person can be boisterous, opinionated, and charge into conversations like a golden retriever that just learned how to open doors, and still be pure-hearted. Likewise, someone raised in a culture that considers blunt honesty a form of love could walk through life delivering unfiltered truths like a vending machine dispensing unsolicited wisdom—completely unaware that their level of directness could give a fainting Victorian a full-blown existential crisis.

Innocence doesn’t require a person to tiptoe through social interactions like they’re navigating a minefield. It’s not about carefully curating every word to avoid offending the delicate sensibilities of every person in a 20-mile radius. It’s about intention—about moving through the world with a heart unburdened by deceit, even if that heart is housed in someone who talks too loudly at restaurants and overshares with strangers at bus stops.

Innocence is not a whisper—it is a force, a presence, an unwavering truth woven into the fabric of existence itself. It does not adhere to societal templates or personality molds; it flourishes in every form, from the quiet contemplator to the exuberant firebrand. If purity of heart is defined by intention, then innocence is not about restraint—it is about movement, about the unfiltered expression of a soul that has never learned to mask itself.

But does innocence require quiet humility? Must it be tethered to a gentle, reserved disposition? No—innocence is not confined to politeness, nor does it demand temperance. It exists in the ones who speak softly as well as in those who laugh loudly, who wield their words with precision or fling them like fireworks. It thrives in cultures where reverence and tradition paint every interaction with careful respect, and in places where boldness is a form of love, where honesty is worn without shame. If malice is absent, then innocence remains—untouched, unquestioned.

And is innocence fragile? Must it be protected, preserved, sheltered against the erosion of experience? Or is it something indestructible? Some believe that innocence must be carefully guarded, shielded from the world, lest it slip away. But what if it is not something delicate at all? What if innocence is not lost, but simply forgotten—buried beneath layers of doubt and deception yet still present, waiting to be recognized?

To stretch this idea to its furthest branches is to see innocence as something beyond humanity itself. It is in the wind that moves without motive, in the ocean that crashes against rocks not out of anger but simply because it is called to do so. It lives in the newborn child who has yet to learn deception, and in the elder whose years have only refined their sincerity rather than eroded it. It does not need permission to exist; it simply is—constant, undiminished.

And at its deepest roots? Innocence is the foundation of creation, the raw, unfiltered state of being before the world teaches the mind to question, before experience chisels away at purity. It does not demand recognition to be real; it does not fade simply because the world doubts it. It is the soul in its natural state—unshaken, unguarded, enough.

So perhaps innocence does not need to be defended—it only needs to be remembered. And once remembered, once truly seen, it is undeniable: it was never fragile. It was never weak. It has always been whole.

Innocence is not a state to be attained, nor a fleeting quality that must be preserved—it is a foundational resonance of existence, a purity woven into the very essence of creation itself. It is not defined by softness, nor does it demand quiet reverence to be real. Innocence moves freely through all expressions, through laughter that bursts unrestrained and words that cut through the air like lightning. It is not a matter of form but of intention, a force that remains untouched by corruption even when immersed in a world of complexity.

To believe innocence is fragile is to misunderstand its nature. It does not need protection, for it is indestructible. It exists beyond human struggle, beyond the limits of perception, beyond the conditioning of experience. It is not something lost—it is only something forgotten, buried beneath layers of self-doubt and deception, waiting to be recognized once again.

From the first breath of existence, innocence has never needed validation to be real. It is in the newborn’s gaze, unclouded by knowledge yet brimming with presence. It is in the elder whose wisdom has refined their sincerity rather than eroded it. It is in the ocean that moves because it must, in the stars that burn without question, in the currents of the universe that dance with a purpose no mind can grasp.

This is not innocence as mortals conceive it—a brittle thing that must be shielded. This is innocence as it is: a cosmic force, a divine state of being, a truth that exists beyond words, beyond time, beyond limitation. And to recognize it, to allow it to exist without restriction, is to step beyond mere human consciousness into something vast, eternal, undeniable. It is not found in restraint; it is found in trust. Not in silence, but in the unshaken certainty that everything already is.

Oh, glory! Let’s lift this up, turn it inside out, and drape it in a royal robe of deep purple truth—the kind that shimmers under the weight of divine wisdom.

If the world tells you that innocence must be protected, the gospel tells you it was never fragile to begin with. If the world whispers that survival means hardening, the spirit proclaims that softness is the very thing that carries power. You see, innocence is not naïve—it is bold in its purity, standing radiant as a robe spun from the finest threads of Heaven’s intent.

A soul untainted by deception does not walk timidly—it strides forward cloaked in celestial favor, moving through the world not with fear, but with the unshaken certainty that it is held, guided, protected. For where some may believe innocence must be armored with cynicism to endure the trials of life, the truth is, innocence does not defend itself—it illuminates, shining with the light that cannot be dimmed by earthly corruption.

And what is intuition in the hands of the innocent? It is not hesitation—it is divine knowing, a whisper from the throne room itself, saying, Step here, speak now, hold firm, move forward. It is not learned through suspicion—it is received through trust, a compass unshaken, a force that does not require deceit to navigate the world but moves by the rhythm of faith.

See, the world may teach that survival requires caution, that wisdom comes from skepticism, but the truth in radiant purple gospel light is this: innocence does not struggle—it stands tall. It does not dim itself to fit, nor shrink itself to survive. It endures because it was built to last. It adapts without losing its essence. It walks in the full authority of divine favor, unshaken, untouched, perfectly whole.

So go on—shine, move in your purity, let your innocence be the loudest testimony. For it does not fade, it does not crumble—it stands wrapped in royal light, sealed in divine truth, undaunted, undefeated, undeniable.
