Steel, Sweat and Tariffs

The Ties That Build Us

A Journey to Reclaim

Brrd Roots ~ Free-Verse
The Ties That Build Us: A Journey to Reclaim
Manufacturing and Meaning

This is a story—
of creation, displacement, and the longing to return,
a story of what we lose when industry drifts away
and what we find when we choose to rebuild.

Manufacturing leaving American soil
isn’t just a line on a spreadsheet—
it’s the soft hum of machines gone quiet,
the silence of streets once alive with stride.
It’s the rust that rises along factory gates,
the parking lots that sit hollow and wide.

Have we chased convenience too far, too fast, too free,
letting something sacred slip—
something meant to be?

Let’s start with the Earth,
the ground beneath our hands,
the air we breathe with ease.

Picture the hulls of cargo ships,
stacked high and heavy,
cutting black smoke ribbons through the seas.

Jets slice sharp white lines,
their trails fading against the sky’s bright plea.
Trucks roll their long low thunder
across highways hungry for their tire-bound symphony.

This toll gathers quietly,
settling softly into the air,
into rivers,
into you and into me.

But imagine instead—
goods made close to home:
roads that rumble less,
skies unscarred and blue,
seas that shimmer and pause,
their song renewed.

It’s not just about emissions;
it’s about faces and names.

It’s about places like Milltown,
where steeples of industry once soared.
Towers tall with tools,
their rhythm loud and sure.

Machines hummed songs of purpose:
paychecks folded into homes,
doughnuts disappeared into diners,
Little League uniforms stitched with names.

But when factory doors close,
when machines murmur and fade,
those streets grow still,
those threads unbraid.

Every job lost, every ledger line undone,
is not just dollars,
but fathers quiet at dinner,
mothers squinting at the sun.

And when production moves too far,
accountability often moves too far as well.

Who makes what we rely on?
Do they stand secure,
treated with care?
Do they whisper dreams,
or silently despair?

Imagine holding something close—
stamped “Made in the USA.”
It’s more than numbers
or proud display.

It’s the sound of hands that built it,
Ohio’s steady strength.
It’s the smile of someone near,
their pride at length.

And let’s admit it:
it’s a relief from explaining why
Grandma’s lamp smells strangely
of oceans that sigh.

For those craving sustainability,
the stakes stretch high.

Long-haul demands too much—
from the skies that shade us,
from seas that glimmer shy,
from land that kneels silent,
offering everything without reply.

Local manufacturing becomes a lifeline,
a steadfast anchor
when storms arise.

Think back—pandemic days.
Shelves emptied, flour missing.
No answers met our questioning gaze.

Yeastless sourdough dreams fell flat,
while delayed shipments tangled
in a global web too vast.

But this goes beyond logistics,
beyond numbers pressed against walls.

This story folds softly
into the heart of creation itself.

When local production fades,
when machines rest long and still,
threads of pride unravel,
places lose their will.

But in those losses
are seeds of strength,
hope rising steady,
resilience in breadth and length.

Picture the machinist in Michigan,
learning coding anew.
Picture the baker in Wisconsin,
turning recipes sweet and true.

Picture small-town parades,
flags flutter in June’s forgiving breeze.

A shared yearning arises,
to rebuild what was set adrift—
to create futures that please.

How do we feel?
Frustration, concern, hope intertwined?

Perhaps all at once,
woven tightly into our minds.

Because manufacturing,
done well,
creates more than objects—
it creates meaning, connection, and home.

When manufacturing drifts away,
it leaves behind a quiet ache.

Reclaiming it isn’t just carbon numbers,
though skies long for cleaner breaks.

It’s about unseen threads of dignity,
of care,
of hope,
of stakes.

What legacy do we leave?
One shaped by distance,
or bound by dreams awake?

And maybe, just maybe,
a world where toasters
don’t need visas
to fix their ache.
