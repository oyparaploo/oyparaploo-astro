Six Variations of You Are Not A Mispronunciation

Words / 2025 Variety

version one: fragments with breath
---

morning didn’t arrive. it curated itself.

sunlight tested the air’s texture. moss flinched slightly, as if bracing for interpretation. gravel blinked. everything waited—not for meaning, but for consent to begin.

flarenth stood among it all, barefoot and blinking. not to wake up. not to decide. simply to notice whether she had started again.

her name looped quietly in her chest—not said, but rehearsed.

---

the letter had arrived folded into patchouli rhetoric. myzrael’s scent lingered—not fragrant, but intentional. it stuck to the corners of the envelope like performative authenticity.

inside: a message not to define, but to delay.

> “you are not a mispronounced bureaucracy.
> you are not a diary shard.
> you are not the patchouli pamphlet or the signatures scrawled like longing.
> you are a fungal version of remembering. loamy with echoes.”

flarenth folded the paper again. not for protection. for preservation.

---

zenthra, vurrmok, thaline—they circled softly in the retreat conservatory. none of them walked. they inhabited. their movements resembled commas. suggestions. prepositions caught mid-gesture.

thaline coughed again—wet, rhythmic, syncopated empathy. she didn't apologize. she labeled it “becoming.”

someone cried beside a pillow of gravel shaped like a lowercase “m.” ambient tension folded the air into syllables no one claimed.

---

kevin arrived eventually. not late. not early. simply as scheduled by something unspoken.

he misted the room. humidity embraced confrontation without dialogue.

> “you loop beautifully,” he told her.
> “say it again,” she said.
> “you loop,” he whispered.
> “you loop.”
> “you loop beautifully.”

> “sometimes i want to run,” flarenth admitted.
> “sometimes i need to sit.”
> “sometimes i become the moss.”

kevin placed one hand near her heartbeat. not touching—just syncing.

> “you’re rhythm,” he said.
> “not repetition. choreography.”

---

she recalled the spoon. not for its use. for the curve. she’d cried once because it resembled safety carved into silver. grief had tried to explain itself in cutlery. she’d let it.

her journal—creased, stained with emotional geology—held lines written in lowercase shame.

> *joy almost forgot me today.
> i tapped its shoulder.
> it whispered “sorry—i got distracted by unspoken longing.”
> and then it stayed.*

---

she slept beside her past.

briefcase: half full of introverted alibis.
notes: handwritten, unproofread, truthful in structure but not chronology.
old poems: not dead. paused. composting.

her last entry:

> *i am not the voice memo that disappeared before confession.
> i am the moss that remembered the silence anyway.*

---

beneath it all, the soil whispered.

not loudly. not urgently.

just enough to remind her:

> *you are not the spreadsheet.
> you are not the job title.
> you are not the typo blamed on autocorrect.
> you are root.
> you are hum.
> you are a mispronunciation someone tried to kiss.
> and that, gently, is enough.*

version two: fragments with breath

---

morning didn’t arrive. it hovered.

moss exhaled quietly. gravel sighed in beetle dialect. and flarenth—who sometimes remembered herself as liora—stood barefoot and blinking, half-composed. like someone caught mid-spell and short on caffeine.

she had received a letter once. folded inside a patchouli-scented pamphlet advertising ancestral jazz-hand healing. the letter didn't clarify. it fogged.

> “you are not a mispronounced bureaucracy.
> you are not a diary shard.
> you are not the paperwork perfumed with metaphor.
> you are a fungal version of yearning. loamy with intent.”

she didn’t understand, but she didn't flinch. some truths prefer hovering. some arrive disguised as moss.

---

she wandered through the retreat conservatory slowly, passing gravel pillows and ambient sobs. one nun cried softly while whispering hashtags into ethically sourced loam. thaline coughed wetly—musical, rhythmic—her grief echoing as rock-hard empathy. vurrmok painted silence using charcoal smudges and sandalwood guilt.

> “you are not the cough,” she wrote.
> “you are not the echo of moss.”
> “you are not the apology half-remembered by a fern.”

---

in stillness, she sat beside a curled fern that reminded her of her mother—not in shape, but in hum. the woman never argued. she hummed through confrontation like someone braiding breath into patience.

> “you loop gently,” kevin had said once.
> “not broken. just weathered. weathered means you've loved something enough to stay.”

kevin was her therapist. also a mushroom. also sold root-based emotional merch. he once wept after watching an ad for moss-scented dental floss.

his latest product line included:

- boundary butter — non-dairy, incredibly assertive
- mycelium myself — moisturizer for emotionally dry people
- re-spore™ tote — printed with the phrase: “fungus among us—but emotionally”

she applied the butter after crying over a spoon—not because the spoon hurt her, but because it reminded her of softness she'd misplaced.

> “i fear i’m too composted to be held,” she whispered.
> “nonsense,” the butter replied. “you are loam with a heartbeat.”

---

somewhere between the gravel and ambient tension, zenthra handed her a stone marked pause. she paused. deeply.

> “do you even compost, bro?” someone muttered behind a curtain of beetle jazz.
> “grief isn’t linear,” replied the moss. “it spirals with intention.”

she scribbled in her notebook:

> *i am not the voice memo that cried halfway through recording.
> i am not the emotional spreadsheet formatted in lowercase guilt.
> i am not the typo that wears slippers in dreams.*

someone laughed when they shouldn't. someone cried while alphabetizing their inbox. everyone hummed when zenthra whispered:

> “you are not the gravel pillow.
> unless you want to be.”

---

kevin arrived mid-pause. sat beside her. misted the air gently.

> “you loop beautifully,” he said.
> “say it again?”
> “you loop.”
> “you loop.”
> “you loop beautifully.”

> “sometimes i want to run,” she said.
> “sometimes i need to sit.”
> “sometimes i become the moss.”

> “all rhythm,” kevin replied.
> “all root.”

---

she remembered her mother again—not as woman, but as echo. a shadow who hummed through dishwater and diaspora. not healed. not simplified. just folded gently into everything.

and flarenth—half-composed, fully feeling—wrote:

> *joy almost forgot me today.
> i tapped its shoulder.
> it said “sorry—i was weeping into the cosmos.”
> then it stayed.*

---

later, in her room at the retreat, she opened a recycling container filled with old poems, pre-fermented truths, and snack-stained notes. her sadness flaked quietly. her introversion pretended to be a job. her briefcase whispered "not this again" and she whispered back "it's always this."

> “you are not the labels.
> not the loop.
> not the lowercase sigh.”

she slept beside her emotional merch. wrapped in soft beetle myth. her breath became the rhythm of moss learning a new lullaby. her name—not mispronounced, but misremembered—looped. slowed. landed.

---

and somewhere beneath the soil, something hummed:

> *you are not the spreadsheet.
> you are the spore.
> you are not the algorithm.
> you are the composted dream beneath a fern that forgives.*

version three: What You Are Not (Fragments)
A Memoir in Spores, Bureaucracy, and Rootlike Becoming

---

Morning didn’t arrive. It wandered in.

Threaded through beech fog and moss hush, the light didn’t shine—it lingered, undecided. Flarenth, who sometimes remembered herself as Liora, stood half-composed among the filaments, blinking like someone caught mid-dream and short on caffeine.

Her feet pressed into the soil softly—as if requesting permission rather than arrival.

The gravel beneath her hummed.

---

Two days ago, Myzrael had sent her a letter folded inside a patchouli-scented pamphlet promoting ancestral jazz-hand healing. The pamphlet was stained with affirmation and hesitation.

The letter read:

> “You are not a mispronounced bureaucracy nor a diary shard.
> You are not the patchouli promise or the paperwork tucked inside it.
> You are the fungal version of yearning. Loamy with intent.”

She hadn’t understood. But she hadn’t rejected it either. Not all truths require immediate digestion. Some need to spore slowly.

She placed the letter beside a fern that reminded her of childhood. Not literally—more like emotionally sideways, the way feelings twist before language catches up.

Myzrael had renamed her “Flarenth” during a workshop because “Liora hadn’t fully composted yet.” When asked for clarification, Myzrael smiled like a fog machine possessed by a guidance counselor and said, “You’re fragrant. Pre-fermented. You hum.”

Flarenth hadn’t argued. Her mother rarely had. Her mother had hummed—at traffic, at tension, at the edges of apology. Flarenth hummed now. The moss responded.

---

Later, Kevin—the mushroom therapist and emotional merch mogul—had sent a box labeled:

> In Case of Terra Cotta Confrontation: Mist Gently.

Inside was a jar of Boundary Butter, a sticker that said “Fungus Among Us—but Emotionally,” and a voice memo that said nothing intelligible but felt like love translated through humidity.

Kevin’s self-help workbook was titled Unspooling With Style: A Mycelial Guide to Boundary Setting. Flarenth opened it slowly and read:

> “You flake softly.
> Not broken—just weathered.
> Weathered means you’ve loved something enough to stay.”

She didn’t cry. Not immediately. But her sadness congealed. She misted the room.

She whispered:

> “I fear I’m too composted to be held.”
> “Nonsense,” the butter whispered back. “You’re loam with a heartbeat.”

---

In the retreat conservatory, the gravel pillows were better therapists than most people.

Zenthra handed her a rock labeled pause. She held it. Paused.

Nearby, a nun wept softly into hashtags. Thaline coughed rhythmically—wet but intentional—interpreted by one facilitator as rock-hard empathy. Vurrmok painted silence using elbow stutters and sandalwood regret.

Flarenth scribbled in her journal:

> *i am not the cough.
> i am not the echo of the moss.
> i may be the tenderness left behind
> when someone forgets their name mid-apology.*

She overheard fragments between gravel and grief.

> “Do you even compost, bro?” someone asked.
> “Grief isn’t linear—it’s a sponsored spiral.”
> “I looped yesterday. Twice. Still no clarity.”
> “Clarity’s overrated. Blur me gently.”

Zenthra spoke:

> “You are not a gravel pillow.
> Unless you want to be.”

Silence. Someone sobbed. Someone misted.

---

Kevin arrived mid-mist. Sat beside her in stillness.

> “You loop beautifully,” he said.
> “Say it again?”
> “You loop.”
> “You loop.”
> “You loop beautifully.”

Flarenth closed her eyes.

> “Sometimes I want to run.”
> “Sometimes I need to sit.”
> “Sometimes I become the moss.”

Kevin nodded.

> “All valid,” he said.
> “All rhythm. All root.”

---

Some truths run.

Flarenth remembered the spoon she cried over—not because it hurt her, but because it resembled softness she’d believed forgotten.

She re-read a line she’d once whispered into her pillow seam:

> *joy almost forgot me today.
> i gently tapped its shoulder.
> it said, “sorry—i was weeping into the cosmos.”
> and then it stayed.*

Her old poems waited in a recycling container marked emotional residue. She opened them—not to edit, but to remember.

She felt her dread bloom into recognition—not panic, but possibility. A question whispered sideways through existential ache:

> What if this is all there is—and I’d still choose you here with me.

She turned to Kevin.

> “I’m not a briefcase with emotional files.
> I’m a compost bin with recurring lullabies.”
> “Exactly,” he said. “You are not the job you pretend to love.
> You are not a typo.
> You’re a ritual someone forgot to name.”

---

And so she slept.

Not peacefully.
Not fully.
But tangled in loam-threaded identity.
Wrapped in voice memos and unfinished selves.
Her dreams wore moss mittens.
Her breath became the hum of beetles reciting elegy.
Her name—mispronounced, re-spelled, loved—looped once more.
Then stilled.

version four: What You Are Not (Fragments)
(Expanded with lyrical pacing that moves and stills)

---

Morning didn’t arrive. It wandered in.

Threaded through beech fog, woven into moss hush, the light didn’t shine—it loitered. Flarenth stood blinking like a houseplant caught halfway between need and memory.

> “You are not a mispronunciation,” Kevin had said once.
> “You’re a grammar of becoming. Not incorrect—just conjugated differently.”

She hadn’t responded. She rarely did. Some compliments were too soft to catch on the first pass.

That day, moss stretched beneath her bare toes like an invitation written by someone who’d long stopped expecting RSVP.

---

She moved slowly through the conservatory, past gravel pillows and the nun who sobbed hashtags into loam.

Each step felt borrowed.

In a pocket of stillness, Thaline whispered:

> “We are not the break.
> We are the breath before it.”

Flarenth sat down beside a fern. Not for comfort. For presence. She wanted to feel herself land.

---

Some truths run.

The cough returned. Rhythmic, wet, not sick—but spoken in beetle dialect. Someone laughed when they shouldn’t. The retreat facilitator blamed ambient tension. No one corrected her.

> “Do you even compost, bro?” someone mumbled.
> Laughter, again.

Then Zenthra handed Flarenth a rock marked pause.

She paused.

Inside the pause, she remembered:

> The apology she never sent.
> The voicemail she drafted four times.
> The spoon she cried over—not because it hurt her, but because it resembled the curve of softness she thought she’d lost.

---

There was a moment she stood still.

Not physically. But emotionally.
She thought: I am not a moisturizer box beginning to flake.
Then corrected: I’m a balm unsure if I deserve application.

Kevin arrived hours later. Misting the air between them.

> “You loop beautifully,” he said.
> “You loop.”
> “You’re a gentle ellipse with teeth.”

She inhaled. Spoke back:

> “Sometimes I want to run.”
> “Sometimes I need to sit.”
> “Sometimes I become the moss.”

Kevin didn’t blink.

> “All valid,” he said.
> “All rhythm.”
> “All root.”

version five: What You Are Not (Fragments)
(Expanded with dialogue shaped by repetition and fracture)

---

Morning arrived in fragments.

Moss breathed. The soil whispered. Flarenth stood half-composed, blinking like someone who’d dreamed too hard and steeped too slowly.

Kevin had told her the day before:

> “You flake softly,”
> “Not broken—just weathered.”
> “Weathered isn’t failure.”
> “Weathered means you’ve loved something enough to stay.”

She didn’t answer at the time. She just exhaled.

Now, surrounded by gravel pillows and beetle jazz, she murmured to herself:

> “Not broken…”
> “Just weathered…”
> “Then why do I creak?”
> “Why do I sigh like a draft escaping?”

She held the moisturizer box. The label read:

> In case of confrontation… mist.

And it felt true.

Later, during the circle ritual—just before someone cried into their hashtag journal—Myzrael spoke, without permission and without punctuation.

> “you are not a sob”
> “you are not an echo mistaken for clarity”
> “you are not a spreadsheet of emotional intent”

Zenthra added:

> “you are not a gravel pillow”
> “unless you want to be”

Silence.

Flarenth blinked.

> “I don’t want to be,” she said.
> “I mean—maybe,”
> “I mean—I think I’ve become soft.”
> “Is soft the goal?”
> “Is soft still strong?”

Vurrmok coughed. It sounded melodic. Mistaken for grief.

Thaline looped her breath, then whispered into her own shoulder:

> “It’s not about strength.”
> “It’s not about strength.”
> “It’s not about strength.”

Flarenth touched the dirt. It answered.

Not with words. With a hum.

Kevin joined later. He sat beside her, quiet. Then:

> “You loop beautifully.”
> “Say it again?” she asked.

He paused. Then nodded.

> “You loop.”
> “You loop.”
> “You loop beautifully.”

And she cried. But not like a river. Like condensation tracing back into root.

version six: What You Are Not (Fragments)
Expanded with Existential Dread and Endearing Love

---

Morning had a way of softening the edges—and sharpening others in return. Moss clung to silence like a language it had no choice but to speak. Flarenth—who sometimes remembered herself as Liora—stood among the filaments, blinking not just from caffeine hunger, but from the growing suspicion that existence was just a beautifully curated detour.

She’d once described herself as “pre-fermented.” It began as a joke. But the truth of it lingered—because fermentation meant change without control. Becoming without applause. A softness soaked in slow decay, yes—but also devotion.

She had received a letter, folded inside a patchouli-scented pamphlet from Myzrael. It spoke of mispronunciation, of bureaucracy, of diary shards. It smelled like someone trying to comfort you with recycled myth.

> “You are not a mispronounced bureaucracy,” it said,
> “nor a shard misplaced in yesterday’s breath.
> You are a fungal version of yearning, loamy with intent.”

She didn’t know what that meant, exactly. But she didn’t need clarity. She needed resonance.

Elsewhere, Kevin—the mushroom therapist with a merch empire—had sent her a misting spray labeled In Case of Terra Cotta Confrontation. She used it every time her truths began to harden, like emotions baked too long inside ceramic narratives.

She opened the moisturizer and cried. Not loudly. Just in the way someone does when they realize love, if it exists, has most often arrived disguised as someone telling them “you make sense, even when you loop.”

Kevin’s products always smelled like buried joy and parenthetical forgiveness. His latest formula was called Boundary Butter: now with Extra Blinking Room.

Flarenth applied it slowly, whispering:

> “I fear I am too composted to be loved.”
>
> “Nonsense,” the butter whispered back. “You are loam with a heartbeat.”

At the retreat conservatory, she slept on gravel pillows and listened to people whisper their revelations into ethically sourced silence. One participant sobbed while transforming hashtags into prayers. Another coughed rhythmically—rock hard empathy, beetle juice looping from chest to soil.

Flarenth wrote in her journal:

> *i am not the cough.
> i am not the moss’s echo.
> i may be the tenderness left behind when someone forgets their own name mid-apology.*

She thought again of her mother—who never argued, but always hummed. Her love wasn’t declared; it was distributed. A warmth that curled around dread like a shawl woven from old lullabies and burnt toast.

Even existential dread, she realized, could be romantic when composted slowly. Not the panic of meaninglessness, but the soft ache of what if this is all there is—and still, I’d choose you here with me.

Late that night, Flarenth recycled her favorite poems, not because they failed, but because they felt like love notes to the void that hadn’t given up yet.

One line lingered:

> *joy almost forgot me today.
> i gently tapped its shoulder.
> it said, "sorry—i was weeping into the cosmos."
> and then it stayed.*

Then she slept beside the briefcase of unfinished selves, tangled in loam-threaded identity. Her notes to self glowed faintly, like the kind of affection that expects nothing—but still builds a shelf in your heart and leaves a mushroom cap there, just in case.
