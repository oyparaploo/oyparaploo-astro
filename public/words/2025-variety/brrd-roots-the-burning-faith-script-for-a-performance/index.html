Brrd Roots ~ The Burning Faith ~ Script For A Performance

Words / 2025 Variety

Title: The Burning Faith

---

Dramatis Personae:
- Raphael: A revered leader, torn between his reformist vision and the moral cost of his choices.
- Eva: His sister, a relentless advocate for justice, urging him to see beyond the empire he built.
- The Wanderer: A figure of wisdom and divine humility—offering truth that both disrupts and redeems.
- Jonah: A lost soul, abandoned by society, yearning for grace but fearing he is beyond salvation.
- Silas: A man obsessed with power, willing to sacrifice faith for influence.
- Victor: A scholar disillusioned by knowledge, realizing intellect alone does not bring meaning.
- Ivy: A woman who once traded her soul for material gain, now facing the abyss of her decisions.
- Aurora: A radiant figure of divine grace, guiding souls toward redemption.
- The Chorus: Voices woven between time, bearing witness to humanity’s struggle between light and shadow.

---

Setting:
The stage mirrors a divided world—one side adorned with Raphael’s towering cathedral, draped in gold and authority; the other, a barren wasteland where the forgotten reside. Between them, a fire flickers—neither destructive nor welcoming, but waiting.

---

Act I: The Collapse of Certainty

(Raphael stands before his grand cathedral, surrounded by marble and gilded light. Yet the Chorus hums from the shadows, filling the air with doubt.)

Chorus (whispering softly):
The walls rise, the bells resound,
Yet truth cries beyond the gates.
What is power, when hearts remain broken?

Raphael (fragmented prose, laced with inner turmoil):
I built this world—
Gave them laws, gave them sanctuary.
But why does silence follow my sermons?
Why do shadows crawl beneath gold?

(Eva enters, carrying a single candle—its flame trembling in her grasp.)

Eva (blank verse):
You stand upon a throne, brother,
Yet below, suffering remains.
What good is power,
If the forgotten still fall beneath its weight?

Raphael (defensive, bitter prose):
I gave them shelter!
I built sanctuaries, fed the hungry.
Must I tear down all that I have raised,
Simply because the night is not yet over?

(Victor steps forward, his journal clutched tightly, ink smudging the pages.)

Victor (halting prose, broken by disillusionment):
I sought salvation in knowledge—
In equations, in doctrine,
In words etched upon parchment.
Yet every answer I find
Is merely a shadow of what was lost.
What good is wisdom,
If the soul remains empty?

(The Wanderer emerges from the darkness, carrying a loaf of bread. He places it beside Eva’s candle.)

Wanderer (softly, hymn-like prose):
Raphael, Victor—
You raise towers, yet have you raised love?
You chase knowledge, yet have you sought truth?
Ambition without grace is fire—
It burns but never warms.

---

Act II: The Surrender of Souls

(The wasteland takes form—cracked earth and hollow streets. Jonah sits alone, his breath slow, staring at his calloused hands. Ivy stands behind him, gripping a crimson ribbon.)

Jonah (halting free verse):
They told me mercy was endless.
That forgiveness had no gate.
Yet here I kneel,
And heaven is silent.
Will faith find me,
Or has it abandoned me?

(Raphael kneels beside him, holding the bread.)

Raphael (softly, uncertain):
Take this.

Jonah (bitterly, broken verse):
Bread?
A fragment of mercy?
Will bread undo suffering?
Will it bring back the warmth lost?

(Silas steps forward, his voice like cold steel.)

Silas:
Faith is a vessel for the weak.
Power is its own salvation.
Raphael, why do you kneel before dust,
When your throne awaits you?

(Raphael hesitates, turning toward him.)

Raphael:
Power without love is hollow.
It crumbles, even in gold.

(Ivy steps forward, her ribbon tangled around her wrists. Her voice shakes under the weight of guilt.)

Ivy (fragmented prose, near breaking):
I traded my soul for wealth.
I sold mercy for influence.
And now I stand upon my throne,
Yet my hands tremble—
Why is it that the higher I climbed,
The colder the air became?

(Victor kneels beside her, his voice softened.)

Victor:
Grace does not count what is deserved.
It only waits for surrender.
Let go, Ivy—let the burden fall.

(The Wanderer appears once more, his presence luminous yet quiet.)

Wanderer:
Ivy, will you lay down your crown?
Will you unbind the chains that tie you
To a kingdom of ashes?

(The candle flickers, casting shadows upon Ivy’s hands. The Chorus hums in rising lament.)

Chorus:
To love… To heal… To restore…

(Ivy collapses, gripping the ribbon as it dissolves into golden threads. Aurora steps forward, placing her hands over Ivy. Her shadow lifts as she weeps.)

---

Act III: The Light Beyond the Ruins

(The wasteland begins to shift. Golden lilies bloom through the cracked earth. The fire flickers, but now it carries light instead of destruction.)

Eva (to Raphael):
Power must kneel before love,
Or it will crumble.
Lay down your throne,
And carry light instead.

(Raphael kneels beside Jonah, lighting the candle together. Aurora steps forward, radiant in golden light, placing her hands upon Raphael’s shoulders.)

Aurora (softly, divine prose):
Greatness is not in what is built,
But in what is mended.
Let love carry you—
Beyond the ruins.

(Victor kneels beside them, placing his journal upon the fire. His voice carries clarity.)

Victor:
I sought truth in reason,
But found wisdom in surrender.
Not in what I calculated,
But in what I let go.

(The Wanderer lifts his arms, his voice resonating like a hymn.)

Wanderer (final blessing, rhythmic hymn):
Greatness does not rise in towers,
But blooms where love takes root.
Build not monuments,
But bridges.
Let this light carry you
Beyond the ruins.

(The candle burns brighter, stretching toward the stars. The bread ascends, dissolving into radiant light. The Chorus rises, a hymn of restoration. The curtain falls.)
