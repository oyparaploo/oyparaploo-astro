**Chapter Eighteen ~ The Pigalle Return**

Words / 2025 Variety

Paris breathed its old perfume: damp stone, bread, exhaust, perfume that had once been roses and was now something sharper. Pigalle at night was as lit as confession and as crowded as an excuse. Neon combed the wet streets with light; bass from the clubs walked the pavements with a swagger that claimed no shame.

Troll Tearsen and Libaux Torrini climbed from the Metro into the noise that pretends to be freedom. They had come back to hear a rehearsal—not of music, but of something that wanted to be policy disguised as performance.

The club door wore a velvet rope it didn’t need; the doorman recognized neither of them and all of them at once. A few words from Libaux, two folded euros, and they were inside.

---

The room was low-ceilinged, padded, dressed in red. Mirrors sweated at the edges. The stage was a rectangle of cheap light, microphone waiting like a throat unsatisfied. Sound filled the place before anyone spoke. **boom-thrum**, bass from the subs; **shhk-shhk**, high hats like knives closing; chatter folded into the noise until it became furniture.

On the stage: the liaison. Smooth suit, that same smile designed for transactions. He was testing levels. “One, two—” the mic gave him back **one, two**—“oversight—” and the PA delivered the word like a psalm.

“Not Stremler,” Libaux muttered.

“Never is,” Troll said. “He prefers ventriloquists.”

The liaison rolled a marble across a table mic’d with too much gain. The tiny **tik** arrived through the subs as a subterranean **BOOM**, shuddering the walls, making glasses rattle their small applause. Patrons looked up, smiled, thought it part of the entertainment. The liaison smiled wider, as though they had confirmed his faith.

---

Troll pushed closer through the bodies. The floor flexed under each bass note. She let the crowd hide her as she watched his hands: three marbles in a dish, each seeded with bubbles that caught light like a confession. He rolled them one by one; the sound engineer sampled each and looped it, folding them into the bass.

Soon the club wasn’t playing music at all—it was playing marbles, layered over a skeletal rhythm. The liaison spoke over it: *“Noise is narrative. Repeat it, and it becomes true.”*

The crowd cheered. They thought it poetry, or irony. They didn’t hear the instruction inside the line.

Libaux leaned into Troll’s ear. “He’s teaching them the score.”

Troll touched the recorder at her coat’s pocket. “Then we take notes.”

---

The liaison’s speech shifted from metaphor to manipulation. “Oversight is the art of tuning a room,” he said, voice buttered through the PA. “Every silence can be purchased. Every echo can be rehearsed.”

He let a marble roll again. The **tik** spread like an organ note. The crowd clapped to it without meaning to, a rhythm they hadn’t chosen.

Troll felt her stomach knot. The same technique from Vienna, but rawer, testing ground before theater. Here the experiment had alcohol to help it succeed.

Libaux moved to the sound booth, slipping past a couple too distracted to notice. He bent over the engineer’s shoulder. A few quiet words, a badge shown just long enough to mean trouble, and the man stepped back. Libaux’s hands found the faders.

The next marble rolled. **tik**. Instead of blooming into bass, it fell flat, naked, weak as glass. A ripple went through the crowd—they expected thunder, received toy. Some laughed. The liaison’s jaw tightened.

Troll took the mic before he could. Her voice, clear, low: “Marbles tell the truth when you don’t make them lie.”

The crowd reacted with the half-curiosity clubs grant a surprise act. Some booed, some cheered, most kept drinking. But the sound had shifted: Troll’s words had arrived without echo, unamplified by trickery. The room heard difference.

The liaison tried to recover, rolling two marbles at once. Libaux killed the loop. The sound landed as a child’s game, ridiculous in its smallness.

“Noise,” Troll said into the mic, “is not narrative. It’s grief pretending to be law. Don’t dance to it.”

The crowd broke, half cheering, half confused, the way crowds do when art becomes indictment. But Stremler’s agent had been cut. The rehearsal failed.

---

After, outside, Pigalle roared its ordinary roar—taxis honking, girls laughing, a drunk arguing with a poster. Troll stood at the curb, hands trembling slightly. Libaux lit no cigarette, only watched the smoke of someone else’s drift across neon.

“We humiliated him,” he said.

“We interrupted him,” Troll corrected. “Humiliation is optional. Interruption is permanent.”

Her phone buzzed. **Mage Meuller**: *Captured the stream. Your sabotage worked—loops collapsed. But careful. Stremler will shift from rehearsal to premiere sooner than you expect.*

Another: **Helene Klausmun**: *The valley holds, but the silence feels staged. Come back when you can. Something waits.*

Troll slipped the phone away. The marbles in her pocket felt heavier, as though each had recorded the evening’s fracture.

Libaux looked up the boulevard, where neon hummed louder than stars. “Next?”

“Next,” Troll said, “he takes the valley itself.”

The street echoed the word back—**next, next**—a game of walls and stone. Pigalle grinned and went on with its night.
