A Call for More Fairness

Words / 2025 Variety

In our nation—and surely in others—we're told that obeying the law is the straightest path to financial success. Follow the rules, work hard, and prosperity will follow. But those not born into privilege eventually discover an uncomfortable truth: the laws we're told to respect often fail to deliver fairness for all citizens.

These laws were crafted by the most financially powerful among us, shaped for the benefit of the rule makers and lawmakers themselves. Whether by design or accumulated advantage, the result is the same: a system where those who influence the rules rarely face the same struggles as those who must live by them.

Questioning and changing these unjust laws isn't just an option—it's a purposeful and noble cause that defines the health of any democracy. But this cannot be accomplished through podcasts that preach to the converted or social media rants that dissolve into the digital void. Real change demands that real people collect in real spaces, organize with discipline, and work tirelessly to reshape laws that have been entrenched through decades of influence and lobbying.

This struggle isn't new. Since the very beginning of our democracy, each generation has faced this same challenge: will we accept the injustices we inherited, or will we labor to leave something better for those who come after? The answer has always been the same—fairness is never given; it must be taken by those brave enough to demand it, organized enough to sustain the fight, and stubborn enough to outlast those who profit from the status quo.

Our nation is always changing, as it always has. The question is whether it will change through our collective action or continue to be shaped by the few for their own benefit. The choice, as always, is ours.
