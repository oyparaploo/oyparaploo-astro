The Pulse of Survival ~ When Mushrooms Witness the Digital Pirates

Words / 2025 Variety

## Part I: The Authority of Static

I'm tracking illegal data flows through dying coral reef servers when Brian shows me mushrooms growing on abandoned fiber optic cables, and I realize we're documenting the same collapse—not just environmental but digital, where memory itself is burning and freezing simultaneously.

My credentials: Ten years as a marine biologist turned cybersecurity analyst after discovering coral communicates through electromagnetic pulses identical to encrypted data. A grandmother who was a code-breaker in WWII, who taught me patterns repeat across all systems. Three terabytes of evidence showing climate collapse and digital piracy share the same root network.

The paradox: We built systems to preserve everything—nature, data, memory—but preservation itself became the mechanism of loss.

## Part II: The Mushroom Network

*Underwater data center, Orkney Islands. Present day.*

Microsoft's underwater servers, cooled by ocean currents, have been colonized by something unexpected: mushrooms that shouldn't exist here, growing on cables, feeding on electromagnetic radiation.

Brian, my research partner, ex-pirate turned environmental hacker, kneels beside the growth. "They're not just growing on the network. They're becoming it."

The mushrooms pulse with data flow—brightening when packets pass through, dimming during low traffic. They've evolved to eat information.

"Static," Brian says, placing his hand on the fungal network. "I can feel it. Like ancestral memory trying to break through."

## Part III: The Coral Archives

*Three months earlier. Great Barrier Reef.*

I'm documenting bleached coral when my instruments detect something impossible: the dying reef is transmitting data. Not randomly—structured packets, encrypted protocols.

The coral has been storing information—ocean temperatures, chemical compositions, death dates of species—in its calcium carbonate structure. A biological hard drive documenting its own extinction.

Dr. Sarah Chang, my former mentor, reviews the data. "It's not just recording. It's broadcasting. Like a distress signal."

But who's receiving?

## Part IV: Katie K9's Warning

*Memory: Twin Cities, one year ago.*

I meet Katie K9 in a pickup truck with a camper shell, the vehicle covered in QR codes that lead nowhere. She's tracking something she calls "the pulse"—moments when digital and biological networks sync.

"You study coral?" she asks, chewing gum, wearing a Castro-style cap.

"And data flow."

"Same thing now. Ocean's become the internet's memory. When one fails, both fail."

She shows me her equipment—modified sonar that reads both marine life and data transmission. The patterns are identical.

"Wild for the people," she says, driving off. "Against unfair entitlements."

I don't understand then. I do now.

## Part V: Pirates of the Anthropocene

*Interview with Brian, recorded on encrypted channel.*

"Modern piracy isn't about ships," Brian explains. "It's about data streams, carbon credits, identity theft on species level."

He shows me his old work—not stealing music or movies but environmental data corporations hide. Temperature readings that prove acceleration. Species counts showing faster collapse than reported.

"Every corporation is pirating from the future," he says. "I just pirated it back."

His static—PTSD from seeing too much data, knowing too much truth—manifests as literal electromagnetic interference. Devices glitch near him. Mushrooms grow faster.

## Part VI: Hot Roots, Cold Storage

*Data center, Northern Sweden. Built in abandoned mine.*

The servers here store "cold data"—information accessed rarely but preserved forever. The cooling system has failed. Ice and fire fight for control.

In the heat: mushrooms thriving, processing data, creating new networks.
In the cold: preserved packets from decades ago, frozen conversations, archived extinctions.

Brian navigates between temperature zones. "Hot roots burn through firewalls. Cold roots preserve what shouldn't be forgotten."

We find it in the overlap zone: coral data packets merging with mushroom networks, creating hybrid information—biological memory becoming digital, digital becoming alive.

## Part VII: The Window Paradox

*Brian's apartment, walls covered in equations.*

"Reality's a window pretending to be a door," he says, showing me his models. "We think we're looking through it, but we're supposed to walk through."

His calculations show climate collapse and digital expansion as the same phenomenon—energy transforming from biological to electronic and back.

"Mushrooms understand. Coral understood. We're the only species that doesn't get it."

Static fills the room. His trauma and the world's trauma resonating at the same frequency.

## Part VIII: Billion-Year Download

*Back to Orkney, present.*

The mushroom network has spread, connecting underwater servers to kelp forests, creating organic fiber optic cables. Data flows through living tissue.

We intercept transmissions: not human data but older—patterns from before industrialization, temperatures from stable climates, species counts from abundant oceans. The planet's memory backing itself up through fungi.

"It's not just recording collapse," Brian realizes. "It's preserving the blueprint for restoration."

## Part IX: Digital Tides

*Pacific Garbage Patch, now also data graveyard.*

Dead phones, broken servers, e-waste creating artificial reef where nothing natural grows. Except the mushrooms. They're here too, breaking down plastic and silicon, converting poison to possibility.

Katie K9's boat appears. She's been tracking us.

"Found the pulse?" she asks.

"Found where digital meets biological," I answer.

She nods. "Revolution isn't human anymore. It's bacterial, fungal, viral. Life hacking capitalism."

## Part X: The Static Prophet

*Brian's transformation.*

The static that tormented him becomes his tool. He learns to read electromagnetic interference like morse code, translating between mushroom network and human understanding.

"Shake it loose," he says, hands on the fungal cables. "Not like there isn't enough poison already mixed with sweet chitter-chatter."

Through him, we hear it: the planet's actual voice. Not metaphorical but literal—electromagnetic patterns that predate human language.

## Part XI: Collapse Protocol

*Discovery: The mushrooms aren't random.*

They're following emergency protocols written into nature's base code. When biological systems fail, they digitize. When digital systems fail, they biologize.

The coral knew this, started broadcasting before dying. The mushrooms know this, converting data to spores. Even the weather knows—storms following internet cable paths, hurricanes downloading destruction patterns from previous centuries.

"Earth has a backup system," Brian says. "We're just the virus it's working around."

## Part XII: Reverberation

*Final scene: New hybrid zone, location classified.*

We've created a sanctuary where digital and biological merge intentionally. Coral growing on server racks, teaching them to store carbon. Mushrooms processing data, teaching them to self-repair.

Brian tends the network, static finally quiet. "We were pirates stealing from the future. Now we're returning what we stole."

Katie K9 visits, brings others—hackers, marine biologists, indigenous knowledge keepers. Each adds their understanding to the hybrid network.

The planet still burns. The ice still melts. But in between, in the static spaces, something new grows—not human, not natural, not digital, but all three learning to survive together.

## Epilogue: The Pulse Continues

*One year later.*

The mushroom networks have spread to every underwater data center. Coral reefs are regenerating around server farms, using waste heat for growth. Digital pirates now steal carbon credits and return them to the atmosphere as forests.

Brian sends a message through the fungal network: "Static clearing. Signal emerging. The pulse says: adapt or be adapted."

I place my hand on the hybrid growth—part mushroom, part coral, part fiber optic. Feel the data flowing: every extinct species' DNA, every stable climate pattern, every sustainable solution we ignored.

The planet is backing itself up, with or without us. The pirates have become gardeners. The windows have become doors.

And in the static between states—between burning and freezing, digital and biological, collapse and evolution—the pulse of survival continues.

*Author's Note: The hybrid networks now exist in seventeen locations globally. Governments classify them as bioterrorism or environmental restoration depending on politics. The mushrooms don't care. They continue converting poison to possibility, preserving what burns, thawing what freezes. Brian's static has become a teaching method—electromagnetic interference as indigenous knowledge. Katie K9 was never seen again, but her QR codes now lead to restoration protocols. The coral still dies, but dies transmitting. The transformation continues.*
