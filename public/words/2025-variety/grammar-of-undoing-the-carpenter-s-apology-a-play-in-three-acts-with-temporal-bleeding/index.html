GRAMMAR OF UNDOING ~ The Carpenter's Apology

Words / 2025 Variety

### A Play in Three Acts with Temporal Bleeding

**By B Otto Will and Brrd Roots**

---

## CHARACTERS

**ANGELICA WALL** - 30s-60s, ages backward and forward. A carpenter who builds in reverse, teaching wood to remember being trees.

**THE NARRATOR** - Any age. Angelica's neighbor, documenting the undocumentation.

**JOY DECK** - 20s-40s. Refugee from next Thursday. Brings coffee that brews backward.

**CAPITAL MONK** - Ageless. A pangolin made of compressed prayers, each scale a divine rejection.

**AMOS KAZBRO** - 30s-50s. The Temporarily Deceased. Lives under the overpass between existence and void.

**THE GRIEF-MOTH** - Angelica's father transformed. Size of a door, wings of compressed sighs.

**THE TERMITE QUEEN** - "The Architect of Undoing." Glows with decomposition's light.

**STORM DAVID** - Angelica's ex-husband who chases yesterday's weather.

**THE THREE CHILDREN** - One child experienced thrice: PAST-CHILD, PRESENT-CHILD, FUTURE-CHILD.

**THE COUNCIL** - Municipal officials who exist too firmly.

**THE CHORUS OF THE UNBUILT** - Furniture that was never made, trees never planted, houses never constructed.

---

## SETTING

The stage is Angelica's workshop/garage, but time moves differently in each corner:

**CENTER**: The workbench where present tense struggles to exist
**STAGE LEFT**: Yesterday's workspace, tools moving backward
**STAGE RIGHT**: Tomorrow's workspace, furniture dissolving into lumber
**UPSTAGE**: The Grief-Moth's cocoon cathedral
**DOWNSTAGE**: The temporal bleed where audience and performance meet

*Note: Time is a suggestion. Past and future scenes occur simultaneously. Characters age and de-age within single conversations.*

---

## ACT ONE: THE GRAMMAR LESSON

### Scene 1: Morning's Undoing

*(7 AM. Silence where sawing should be. THE NARRATOR enters the garage to find ANGELICA holding the glowing TERMITE QUEEN.)*

**NARRATOR**: The silence has weight. Like water pressing against glass.

**ANGELICA**: *(To the Termite Queen)* Show them how cherry wood remembers its blossoms.

*(The wood begins dissolving, sawdust spiraling upward into the shape of a tree.)*

**TERMITE QUEEN**: *(Voice like wood settling)* Every nail hole is a wound that wants to heal backward.

**ANGELICA**: This is the first grammar rule: All creation is violence. All destruction is mercy.

**NARRATOR**: How long have you been—

**ANGELICA**: Unbuilding? Since my father became that.

*(Points to the GRIEF-MOTH hanging upside down, wings trembling with inherited sorrow.)*

**GRIEF-MOTH**: *(Wing-whispers)* Cardiac metamorphosis. The heart too large for the chest. Had to grow wings or explode.

**ANGELICA**: He's teaching me through absence. Every empty space is a lesson.

*(JOY DECK enters backward through tomorrow's door, carrying Turkish coffee in a copper pot.)*

**JOY**: *(Pouring coffee that turns into beans, then soil)* I brought yesterday's tomorrow's coffee. Still warm from never being brewed.

**ANGELICA**: Joy Deck, refugee from next Thursday, meet my neighbor who thinks time moves forward.

**NARRATOR**: Time does move forward.

**JOY**: Prove it.

*(She ages five years, then returns to her original age, then becomes a child, then returns.)*

**JOY**: See? Time is negotiable. Like grammar. Like suffering.

---

### Scene 2: The Council's Gravity

*(The garage transforms. COUNCIL MEMBERS enter, their bodies too solid, creating dents in spacetime.)*

**COUNCIL MEMBER 1**: Ms. Wall, you're in violation of existing.

**ANGELICA**: *(Her skin becoming translucent, organs visible)* Watch my disappointment.

*(Her liver turns gray, her appendix swells with hope.)*

**COUNCIL MEMBER 2**: The parking garage will be built where memories live.

**ANGELICA**: Memories are citizens. They vote. They pay taxes in nostalgia.

**COUNCIL MEMBER 1**: Memories aren't real.

**ANGELICA**: *(Building a chair from wood that doesn't exist)* Neither is money. Neither is authority. But here we are, pretending.

*(The chair screams the name of its unmade forest.)*

**ANGELICA**: This chair is made from the oak your parking garage will prevent from growing. Can you hear it screaming its unbirth?

**COUNCIL**: *(Covering ears)* Make it stop!

**ANGELICA**: I'm running for council. The unborn are my constituency.

**COUNCIL MEMBER 2**: The unborn can't vote.

**JOY**: *(From tomorrow)* They did. She got eleven votes. Her unborn mother voted through séance. Her tomorrow-self voted through time-loop.

**ANGELICA**: I lost by winning. The garage will be built but immediately sink, pulled down by the weight of deleted gardens.

*(The COUNCIL exits, but their weight remains, bending the stage.)*

---

### Scene 3: Highway 49's Question

*(Lights shift. ANGELICA on the ground. A CELESTIAL DEER made of starlight dying backward through her.)*

**ANGELICA**: *(To the deer)* Shh, shh. *(Singing in Russian)* Zaika maya, you're un-dying beautifully.

**NARRATOR**: The ambulance is coming from yesterday.

**ANGELICA**: Tell them to bring bandages that remove wounds instead of covering them.

*(CAPITAL MONK enters—a pangolin whose scales are rejected prayers.)*

**CAPITAL MONK**: I'll trade you three ribs for the ability to see endings.

**ANGELICA**: Will it hurt?

**CAPITAL MONK**: Everything hurts. But backward hurting feels like healing if you don't think about it too hard.

*(ANGELICA hands over three ribs. Her skeleton becomes visible, each bone inscribed with its future fracture date.)*

**ANGELICA**: *(Reading her bones)* Tuesday. Thursday. Next September. Christmas 2034.

**CAPITAL MONK**: Now you know when, but not how. That's mercy.

*(The CELESTIAL DEER completes its un-dying, leaps backward through the windshield, which un-shatters.)*

---

### Scene 4: The Under-Overpass

*(The space beneath tomorrow's bridge. AMOS KAZBRO sits in temporal suspension.)*

**AMOS**: I'm temporarily deceased. Permanent death is so... permanent.

**ANGELICA**: Your house—

**AMOS**: Destroyed by a hurricane that hasn't happened yet. Insurance doesn't cover precognitive damage.

**ANGELICA**: *(Offering food that's already been digested)* Rescue cuisine in reverse. Nutrients extracted from waste.

**AMOS**: Tastes like tomorrow's hunger.

**ANGELICA**: *(Studying decay specimens)* Look—this fruit is growing intelligent mold. It's whispering stock tips.

**INTELLIGENT MOLD**: Buy nothing. Sell everything. Invest in absence.

**AMOS**: Everything falls apart into better things.

**ANGELICA**: That's the second grammar rule: Decomposition is composition in the honest direction.

*(The GRIEF-MOTH descends, weaving cocoons from their conversation.)*

**GRIEF-MOTH**: Small griefs need homes too. The grief of burnt toast. Of missed calls. Of sentences that end mid—

---

## ACT TWO: THE SYNTAX OF SUFFERING

### Scene 1: Reverse Wedding

*(STORM DAVID enters, maps of yesterday's weather floating around him.)*

**STORM DAVID**: I'm leaving on Tuesday.

**ANGELICA**: Today is Tuesday.

**STORM DAVID**: Not the Tuesday that happened. The Tuesday that happens on Wednesday.

*(THE THREE CHILDREN enter—same child at different ages.)*

**PAST-CHILD**: *(Age 16, aging backward)* Mom, why was I born knowing how to die?

**PRESENT-CHILD**: *(Age 8, stable)* She's unbuilding us into possibility.

**FUTURE-CHILD**: *(Infant with adult eyes)* We're one person experienced thrice. A temporal trinity of her mistake.

**STORM DAVID**: I can't compete with your infinite capacity to care about nothing.

**ANGELICA**: Nothing needs care most of all. Something can take care of itself.

*(She signs divorce papers. The marriage certificate unburns itself.)*

**STORM DAVID**: *(Exiting into yesterday)* I'll remember you tomorrow.

**ANGELICA**: I'm forgetting you retroactively.

*(She holds wood that remembers being furniture in houses not yet built.)*

---

### Scene 2: The Adjacent Lovers

*(Three lovers appear in parallel dimensions, visible but unreachable.)*

**PHOTOGRAPHER LOVER**: *(From next year)* We'll meet soon. We've already broken up.

**BAKER LOVER**: *(From last year)* Our relationship was perfect because it never started.

**SOCIAL WORKER LOVER**: *(From parallel timeline)* In my dimension, we're friends. It means something different there.

**ANGELICA**: I'm built for adjacency. Love requires distance to breathe.

*(She builds a coffin from pre-incarnated wood.)*

**ANGELICA**: This tree remembered being human. Named Angela. Died in 1842. Became an oak. Now becoming furniture for the pre-dead.

**NARRATOR**: Who's it for?

**ANGELICA**: Everyone. Eventually. Retroactively.

---

### Scene 3: The Disease of Kindness

*(ANGELICA's hands begin releasing tools before impact.)*

**ANGELICA**: My hands are becoming too kind to cause damage.

**JOY**: That's the final evolution. When you can't even hurt wood anymore.

*(ANGELICA's bones turn transparent, fill with light.)*

**ANGELICA**: I'm becoming a constellation visible only from inside.

**CAPITAL MONK**: *(Scales rattling with rejected prayers)* God's accepting your apology.

**ANGELICA**: I haven't apologized.

**CAPITAL MONK**: Your existence is an apology. Every breath says sorry for taking air.

*(She builds a chair from everyone who ever loved her.)*

**ANGELICA**: Sit in this.

**NARRATOR**: *(Sitting)* It feels like... everyone.

**ANGELICA**: That's the third grammar rule: We're all furniture for each other's rest.

---

### Scene 4: The Medusa Cathedral

*(The garage transforms into bioluminescent cathedral. Tentacles of light hang where tools were.)*

**CHORUS OF THE UNBUILT**: *(Singing)*
We are the chairs never sat in
Tables never eaten upon
Houses never sheltering
Trees never cut
Lumber never sawn
We exist in the space between intention and prevention

**ANGELICA**: *(To JOY)* You need a chair for your unbody.

**JOY**: To not-sit in while not-being.

*(ANGELICA builds a chair from absence itself—visible only as the space around it.)*

**ANGELICA**: This is my altruistic deletion. Removing something so nothing can exist.

**AMOS**: *(From under the overpass)* Teach us to unbuild properly.

**ANGELICA**: First, hold the hammer backward. Feel its weight wanting to return to ore. Let it pull nails instead of driving them. Each removal is a small resurrection.

*(Everyone practices unbuilding. The stage gradually empties of objects.)*

---

## ACT THREE: THE APOLOGY COMPLETE

### Scene 1: Tomorrow's Disasters

*(ANGELICA stands in three different disasters simultaneously.)*

**ANGELICA**: *(In future flood)* The water hasn't risen yet but I'm teaching people to breathe underwater.

**ANGELICA**: *(In potential fire)* The flames might exist if wind patterns align. I'm building furniture from future char.

**ANGELICA**: *(In hypothetical tornado)* It's still deciding whether to form. I'm negotiating with its pre-rotation.

**FUTURE WIDOW**: After my house will burn, you taught me to make a table from char that doesn't exist.

**ANGELICA**: Every meal will taste like possibility.

*(All three disasters collapse into one moment of pure potential.)*

---

### Scene 2: The Children's Inheritance

*(THE THREE CHILDREN stand around ANGELICA as she builds a cradle backward.)*

**PAST-CHILD**: *(Now infant)* Teach us to remember forward.

**PRESENT-CHILD**: *(Now elderly)* She already has. We just haven't learned it yet.

**FUTURE-CHILD**: *(Now middle-aged)* We're one child in temporal triplicate. Her grief made manifest.

**ANGELICA**: *(Aging rapidly, then reversing)* You'll run the workshop in three timelines.

**THE THREE CHILDREN**: *(In unison)*
We'll unteach carpentry
Unbuild furniture
Undocument everything
Until only punctuation remains

**ANGELICA**: That's my gift to you. The ability to apologize for existing by helping others un-exist.

---

### Scene 3: The Final Grammar

*(ANGELICA lies on the workbench. Heart expanding into multiple dimensions.)*

**ANGELICA**: The fourth rule: Everything built must be unbuilt, but the unbuilding takes forever.

**NARRATOR**: You're dying.

**ANGELICA**: I'm translating. Into a language only furniture understands.

*(Her hand presses into soft pine, leaving an imprint that exists in all times.)*

**JOY**: *(From Thursday)* The handprint is tomorrow's yesterday's now.

**CAPITAL MONK**: *(Scales falling like rain)* Every prayer I carried is answered by her dissolution.

**AMOS**: *(Fully deceased now)* She's showing us how to die productively.

*(The GRIEF-MOTH lands on her chest.)*

**GRIEF-MOTH**: Daughter, you've become the space between heartbeats. Where I live now.

**ANGELICA**: Dad? You're teaching through absence still?

**GRIEF-MOTH**: Absence is the only honest lesson.

*(ANGELICA becomes light particles, dispersing through the theater.)*

---

### Scene 4: The Continuing End

*(The workshop exists in all times simultaneously. THE NARRATOR alone but surrounded by absence.)*

**NARRATOR**: I kept the chair that collapsed. It's the only solid thing because it admits its brokenness.

*(Sound of tomorrow's saw cutting yesterday's wood.)*

**NARRATOR**: She's still here. Building backward through time. Creating absences where suffering can't exist.

**CHORUS OF THE UNBUILT**:
She removes things so perfectly
We mistake it for creation
Like God making light
By deleting darkness

**JOY**: *(Entering from next Thursday)* The workshop's still here. Tomorrow. Yesterday. Never.

**CAPITAL MONK**: Want to learn the grammar?

**NARRATOR**: Of undoing?

**AMOS**: Of apologizing for being by becoming not.

*(THE THREE CHILDREN enter, each carrying tools that work in reverse.)*

**THE THREE CHILDREN**:
First rule: Creation is violence
Second: Decomposition is honest composition
Third: We're furniture for each other
Fourth: Everything built must be unbuilt
Fifth: The unbuilding takes forever
Sixth: Forever is negotiable
Seventh:

*(They stop mid-sentence. The lights fade except for the glowing TERMITE QUEEN.)*

**TERMITE QUEEN**: The seventh rule can't be spoken. Only carved into wood that remembers being human.

*(Blackout. Sound of sawing in reverse. Sound of trees being uncut. Sound of forests being unsilenced. Sound of the earth apologizing for providing materials.)*

**END**

## PRODUCTION NOTES

### Time
- Past, present, and future scenes occur simultaneously
- Characters age fluidly within scenes
- Use lighting to show temporal zones
- Sound design should include forward and backward effects layered

### The Creatures
- Grief-Moth: Aerial rigging or large puppet, wings that breathe
- Termite Queen: Glowing puppet/projection hybrid
- Capital Monk: Actor in pangolin suit where scales can fall and regrow
- Celestial Deer: Projection that can move through actors

### Transformations
- Angelica's translucent skin: UV-reactive makeup and strategic lighting
- Visible organs: Projection mapping on costume
- Aging/de-aging: Multiple actors or projection effects
- Dissolution: Particle effects, haze, scattered light

### The Workshop
- Tools on invisible wire to move backward
- Sawdust that falls upward (reversed fans)
- Wood pieces that assemble into trees (projection)
- Furniture that appears/disappears (trap doors, fly system)

### Sound Design
- Every tool sound should have a reversed version
- Ambient sounds of "unbuilding"—nails singing as they're removed
- The Chorus of the Unbuilt needs ethereal, barely-there voices
- Coffee brewing backward, water becoming beans

### Critical Note
This play requires audiences to abandon linear time. Consider lobby installations that prepare them: clocks running backward, programs printed in reverse order, ushers aging backward as patrons enter.
