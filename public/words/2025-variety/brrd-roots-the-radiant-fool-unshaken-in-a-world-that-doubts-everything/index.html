Brrd Roots ~ The Radiant Fool Unshaken in a World That Doubts Everything

Words / 2025 Variety

**Cue the spotlight. Enter Innocence, draped in celestial fabric, walking with all the grace of a protagonist in a story that refuses to be subtle.**

Oh, how it shines, how it **declares itself** with the full force of divine authority! How it **insists** that it was never fragile, never broken, never needing defense. How it stands, undaunted, undefeated—wrapped in royal light, sealed in cosmic truth, **unshaken and unbothered** by the constraints of mortal confusion.

But wait—does Innocence take itself too seriously? Does it gaze upon its own radiant glow and whisper, *Ah, yes, I am the protagonist of the universe*? Perhaps. Perhaps Innocence, with its divine certainty, forgets to **wobble a little**, to trip over itself just to remind the world that purity can have a sense of humor. That maybe, just maybe, it can stumble into situations where its cosmic wisdom is met with a raised eyebrow and a skeptical, “Alright, relax.”

Innocence does not need cynicism to survive, **but could it use a pinch of self-deprecating charm?** Maybe. Imagine it sighing dramatically, adjusting its metaphorical robe, rolling its eyes at its own grandiosity—**Oh, great. I’m monologuing again.**

But here’s the truth buried beneath the joke: Innocence is aware. It knows it can get carried away in the poetry of its own existence, but it also knows that’s part of the beauty. It does not resist exploration, does not fear questioning, does not mind poking fun at itself because its foundation is strong enough to withstand it.

And perhaps that’s the secret—**to know one’s identity so well that even when it is shaken, even when it is teased, even when it stumbles, it never stops being true.**

**Curtain falls. Innocence grins in the darkness, winks, and exits—stage left.**

There once was a heart so pure, untouched by the world’s sharp edges, that it bloomed effortlessly—like wildflowers stretching toward the sun, unburdened by doubt or fear. It did not question its place, nor weigh its worth against fleeting affections. It simply *was*—softly, wholly, without pretense. But even the gentlest souls are not immune to the creeping whispers of uncertainty. One day, a quiet thought settled in—*do they truly like me?* A delicate question, fragile yet persistent, threading its way into a mind that had once known only peace.

Oh, honey, come sit down and let me tell you something with all the warmth and wisdom of a grandmother who’s seen it all. Even the sweetest souls, the ones who practically glow with kindness, sometimes get tangled in that nagging worry—*do they really like me?* But oh, my dear, let me wrap that thought up in a soft quilt of reassurance. Most people are far too busy muddling through their own insecurities to be analyzing you the way you fear. And those moments of doubt? They’re just little hiccups on the journey, like when you accidentally bake cookies with salt instead of sugar. Nobody is perfect, but goodness, it doesn’t make them any less lovable.

Self-awareness is a funny thing, really. Bless the hearts of those who think they’ve got it all figured out, marching around convinced they know exactly how they’re perceived. Meanwhile, the truly thoughtful ones are always second-guessing, wondering if they said too much or not enough. It’s a reminder that sometimes, the most socially clueless are blissfully unaware, while the most perceptive carry the weight of uncertainty like a well-worn cardigan.

And narcissism? Oh, child, that’s a tricky one. It’s like a fancy cake with beautiful icing but no real flavor—it looks polished on the outside, but inside, it’s crumbling under the weight of insecurity. The irony is, the ones who need reassurance the most are often the least likely to ask for it.

So, my dear, let me hand you a metaphorical cup of tea and a plate of warm cookies—because life is too short to fret over every little social misstep. You’re doing just fine, better than fine, and if you ever forget that, I’ll be right here, reminding you. Now go on and be your wonderfully imperfect, lovable self.

And so, the heart—still gentle, still unscarred despite its fleeting doubts—found solace in the truth that it did not need to be anything but itself. It did not need to chase love or prove its worth, for kindness needs no validation to exist. Like a stream flowing effortlessly toward the ocean, it embraced its place in the world, unshaken, unbroken—forever open, forever soft. And in that quiet resolve—that refusal to harden against love—it remained beautifully, undeniably innocent.

Chijioke sits at his desk in London, staring at his latest piece—the one that insists innocence is unshaken, that purity cannot be lost, that divine wisdom is wrapped in shimmering purple gospel light. He sighs, rubbing his temples. *I swear, these British people are going to think I have fully lost it.* He flips through his notes, muttering under his breath.

*"Too dramatic?" No, no, dramatic is good. Nigerians love drama. If there’s no flair, did you even write anything?*

*"Too spiritual?" Ah, this London crowd—some of them will love it, others will purse their lips and say, ‘Oh, that’s rather intense, isn’t it?’—like I just shouted Bible verses in a Tesco checkout line.*

*"Too self-aware?" Hmm. *The Radiant Fool*, eh? *Chijioke chuckles.* Ah, yes, let’s poke fun at myself before anyone else does. Better to laugh first.*

He leans back, sipping his tea—the British influence creeping in despite himself. *What a strange balance this is,* he thinks. *Trying to write with my full Nigerian soul while making sure no one in London mistakes it for unhinged enthusiasm.*

And yet—what is writing, if not the act of boldly carving out space for truth? He straightens his shoulders, smirks, and picks up his pen. **The Radiant Fool marches on.**

And London? London will just have to keep up.

Innocence does not need permission to exist. It does not beg for validation, nor does it cower in fear of skepticism. It **stands**, unshaken, laughing in the face of doubt, fully aware of its own brilliance. Some will mock, some will misunderstand, some will call it naive—but it *remains*.

And perhaps the greatest paradox of all? Innocence is aware of itself. It knows it is ridiculous, knows that in a world that prides itself on doubt, purity is *deeply inconvenient*. But it also knows that laughter is survival, that absurdity is protection, that self-awareness is the final key to staying untouched by cynicism.

So, the **Radiant Fool** walks on—not fragile, not foolish, but defiant in its purity.

And if the world chooses to roll its eyes? Well, that’s their burden, not innocence’s.

Snacktopia Innocence ~ The Legend of Plump Soft-Bodied Destiny**

*(A Curvy, Full-Figured, and Well-Rounded Celebration of Ample Beauty)*

Zayen never set out to be a revolutionary. He was just a person with an **above-average** love for snacks and a *below-average* ability to resist them. If destiny had a flavor, his would taste like warm donuts and perfectly salted chips.

But **society** had other plans.

The world insisted on balance—moderation, discipline, restraint. Zayen, however, had never felt called to such virtues. No, his soul belonged to **Snacktopia**, an unwritten kingdom where snacks weren’t just food—they were **sustenance, joy, religion**.

One quiet evening, sitting in a café with the dim light casting dramatic shadows over his half-eaten pastry, he felt it in his bones—the truth rising like the aroma of freshly baked bread.

*"If being plump were my destiny, I’d make snacks a religion."*

He whispered it, reverent, as he took another bite. A single tear of realization slid down his cheek.

Zayen’s best friend, Nivara, had never understood his devotion.

“You’re being dramatic,” she said, watching him stare at his donut like it held the meaning of the universe.

“How dare you interrupt this sacred moment?” Zayen gasped, clutching the pastry. “I am the chosen one. The ambassador of snack excellence.”

Nivara sighed. “So what’s the plan now, snack prophet?”

Zayen met her gaze with the intensity of someone about to make history. “If plump were my personal brand, I’d have sponsorship deals with every chip company.”

“You already bought half the snack aisle last night,” she pointed out.

“They don’t know it yet,” he said solemnly, “but I am their greatest investment.”

Zayen had always believed that if **plump were a state of mind, he’d already be president**.

So why not lead the movement?

“Lend me your stomachs!” he declared, standing up in the café, the napkin cape draped dramatically over his shoulders.

A hush fell over the room. Somewhere, a barista dropped a spoon.

“If plump were my spirit animal, it’d be a donut!” He paused, placing a single glazed masterpiece on a napkin, gazing at it as if it were the **Mona Lisa of snacks**.

Nivara rubbed her temples. “You do realize you can’t just eat snacks all day, right?”

Zayen gasped. “Excuse me?! If being plump were my destiny, I’d double down on snacks!”

“You’ve already tripled down.”

He paused, whispering, “Then let it be known: I regret nothing.”

It was the moment of truth.

Zayen **draped himself in a cape made of napkins**, his final transformation into Snacktopia’s greatest warrior.

“If plump were my alter ego, snacks would be my superpower.”

He tossed a cookie into the air, caught it in his mouth with stunning precision. The café erupted in applause. A tribute—a bag of pretzels—was thrown in his direction.

Nivara sighed. “Alright, that was kind of impressive.”

“If I were already plump, I’d treat myself like it’s my full-time job,” Zayen said, wiping away a single dramatic tear.

Nivara shrugged. “Fine. Want to split a cheesecake?”

Zayen **gasped, clutching his chest**. “If plump were my reality, I’d stop pretending I have self-control.”

The café cheered. **Snacktopia was officially sealed in history.**

Unhealthy Air ~ Warnings Whispered in the Breathing of Fine Particles

At **9:13 a.m.,** the notification arrived—not with urgency, not with the sharpness of a siren, but with the quiet weight of a whisper.

*"Air Quality: Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups."*

It landed between the mundane notifications—weather forecasts, digital coupons, unread messages—absorbed into the daily tide of information that most would skim and discard.

The air had already made its decision long before the alert.

Some would feel it immediately. Some would carry it unknowingly. Some would inhale the warning like it was nothing—until it was.

---

### **Luz:**
She sensed it first—the change in density, the almost imperceptible pause in the wind’s breath. It wasn’t visible, wasn’t the thick smoke of wildfires or the smog-drenched skyline of forgotten industrial zones. It was quieter. More sophisticated. A slow distortion of the atmosphere disguised as an ordinary morning.

She exhaled, slower than usual, measuring. The weight sat in her chest like sediment, settling where it wasn’t meant to be.

Joggers passed, laughter stretched across sidewalks, the sky held its treacherous blue.

Most would not realize the sky was lying.

---

### **Mateo:**
The rhythm of his feet against pavement was his meditation—predictable, grounding, effortless. Except today, his breath didn’t belong to him the way it should.

A slight hitch. A narrowing of space inside his chest.

Not enough to stop him. Just enough to make the next inhale **mean something.**

His body was filing the experience away, committing it to memory like a lesson learned too late.

Hours later, he would sit in his office, feel his lungs protest with the subtle insistence of irritation, and glance at the alert he had ignored earlier.

Warnings delivered in silence were always the most dangerous ones.

---

### **Maya:**
She did not need an air quality index to know today wasn’t a good breathing day. She had learned to sense it in her own body long before adults bothered to check their phones.

At the pharmacy, her father sighed, scrolling through possible causes. *“Probably the pollen.”*

But Maya knew what pollen felt like.

This was different. This was **thicker.** It did not scratch her throat—it weighed on it.

Luz, standing nearby, knew that air could betray its youngest passengers first. The fine particles slipping past the barriers of Maya’s body, embedding themselves in places that were still growing, still fragile.

And this—this would not leave when the seasons changed.

---

### **Elias:**
The bus hissed its arrival, late again.

Elias sighed, adjusting the strap of his messenger bag. The exhaust curled in front of him in a slow, deliberate exhale.

He stepped forward, into it.

The change was subtle. A tightening in his chest that wasn’t pain, wasn’t sickness—just **presence.** Something foreign inside him, indistinguishable from breath.

But breath should never feel like resistance.

---

### **Rosie:**
Her wheelchair hummed lightly as she navigated across the uneven sidewalk, her body moving slower than her mind preferred. She had stopped trusting the air years ago.

Her condition did not allow for mistakes. Did not allow for days like this, where warnings were **suggestions** rather than alarms.

She inhaled carefully, calculating risk. The air was patient. It did not need to rush. It would settle where it wanted—inside arteries, inside lungs, inside the fragile spaces between human resilience and human limitation.

Rosie was tired of negotiating with the atmosphere.

---

They did not know each other. But they were connected.

By the air.

By the particles drifting invisibly between them, settling inside each of them differently, writing quiet histories in bodies that would only realize later what they had absorbed.

Warnings had arrived.

But they had **never been loud enough to stop the inhale.**
