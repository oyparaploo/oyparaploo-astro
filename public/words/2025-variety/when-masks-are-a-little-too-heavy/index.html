When Masks Are A Little Too Heavy

Words / 2025 Variety

Notes on the Moment Two People Stop Pretending

---

Sometimes space opens between two people.
Nobody runs.
The costume drops.

Not the version practiced in mirrors. Not the story worn smooth. But the actual existence—fears carried like pocket change. Tenderness buried under concrete. The specific way we move when nobody's watching.

*

How we are matters as much as who.

Not just names and histories. But method. Stumbling. The particular way we break things, tape them back together.

Like showing someone the garbage can full of failed sketches.
Hands still shaking.
Still paint-stained.

*

That holy moment—arrives like weather.

The air shifts. Bodies know before minds do. Room appears where there wasn't room.

Step into it with your real face.
Or it's gone.

*

We need masks.
For grocery stores.
For mothers.
For mirrors.

But sometimes the weight becomes sickness. And right then—luck or grace—someone else stands there, equally exhausted from their own performance.

Two people getting real at the same time.
That's the thing we're looking for.

*

Truth isn't pretty.

"I'm drowning."
"I hate everyone today."
"I don't know how to love anyone including myself."

Spoken in that opening, even ugly truth becomes necessary. Someone hears it. Doesn't leave. Everything shifts.

*

The moment knows lies.
Closes.
Sometimes for years.

When it comes—stomach tells you—don't think. Let whatever needs to fall from your mouth fall.

The other person might run.
Might take off their own mask.
Might show you who they are under all that trying.

Either way, you've told the truth once.

Changes the shape of everything after.
