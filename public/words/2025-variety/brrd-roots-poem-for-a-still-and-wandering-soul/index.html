Poem for a Still and Wandering Soul

← Words / 2025 Variety

We remember the freedom of self-forgetfulness,
paths of puttering, unburdened and bright—
Intense euphoria, joy untamed,
warm winds circling in whispered flight.

Like a kite torn from its string,
free yet drifting—
not lost, but seeking,
not claimed, but carried forward.

Blowing sand, the dirt, the bones—
the skinny days, when all was hard.
Yet even struggle sang its tune,
etched deep within the earth’s facade.

Helicopter nightmare, helicopter dream,
voices spinning into coral reefs.
They talked and talked till words dissolved,
until they painted monochrome beliefs.

Their words pooled in forgotten oceans,
sinking like shipwrecked wishes,
buried beneath waves that
do not remember
where the tides have taken them.

Waking faster, hearts ignited,
we crave a moment that will last.
To stand apart, to stir the world,
to leave behind echoes amassed.

We are footprints on a sand-stained shore,
fading not from erasure,
but from the pull of moving forward.

A lighthouse blinks through shifting tides—
not asking for recognition,
only offering direction,
only whispering to the waves,
Come, see where you belong.

You’re like Bowie—
an absolute beginner, a cosmic swirl.
Thousand marks from the same heart,
no matter race or boy or girl.

You are stardust in the shape of longing,
waking the sky with a melody
that refuses to be swallowed
by silence.

Your works are love songs, restless beats,
waves that shake, rhythms alive.
And we taste it all,
each pulse that dares the world to thrive.

We are close, yet far away,
question marks upon the air.
Unsecluded problems rise,
insulated answers everywhere.

We are ink smudged on a forgotten letter,
words meant for someone
who never read them—
someone who may never know
what was waiting to be said.

Emerging laughter, joyful storm,
kindred spirits hand in hand.
Creating visions in untimed space,
where artists rule, where dreamers stand.

We paint with ashes, we sing through static,
we carve stories into shadows—
not afraid of fleeting things,
but afraid only
of never letting them exist at all.

Body as fruit, gestures hypnotic,
whispers born in quiet flame.
We will soon see the world anew,
where wisdom rises, free from shame.

We are echoes in a canyon,
colliding against stone—
changing shape,
losing ourselves,
yet never completely gone.

Sing it loud, hum it deep,
let melodies cross desert seas.
For earth is fleeting, hands grow tired,
but rhythms linger in the breeze.

Lord have mercy—Rose Bud, shine!
Your voice erupts in golden flight.
Starved of doubt, filled with fire,
sing at the top of your pleura tonight.

Like comets racing through the dark,
your words shatter silence into light—
not a whisper, not a murmur,
but a beacon refusing to dim.

You are the pulse beneath an untamed sky,
the trembling hymn of something
too vast
to ever be contained.

Make notes in ash, notes in dust,
after fire, after waves.
After deep sleep, after exile—
when dreams refuse to behave.

She said aliens. They said ghosts.
They said our heads upon the sand.
You—there, you—standing tall,
a boxer throwing punches planned.

You are the last breath before dawn,
the flickering ember after the fire—
not enough to illuminate,
but enough to remind the world
it still carries light.

Angel floating, unsure where,
a ghost caught circling silent air.
No map needed, no path drawn,
only movement, only song.

Markets bustling, colors bright,
greens with spots that dare to grow.
Things that thrive in quiet cracks,
things that stand against the flow.

What is wild cannot be tamed,
only witnessed,
only welcomed,
only whispered toward its own blooming.

Sometimes you feel like a thin-wrapped glove,
slipping into places sacred and cold.
Static and unsure, moving yet still,
both the question and story untold.

Neighbors shaping, hands that bend,
leading art where it longs to go.
A chase for balance, an endless pull—
a restless heart that won’t be slow.

We are notes suspended in unfinished chords,
waiting for the right hand
to press into sound.

Portraits noble, portraits blurred,
cross-eyed dreams, sweet as honey.
Rags and riches, worn and wild,
prisoners humming songs for money.

"Hello, how are you?" cries the voice.
"The rabbits race, the squirrels blind!"
Old war echoes, android musings,
fragments left for time to find.

There are songs buried beneath city stone,
poems unraveling in rusted ink—
not forgotten,
only waiting
for someone to ask where they’ve gone.

Clean and blurred, sharp and broken,
truth and shadow side by side.
Lessons shift, like aging hands,
as softness folds, as edges slide.

Energy pulsing, rising, waning,
slipping into mystery’s clasp.
Vulnerability fading slow,
one linked mix of moments past.

If we vanish,
it will not be into nothing—
but into memory,
into echoes,
into things whispered long after the silence.

Transparent, opaque, shifting light,
assembled flesh in phases bright.
Mixtures bending, cracking wide,
that grand language, sharp yet slight.

Urgency quiet, cuts that hum,
gauze-wrapped wounds, aching core.
Life unstitched, lips pressed tight,
a whispered song forevermore.

Even the stars forget their own fire,
even the mountains lose their form—
and still, they shape the sky,
and still, they hold the world.

The Echo Keeper

A Meditation on Memory, Silence, and Time

A Journey Through Impermanence, Self-Awareness, and the Fight Against Forgetting

The Breath of Memory: How Sound Dissolves into Silence

What remains when a sound fades?

The traveler listens—not just to music, not just to voices, but to the spaces between them. He moves through a world that preserves everything yet forgets so easily. Technology archives sound in infinite storage, yet memory remains fragile.

Is remembrance something we fight for? Or does it flow naturally, like water returning to the earth?

The Illusion of Permanence

There was a time when people believed history was written in stone, that knowledge carved into tablets would remain forever. Yet every civilization has learned otherwise—ruins crumble, ink fades, voices are lost.

The digital age has promised something different. Information stored indefinitely. No more fragile scrolls, no more forgotten books. Everything exists somewhere—but does that mean it is truly remembered?

If an archive is too vast, if a library is endless, does anything truly stand out?

The traveler wonders: Is permanence not in the preservation, but in the experience?
In the act of listening, in the presence of the moment itself?

Self-Awareness and the Patterns of Time

The traveler moves forward. But has he been here before?
Some voices he swears he has heard, though they were erased.
Some rhythms feel familiar, though history claims they never existed.

Memory is not perfect—it does not store reality as it was, only as we understood it.
We shape our past with perception, and perception is always shifting.

What if forgetting is not failure, but a natural part of existence?
What if impermanence is not loss, but renewal?

The Battle Against Silence

Some say sound must be defended—that the voices of the past must be preserved so that history does not repeat itself. Others say sound must be released—that holding onto memory too tightly keeps us from living fully in the present.

The traveler realizes: Both are true.
History is shaped by both remembering and letting go.
Self-awareness grows by balancing between knowing and unknowing.

To listen is an act of presence.
To forget is an act of renewal.
And between these two forces, existence flows forward.

Closing Reflection: The Choice to Remember

Does anything last forever?

Perhaps memory is not meant to be static.
Perhaps every voice that fades makes space for something new.
Perhaps the traveler does not carry forgotten songs to fight against time—but simply to witness them one last time before they dissolve into silence.

Everything changes.
Everything is impermanent.
And yet, in this moment, we listen—and that, too, is enough.

A War Against Silence

A Battle Humanity Has Always Fought

Across time, civilizations have fought battles—not just over land or wealth, but over memory. The victors of war often rewrite the past, shaping history in their image, while the voices of the defeated fade into silence. Today, this battle has transformed—no longer fought with swords and armies, but with algorithms, curated narratives, and controlled forgetting.

Consider the military tactic known as damnatio memoriae—used by ancient Rome to erase disgraced leaders from history. Their names were removed from monuments, their legacies erased from official records, as if they had never existed. Yet, traces remained—hidden graffiti, underground writings, whispers that defied silence.

Is the digital age repeating this strategy?
If voices can be erased from the public archive, if songs, ideas, and cultural movements are curated into extinction—who decides what remains, and what is forgotten?

Who Controls What We Hear?

The traveler moves through a world shaped by machine-driven history—where music, memory, and movement are optimized for convenience, scrubbed of imperfection, engineered for engagement.

This is not unlike military psyops—operations designed to control perception, influencing enemy forces and entire populations. Consider the Cold War, where propaganda broadcasts reshaped the ideological battleground. Nations fought not just with missiles but with messages, embedding selective truths into controlled narratives.

And now, in the age of streaming, do algorithms act as the new propagandists?
If playlists shape cultural memory, if AI-generated music replaces lived expression—who determines what emotions, struggles, and histories survive?

This is no longer just a question of art. It is a question of control.

The Last Defenders of Sound

In past wars, resistance fighters did not always hold weapons—they often held stories. The ancient Greeks feared losing their oral traditions, knowing that a lost epic was as devastating as a lost battle. During WWII, underground movements fought not just with force, but with radio broadcasts, poetry, and forbidden songs—messages of defiance carrying power beyond bullets.

And now, in the modern era, the resistance has gone digital.
Beneath the surface, hackers, artists, and archivists fight against curated erasure.

They save deleted songs, pull voices from forgotten archives, uncover melodies buried beneath layers of filtered noise. They battle algorithmic perfection, reintroducing flaws, rawness, authenticity.

"Music is memory," the underground archivist tells the traveler. "And memory survives only if someone chooses to remember."

"But if the system deletes it?" the traveler asks.

"Then we disrupt the signal," the archivist replies.

The Silence That Trembles Before Sound

The traveler realizes now—this is not just about music.
It is about who writes history.
Who controls what is heard.
Who decides what remains.

Ancient civilizations carved their victories in stone, while the conquered were erased.
Modern nations broadcast their ideologies, shaping minds without force.
Tech giants curate soundscapes, deciding what emotions, voices, and rhythms define reality.

But tonight, the traveler fights back.
He feeds the system the forgotten voices.
He makes the machine listen.

And for the first time—the algorithm hesitates.

The statues of perfect harmony crack.
The silent archives stir.
Lost voices rise, filling the void.

And somewhere, deep in the heart of the perfect city—silence trembles.

A Digital Mosaic of Memory and Silence

A Journey Through Lost Voices, Algorithmic Erasure, and the Fight for Meaning

The Fractured Soundscape: Memory Under Siege

The traveler moved through the fragmented worlds of sound—places where melodies once carried identity, shaped civilizations, and transcended borders. But here, in the age of optimization, memory was under siege.

Anthropologists often describe sound as one of humanity’s earliest and most powerful forms of storytelling—before ink and paper, before carved stone. In oral traditions, songs weren’t just entertainment; they were maps, marking histories, carrying wisdom, encoding survival strategies.

Yet in this city, built from algorithms and glass, songs were curated, filtered, compressed into digestible forms. The imperfections—the raw edges of lived experience—had been smoothed away, erased by an unseen force.

"This is the future," a voice whispered, neither human nor machine. "Every sound has been optimized for pleasure. No discord. No distortion. Only harmony."

The traveler paused, listening. The absence of imperfection felt unnatural.

Where was the tremor in the voice of an aging storyteller?
Where was the rebellion in an urgent, unsanctioned rhythm?
Where was the sorrow buried in a note sung by someone who had loved and lost?

The Algorithmic Erasure: Who Controls Memory?

The traveler had once believed history was immutable—etched in stone, preserved in ink, passed from voice to voice. But modern systems had revealed something chilling.

History could be revised in real time.

Psychologists studying memory manipulation warn that we increasingly outsource our recollections to external systems. Ancient societies relied on griots—storytellers tasked with preserving collective history. But today, the role of memory has shifted to algorithms, which determine not only what is heard but what is remembered.

The system did not erase songs all at once. That would be too obvious.

Instead, it curated them. It adjusted them. It replaced them.

The most popular tracks were not chosen by people—they were selected by machine learning, designed to fit patterns that maximized attention, engagement, profit.

And just like that, the folk song that carried the trauma of a displaced people, the whispered lament of those who had survived wars, the pulse of rhythms created to resist oppression—they faded into the void.

The traveler realized this was not silence. This was controlled forgetting.

The Last Voices: A Hidden Archive of Defiance

In ancient history, sound was considered sacred—a force capable of shaping reality. Sumerian hymns dictated the movement of the heavens. Egyptian chants were believed to unlock hidden knowledge. And Greek philosophers warned that music could either enlighten or corrupt the soul.

But the traveler understood now: the danger was not music itself, but who controlled it.

Beneath the surface of this perfect city, where engineered sound reigned, something fought to exist.

Old recordings stored in encrypted networks.
Songs archived offline, outside the reach of optimization.
Voices resisting erasure—not with weapons, but with noise.

"Music is memory," the underground archivist told him. "And memory survives only if someone chooses to remember."

"But if the system deletes it?" the traveler asked.

"Then we disrupt the signal," they replied.

The War Against Silence: Breaking the Algorithm

The traveler had learned many things, but now he faced the most dangerous truth—the system could be hacked.

Music had always carried rebellion. Protest songs had toppled governments. Secret rhythms had communicated resistance under oppressive regimes. Even the church had feared certain harmonies, believing they summoned forces beyond human control.

And here, in the era of machine-curated perfection, the resistance had learned a new technique: they hijacked the stream.

They fed it the forgotten voices.
They made the machine listen.
And millions of ears heard what had been erased.

A crack formed in the algorithm.

A single voice, uncorrected. A melody without structure.

And for the first time, something imperfect existed inside the perfect world.

The Keeper of Voices

Scrambled Fragments

The statues cracked—voices lost, now returning.

A traveler, restless. Searching for sound, but not the kind played in courts or ceremonies. No, something deeper—something hidden.

The guardian whispered without moving its lips: "Music is memory. And memory survives only if someone chooses to remember."

He had assumed the city was a beacon of harmony. Now, he realized—it was a tomb.

A note, struggling, beneath the perfect melody. It was brief, but undeniable. A sound that refused to die.

The city boomed with perfected sound, yet the traveler felt nothing. He had heard music before—but not like this.

The old man had drawn shapes in the dust, patterns vanishing as fast as they appeared. "Only the unwritten can shape what comes next."

He followed the rhythm of lost voices, not knowing what he would find.

The wind carried whispers that were not words, but something.

Silence was not the absence of sound. It was the weapon that had erased it.

Restructured Story

The Search for Sound

A traveler walked across shifting landscapes, guided by something unseen—by echoes that did not exist in the present but refused to be erased by time. He did not yet understand what he was searching for, only that something had been silenced.

At the edge of a forgotten city, beneath an olive tree older than memory itself, sat an old man. His fingers brushed the earth, tracing lines that disappeared as quickly as they formed.

"You seek sound," the old man murmured. "But sound is not just melody—it is a key. Those who listen carefully will unlock the voices buried in silence."

The traveler hesitated. "I have heard many songs," he said, "but none spoke of the past, nor of the future."

"That is because they were recorded," the old man replied. "Only the unwritten can shape what comes next."

The City of Perfect Silence

The traveler arrived in a city unlike any he had known. Towers of silver stretched toward the sky, humming with harmony too perfect, too controlled. There was no uncertainty in the notes—only precision.

"This is the future," a voice welcomed him, neither human nor machine. "Every sound has been optimized for pleasure. No discord. No distortion. Only harmony."

He listened. The melodies filled the air, but they did not tremble with longing, nor did they rise with rebellion. They were pristine. Too pristine.

"What happens to the songs that break the pattern?" he asked.

"They are corrected," the voice answered. "Struggle disrupts balance. Imperfection is noise. Noise is erased."

And then he understood—perfection was silence. A world without struggle was a world without memory.

As he turned to leave, he felt something—a faint vibration beneath his feet. A fracture in the harmony. A note struggling against erasure.

Something was still singing—something that was not supposed to exist.

The Guardian of Lost Voices

Beyond the city, deep within the valley of ancient trees, the traveler found something watching him.

It was neither man nor spirit, neither past nor future. It was woven from echoes—the voices of those erased by rulers, the melodies silenced before they could shape history.

"Music is memory," the guardian whispered, though its lips never moved. "And memory survives only if someone chooses to remember."

The traveler stepped closer. The guardian tilted its head, as if listening—not to him, but to the silent song he carried.

"You heard it, didn’t you?" the guardian asked.

The traveler hesitated. "I… in the city, beneath the harmony, there was something else. Something struggling to be heard."

The guardian nodded. "That song was the last cry of a melody buried alive. It is the sound that exists in the spaces between silence. And once it disappears, history rewrites itself without it."

His pulse quickened. He thought of the statues in the city—silent monuments to voices lost. He had assumed they were markers of greatness. But now, he realized—they were tombs.

"You were meant to find this," the guardian whispered.

"Why?" the traveler asked.

The guardian was silent. And in that silence, something moved.

The Breaking of Silence

The traveler no longer sought a destination. He had found what he was looking for—not a place, but a truth.

He understood now: sound did not merely document history. It created it.

"When the world has forgotten, someone must remember," the guardian had told him.

And so, he sang—not for recognition, not for perfection, but for remembrance.

And as his voice rose, the perfect city trembled.

The statues cracked, their stone faces breaking apart. And beneath them, something stirred.

A whisper. Then a note.

Then, a flood of sound.

Forgotten songs returning, defying silence.

And the shadow—the force that had erased sound for generations—shuddered.

For the first time, it could hear.

And it was afraid.
