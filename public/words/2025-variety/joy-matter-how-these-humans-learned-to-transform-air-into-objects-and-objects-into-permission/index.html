*JOY MATTER ~ How These Humans Learned to Transform Air Into Objects and Objects Into Permission
An Experimental Manufacturing of Delight for Lyon

A Participatory Installation Where the Audience Makes Things Badly and Discovers Joy

---

THE EXPERIENCE ARCHITECTURE

Pre-Show: The Idea Weight Experiment (48 Hours Before)

Ticket holders receive:
- A small ball of clay in a sealed box
- Instructions: "Hold this. Feel its weight. This weighs exactly as much as your next idea."
- An app prompt: "Document one idea becoming thing each day" (making coffee, writing an email, cooking dinner)
- A collection bag for "failed objects" - things that didn't turn out as intended

Arrival: The Workshop Gathering

The experience begins at Les Halles de Lyon Paul Bocuse after closing. The space is transformed into a maker's workshop with five stations. Audience members see their documented "idea-to-thing" transformations projected on the walls.

---

ACT ONE: The Weight of Nothing
Location: The Central Workshop Space

NAH (standing at a pottery wheel with unmolded clay):
This clay weighs 2.3 kilograms.
The pot I'm imagining weighs nothing.
Watch.

She begins shaping, badly

NAH:
My idea is possessing matter.
Matter is resisting.
We're negotiating.
What emerges will be neither my idea nor the clay's preference.
It will be our argument made solid.

LUIGI (at a woodworking bench, holding air):
I'm holding a chair.
You can't see it because it weighs nothing.
It exists only in the electromagnetic patterns of my neurons.
But in twenty minutes, you'll be able to sit on my thought.

ANOUSHKA (with fabric and thread, sewing wildly):
Every stitch is an idea puncturing reality.
Thought. Pierce. Pull through. Thought. Pierce. Pull through.
I'm literally sewing my mind into matter.
It's going terribly.
That's perfect.

DAVID (surrounded by broken objects):
These are all ideas that negotiated badly with reality.
This chair wanted to be stable. Wood said no.
This cup wanted to hold water. Ceramic said "not like that."
Every broken thing is a conversation that went weird.
Beautiful, broken conversations.

DINA (holding the audience's clay from their boxes):
You've been carrying potential for two days.
Clay that could become anything.
Still becoming nothing.
The weight of possibility.

ACT TWO: The Permission Factory
Location: The Five Making Stations

The audience divides into five groups, rotating through stations where they make objects badly while learning about joy.

Station 1: The Wild Joy Laboratory (NAH)

Participants make pinch pots while jumping

NAH:
You cannot make a pot well while jumping.
Your hands are confused.
Your body is choosing joy over perfection.
This is wild joy - the animal choice.
The pot will be terrible.
You will love it more than any perfect thing.

She demonstrates - jumping, laughing, clay flying

NAH:
Feel that? Your body overriding your mind's desire for control?
That's 40,000 years of humans making things for joy, not just function.
Your terrible jumping-pot is archaeologically correct.

Station 2: The Cultivated Joy Museum (LUIGI)

Participants sand wooden blocks into "sculptures" while being filmed

LUIGI:
Now you're being watched.
Feel your joy change?
It's putting on clothes.
Getting presentable.
This is cultivated joy - performed, purposeful.
Still real. Just... dressed up.

He shows two objects

LUIGI:
This one I made alone at 3 AM, laughing like a lunatic.
This one I made for an exhibition.
Both are joy. Different species.
Like wolves and poodles.
Both dogs. Both real. Different.

Station 3: The Time Signature Workshop (ANOUSHKA)

Participants create fabric objects while Anoushka controls the tempo

ANOUSHKA:
Staccato joy!
Everyone makes quick, sharp stitches
Legato joy!
Long, flowing movements
Fermata joy!
Everyone holds their position, feeling the suspension

Each joy has its own time.
Your object is recording temporal joy.
Look - you can see the rhythm in your stitches.
Fast joy here. Slow joy there.
Your fabric is a joy timeline.

Station 4: The Wobble Academy (DAVID)

Participants deliberately make unbalanced objects

DAVID:
Make it wobble.
More wobble.
Perfect things don't give permission for imperfection.
This wobbling sculpture you're making?
It's a permission slip.
Every time you see it, it says: "I exist imperfectly. You can too."

He shows a deliberately terrible mobile

DAVID:
This gives me permission to be unbalanced.
I look at it every morning.
It reminds me: Things don't have to be perfect to be.
Just to be is enough.

Station 5: The Translation Booth (DINA)

Participants try to recreate a simple drawing in 3D

DINA:
Your idea: "circle."
Reality: "What kind of circle? How thick? How large? What color?"
Watch your perfect mental circle negotiate with wire.
The wire has opinions.
Gravity has opinions.
Your hands have opinions.
What emerges is democracy, not dictatorship.

ACT THREE: The Joy Permissions
Location: The Assembly Area

All participants bring their badly-made objects to the center.

NAH:
Every object here is a transformation.
Idea → Matter → Permission → Joy.

LUIGI:
Who has the worst pot?
Someone raises hand
Hold it up.
Everyone, what does this give permission for?

AUDIENCE RESPONSES:
"Being bad at things!"
"Trying anyway!"
"Laughing at failure!"

ANOUSHKA:
This isn't failure.
This is matter and mind having a conversation.
Sometimes they disagree.
The disagreement is where joy lives.

DAVID (creating an installation from everyone's objects):
Look. Together, these terrible objects are beautiful.
A museum of imperfection.
A cathedral of try-anyway.
A parliament of wobble.

DINA:
Now the real experiment.
Take someone else's object home.
Not yours. Someone else's.
Their badly-made thing becomes your permission slip.

ACT FOUR: The City as Studio
Location: Moving through Lyon streets

The River Teaching

At the Rhône, participants throw clay into the water.

NAH:
The river is ideas in constant negotiation with banks.
Never the same river. Never the same idea.
Always transforming.

The Building Reading

LUIGI (at ancient Roman ruins):
Someone's idea from 2000 years ago.
Still standing. Still permitting awe.
Ideas that become matter outlive the thinker.
Your terrible pot might outlive you.
Someone in 4024 might wonder what you were thinking.

The Market Demonstration

Back at Les Halles, now reopened for morning market

ANOUSHKA:
Watch people shop.
Idea: "dinner."
Becoming: ingredients.
Transformation: cooking.
Result: joy or disaster or both.
Every meal is ideas becoming matter becoming feeling.

ACT FIVE: The Dawn Making
Location: Fourvière Hill at sunrise

The Final Making

Everyone receives new clay.

ALL PERFORMERS:
Make something in seven minutes.
Don't think.
Let your hands think.
Let the clay think.
You just mediate.

Seven minutes of silent making

DAVID:
Now look what emerged.
Not what you planned.
Not what clay planned.
What you made together.
The negotiation made solid.

The Exchange

DINA:
Find someone whose object speaks to yours.
Trade.
You now own someone else's idea-made-matter.
Their neurons shaped this clay.
Their joy is in your hands.

The City Installation

NAH:
These objects stay in Lyon.
Installed throughout the city.
Terrible beautiful things.
Permission slips for anyone who sees them.
"Someone made this badly and displayed it proudly."
"I can exist imperfectly too."

THE INTERNATIONAL DESTINATION ELEMENTS

The Global Making Network

- Participants' objects are documented and added to a global database
- "Sister objects" are identified across performances using AI pattern recognition
- People can find their "object twin" in another country
- Annual gathering where object twins meet

The Material Exchange Program

- Clay from Lyon soil mixed with clay from participants' home countries
- Each performance adds new earth to the mixture
- By year 5, the clay contains soil from 50+ countries
- You're literally making objects from global matter

The Failed Objects Museum

- Permanent installation in Lyon of the "worst" objects from each performance
- Voted by audiences
- Winners get "Terrible at Things" certificates
- Museum grows weekly, becoming monument to joyful failure

The Permission Economy

- Participants can "deposit" permissions and "withdraw" others
- "I give permission to sing badly" → "I take permission to dance alone"
- Creates actual currency of joy permissions
- App tracks global permission flow

The Biometric Joy Documentation

- Sensors in clay measure pressure, showing exactly where ideas meet matter
- Heart rate monitors during making show joy spikes at moments of "failure"
- Scientific proof that imperfection creates more joy than perfection
- Published research: "The Neuroscience of Making Badly"

The Celebrity Failure Gallery

- Famous artists invited to make things badly
- Marina Abramović's terrible pot
- Jeff Koons' wobbly sculpture
- Displayed with equal prominence as participant works
- Proves everyone fails beautifully

The Time Capsule Practice

- One object from each performance sealed for 100 years
- Future humans will find thousands of badly-made objects
- With notes: "We knew these were terrible. We loved them anyway."
- Archaeological joy for future generations

THE IMAGISTIC AMPLIFICATIONS OF THE JOYFUL BODY

The Making Body

The Hand-Brain Conspiracy
Your hands contain 17,000 mechanoreceptors that talk directly to your brain. When making, your hands are thinking at 120 meters per second - faster than conscious thought. Your terrible pot is your hands' opinion about clay, expressed before your brain can interfere.

The Grip of Joy
There are 35 muscles in your forearm controlling your hand. When you make something badly with joy, different muscles engage than when you make carefully. Joy-making uses the same muscles as play-fighting, the ones that know how to be strong but not serious.

The Pressure Signatures
Every maker leaves unique pressure patterns in clay - like fingerprints but deeper. Your joy pressure is lighter than your concentration pressure. The clay remembers which emotion shaped it. Archaeologists can tell if ancient potters were happy by pressure analysis.

The Wobble Body

The Balance Negotiation
Your body makes 100 micro-adjustments per second to stay upright. When you make wobbly objects, you're acknowledging this constant negotiation. Perfect balance is a lie. Everything wobbles. Your terrible sculpture is honest about this.

The Proprioceptive Joy
Proprioception - knowing where your body is in space - lights up during making. Bad making lights it up more because you can't predict what will happen. Your body enjoys being surprised by its own movements. This is why children make "badly" - their proprioceptors are partying.

The Failure Recovery Reflex
When something goes wrong while making, your body initiates a "failure recovery cascade": pupils dilate, breath quickens, then either stress hormones OR play hormones release. Choosing play hormones is choosing wild joy. Your badly-made object is evidence of choosing play.

The Permission Body

The Mirror Neuron Liberation
When you see someone else's badly-made object, your mirror neurons fire as if YOU made it badly. You literally experience their permission for imperfection. This is why the exchange is powerful - you're taking home someone else's neurological permission slip.

The Tension Release Pattern
Holding perfectionist standards creates specific muscle tension: shoulders up, jaw clenched, breathing shallow. Seeing wonky objects triggers tension release. Your body literally relaxes when given permission to be imperfect. The worse the object, the deeper the relaxation.

The Laughter Architecture
Laughing at badly-made things engages 15 facial muscles, intercostal muscles between ribs, and your diaphragm. This specific combination - recognition laughter, not performance laughter - releases endorphins that bind to the same receptors as morphine. Bad objects are literally pain medicine.

The Transformation Body

The Idea-to-Matter Pathway
Idea → Prefrontal cortex → Motor planning → Cerebellum → Spinal cord → Peripheral nerves → Muscles → Hands → Clay. This journey takes 0.2 seconds. In that fifth of a second, thought becomes thing. You perform this miracle thousands of times daily without applause.

The Material Feedback Loop
Clay pushes back with 2.5 pounds per square inch. Your mechanoreceptors report this to your brain. Brain adjusts plan. Sends new signals. Clay pushes back differently. This conversation happens 1000 times per pot. The pot is the transcript of this dialogue.

The Completion Reward
Finishing making - even badly - triggers dopamine release in the ventral tegmental area. This is the same system that rewards eating and sex. Making things is a biological imperative. Quality is cultural addition. Your body doesn't care if it's good, only that it exists.

The Time Signature Body

Staccato Making
Quick movements engage fast-twitch muscle fibers. Heart rate spikes to 120 bpm. Breathing becomes percussive. This is your body's "urgent joy" mode. The objects made this way carry the rhythm in their form.

Legato Making
Slow, flowing movements engage slow-twitch fibers. Heart rate steadies at 60-70 bpm. Breathing deepens. This is "sustained joy" mode. Objects made this way have smoother transitions, even when badly made.

Fermata Making
Holding position engages isometric contraction. Blood pressure rises slightly. Time perception slows. This is "suspended joy" mode. Objects made with pauses have visible hesitation points - places where joy held its breath.

The Wild vs Cultivated Body

Wild Making Body
- Pupils dilated
- Breathing irregular
- Movements unpredictable
- Vocal expressions unconscious
- Center of gravity shifting
- Face asymmetrical from genuine expression

Cultivated Making Body
- Pupils normal
- Breathing controlled
- Movements planned
- Vocal expression conscious
- Center of gravity stable
- Face symmetrical from performed expression

Both are real. Both are joy. Both leave different marks in matter.

The Exchange Body

The Object Attachment System
Your brain forms attachment to objects you make in 45 seconds via the endowment effect. Trading breaks this attachment, creating momentary loss, then new attachment forms to received object. This emotional journey - attachment, loss, renewal - is joy practice.

The Trust Chemistry
Exchanging made objects triggers oxytocin release - the bonding hormone. Higher than handshakes, lower than hugs. You're literally chemically bonding through badly-made things. The worse the object, the more trust required, the more oxytocin released.

The Recognition Response
Seeing your badly-made object valued by others activates the same brain regions as seeing your child succeed. Parental pride for clay offspring. This is why the museum matters - your terrible pot is your material child, being raised by community.

THE CLOSING MANIFESTO

ALL PERFORMERS (holding their badly-made objects from the dawn session):
We transform ideas into matter daily.
Coffee. Emails. Dinners. Conversations.
All ideas becoming real.

Usually we demand perfection.
Today we chose joy.
We made badly.
We made anyway.
We made together.

These objects are permission slips.
For imperfection.
For play.
For the negotiation between mind and matter.
For the wobble.

Take them into Lyon.
Into the world.
Let them give permission.
For wild joy.
For cultivated joy.
For the joy that lasts a microsecond.
For the joy that hums underneath everything.

You are transformation machines.
You eat ideas.
You birth objects.
Objects birth permissions.
Permissions birth joy.
Joy births more ideas.

Make something badly tomorrow.
And the next day.
And the next.
Until your life is full of wonky, terrible, joyful evidence
That you existed
That you tried
That you chose joy over perfection
That you transformed nothing into something
And something into permission
And permission into joy.

The play ends with 200 badly-made objects arranged in the sunrise light, casting imperfect shadows, each one proof that ideas can become matter, matter can become permission, and permission can become joy.
