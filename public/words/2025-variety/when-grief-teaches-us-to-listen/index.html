When Grief Teaches Us to Listen

← Words / 2025 Variety

Everything speaks. Moss whispers its way toward moisture. Stones hold yesterday's heat in their bellies, breathing it out when evening comes. Grief moves through the body like water through soil - finding every hidden channel, feeding roots we didn't know were thirsty.

In sorrow, we scatter like seeds. In reverence, we call ourselves home. But home was never singular - we exist in the web where your grief touches mine, where the ancestor's tears still water tomorrow's medicine plants. Ceremony doesn't create this connection. Ceremony helps us remember what was always true.

Follow any thread of pain deep enough and you'll find the place where all roots tangle together in the dark. This is why we pause before picking medicine. Why the taking of any life - even carrot, even leaf - carries weight. Not guilt, but gravity. The difference between theft and communion lives in that pause.

Colonial language taught us to see death everywhere. But the old knowing runs deeper - everything continues. Time spirals rather than arrows. Your grandmother is both memory and tomorrow's child. The tree you grieve today is already teaching its saplings how to bend without breaking.

Here in this speaking world, we practice a different kind of witnessing. Not fixing. Not solving. Just seeing and being seen. This makes a space where grief can be itself - wild, necessary, transformative as fire that clears dead brush for new growth.

We are small and temporary, tending something ancient and ongoing. Each whispered thanks to morning coffee, each tear for a broken bird, each hand that plants seeds in ruins - we're all weaving the same cloth. Not improving the world but keeping it alive. Keeping it real. Letting it speak.

The atoms in our bones remember being stars. They seek their original song. This isn't metaphor - it's the cellular hunger for truth that makes integrity not a choice but a way of breathing. We inhabit truthfulness like a dwelling place. We stay there, even when it burns.
