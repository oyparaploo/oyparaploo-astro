Brrd Roots Good Health Sustaining Improvisation ~ Including Absurdities

Words / 2025 Variety

Sometimes life feels like stepping into the unknown wearing socks on a polished stage—a little slippery, a lot nerve-wracking. Improv asks us to trust that slide, to leap into moments where control is not just an option—it’s a mirage. It's absurd, liberating, and oddly profound. Imagine a voice whispering to you, “Don’t take yourself too seriously. Oh, and maybe bring a chicken along for the ride.” That’s the improv spirit—equal parts ridiculous and enlightening.

Picture a performer, standing just shy of the spotlight’s warm reach. Their heart beats a symphony of uncertainty, the kind of rhythm you can only feel in moments where bravery debates retreat. They take one trembling step forward, then another. The line bursts out of them—not polished, but absolutely alive: “Who ordered manure?” The room freezes, before erupting in laughter that fills the cracks in every wall. It’s not the absurdity of the line—it’s the courage in its delivery that stirs the air. They didn’t aim for perfection—they aimed for connection. And isn’t that the story of being human?

Improv, though, doesn’t live solely in the glowing light of a stage—it spills into our daily chaos, wrapping itself around moments that refuse structure. It’s the Monday morning where your coffee defies gravity and your toddler wails about pants being an outdated concept. Then, your neighbor, a beacon of warmth in your storm, grins and throws a squirrel joke your way. You laugh—a laugh you didn’t think you had—and suddenly, chaos seems conquerable. That’s improv sneaking into your life, unannounced and unforgettable.

“Yes, And” isn’t just words—it’s a mindset. It’s the shy kid who finds their voice in a classroom story about zombie chickens. Their laughter becomes the soil where their confidence quietly blooms. Or the young woman crowned “Queen of the Raccoons,” who transforms her stage fright into regal humor and holds the room captive. These moments of bravery remind us that showing up matters—not perfectly, but wholly. Vulnerability isn’t a curse—it’s a bridge.

Improv unearths humanity in the awkward, the raw, the unfiltered. It gives us permission to stumble, and in stumbling, it gifts us laughter. It doesn’t demand perfection—it asks for presence. In return, it sparks connection, the kind that lingers like the memory of a wild, beautiful night.

So let the absurdity guide you. Let the unknown become your script. Whether it’s manure or squirrels or coffee stains, embrace the possibility that within chaos lies courage. Improv teaches us to laugh at our missteps, to lean into discomfort, and to find the spark in imperfection. Take the leap. Because even when you land somewhere strange, you land alive—and that, really, is the truest magic of all.

There’s a certain magic in trusting the unknown, isn’t there? It’s like showing up at a party and realizing the dress code is absurdity. “Be bold,” it whispers. “And maybe bring a chicken.” Improv is the art of stepping into that light, the hum of uncertainty vibrating under your feet. There are no scripts, no guidelines, just a raw, electric invitation to create something unforgettable.

Picture the performer, standing tall—or perhaps wobbling slightly—as the spotlight nudges them forward. Their heart races, not with fear but a curious cocktail of nerves and possibility. The first words tumble out, jagged but honest: “Who ordered manure?” The audience hesitates, then bursts into laughter, the kind that fills every corner of the room. It’s ridiculous. It’s imperfect. But it’s real. And isn’t that what we’re all craving? Those moments that prove beauty doesn’t need polish—it thrives in the cracks.

Improv’s true stage, though, is everyday life. It’s the chaos of your Monday morning when your coffee tries to escape while your toddler stages a mutiny against pants. It’s the neighbor grinning at your disheveled despair and tossing you the gift of a ridiculous squirrel joke. You laugh, and for a second, you forget the mess. Connection blooms right there, between spilled coffee and misplaced keys. No scripts required—just a willingness to lean in and let the moment carry you.

The “Yes, And” mantra isn’t just for the stage—it’s a way of living. Think of the shy teenager who joins an improv class, suddenly starring in a story about zombie chickens. As they embrace the absurd, confidence takes root, growing quietly under the laughter. Or the young woman dubbed “Queen of the Raccoons,” who transforms her panic into a regal bow that has the crowd roaring. These moments aren’t rehearsed; they’re brave. They remind us that showing up is half the battle, and the other half? Trusting ourselves enough to play.

What improv does so beautifully is remind us that vulnerability isn’t something to avoid—it’s something to celebrate. It’s in the shaky voice that stumbles over the first line, the awkward joke that somehow lands, the laughter that lets us know we’re not alone. It’s messy and raw, but isn’t that what makes it divine? Improv doesn’t ask for perfection; it asks for presence. And in return, it gives us connection—the kind that lights up a room, a morning, or a life.

So let’s embrace the unknown. Let’s say “Yes” to the absurdity and “And” to the possibility. Because the best moments are the ones we can’t predict—the ones that remind us that imperfection isn’t a flaw, but the spark that makes life unforgettable. Take the leap. Even if you land in manure. There’s laughter—and maybe even magic—waiting on the other side.
