She Was ~ A Minimalist Fugue

Words / 2025 Variety

Heartfelt Advice Shared in Houses of Worship and Empty Warehouses

Introduction

This story breathes in resonant spaces—recorded in the echo of abandoned warehouses where truth reverberates naturally, in churches where confessions hang in air like incense. Each movement follows the gentle rise and fall of string quartet accompaniment: two violins asking questions, viola offering comfort, cello providing the heartbeat. The progression Am-F-Dm-E7 becomes a reassuring cycle, blues-tinged but hopeful, folksy without pretension. You can hear the air around each word, the warmth of memory softening sharp edges. This is passion told quietly, curiosity whispered rather than shouted—heartfelt advice from someone who learned these truths in empty rooms where honesty echoes back threefold.

---

She Was: Notes in Resonant Spaces

First Movement (Am - The Recognition)

She was a glass harmonica when I knew her—transparent vessels singing when touched with wet fingers, each note hanging in church air. Her voice moved through rooms with pizzicato gentleness, plucked strings marking 12/8 time like raindrops on warehouse roofs.

In her kitchen (sparse, warm, filled with plants that hummed with growth), she'd trace chord progressions on the wooden table. The two violins of her thoughts asking questions, viola offering comfort, cello providing that low drone heartbeat. A minor settling into F with subtle blues inflection, breathing through D minor before lifting to E seventh—never quite resolving but somehow complete. This was her wisdom: questions can be answers if you hold them right.

Second Movement (F - The Teaching)

When she was home, she became herself—no transformation needed, just deepening into who she'd always been. A stable backdrop for everyone else's chaos, diatonic harmony providing reassurance. Her movements carried that laid-back rhythm, unhurried but purposeful, like rhythmic bowing on open strings.

"Tea?" she'd offer—chamomile with honey, steam carrying stories. The word had a slight quiver, a lilting quality that made you lean in.

The kettle sang at 107 beats per minute, the tempo of remembering without rushing. We'd sit in comfortable silence, letting the warmth fill spaces between words. Behind us, I imagined sustained chords swelling gently—that dream-pop ambiance without losing intimacy.

Third Movement (Dm - The Blues)

When she was young (she shared this gradually, like mixing colors), the strings began to swell slightly, adding complexity to match her growing passion. She collected lessons the way others collect records:

- Age seven: Learning piano from grandmother's patient hands (violins entering softly)
- Age eleven: Discovering that silence has its own music (viola adding warmth)
- Age seventeen: Understanding that authentic passion requires patience (cello deepening)

Her internal world unfolded in layers—folk simplicity meeting jazz complexity with that subtle blues inflection, like a trip-hop heartbeat beneath acoustic surfaces. She'd speak in sustained inflections with that whispery quality: "I was learning to glow from inside," then wait for understanding to bloom naturally, the melody climbing to convey growing passion.

Refrain (E7 - The Hook)

Go, go, go—not away but forward. Each repetition gaining confidence, a hook that invites you to jump for joy despite the gentle delivery. Sometimes whispered, sometimes sung with confident clarity, always that seventh chord suggesting possibility. The beat restrained and complimentary, driving the rhythm forward without overpowering the softness.

Fourth Movement (Am - The Courage)

When she was out in the world, she moved with confident clarity—not loud but unmistakable. Like cello notes in an empty warehouse, she resonated in spaces others overlooked.

I watched her navigate difficulty with grace—never flashy, just well-placed moments of truth. She taught through example: follow passion honestly, fearlessly, but gently.

Fifth Movement (F - The Following)

B Otto Will (she explained once: "Be Ought To Will"—the space between desire and action) guided her through cities that forgot how to listen. She carried Brrd Roots—bird roots, she laughed, "songs that grow into trees."

Her passion was curious, exploratory but patient. She'd touch old walls to feel their stories, pause mid-conversation to catch melodies in traffic noise. This was authenticity: following threads others missed, weaving them into something healing.

Sixth Movement (Dm - The Peak)

When she was older (not ill but deepened), the performance reached its emotional climax. Fuller bowing, richer harmony—she became even more transparent, you could see through to her essence, that warm soulful core.

She spoke in bridges then, philosophical turns with restrained craftsmanship:

"Sometimes I wonder if my child will hear what I heard—not the words but the spaces between. When I become silence, will anyone recognize the song?"

(And yes, when she died inside my arms—the strings responded with that low cello drone, poignant chord holding us both—it wasn't ending but transformation. The dynamics dropped to hushed, tender level as we pondered rebirth together.)

She'd share this over dinner, candles flickering, the arrangement exemplifying how simplicity and steady rhythm combine to support magnificent soul.

Final Refrain (E7 - The Breath)

The story doesn't end—it cycles like her refrain, each go forward softer but more certain.

In her kitchen (I return there in memory, in dreams that smell like baking bread), the kettle still sings. The walls hold her harmonies—Am, F, Dm, E7—cycling eternal, never quite resolving but somehow perfect.

Coda (The Heartfelt Truth)

She was:
- Morning light through clean windows (hushed beginning)
- The pause that makes music possible (breathing space)
- A song hummed while cooking (wide range from whisper to power)
- The gentle pulse beneath all things (that laid-back trip-hop pattern)

I carry her rhythm now—that unhurried flow with blues undertones, that swaying meter that makes time itself negotiable. The dynamic shaping—gentle rise and fall—makes the story breathe with lived experience. When I listen carefully in resonant spaces, I hear her in the natural reverb of empty rooms, in the way certain light falls at dawn.

Go, she whispers through memory, go, go forward.

It means: Follow your passion with patience and wholeheartedness. Be authentic without force. Let your truth echo in empty spaces until it fills them. This is heartfelt advice from someone who learned it note by note—not a loud anthem but contemplative inspiration, urging you to follow passion honestly and fearlessly.

Like a string quartet in a church, it exists most fully in the space between notes—where silence teaches us how to listen. The composition exemplifies restrained craftsmanship while remaining soulful and captivating.]
