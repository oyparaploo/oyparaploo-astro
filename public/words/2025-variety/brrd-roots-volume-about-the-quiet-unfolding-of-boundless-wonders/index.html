Brrd Roots ~ Volume ~ About The Quiet Unfolding of Boundless Wonders

Words / 2025 Variety

Volume moves through space like a song with too many choruses—sprawling, spilling, refusing to settle. It is not delicate. It is a loud gesture thrown across the sky, a body stretching to take up more than it was given.
One installment in something sprawling, breathing, unfinished.
A novel writing itself. A painting dripping past the edges of its frame.
An argument too good to end on time.
Three-dimensional proof that something exists in this world,
The space a fig takes up in your palm.
The weight of a house filled with ghosts who refuse to leave.
The amount of sky squeezed between your fingers when you reach for nothing.
Density pressed into motion,
The number of figs falling before you realize the tree is trying to tell you something.
The volume of mistakes that taste better after fermentation.
The weight of one hundred people laughing at the wrong moment—but laughing anyway.
Not just sound, but consequence**,
The hum of the fig grove too deep to be ignored,
The way soil breathes so loud it changes the air,
The quiet after someone says something true and there’s nothing left to say but the wind.
Volume hums—not quietly, not passively, but like the swell of a chorus, the rise of something pushing past its own edges.
Not simply written, but building itself midair, stepping upward like scaffolding growing toward something unseen.
A book with pages that lift as if caught in a gust.
A canvas that refuses to sit still.
A movement not waiting for approval, but insisting on forward motion.
Not static, but pressing outward, something that refuses to stay within walls, spilling beyond the limits of what was measured.
The space a fig takes up when it falls.
The sky reaching wider when someone decides to stretch.
The moment a door opens not gently, but all at once.
Magnitude in motion, momentum disguised as mere accumulation.
A rush of voices pressing upward.
A wave swelling too large to ignore.
A laugh that does not stop at the throat, but tumbles into the air, shaking loose everything that kept it small.
Not just sound, but lift,
A frequency pushing itself into the sky,
A hum that finds its way into the bones of things that forgot how to vibrate.
Like a hand pressing into limestone**, grinding against the surface, shaping grooves where there were none—until water arrives, unbothered, lifting what the stone refused to release.
Etched, scraped, shaped into permanence, until laughter washes through like a river slipping under ruins.
A novel carved into bedrock before being softened by rain.
A sculpture weathering itself into memory.
A movement cut sharp enough to survive, but light enough to keep moving.
Undeniable, pressing into space with weight until the water gathers, finds its way into the cracks, carries the dust into something unseen.
The depth a fig takes up when it falls.
The sky pushing against mountains, then erasing their edges with mist.
The shape of hands folded around stone before water smooths them open again.
Density in motion, force softened at its edges.
A canyon forming from years of erosion.
A sound bouncing between cliffs until the echo no longer remembers what it was repeating.
A laugh trapped in marble before the stream carries it somewhere it can breathe.
Not just noise, but the balance between impact and release, between stone and stream, between something solid enough to last and light enough to be carried away.
It cuts through, presses in, erodes, reshapes itself.**
It is never just weight, never just water—
**It is permanence at war with movement, it is laughter pressing into stone before being lifted into sky.
Volume Unbound does do not ask for permission. It does not wait for the edges to soften before slipping past them. It move—not hesitantly, not cautiously, but with force, expansion, and the unstoppable rise of something that refuses to shrink.
To be unbound is to exist in a state of momentum, never static, never fixed. It is the absence of tethers but the presence of motion—the way a river finds its own shape, the way wind presses against its own limits, the way a thought unfolds beyond expectation.
But momentum alone is not enough. It needs volume.
Volume presses against unbound, pressing against margins, stacking in depth, amplifying presence. It does not merely fill space—it reshapes it.
A book without a binding—pages loose, slipping between fingers, drifting across tables, arriving wherever the breeze allows. Its volume presses outward, not just in numbers but in reach, in the way a story will not be contained once it begins. A mind untethered—leaping beyond what was taught, bending rules not for destruction but for discovery. Expanding beyond its known edges, accumulating thought the way waves accumulate depth.
In chemistry, unbound is pure motion, free-floating electrons carrying charge without asking where to settle. Its volume is not in weight but in presence, energy without restraint, vibration stretching into space. In emotion, unbound is surrender, love unmeasured, grief uncounted, joy that exists simply because it can. Its volume is not in containment but in intensity, in the swell of a laugh that fills the air, in the hush of a sigh too deep to be ignored.
Unbound is the root breaking past stone. Its volume is measured in persistence—the density of each push forward, the mass of something refusing to stay buried.
The pulse without a rhythm. Its volume in the force of sound, in the way it arrives, presses, demands recognition.
The question that does not wait for answers before asking again. Its volume in repetition, in the way a thought grows simply because it was spoken aloud.
To be unbound is not to exist without consequence, but to exist without hesitation. It is the laugh that is too loud, the step that reaches beyond caution, the hand that stretches past its own shadow. It is volume in motion—never reduced, never silenced, only pressing forward into more.
But it is also the echo that remains after the movement has passed, the resonance that lingers in the space it left behind.
Volume unbound is the breath that takes up space, the rhythm that carves through silence, the sound that does not apologize for arriving.
