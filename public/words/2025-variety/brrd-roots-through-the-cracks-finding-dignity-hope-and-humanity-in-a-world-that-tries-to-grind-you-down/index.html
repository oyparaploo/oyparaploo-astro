Through the Cracks

Finding Dignity, Hope, and Humanity in a World That Tries to Grind You Down

← Words / 2025 Variety

Every day’s a journey—an expedition, a haul, a wander, a trek, a flight. And let’s be honest, some days it feels less like you’re crossing the great unknown and more like you’re crossing your fingers that you make it through. Some roads are pothole-ridden nightmares dug deep by systems that don’t just bruise your pride—they grind it right down to dust. But even when the world’s stacked against you, even when dignity’s hanging by a thread, somehow, we keep standing. We keep going. God knows how, but we do.

The Stardust trees? They’ve seen it all. They’ve stood tall while people buckled under the weight of it. They’ve watched workers leave shift after shift, wiped out, hollow-eyed, lives dictated by quotas and cameras watching their every move. But one day, someone took a marker and scribbled on a scrap of cardboard: “You matter.” They tied it to a branch. Was it big, fancy activism? Nope. But when folks saw it, they paused—like really paused—and maybe, just maybe, felt the smallest spark of hope. Because sometimes dignity’s not about loud fights or fiery speeches; it’s about someone bothering to say, “You’re worth something.”

Going through this world on foot—no wheels, no speed, just your own legs—is one heck of a rebellious act. You feel every blister, every scrape, every ache that reminds you: this ain’t an easy journey. One day, we saw a young woman sitting on a soaked park bench after what looked like the worst shift of her life. She muttered, “They don’t care if I’m tired.” Her voice cracked like she didn’t even expect anyone to listen. Someone nearby didn’t say a word—just walked over, opened their umbrella, and held it over her head. And yeah, it didn’t fix her crappy job or the rain or whatever else was weighing her down. But for one moment, it told her, You’re still here. You’re still seen.

Madness shows up where systems run the show. It’s the kind of madness where you stand in line for hours at some soul-sucking office only to leave with nothing but frustration. It’s feeling like a cog in a machine that grinds on, whether you make it or not. But even there, humanity sneaks through the cracks. It’s the coworker who shoves half their sandwich into your hand because your lunch went “missing.” It’s the stranger who catches your eye and gives you a nod that says, “We’ll get through this.” Those moments? They’re not miracles, but man, they feel like it.

Run like the world’s on fire if you’ve got the strength for it. But when running feels impossible, when every step’s weighed down with defeat, it’s okay to stop. It’s okay to sit. Find someone else who’s feeling stuck—share a laugh, a cry, an awkward silence. Those tiny exchanges, those gut-level honest moments of connection, they’re defiance. They’re rebellion against the idea that we’re alone in this.

Pain thrives where systems crush souls. Long shifts break backs, surveillance strips privacy, endless forms suck out your humanity one checkbox at a time. But still, kindness finds a way. It’s the person who lets you cut ahead in line, the coworker humming a tune that reminds you of better days, the stranger who hands you a tissue when you’re on the verge of breaking down. Little things, but when you’re scraping bottom, little things are what keep you going.

Cities strung together by lakes or crumbling concrete know all about systems that make life a grind. Surveillance cameras stare like unblinking eyes, voices bark orders that care nothing for you, and bureaucratic red tape stretches forever. But humanity fights back: graffiti scrawled on a wall screaming “I EXIST!”, park benches where strangers gather to share food like a quiet protest against isolation, neighbors who band together when the system forgets them. It’s messy, imperfect, but it’s beautiful—and it’s real.

So hold onto your humor—it’ll keep your soul intact. Armor up with kindness, because the world needs it more than ever. Let hope burn bright, even when the dark feels endless. Shine not because you’ve beaten the odds, but because you’re standing tall in spite of them. Whether faced with alien spectators or the grinding wheels of dehumanization, let your light say loud and clear: we’re here, we’re human, and that’ll never be taken away.
