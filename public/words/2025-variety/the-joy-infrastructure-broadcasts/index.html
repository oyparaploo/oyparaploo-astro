THE JOY INFRASTRUCTURE BROADCASTS

Words / 2025 Variety

A 12-Episode Series in the Spirit of Gentle Revolutionary Teaching

Episode 1: "Your Cells Are Having a Party Without You"

[GARY's garden shed, converted into a broadcasting space. Mismatched furniture, badly-made clay objects everywhere, a doorway with googly eyes, colorful scarves hanging from beams. GARY enters with vegetables, but also with HAL LUCY's scarves and some of KIRI TE's crystals in his pocket.]

GARY: Hello, neighbor. Come in, come in. Watch the doorway - it's a transformation portal. At least, that's what Hal Lucy tells me. I just think it's nice to notice you're going from outside to inside.

[He sets down vegetables, picks up a badly-made clay object]

You see this? August Palo made it in seven minutes. He traded it for tomatoes. It's supposed to be a bowl, but it's more like... a suggestion of a bowl. He says it gives everyone permission to exist imperfectly.

[Sets it down gently]

But that's not why you're here. You're here because you're tired. Not sleepy tired - though maybe that too. You're tired in that way where your thoughts keep thinking themselves, round and round, like a hamster wheel in your skull.

[He sits, looks directly at viewer]

Right now - this very second - your body is doing 37 trillion things correctly. Your cells are running what Kiri Te calls a "gift economy." Your liver is gifting clean blood to your heart. Your lungs are gifting oxygen to your brain. No invoices. No payment required.

[He picks up a piece of paper with strange notes]

Hal Lucy made me write this down: Your mitochondria are spinning at 1000 RPM. Right now. Like tiny disco balls in every cell. You're literally buzzing with cellular parties, and your thinking mind wasn't invited. That's why it's sulking up there, making you worry about tomorrow's meeting.

[He stands, walks to a chart on the wall showing body systems]

Want to know something wild? Your body replaces 330 billion cells every day. That's 330 billion construction projects completing successfully. While you were worried about that email, your body rebuilt itself 330 billion times and got it right basically every time.

[He picks up a glass of water]

Watch this. [Drinks] That water just began a journey through 60,000 miles of blood vessels. My body knows exactly what to do with it. I don't have to think "process water now." My kidneys just... know. They've been knowing since before I was born.

[KIRI TE suddenly appears from behind a plant]

KIRI TE (whisper-shouting): The bacterial colonies in your gut are having democratic elections right now! They're voting on what you should crave for dinner!

GARY: Kiri Te's been living in my garden shed. She says my vegetables have the highest joy density per square foot.

KIRI TE: Your viewers need to know - their bodies are communist! Every cell shares everything! No cell hoards resources! It's the only functioning communist society and it's inside everyone!

GARY (gently): That's true. Your cells don't have property rights. They just... share. And somehow you stay alive. Actually, not somehow - BECAUSE they share.

[HAL LUCY enters with a flourish of scarves]

HAL LUCY: Are we telling them about the convenience store?!

GARY: We were getting there—

HAL LUCY: Your body is running a 24-hour convenience store that never closes! Your liver is on night shift right now, cleaning everything, no overtime pay!

GARY (to viewer): What they're trying to say - in their own ways - is that you're already joy. Not having it. Being it. Your existence is 37 trillion cells successfully cooperating every second.

[He returns to center, speaks quietly]

You know what's funny? You've been sitting there worried you're not enough, while your body has been performing actual miracles. Your eyes have been adjusting to light changes 100,000 times today. Your heart pushed 2,000 gallons of blood around. You made 25,000 breaths happen.

[Picks up the badly-made bowl]

This wonky bowl holds water. Not perfectly, but enough. Your body holds life. Not perfectly, but enough. More than enough, actually. Abundantly.

[Long pause]

Tomorrow, we'll talk about doorways. Why Hal Lucy thinks they're magic. Why your brain forgets things when you walk through them. Why that's actually a gift.

But for now, just... notice your breathing. You don't have to change it. Just notice it's happening. Without your management. Without your permission. Successfully.

That's joy. The quiet kind that's been there all along.

Episode 2: "Doorways Are Magic (And So Is Your Spine Fountain)"

[The garden shed. GARY is standing in the doorway, not moving.]

GARY: I've been standing here for five minutes. Hal Lucy says I'm "in transformation space." My wife says I'm blocking the door.

[He steps through]

But something did happen. I was outside, now I'm inside. I was in the world, now I'm in a room. My brain actually reset a little. That's why you forget what you came for when you enter rooms. Your brain is busy transforming.

[HAL LUCY is already inside, arranging scarves on the badly-made objects]

HAL LUCY: Tell them about the spine fountain!

GARY: We're starting with doorways—

HAL LUCY: Your spine is a fountain! Not metaphorically - LITERALLY! Cerebrospinal fluid pulses up and down 12 times per minute! You have rapids in your backbone!

[AUGUST PALO enters through the door, pauses in the threshold]

AUGUST PALO: Feel that? Neither here nor there. Neither outside nor in. This liminal space is where joy lives.

GARY (to viewer): They all moved into my shed. I grow vegetables. Now apparently I grow joy instructors.

[He sits down, everyone arranges themselves around the space]

GARY: Let's talk about what's actually happening in your body right now. Your spine - Hal Lucy's right - it is a fountain. That fluid cushions your brain, carries nutrients, removes waste. While you've been worrying about looking professional, your spine has been running a waterpark for your nervous system.

HAL LUCY: Show them the fountain exercise!

[She demonstrates undulating her spine]

HAL LUCY: Children do this naturally! They can't NOT wiggle! Because their fountain wants to flow!

AUGUST PALO: Every threshold is an opportunity. Watch. [He walks through the doorway slowly] I'm leaving who I was. [Steps through] I'm becoming who I am.

GARY: That's a lot of pressure for going to the bathroom.

AUGUST PALO: Every transition matters!

[KIRI TE emerges from under a table]

KIRI TE: While they're talking about doors, 50 million of your cells just died and were reborn! You're transitioning constantly! You're a walking doorway!

GARY (to viewer): Here's what I've learned from having them around: Everything is more interesting than you think. That doorway you walk through twenty times a day? Your brain treats it like a tiny adventure. Your spine that you ignore? It's performing water acrobatics.

[He stands, walks to the doorway]

Try this with me. Walk through a doorway - any doorway - and pause right in the middle. Feel that? You're between rooms. Between states. Between who you were and who you're becoming.

[Steps through]

Hal Lucy wants me to tell you this is "transformation portal activation." August says it's "liminal consciousness." Kiri Te is writing down the cellular implications.

I just think it's nice to notice you're moving through space. That you can be here, then there. That simple walking is actually traveling.

[Returns to his seat]

HAL LUCY: Tell them about the seventeen seconds!

GARY: Joy lasts seventeen seconds naturally. That's it. Not hours, not days. Seventeen seconds. Then it has to renew.

HAL LUCY: Unless it popcorns!

GARY: Unless it popcorns. That's when one joy triggers another triggers another. Like... [picks up popcorn] One kernel pops, hits another, that one pops...

AUGUST PALO: It's called "cascading positive affect" in the literature—

GARY: It's called happiness in the garden.

[KIRI TE whisper-shouts from her corner]

KIRI TE: Your cells don't know about the seventeen seconds! They're having constant micro-joys! Successful division = joy! Successful metabolism = joy! You're a joy popcorn machine at the cellular level!

GARY (to viewer): You want to know the real doorway secret? Every moment is a doorway. Between past and future. Between one breath and the next. Between who you think you are and who you actually are - which is 37 trillion cells successfully cooperating.

[He stands by the door again]

Tomorrow, we'll talk about color. Why Hal Lucy insists beige causes depression. Why August Palo painted his entire wall orange. Why Kiri Te believes your cells can see color from the inside.

But tonight, when you go through doors, maybe pause. Just for a second. Feel yourself transforming. Even if it's just from someone in the kitchen to someone in the bedroom.

You're allowed to transform. Seventeen seconds at a time. Or all at once in a doorway.

Your spine fountain will keep flowing either way.

Episode 3: "Your Living Room Is Trying to Kill Your Joy (And Other Color Emergencies)"

[The shed has been attacked by color. Orange wall, yellow fabric, blue scarves, green plants everywhere. GARY sits in the middle of the rainbow explosion, calm.]

GARY: Hal Lucy painted my wall while I was at the farmers market. She said it was a "chromatic emergency."

[HAL LUCY bursts in with more paint cans]

HAL LUCY: Beige is literally depression! The University of Texas proved it! Your neutral walls are making you sad ON PURPOSE!

GARY: I don't think walls have purposes—

HAL LUCY: Gray offices cause measurable serotonin drops! Within hours! We're medicating depression we're creating architecturally!

[AUGUST PALO is painting dots on clay objects]

AUGUST PALO: Colors are medicine. Blue reduces blood pressure. Orange increases appetite. Green reduces pain perception by 20%.

GARY (to viewer): Last week my shed was brown. Now it looks like a rainbow got sick in here. But something interesting happened...

[He stands, walks around the space]

I've been sleeping better. Not because colors are magic, but because... well, maybe because colors are magic. My eyes have something to do now. They were bored before, firing the same signals over and over. "Brown. Brown. Still brown."

[KIRI TE pops up from behind the orange wall]

KIRI TE: Your cone cells were unemployed! Now they're working! Three types of cones, each hungry for different wavelengths! You were starving two-thirds of your vision!

GARY: She's been measuring my "chromatic nutrition" all week.

HAL LUCY (holding up gray fabric): This is what your living room is doing to you! [Throws it down, holds up orange] This is what it could be doing!

GARY: The thing is, they might be right. When everything was beige, I didn't notice I was sad. It was like... background sadness. Like elevator music but depressing.

[He picks up a bright yellow squash]

This squash is the same color as that wall now. And every time I see the wall, I think of squash, of growing things, of sunshine that made them grow. The color carries memory.

AUGUST PALO: It's called "chromatic association networking"—

GARY: It's called being happy when you see yellow.

[HAL LUCY has set up a demonstration with gray blocks and colored blocks]

HAL LUCY: Watch this! Stare at the gray for 30 seconds. [Everyone does] Now look at the colors!

EVERYONE: Ohhhhhhh!

HAL LUCY: Your eyes just threw a party! They were so bored with gray they're celebrating color like it's New Year's!

GARY (to viewer): Your grandmother probably knew this. Flowers on everything. Patterns on patterns. "Too much!" visitors would say. She'd laugh. She lived to 97, joyful daily.

[KIRI TE is now painting her hands]

KIRI TE: In prison, when they painted polka dots on walls, violence dropped 50%! The dots gave permission to be human instead of inmates!

GARY: We're not in prison—

KIRI TE: Aren't we?! [Gestures at imaginary beige walls] Beige prison! Gray prison! Neutral prison!

[AUGUST PALO has made a color wheel from vegetables]

AUGUST PALO: Every color has a job. Red: urgency and passion. Blue: calm and distance. Green: growth and balance. Yellow: attention and joy.

GARY: And beige?

AUGUST PALO: Beige's job is to not have a job. To be nothing. To make you feel nothing.

GARY (to viewer): Here's what I learned this week: You can paint a wall orange and the world doesn't end. Your landlord might get upset, but your cells throw a party.

[He picks up a paintbrush]

Hal Lucy wants everyone to paint everything immediately. August says to start with one wall. Kiri Te is painting her entire body.

I say: maybe start with a scarf. Or a pillow. Or paint one shelf. Something your eyes can rest on when they're tired of beige.

[HAL LUCY is now wearing every color]

HAL LUCY: Tell them about the two-week adjustment!

GARY: Oh right. When you add color, your brain panics for two weeks. "Too much!" it says. Then it adjusts. Week three, you can't imagine living without it.

HAL LUCY: I haven't seen beige in five years! I might be immortal now!

GARY: She's not immortal. But she is... persistently joyful.

[He looks around the rainbow shed]

Tomorrow we'll talk about making things badly on purpose. August has been making terrible pottery all week. Kiri Te made something that might be a hat or a plant holder. I made a bowl that actively repels water.

But for now, look around your space. Count the colors. If you can count them on one hand, your eyes are unemployed.

Your cone cells are sending out resumes: "Willing to see color. Extensive experience. Please hire."

Maybe hire them. Paint something. Wear something bright. Let your eyes have a job that isn't "seeing nothing."

Your joy might be hiding in the color you're afraid to paint your walls.
