Brrd Roots ~ Art of Redemption ~ Finding Wholeness in Fragments

Words / 2025 Variety

Transformative journey, emphasizing the potential found in objects, imperfection, curiosity, and reflection.

There was this one time, years ago, when I found myself in a tiny thrift shop on the corner of a street I can’t remember the name of. The kind of place where time feels suspended, filled with mismatched shelves holding oddities—tarnished silver spoons, weathered books with inscriptions from decades past, a lone porcelain elephant missing one ear. I picked up an old glass bottle, cloudy and chipped, its former contents long gone. I turned it over in my hand, and for reasons I still can’t quite explain, I felt its presence. Not just as an object, but as a story. Where had it been? What hands had held it? Did it once glint under the sun at a picnic, brimming with lemonade? Was it carelessly tossed, forgotten in the chaos of moving day? And why was it here now, waiting?

That bottle sparked something in me—a quiet revelation, I suppose. If this forgotten piece of glass could find its way into my life, wasn’t there something I could do to honor its journey? So I took it home. At first, I didn’t know what I’d do with it. It sat on my desk for weeks, catching the light in the late afternoon and whispering its mysteries. And then one day, I began to weave. Not with thread, but with fragments—bits of wire I’d saved from an old project, strips of fabric from a torn scarf, beads from a bracelet that had broken years ago. The bottle became a canvas, its cracks and flaws the foundation of something new.

As I worked, I realized that what I was creating wasn’t just an assemblage of objects; it was a reclamation. Of beauty in imperfection. Of the value in things we discard too soon. Of the idea that nothing—not even a chipped, cloudy bottle—is beyond redemption. And in some unspoken way, it felt like a reclamation of myself. A quiet affirmation that, even when I felt fragmented and unseen, there was potential for transformation.

Now, whenever I work with post-consumer or post-industrial materials, it feels like I’m participating in a kind of alchemy. A broken piece of mirror becomes a fragment of a moonlit sky. A scrap of metal finds its way into the arch of a bird in flight. Every piece tells a story—not just of where it’s been, but of where it’s going. And in the act of creating, I find myself asking deeper questions. Isn’t this what we all long for? To be seen, to be reimagined, to become part of something greater than ourselves?

There’s a quiet humor in it, too. Sometimes I’ll pick up an object—a soda can, a torn strip of cardboard—and think, “You’ve had a more exciting life than I have.” But then I remember: it’s not about how we begin, or where we’ve been tossed along the way. It’s about what we choose to become.

So, if you have materials to share—those scraps and remnants, those forgotten and forsaken pieces—I welcome them. Not just because they help me create, but because they remind me, time and again, of this profound truth: we are all a little broken, a little discarded, a little imperfect. But within each of us lies the extraordinary potential to be made whole—not in spite of our flaws, but because of them. And isn’t that the most beautiful story of all?
