Tariffs and Ripples of Resilience

Uniting Humanity

Brrd Roots ~ Free-Verse
Tariffs and Ripples of Resilience: Uniting Humanity
in the Wake of Change

This is a story—
not of numbers etched on screens,
not of headlines hammering havoc,
but of ripples rolling through rivers unseen,
a world of webs, woven tight, trembling.

When President Trump unleashed the "Liberation Day" tariffs,
the stock market shook,
then shattered.
It didn’t merely stumble—it spiraled headlong,
dragging investors, industries, and quiet hopes behind it.

Picture the machinery of global trade—
its gears grinding with grace,
its rhythm soft as a song.

The tariffs tore into it like a weight hurled from nowhere—
teeth grinding, sparks flying,
the hum of harmony shattered into shrill.

(And somewhere, an economist clutching cold coffee muttered,
“Well, guess I’m not sleeping this century,”
as the stock tickers blinked bright,
tiny merciless stars in a sky of uncertainty.)

A 10% tariff for some,
46% for others—
a heavy toll thrown into fragile scales,
tilting, toppling, scattering order into oblivion.

(Imagine global trade as a delicate tower of glass.
Now picture someone pulling out every middle pane at once—
the structure swaying like a willow in a storm.)

The first domino fell,
and with it came echoes.

Picture Jane—
a small-business owner in Chicago.
Her hands carve furniture from wood, smooth and sacred,
each grain a testament to care.

But overnight, her material costs climb.
Her choices shrink—raise prices,
or risk the fragile livelihood she has built with love.

Yet, Jane falters.
She hesitates to charge more,
fearing the rejection of loyal hands that hold her craft.
Weeks pass, her profits plunge,
her confidence shatters under the weight of doubt.

It takes a letter from a customer—
words inscribed with belief,
each syllable glowing with gratitude—
to restore Jane's courage.

(She also swears the letter smelled of lavender,
a whisper, perhaps, from a cosmos urging resilience.)

Picture Michael—
an assembly-line worker in Detroit.
The automaker whispers layoffs,
his chain of supply unraveling at its seams.

Michael panics.
He overdrafts his account,
stockpiling supplies as though scarcity rises.

(He now owns 12 boxes of beans,
stacked in solemn towers,
fortresses of fleeting certainty.)

He fights with his wife,
his worry spilling in jagged shards.
His child overhears,
their quiet tears reflecting his own.

Ashamed, Michael apologizes,
promising to build laughter,
even from the cracks.

Though layoffs reach his factory,
Michael finds solace in union halls—
spaces where solitude dissolves,
and solidarity rises.

(And the beans, in their quiet dignity,
are donated—Michael whispering,
“This pantry deserves better tomorrows, and so do we.”)

At the heart of chaos lay uncertainty.

Would China counter, claws raised high?
Would Europe weave tighter walls,
its markets slipping like mist through fingers?

Chains once seamless,
now trembling webs.
Shortages stretching toward semiconductors,
soaring costs scaling corn and steel.

Investors grasped at glimmers,
their stocks swaying as fear soared louder than logic.
The Dow, the S&P, sinking—
their charts tracing the contours of collapse.

(Somewhere, a stockbroker watched the red lines curling,
writhing like restless flames,
and murmured,
“Guess decaf it is.”)

For industries tied to oceans and orders—
tech giants leaning on distant hands,
automakers clutching pieces scattered across tides—
these tariffs weren’t mild.
They were monoliths, looming existential.

But beneath the numbers,
beneath the calls cutting through Wall Street chambers,
there were people.

People like Lena—
a mid-level manager steering routes of shipping.

Lena overlooked an email,
a detail, a ripple—
a tariff adjustment she failed to see.

Her misstep marred the routes,
costing thousands,
leaving Lena clutching failure,
believing she had betrayed her team.

But Lena’s supervisor arrived softly,
offering words not of reprimand,
but of rhythm—
a reminder that the symphony of mistakes
still leads forward to new crescendos.

(And as Lena squeezed a stress ball shaped like the Earth,
she imagined the world, wobbling, waiting to hold,
a fragile sphere, sturdy after all.)

And yet,
in turbulence,
there lies tethering.

Every tariff,
every thread tugged tight,
sends ripples that weave through rivers unseen.

The global economy isn’t a machine of steel.
It’s human hands,
human hearts,
woven whispers crossing oceans.

"Liberation Day" was a reminder—
an unflinching one—
of our frailty,
of our delicate balance,
of how the smallest wave sends tremors to the farthest shore.

(And how trade feels like balancing moonlight—
ethereal, shimmering, slipping through fingers
held too tight.)

But in frailty,
there dwells force.

Jane, with hands steady,
shapes her designs anew,
bearing her customers’ trust like a lantern.

Michael, holding his family in laughter,
lays bricks of hope
on walls not yet risen.

These moments echo—
change, however violent,
doesn’t only break.

It bursts apart,
revealing strands that bridge,
reminding us that every storm is born of a ripple,
every resolve forged in resilience.

(And every pantry deserves something finer than fortress beans—
a quiet, gleaming triumph.)

There is wisdom wound in noise.

No decision stands alone.
Every move ripples—
across tides, across towns,
across hands linked by threads unseen.

The answers we seek
emerge from compassion,
from courage,
from hands reaching,
from binding fear in unity.

The human spirit is unyielding—
a blazing investment of worth,
a treasure of boundless light.

Let the ripples remind us,
not just of what we’ve fractured,
but what waits to be rebuilt—
together.
