The Grief Workshop

Words / 2025 Variety

## Asheville Community Theater
## January 2024

LIORA CHEN, 42, stands on empty stage. Twenty folding chairs in a circle. Sign: "Somatic Grief Processing - $75"

First participant arrives. KEVIN, 38, Tesla keys visible.

**KEVIN**: Is this where we learn to compost trauma?

**LIORA**: We explore embodied healing through movement.

**KEVIN**: Your flyer said something about mushrooms.

**LIORA**: Metaphorical mushrooms.

**KEVIN**: For seventy-five dollars they better be real.

---

## The Participants Arrive

KEMI (31), JASPER (55), MINH (47) trickle in. Outside, Asheville floods. Fourth time this year.

**JASPER**: Bridge is out. We're stuck here.

**LIORA**: The workshop is three hours—

**JASPER**: No, literally stuck. Roads are flooded.

**KEMI**: I need to get home. My mom's alone.

**LIORA**: I'm sure it'll clear—

Thunder. Lights flicker.

**MINH**: This building's in a flood zone.

**LIORA**: We're on the second floor.

**MINH**: I was on a second floor in Houston. During Harvey. Didn't matter.

---

## The Exercise Begins

**LIORA**: Let's start. Everyone stand. We'll begin with rooting.

**KEVIN**: While flooding?

**LIORA**: Especially while flooding. Feel your feet—

**KEMI**: I can't stay. My mother has dementia. If the power goes—

**LIORA**: Can someone check on her?

**KEMI**: She only speaks Yoruba now. Who's going to—

Water drips through ceiling. Small, but persistent.

**JASPER**: That's new.

---

## The Truth Comes Out

**LIORA**: Okay. Cards on table. I'm not certified in anything. I read a book about fungi and trauma. My therapist dumped me for missing too many appointments. This is my first workshop.

**KEVIN**: What?

**LIORA**: The theater needed rent. I needed money. You needed... something.

**MINH**: I needed continuing education credits.

**LIORA**: I can't provide those.

**JASPER**: Jesus Christ.

**KEMI**: My mother is alone in a flood because of fake therapy?

---

## Real Processing

Water pools in corner. They move chairs.

**LIORA**: Look, we're all here. Stuck. Might as well talk.

**KEVIN**: About what?

**LIORA**: Why you came. Really.

**KEVIN**: My wife left. Took the kids. Said I was "emotionally absent."

**JASPER**: Mine died. Six months ago. Car accident.

**MINH**: Brother. Overdose. Last year.

**KEMI**: Mother's forgetting me. Piece by piece.

Silence. Water drips louder.

**LIORA**: Father. Cancer. Two weeks ago.

---

## The Exercise Changes

**LIORA**: Forget the workshop. What do you need?

**KEMI**: To call my mother.

**JASPER**: To stop pretending I'm okay.

**MINH**: To stop feeling guilty.

**KEVIN**: To feel anything.

**LIORA**: Then do that.

Kemi calls. Speaks Yoruba. Others listen without understanding, hearing only tone: love, worry, love.

**JASPER**: My wife hated my driving. Always grabbed the door handle. Night she died, I was home. She drove herself. I keep thinking if I'd driven—

**MINH**: You'd both be dead.

**JASPER**: Maybe.

---

## The Water Rises

First floor now flooded. They hear it lapping at stairs.

**KEVIN**: Should we move higher?

**LIORA**: It's a two-story building.

**KEVIN**: Roof?

**LIORA**: In this rain?

They stay. Circle tightens.

**MINH**: My brother called that night. I didn't answer. Working late.

**KEVIN**: You couldn't have known.

**MINH**: I knew he was using again. I was tired of it.

**LIORA**: Being tired doesn't make you guilty.

**MINH**: Doesn't make me innocent either.

---

## Real Movement

Without instruction, they begin moving. Not dance. Just... movement.

Jasper rocks. Kemi sways. Minh paces. Kevin stands still—the most movement of all.

**LIORA**: This is probably what I should have taught.

**KEMI**: This is better.

**LIORA**: Why?

**KEMI**: It's honest.

Water reaches top stair. They hear sirens outside.

---

## Morning

Water receded. Sun out. Asheville steaming.

They've been there all night. Talking, not talking, moving, being still.

**KEVIN**: I should sue you.

**LIORA**: Probably.

**KEVIN**: But I won't.

**JASPER**: We should do this again.

**LIORA**: The flooding?

**JASPER**: The honesty.

**KEMI**: My mother's okay. Neighbor checked.

**MINH**: That's good.

They gather their things. Nobody asks for refunds.

**LIORA**: Same time next week?

**KEVIN**: You're not qualified.

**LIORA**: No. But neither is grief.

They leave separately. But something connects them now. Not healing. Not closure. Just shared flooding.

---

## Next Week

Twelve people show up. Word spread.

**NEW PERSON**: Is this the grief thing?

**LIORA**: I guess it is.

**NEW PERSON**: How much?

**LIORA**: Whatever you can afford.

**NEW PERSON**: What's the method?

**KEVIN**: Flooding.

**NEW PERSON**: Like, emotional flooding?

**JASPER**: Sometimes.

They sit. Circle forms. Someone brings coffee. Someone cries. Someone laughs.

No mushroom metaphors. No root choreography. Just people, circled up, surviving high water.

**End**
