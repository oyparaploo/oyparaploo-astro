Joy Uprisings

THE POSTURE REVOLUTION

An Experimental Joy Laboratory

Les Subsistances, Lyon

PRE-SHOW PROTOCOL

Audiences receive "Joy Passports" tracking:
- Current posture (photographed)
- Mood on gray scale (literal gray paint chips)
- Last time they touched soil
- Minutes since genuine smile

ACT ONE: The Slump Summit

Stage: Five chairs, each increasingly slumped. Gray lighting.

HOLLIE (30s, former dancer, demonstrating perfect slump):
I spent ten years maintaining perfect alignment.
Spine like architecture.
Then discovered: slumping with intention
is harder than standing straight.
Watch—

Slumps dramatically, holds, then reduces by 5%

That 5% improvement?
That's where joy lives.
Not in perfection. In the tiny negotiation.

JORGE (50s, gardener, hands visibly soil-stained):
My back curved from thirty years of planting.
Doctors called it "deformation."
I call it "Earth-shaped."
My spine knows every seed depth.

ROSALIA (40s, meteorologist, studying gray light):
Cloudy days reduce serotonin by 20%.
But increase creativity by 30%.
Gray light makes pupils dilate.
Everything becomes more saturated.
We're not seeing less. We're seeing deeper.

NITIN (25, coder, hasn't left chair in days):
My posture is a CSV file.
Compressed data.
But compression is information too.
Every vertebra stores different defeat.

JOEL (60s, smile researcher, forcibly grinning):
Forty-three facial muscles.
I'm using twelve.
Holding pencil in teeth for science.
Day 47 of forced smiling.
My zygomatic major is exhausted but euphoric.

ACT TWO: The Gray Day Gallery

Lights shift to various gray tones. Audience given gray cards to match.

ROSALIA:
Seven distinct grays in today's sky.
Pearl, dove, charcoal, ash, silver, smoke, graphite.
Your ancestors navigated by gray variations.
You still can.

Projects cloud formations

Cumulus: cotton gray
Stratus: sheet gray
Nimbus: pregnant gray

HOLLIE (moving through gray light):
No shadows with edges.
I'm undefined.
My outline negotiable.
This is why dancers love clouds—
We become watercolor.

Everyone practices "cloud breathing"—pulling gray light in, pushing it out

ACT THREE: The Soil Ceremony

Large containers of local Lyon soil brought on stage

JORGE:
This soil contains Mycobacterium vaccae.
Natural antidepressant.
Your skin absorbs it in seconds.

All performers submerge hands

NITIN (first time touching soil in years):
It's... speaking in temperature.
Cool then warm then cool.
My hands are having conversation.

JOEL:
Seventeen thousand mechanoreceptors activating.
This is data at maximum bandwidth.

HOLLIE:
Every grain is different.
My hands are reading earth Braille.

Audience invited to soil stations

ACT FOUR: The Smile Laboratory

Performers demonstrate increasingly absurd smile techniques

JOEL:
Pencil: basic.
Carrot: intermediate.
Baguette: advanced.
Holds full baguette horizontally in mouth
French adaptation!

Audience given various objects to hold in teeth

HOLLIE:
The absurdity breaks the sadness.
You can't be depressed with banana in teeth.
Biologically impossible.

ACT FIVE: The Savoring Station

Each performer recounts, pre-counts, and current-counts joy

JORGE:
Yesterday: Soil temperature at dawn.
Today: Your faces discovering dirt drugs.
Tomorrow: The soreness from intentional slumping.
Three timelines, one joy.

ROSALIA:
Past gray: Yesterday's pearl-smoke.
Present gray: Current charcoal-silver.
Future gray: Tomorrow's dove-ash.
I'm living in grayscale rainbow.

NITIN:
Recounting my first un-slump.
Pre-counting tonight's second attempt.
Currently counting this moment's micro-adjustments.
Time is a joy accordion.

FINALE: The Integration

All elements combined: slumping, gray light, soil, smiling, savoring

ALL (in various states of intentional slump):
We're not broken.
We're earth-shaped.
Gray-lit.
Soil-touched.
Smile-forced.
Time-traveling through saved joy.

Audience photos retaken—visible posture improvement through intentional slumping

IMAGISTIC AMPLIFICATIONS

The Slump Architecture
Your spine's 33 vertebrae can create 8,436,420 possible curve combinations. Each slump is unique as fingerprint. When you reduce slump by 5%, you're selecting from millions of possible micro-improvements—your proprioceptors calculate the optimal adjustment in milliseconds.

The Gray Light Processor
In diffused light, your pupils dilate up to 45% more than in direct sun. This increased aperture doesn't just gather more light—it literally changes the focal depth of your vision, creating what photographers call "bokeh effect" in real life. You see differently, not worse.

The Soil Synapse
One gram of soil contains up to 10 billion bacteria—more than human population. When you touch earth, your skin's microbiome (1 trillion bacteria) meets soil's microbiome. It's the largest gathering of living organisms you'll ever host, happening across the surface of your palms.

The Smile Cascade
Forced smiling triggers the facial feedback loop: zygomatic major pulls → cranial nerve VII fires → brain interprets as joy → releases dopamine → genuine feeling emerges. The journey from fake to real takes approximately 4 minutes of sustained smiling.

The Memory Muscle
When savoring, your hippocampus performs "pattern completion"—filling in forgotten details with probable data. Each retelling literally rebuilds the memory, making it more vivid than original. You're not remembering joy; you're manufacturing it fresh using memory as template.

The revolution: Joy doesn't require perfect posture, sunny days, or genuine smiles. It requires curious attention to whatever is: the slump, the gray, the soil, the forced face, the flexible timeline of savored moments.

Lyon becomes the city where people travel to learn productive slumping, to celebrate gray days, to get dirty on purpose, to smile with baguettes, to time-travel through joy.
