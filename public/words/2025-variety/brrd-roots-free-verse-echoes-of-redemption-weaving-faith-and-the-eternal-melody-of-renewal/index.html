Echoes of Redemption: Weaving Faith

Words / 2025 Variety

Brrd Roots ~ Free-Verse
Echoes of Redemption: Weaving Faith
and the Eternal Melody of Renewal

The horizon
was no longer infinite—
the sky, once a cradle for dreams,
sagged beneath a haze of particles,
humming, hanging, heavy—
plastic dust, they called it.

To Leah, it wasn’t just pollution;
it was an elegy, an echo,
a mourning stretched across the skyline.
It lingered, low and long,
a shadow of the choices—
hers, theirs—
made in the rush to build
what could never last.

Her grandmother used to say:
**Faith is like a thread—thin, but unbreakable,
steadying even the most frayed of souls.**
Leah clutched the beads,
cool, smooth,
worn round by hands before hers.

“How,” she whispered, “do I find reverence in ruins?
How do I forgive myself
for pulling at the seams—
for all I unraveled,
all I let unravel?”

The Notes Beneath the Noise

In her studio,
cracked light spilling across clutter,
Leah played.

Her guitar hummed, low and loud,
her fingers leaning into strings stretched thin.
She didn’t play to soothe;
she played to provoke—
sharp, raw, restless.
The chords crashed,
the echoes answered.

“Brrd Roots,” they had called themselves once.
Leah whispered the name now,
a hollow hum in her throat,
a memory soft at its edges.
Hers was the only harmony left,
each note an apology for all she had left unsaid.

She thought of the nights she stayed silent,
the words she swallowed.
She thought of the friends she’d lost,
her voice too loud or too soft—
always the wrong tone,
always afraid to say,
“I’m scared. Don’t go.”

The melodies jagged, jumped,
rising, roaring—
her mistakes strung together like broken pearls.
Sharp, sharp, then softer,
each note holding a memory:
the harsh words, the silences,
the pride that built walls,
her truth trapped on the other side.

Her grandmother’s voice lingered,
long and low, steady:
**“Carelessness is a weight
we leave for others to carry.
But care—however small, however slow—
can sew what feels beyond repair.”**

Her fingers pressed, pulled,
the strings bending, aching.
"I failed before," she thought.
"But I will try. I will try again."

Reimagining Ruins

Leah’s apartment glowed faintly,
a sanctuary of second chances.

A lamp rewired burned warm,
its cracks traced with gold—
imperfection turned art.

Dust settled on letters,
folded, forgotten—
words she wrote but never sent.
Letters to the ones she’d hurt,
to the parts of herself she couldn’t yet love.

“Could I,” she whispered, “remake myself—
could I rebuild the pieces—
without carrying all that broke me?”

One evening,
a bulb flickered alive beneath her hands.
Copper twisted, light spilled gold and soft,
a hesitant warmth filling the room.

She felt her grandmother’s presence—
gentle, sure.
Her tears fell, slow, silent.

"Every act of creation is sacred," Leah whispered.
"Even if it begins in brokenness,
even if it trembles at its start."

And there, in the light,
she thought of mercy:
for the hands that fumbled,
for the hesitation that held her still,
for the years she lost
to silence and to fear.

The Whisper of Plastics

The rooftop stretched wide—
a space for her and the world’s truths.

Below, the river moved sluggish,
its surface slick with sheen,
a mosaic of discarded fragments.

She pressed her palms flat
against concrete,
cool, cracked, unyielding.

Her own failings whispered back:
the times she had turned her gaze,
the times she thought,
"I’m too small,
too quiet,
too late to make a difference."

Her prayer rose—
soft, slow, steady:
“Let me not add to this weight.
Let me carry it lightly,
with love,
with forgiveness—
for the earth,
for the sky,
for myself.”

And in the stillness,
something inside her softened.
**Even this, even this
deserves compassion.**

---

A Song for the Shattered

Leah’s melodies grew darker,
fractured like the bonds
she longed to mend.

She thought of the friend she’d hurt—
a rainy night, a fight,
words that cut through the storm.

“I was wrong,” she had known,
but her pride weighed heavier
than her longing to make amends.

Her songs leaned into the memory.
The rain rose around her,
the rhythm sharp, rising:
the missed apologies,
the words that stayed tangled in her throat.

Each chord carried a question:
**Can we heal what we’ve broken?
Can we stitch what we’ve torn apart?
Can we step back into the spaces
where we once left shadows?**

Her grandmother’s thread returned,
steady, slow.
Faith wasn’t certainty—
it was the willingness
to hold broken melodies
and let them hum.

---

A Dawn in Dust

One evening,
the sun slipped low.

Leah held her guitar in quiet hands,
her fingers poised on fragile strings.
She began to play—
low and long, soft and steady,
a thread weaving through dust
and into still air.

The notes swayed,
like rivers she dreamed clean again,
like skies she dreamed clear again,
like hands she dreamed open again.

She let the music hold mercy,
gentle as the breeze:
mercy for the hands that broke
and the hearts that mended,
for the weight carried by soil,
for herself—
for the ways she had failed,
and the ways she chose to begin.

Her grandmother’s voice drifted back:
**“Every act of faith
is a thread in the fabric of eternity.”**

Leah could not undo the past.
But her songs
could hum with hope—
fragile, persistent,
a rhythm asking others to reflect,
to listen,
to act.

She played until the stars rose,
each note trembling,
but true.

And in the tremble,
there was mercy—
wide, soft, deep, full.

For the earth.
For others.
For herself.

**To begin again,
and again,
and again.**
