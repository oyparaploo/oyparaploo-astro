Warm Echoes in a Shadow Cycle

← Words / 2025 Variety

The surface does not simply exist—it remembers. It is a breath held in color, a texture pressed into thought, waiting to be noticed. Pink, fine-grained, almost warm, but not quite—more like something that lingers, something felt before it is understood.

At its center, symmetry hums—not in stark precision, but in quiet inevitability. The grey-toned mandala, the soft echo of a forgotten pattern, the shape of something trying to recall itself. A design, a cycle, a pulse beneath stillness. It does not ask to be recognized; it simply persists.

Framing it, shadows stretch—not rigid, not fixed, but fluid, shifting between presence and uncertainty. Deep maroon, elongated—two mirrored figures that pretend to be bottles, yet fail to hold form. They rest in repetition, their edges blurred, refusing clarity.

Not just what is there, but what is becoming. Not just shadows, but the memory of movement. Not just the surface, but the pause between surfaces.

And then, the fracture.

The otherworldly shadow of a bicyclist—leaning forward, caught in motion where motion should not exist. Its shape bends through stillness, tracing the edge of absence, slipping between symmetry without shattering it.

It does not belong to the pink. It does not belong to the bottles-that-are-not-bottles. It belongs to something deeper, something unseen, something unfolding.

The sky does not shift, yet time does. The wind does not stir, yet voices travel. The silence does not hold, yet the echoes remain.

Knows that everything here is a thought caught between the layers of forgetting. Knows that the pink surface is not color, but an idea pressed against the edge of awareness. Knows that the shadows do not belong to bottles. They belong to something older.

The bicyclist moves, but does not depart. The symmetry hums, but does not explain. The air breathes, but does not speak.

Pink-red surface, textured and granular, stretching like a thought unspoken. Not quite sand, not quite fabric—a quiet tension, a canvas for absence.

At its center, symmetry breathes. A grey-toned abstraction, delicate yet deliberate. A flower, perhaps. A mandala, maybe. A pattern that does not explain itself but continues, repeats.

Framing it, shadows—deep maroon, almost burgundy—mirroring each other without hesitation, elongated, uncertain. Two reflections that pretend to be bottles lying on their sides, yet refuse to sharpen. Their edges blur, details indistinct but deliberate. They belong and yet they do not.

The atmosphere holds its breath. Quiet, contemplative, just on the edge of unsettling—a balance between warmth and weight, between contrast and repetition.

And then—rupture.

Sees what should not be there. Sees what should be still. Sees a break in the rhythm.

An otherworldly shadow—a bicyclist.

It does not exist within the symmetry. It is not maroon, not pink, not a reflection cast upon the surface. It moves, where nothing else moves. It bends without breaking. It slips between stillness and motion, shifting where repetition should hold.

It does not belong, but it does not leave. It waits. It watches. It breathes where shadows do not.

Not all shadows are meant to stay still. Some wait to be seen.

The surface breathes—pinkish-red, textured, granular—a quiet tension stretched beneath stillness. Not sand, yet familiar. Not fabric, yet soft. It holds space without claiming it.

At its center, symmetry pulses—a grey-toned mandala, a stylized flower, an echo without sound. A design that does not explain itself, only reveals, only repeats.

Framing it, shadows linger—deep maroon, stretched twin reflections. Bottles lying on their sides, or the memory of bottles. Shapes with blurred edges, uncertain yet deliberate. They settle into repetition, existing without being questioned.

And then, the shift.

Sees past symmetry. Sees past rhythm. Sees something that does not belong, yet cannot be ignored.

An otherworldly shadow—a bicyclist.

It bends where others remain rigid. It moves through stillness. It carries motion where motion should not exist.

It does not belong, but it does not leave.

The pink surface does not move. The maroon reflections do not change. The symmetry does not break.

Yet something is different. Something is becoming.

Some shadows only exist to shift the pattern.

Flesh Bound Manuscript and More

Divine Sense and Being Human

Snow White’s themes of duality, reflection, and identity fit beautifully within the surrealism of this image. Ultimately, this piece speaks to the way blood defines us—it rises in response to the body’s signals, painting emotion onto the skin, reminding us of life coursing just beneath the surface. Blood circulation plays a huge role in determining the texture, tone, and overall appearance of the skin. Essentially, blood shapes how skin feels, reacts, and heals—it’s the life force beneath the surface! This structure emphasizes a mirrored interplay between the skin and blood, highlighting the subtle balance between presence and absence, reaction and recovery.

The body is, at its core, an ancient manuscript—but not just any manuscript. No, this one was meticulously crafted by celestial scribes with a flair for drama, a questionable understanding of anatomy, and the occasional inclination to doodle inexplicable jousting snails in the margins. Blood, the eternal ink, scrawls its mysterious verses beneath the skin, sometimes in elegant calligraphy, sometimes in frantic scribbles resembling the cryptic warnings of a sleep-deprived monk who just spilled ink all over his robe.

Circulation dictates the body’s aesthetic in much the same way that medieval illustrators decided which colors deserved a generous slathering of gold leaf. A rush of excitement? The vibrant crimson hues of illuminated letters, radiating importance. A sudden wave of embarrassment? That’s the blotched pink of a miscalculated pigment mixture, hastily smudged by an apprentice who absolutely did not follow instructions. Cold hands? The slow fading of ink on aging parchment, betraying centuries of neglect.

Yet the body, like its manuscript counterpart, isn’t merely functional—it thrives in absolute nonsense. Take, for instance, the inexplicable way blood will sometimes pool into a bruise that looks suspiciously like a medieval battle scene. Or the way goosebumps materialize as if the body were attempting to add texture to its surface for dramatic effect. And don't even get me started on the phenomenon of scars—the skin's equivalent of “editor’s notes,” permanently scribbled into the margins by time itself.

And then there’s industry and conflict—two forces that have undoubtedly found their way into both the human body and the illuminated text. The exhausted medieval scribe, fingers stained with ink, carrying the weight of scripture in every stroke—his agony mirrored in the modern factory worker whose hands bear the calloused evidence of labor. The battlefield chronicler, breathless as he records bloodshed in rich reds and sorrowful blues—echoing the body, where the memory of conflict resurfaces in phantom pains and unexpected tremors.

Even illuminated texts, for all their divine wisdom, were prone to sheer ridiculousness—marginalia filled with knights fighting giant snails, rabbits brandishing swords, and the occasional wide-eyed bird that looks entirely too concerned about what’s happening in the next paragraph.

The body plays by the same rules. It blushes when it absolutely should not. It twitches just as you're falling asleep, as if some trickster monk in the celestial scriptorium decided to add "sudden jolt" to the margins of your existence. It betrays you at the worst moments, like when blood drains from your face during an important presentation—turning you into the human equivalent of faded ink on an aging scroll.

Yet, despite all the absurdity, both the body and the manuscript persist. The ink may fade, the parchment may crack, the skin may wrinkle—but the stories remain. The blood beneath the surface and the pigments upon the page continue their silent work, their chaotic, beautiful dance between time and memory.

In the grand library of existence, the body is but one illuminated text—equal parts divine poetry and inexplicable doodles of sword-wielding rabbits. And that’s exactly how it should be.

Bloom of the Mirrored Infant And Circle of Mercy

In the heart of an endless garden—where light drapes itself over leaves with a kindness beyond words—the mirrored child stands, no longer beseeching, no longer bound to ancient echoes. Here, beneath the arching mercy of olive branches, the kneeling infant does not pray for revelation but offers it, hands open in quiet gift.

The weathered clay of the figure softens under the sun’s embrace, no longer a relic of time’s erosion but a vessel of forgiveness. The concentric circles upon its chest do not whisper secrets but pulse with life, carrying the rhythm of gentle renewal. The crimson leaf at its core is no ember of prophecy—no omen—but the first breath of spring, the delicate seed of hope.

Lambs rest peacefully at the statue’s base, unburdened by notions of sacrifice. They lie curled in the shade of towering blooms, their wool kissed by petals of gold. The hieroglyphs, once fragments of forgotten tongues, now form patterns upon the wind, woven into the laughter of those who walk beneath the boughs. The sky sways between hues—deep purples melting into blue, fading to soft sunrise gold. And from the earth, kindness grows. Mercy is not written in scripture or carved into stone. It is in the way the air hums. In the way the trees do not clutch but offer. In the way even the mirrored child, once frozen in silent petition, now stands not to ask, but to give. And those who step into the garden do not seek answers.

The mirrored child stands at the heart of the Garden of Mercy, its once-weathered form softened by time’s embrace. No longer a relic of longing, it has become a beacon of quiet grace, arms extended not in supplication but in welcome. The concentric circles upon its chest pulse with life, shifting in deep blues and blacks like the rhythm of renewal itself. At its center, the crimson leaf is not an ember of prophecy, but the tender first breath of change, the seed of hope nestled within the fabric of the world.

Olive branches weave through the air, not as symbols of surrender but as offerings of peace—unbidden, freely given. Beneath them, lambs rest, their wool kissed by towering blooms. They do not carry the weight of sacrifice; they exist in pure gentleness, a reminder that innocence does not have to be lost. The hieroglyphic fragments, once the remnants of cryptic wisdom, now swirl in the breeze like whispers of quiet understanding—no longer demanding translation, but flowing effortlessly into laughter.

Above, the sky shifts in hues—deep purples melting into blue, fading into golden dawn. This is the rhythm of mercy, the transition from uncertainty into light, from seeking into finding. And in this garden, truth is no longer something that must be unearthed through struggle. It lingers in the quiet hum of life, in the way the air carries kindness without condition, in the way even the mirrored child stands, not to ask but to give.
Here, revelation is not found in distant prophecy. It is simply felt, in the hush of growing things, in the stillness of understanding.

The Garden of Mercy embodies the essence of mercy not through grand gestures or spoken declarations, but in the way it quietly gives without demand. Everything within it flourishes because it is allowed to exist without judgment, without conditions—mercy here is not transactional, but inherent.

The mirrored child, once a seeker of truth, now stands as a reflection of grace. It does not ask, it does not plead—it simply welcomes, offering rather than requesting. Its outstretched arms are an invitation rather than an appeal, reminding us that mercy is an open door, not a test to be passed.

The lambs rest, unburdened by sacrifice, untouched by suffering. They are no longer symbols of loss but of peace without cost, reflecting mercy in its purest form: protection without expectation.

The olive branches, rooted deep, do not clutch at those who pass—they extend, swaying gently as if whispering, take what you need. Their presence reminds us that true mercy does not force itself upon others; it simply exists, ready to be accepted.

Even the hieroglyphs, once cryptic remnants of wisdom, now move freely upon the wind—no longer hidden knowledge but gentle understanding, carried effortlessly. Mercy, in this garden, is not something earned. It is woven into the very air, present without question.

And above it all, the shifting sky reflects the journey from uncertainty to light, the silent transition between longing and fulfillment. There is no moment of revelation, no grand unveiling—only the quiet truth that mercy is not an answer to be found, but a presence to be felt.

This garden does not teach mercy; it is mercy. It offers without demand, embraces without condition, exists without expectation. And that, perhaps, is the deepest kindness of all.

Sanctuary of whispers.

Beneath the hush of ancient olive trees, where the wind carries words softer than breath, stands the sanctuary—a place where mercy is not given, nor taken, but exchanged like quiet conversation. The mirrored figure rests at its heart, arms outstretched, not in offering but in recognition. No supplication, no plea—only the gentle understanding that all who enter have carried burdens, and all will leave lighter.

The concentric circles upon its chest no longer pulse with cosmic uncertainty but with the rhythm of voices past. Every word ever spoken in kindness lingers here, pressed into the air like ink upon parchment. The crimson leaf, settled at its center, is not a prophecy nor a beacon—it is a single moment of forgiveness, the turning of a page that might never have been turned.

The lambs, once symbols of sacrifice, have become listeners. They settle in the shade of towering blooms, their presence a reminder that innocence does not need to be offered, only acknowledged. The hieroglyphic fragments drift through the sanctuary, shifting midair—no longer sealed secrets, but murmured affirmations, shaping themselves into the voices of those who came before.

And within this space, mercy is not a force handed down by distant gods—it is the quiet recognition that every soul, in its own way, has faltered. It does not ask, it does not judge. It only welcomes.

The sky remains fluid, shifting between hues of deep violet and dawn-lit gold, carrying the sanctuary’s promise: no voice is lost, no kindness forgotten, no presence unseen.

And those who stand within its embrace will find that the whispers do not fade when they depart. They carry them forward, woven into their own words, their own acts, their own quiet mercies yet to be given.

Certainly, Brian. Indigenous circle stories reflect cycles—of life, nature, renewal—where wisdom passes through generations and teachings flow like seasons. I’ll shape this narrative into a living cycle, where the journey of mercy is not linear but woven into the land, its presence felt in every breath, every offering, every return.

The garden has no beginning, no end. It is a place where the wind carries the echoes of those who walked before, their laughter pressed into the bark of ancient olive trees, their whispers lingering in the hush between leaves.

At its heart stands the mirrored child, arms open—not to grasp, but to release. Time has softened its form, the clay no longer brittle but warmed by the sun’s embrace. Within its chest, the concentric circles pulse—not in demand but in rhythm, like ripples upon water, carrying stories instead of prophecy. The crimson leaf resting at its center is the seed of change, waiting, breathing, knowing it will bloom when the earth is ready.

Lambs rest beneath the shade of towering blooms, their wool kissed by petals of gold. They do not carry sacrifice, nor do they bear burden; they only listen, watching kindness unfold in the quiet spaces between movement. The hieroglyphs do not seal knowledge away—they drift freely, carried upon the wind, forming patterns in the laughter of children, shaping themselves in the wisdom of the elders who sit beneath the trees.

And above, the sky shifts. The purple hues of uncertainty fold into dawn’s golden light, revealing nothing—and everything. The circle turns, and mercy lingers—not to be claimed, nor earned, but felt in the way the earth breathes, in the way the trees do not clutch but offer, in the way even the mirrored child, once frozen in silent longing, now stands—not to ask, but to give.

No one leaves the garden untouched. And no one enters it alone. Mercy is not a lesson taught. It is a cycle lived, carried forward, shaped in footsteps and echoes, passed from one hand to another—until even the wind knows its name.

And so the circle continues.

Endless Conveyor of New Things Opera

ACT I: River of Impulse Thoughts

Scene opens in a dreamlike marketplace, handwritten words float like lanterns in the air. The hum of a high-fast-stirring-river carries non-decomposing rubbish through the sky. Just as the audience settles into the fluid choreography, the lanterns begin to fall—suddenly, dramatically—transforming into fluttering pages raining down from above.

Chorus sings:
"Abandonment of origins—ghosts in ink, fading strokes of lost messages,
Anthropometries stretch beneath a breath form above, shaping absence into form,
Dancing in the street—limbs bending with time, a silent choreography of memory,
Night stand and bicycle—wheels turning in the underground building, blind mountain rising…
Blood blossoms, spilling into board rooms, blooming poetic nausea,
Things bought on the spot—impulse collisions, letters folded in half,
Precious objects hitting the floor, shattering into meaning!"

As the final note rings out, one performer unexpectedly steps forward, holds out a blank scroll, then unfolds it—revealing an ink-drawn map of a lost city on their back.

ACT II: Collision Course in the House of Accordion Vehicles

The stage transforms into an industrial dreamscape—tables scatter across boardrooms, reels of film cut apart, pictures placed on walls like unfinished thoughts. Just as silence sets in, an enormous film reel unspools from the ceiling, cascading downward, enveloping half the stage.

Soloist sings:
"Pet projects thrown through large windows, break-a-brick! Burn after Brooklyn!
White winter bus shelters—John Cage watches the intersection crash in silence…
Casual candy-ass computer coding, the hum of neon against cell chamber couples,
Chemistry class dissolving into static—the love corridor stretches into wonderful nothingness!"

The film begins to glow—revealing fragmented silhouettes moving inside it, ghost-like figures trapped within the reel, flickering between presence and disappearance.

ACT III: Reptilian Explanation of Sunlight

Golden rays flicker onstage, shifting abruptly to cold metallic light. Eroded elevators rumble ominously, then—without warning—a trapdoor opens and a single performer descends out of sight before reappearing in an unexpected place above the stage.

Monks chant:
"Facts… Art… More facts… the weight of history bending beneath thick ink,
Fat hitting below the belt, stitching scars into the unseen,
High fashion—floating field studies painted in movement,
Food for thought fragments—heavenly honey dripping into dark chocolate!
Fridge with forgotten vegetables—garlic resting beside memories,
Gas prices—ghosts trapped in bottles, spirits leaking through glass…
Grabbing grief, swallowing loss, misery loves company,
Plugging holes in silence, keeping our heads above the slow flood…"

Suddenly, a hidden floor panel slides open—revealing a single, illuminated dinner table in the center of the stage, where statues of past guests sit frozen in mid-conversation.

ACT IV: Universal Dance of Different Cultures

Dancers enter carrying Hay Head Spook Show banners, spinning in rhythmic unity across the Hill Horn Hotel courtyard. Then—unexpectedly—the banners burst into flames, revealing entirely different symbols beneath their surfaces.

Lead singer calls:
"Turn right, take the heart highway, follow the pulse—
Swim with jaguar joint ventures, dive into moving stories!
The biggest independent knot laundry ever made—labyrinth consciousness,
Lightning lines of fire—search for a neighbor’s lost loved one,
If it should happen to occur, walk on eggs, frozen microwaves,
Stuffed animal missiles—soft devastation in the stillness!"

The audience expects a resolution—but instead, a performer interrupts the lead singer mid-note, holding out a scroll of paper. The singer hesitates, unfolds it—revealing new, unknown lyrics, rewritten just moments before.

ACT V: Temples of Networking

The final act unfolds in celestial vaults, memories surfacing—how the wedding cake was, how it crumbled, how it remained. But then—unexpected thunder shakes the room—the vault shatters into a cascade of mirrors, revealing a second hidden world beneath the first one.

Final soloist sings:
"Week of illusions popping up—unfolding, dissolving, shifting,
Taking precedence—terror wild cards drifting into the unseen!
Freedom spills across yards of words translated in light,
Plowed open ground—situations bending with each passing day,
Earth’s spatial splash—patched concrete steps leading to bullet trains…
Storage—stones—symbols—temples for networking,
Transitions theater—shapes forming where language once stood,
A thousand black umbrellas rise—creating a soft great wall,
Transparent politicians moving through fog—words without roots…"

Suddenly, a hidden door in the back of the stage swings open, revealing a procession of tricycles, their wheels wrapped in ancient scrolls. As they slowly glide forward, ink begins to drip from their wheels, forming words on the floor of the stage—messages unseen until now.

The final chant rises into the sky—carrying voices, carrying loss, carrying transformation. The opera ends not with a conclusion, but with a lingering question, a door that remains slightly ajar, an invitation for something beyond what was expected.
