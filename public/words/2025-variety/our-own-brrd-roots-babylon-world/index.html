Our Own Brrd Roots Babylon World

Words / 2025 Variety

Something is spellbinding about holding opposing forces in balance—the strength to liberate paired with the chaos it often brings. Imagine Babylon, fierce and incandescent, her scarlet robes catching the wind like flames breaking free from earthly bounds. The Holy Grail burns bright in her hand, not as a beacon of dominance but as a fragile flame that reminds us: that even strength needs care. She pauses, her gaze anchored on the horizon, where choices dissolve into mist. Does she wonder, even for a moment, about the paths she didn’t take? The weight of divinity clings to her, not as a burden, but as a silent echo of responsibility—a reminder that carrying the universe’s expectations is never light.

You can almost see her after a storm, drenched, her beast resting beside her, its scales dulled by the rain’s persistence. A villager steps forward hesitantly, bearing a simple chalice filled not with divine fire, but plain, humble water. Babylon takes it—her fingers aflame, her essence radiant—and drinks. For a heartbeat, divinity shifts. It feels human. The weight she carries lightens, just enough for her to breathe. In that moment, she discovers something profound: even goddesses need the grounding touch of kindness, the quiet gift of connection.

But Babylon’s world isn’t straightforward. Chaos, her mischievous shadow, whispers in her ear, teasing, taunting: “What direction today, Babylon? Which path will resist you the most?” She grips her reins tightly, aware that no path truly belongs to her. They are shaped by the cries of the earth, the subtle pull of destiny’s threads. Sometimes she envies mortals—the simplicity of their choices, the luxury of imperfection. Yet she steps forward anyway, not because it’s effortless, but because the paradox of creation and liberation is her essence. Her steps carry resilience born from hesitation and courage entwined.

Babylon reminds us of something deeply human: strength isn’t flawless—it’s found in the cracks. The places where doubt seeps in, where uncertainty lingers, and yet, where forward motion prevails. She is luminous not because she never falters, but because she chooses to rise, again and again, despite the shadows. Her flame burns not for invincibility, but for persistence—for the audacity to keep creating in the chaos.

What can Babylon teach us in our mortal lives? That perhaps strength isn’t loud or ostentatious—it’s found in quiet moments when we choose to hold the wild and the steady in our grasp and trust the balance. Life doesn’t ask us to be perfect; it asks us to keep showing up. Even if we falter, even if our hands shake under the weight, we press onward—because carrying both liberation and chaos isn’t weakness. It’s a testament to our humanity, to our ability to thrive in contradiction.

So the next time you feel caught between creation and chaos, look inward. Celebrate the messy moments, the hesitations that turned into leaps. Hold your flame carefully, like Babylon, letting it guide you without the need for perfection. And when kindness comes—humble, unremarkable—let it lighten the load, grounding you in the beauty of connection. Maybe liberation isn’t about soaring; it’s about standing firm, with all your cracks and doubts, and knowing that resilience is the truest strength of all.
