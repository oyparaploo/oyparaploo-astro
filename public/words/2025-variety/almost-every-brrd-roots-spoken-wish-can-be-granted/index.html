Almost Every Brrd Roots Spoken Wish Can Be Granted

Words / 2025 Variety

Kindness is strange, isn’t it? It’s one of those things we all admire in theory but often find impossibly inconvenient in practice—like flossing daily or resisting the urge to text “just checking in” after being ghosted. It’s easy to be kind when life feels like a rom-com montage: sunshine, music, laughter, perfect angles. But kindness asks its real questions when the world gets messy, when someone cuts in line and then *glares* at you as though you’re the offender, or when that one coworker (you know the one) makes it their mission to “play devil’s advocate” for your every suggestion. In these moments, kindness isn’t just an afterthought—it’s a choice. And it’s not an easy one.

Take the cutting-in-line scenario. You’ve got one hand on your grocery cart, your patience hanging by a thread, and then—swoosh—they slide in as if it were their divine right to stand in front of you. It’s infuriating, and let’s be honest, fantasizing about a witty comeback can feel cathartic. But what if, instead, you took a breath (even though you’d rather roll your eyes into another dimension)? What if, for a moment, you thought about the possibility that they’re rushing to get home, maybe to take care of someone who needs them? Letting them stand in front of you won’t heal the injustice of the world, but it might soften the knot in your chest. Not because you’re the “bigger person,” but because you’re no longer carrying the weight of your own irritation.

Kindness doesn’t mean ignoring hurt or pretending everyone’s behavior is justified. It’s not about excusing people; it’s about releasing yourself. Carrying resentment is like dragging around a suitcase full of rocks, hoping the extra baggage will somehow bother someone else. (Spoiler: it won’t.) Letting go—wishing others well, even begrudgingly—isn’t about them deserving it; it’s about reclaiming your own peace. And honestly, nothing feels lighter than setting down what you were never meant to hold.

Picture this: You’re at work, pitching an idea you’ve spent the entire weekend perfecting. And there it is—that dreaded critique from your least favorite coworker, delivered with the kind of enthusiasm that only comes from wanting to see someone squirm. It stings. Your mind starts spiraling with the perfect one-liners you’ll never say out loud. But then, instead of fueling that fire, you remind yourself: their negativity says more about their struggle than your worth. And suddenly, you don’t need revenge or a rebuttal. You offer them the mental equivalent of a shrug and think, “I hope whatever they’re dealing with gets easier.” It’s not saintly; it’s survival. Because the truth is, carrying grudges is exhausting, and your peace is too valuable to sacrifice on the altar of bitterness.

And can we talk about kindness with a wink of humor? It doesn’t have to feel heavy or self-sacrificial. Sometimes, it’s playfully cursing the driver who cut you off and then saying, “Fine, I hope you make it to wherever you’re going in one piece.” Sometimes, it’s laughing with a friend about your shared nemesis while secretly wishing that nemesis finds some joy (and maybe a new job far, far away). Kindness doesn’t have to be perfect to be real.

At its core, kindness is vulnerability—a soft, steady courage that asks you to see the humanity in others, even when it’s hard. It’s the practice of untangling yourself from judgment, of saying, “I see you, and I hope you find what you need.” It doesn’t mean tolerating harm or pretending everything’s okay. It means standing tall enough in your own worth that you don’t have to weigh yourself down with resentment or anger. And in choosing kindness, you give yourself the gift of lightness.

So take this with you: Kindness isn’t always intuitive, and it’s rarely convenient. But it’s liberating. Wishing others well isn’t about them—it’s about you, about the strength it takes to rise above the smallness of negativity. It’s about creating space within yourself for compassion to flourish, even when the world feels sharp and unkind. In that space, you’ll find something radiant—not perfection, but freedom. And that freedom is where grace begins. Let that be your superpower. Let it be what carries you forward.
