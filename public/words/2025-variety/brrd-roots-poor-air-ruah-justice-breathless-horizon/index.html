Poor Air (2)

Ruah Justice ~ Breathless Horizon

← Words / 2025 Variety

LOOP(S)

Jonas had once trusted the sky. It had always been there, stretched wide, endless and unshaken. As a child, he ran beneath it, arms out, lungs open, believing in breath the way one believes in morning—without question, without hesitation.

But now, the air pressed differently. It did not cradle. It did not hum. It folded inward, thick and knowing, curling through the city like something forgotten but never gone. Jonas felt it settle against his ribs, his lungs resisting. Beside him, Lara stood steady, tracking numbers on the screen strapped to her wrist.

“We don’t let them take the air,” she murmured.

Jonas exhaled.

The weight inside him did not move.

The city was forgetting how to breathe.

At first, it was subtle—the way conversations thinned, the way steps dragged. People searched for words that slipped just beyond reach. Memory wavered like mist, soft at the edges, disappearing before anyone could grasp its shape.

Jonas felt it happening inside himself too. His pulse climbed higher without cause. His hands, once steady, trembled beneath a quiet rebellion he had not agreed to. And Lara—sharp, logical, relentless—felt it too.

She would never say it outright.

She would only grip reports tighter, press her lips together, scan readings with a gaze sharper than it should be.

They were unraveling.

All of them.

-One morning, Jonas stepped outside.

The city was hushed, wrapped in smog, stretching into dawn without urgency. He lifted his gaze, staring past the thickened sky, past the poisoned air, searching for something beyond the weight pressing into his ribs.

He remembered a verse—not in full, not clearly, but enough.

"Then the Lord God formed the man from the dust of the ground and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life, and the man became a living being."

Breath was never meant to be stolen.

Breath was sacred.

Jonas closed his eyes, inhaled deep despite the toxins still thick in the air, and let the words settle into him.

“Jesus, lead us.”

The air did not change.

But something inside Jonas did.

Lara was waiting when he returned.

She did not ask where he had been.

She saw him.

And that was enough.

The resistance swelled, pushing against silence, against suffocation. The movement carried scripture on its banners, voices rising in prayer before rising in action.

Breath was not just survival.

It was God’s gift.

It was justice.

Jonas stood beside Lara on the courthouse steps, reports pressed tight against his palm, the truth sharpened into something undeniable.

“It’s not just pollution,” he said.

“It’s biology.”

“It’s survival.”

“It’s a war happening inside us while no one was paying attention.”

The lawsuits moved forward. The fight did not end. But the world finally listened.

The air remained heavy. The road remained long.

But they were not walking it alone.

Jonas inhaled, slow and measured, stepping forward into something he had once feared, into something he now carried with certainty.

The heavens could still breathe.

And so could they.
