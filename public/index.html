Story · 10 minutes

Sandhill Crane Was Never Supposed to Be Here

The sandhill crane lands in my father's hospital parking lot at 6:47 AM, December 3rd—two thousand miles from where it should be, three months past migration. I know because I'm watching from the oncology wing's seventh floor, where Lou has been dying for eleven days, transforming the word wisdom into something I no longer recognize.

My credentials for telling this: I've spent twenty-three years translating my father's Hmong proverbs into psychiatric terminology for doctors who think metaphor is a symptom. I hold a PhD in ornithology that I earned trying to understand why he called me noog—bird—when he meant daughter.
