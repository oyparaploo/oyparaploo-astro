Inspirations and Influences

Loves

The diversity of details, the overall wide array of possibilities, and then when you get your nose close up, the fascination with how a surface or an object conveys a different concept for sensing something.

Work by other artists. None of it was made here. Every picture carries its maker's name, and every name opens the place where their work lives.

If your work is here and you would like it taken down, write to us and it comes down the same day.

Moshekwa Langa

Drawings, paintings and installations · Amsterdam

Moshekwa Langa was born in 1975 in Bakenberg, South Africa, and works in Amsterdam. He makes maps out of whatever comes to hand: spools of cotton, toy cars, bottles, street maps, bubble wrap. From across the room a floor of his tangled thread looks like a mess. Get closer and it turns into roads, borders and the long way home.

Dineo Seshee Bopape

Installations of soil, clay and gold · Johannesburg

Dineo Seshee Bopape, born in 1981 in Polokwane, South Africa, builds whole rooms out of earth. Her materials are soil, clay, healing herbs and gold leaf, and some of her clay pieces are shaped by a clenched fist, so each one keeps the print of a hand. Step close and the packed ground starts to read like a memory of land: who worked it, and what it still holds.

Marshmallow Laser Feast

Immersive installations, virtual reality and film · London

Marshmallow Laser Feast is a group of artists in London: Ersin Han Ersin, Barnaby Steel and Robin McNicholas. They scan real trees and forests with lasers and medical scanners, then turn what they find into places you can walk into. Put on one of their headsets and a tree becomes a slow river of light, or a wood looks the way it might to a dragonfly or an owl. The forest was always this busy. They let you notice.

Sethembile Msezane

Performance, photography and sculpture · Cape Town

Sethembile Msezane, born in 1991 in KwaZulu-Natal, South Africa, asks why so few public statues are of Black women, and then becomes one. She stands for hours on a plinth, as still as bronze. In 2015 she stood for nearly four hours at the University of Cape Town while the statue of Cecil Rhodes behind her was lifted away by a crane. Up close, the stillness is the work: a body holding its place while history moves.

Sir Francis Rose

Painting, drawing and stage design · England, 1909–1979

Sir Francis Rose was an English painter, draughtsman and stage designer who went to Paris as a young man to study with Francis Picabia. Gertrude Stein bought his paintings and championed him, and he designed scenery and costumes for Diaghilev's ballet. This drawing of Venice, dated 1951, is all quick red line: boats, rooftops and a tall bell tower, set down as if the whole city were floating on the paper.

Andy Warhol

Painting, prints, drawing and film · New York, 1928–1987

Before the soup cans, Andy Warhol was one of the busiest commercial illustrators in New York, drawing for Tiffany & Co. and The New York Times. In 1954 he made a small book of cats, printed and then coloured by hand. From a distance it is a quick, almost careless line. Close up, the green wash pooling in the fur and the purple eyes show how much life one loose line can carry.

Gary Hume

Paintings in household gloss · London and New York

Gary Hume, born in 1962 in Kent, England, paints with household gloss, the paint made for doors, often on sheets of aluminium. From across the room his pictures are flat, simple shapes: a mother, a child, a face. Close up, the gloss is so smooth it turns the painting into a mirror, and you find the room, and yourself, inside it.

Christopher Wool

Painting, printing and photography · New York

Christopher Wool, born in 1955 in Boston, has worked in New York since the 1970s, often in enamel on aluminium or linen. He lets one mark sit over another: a line is painted, wiped back, printed and painted again. From a distance it reads as one loose tangle. Close up you can read the order it was made in, which loop came first, what was rubbed away, and what was allowed to stay.

Daniel Boyd

Painting · Sydney

Daniel Boyd, born in 1982 in Gimuy (Cairns), Queensland, is a painter of Aboriginal and ni-Vanuatu heritage who lives in Sydney. He covers his paintings with thousands of small clear dots he calls lenses, so every picture is seen through a screen of points, like light through leaves or water. Step back and a figure appears; step close and it falls apart into gaps. His paintings take the colonial history of Australia and look at it again from the side of the people who were already there.

Rose Wylie

Painting · Kent, England

Rose Wylie, born in 1934, paints large, bright, loose pictures in oil in Kent, England. From across the room they look fast and childlike: three faces in sailor collars, a row of palm trees. Close up you see how much was worked out, the paint scraped, covered and laid down again until the simple thing looks simple.

Cecilia Vicuña

Poetry, sculpture and thread · Santiago and New York

Cecilia Vicuña, born in 1948 in Santiago, Chile, is a poet and an artist who works in New York and Santiago. She calls her small sculptures precarios, precarious things, made from what washes up or falls: feathers, sticks, shells, scraps of cloth and wire. Her hanging threads follow the quipu, the knotted cords the ancient Andes used to keep records. Walk among them and the room becomes a poem you read with your whole body, one knot and one twig at a time.

Isa Genzken

Sculpture · Berlin

Isa Genzken, born in 1948 in Germany, has made sculpture in Berlin for decades, from steel, plaster, wood, mirror foil, photographs and whatever the city throws away. Her early floor pieces are long painted wooden forms that touch the ground at only one or two points. Since 2013 she has dressed shop mannequins in clothes, found things and odd masks, and called them Actors. Each one stands like someone you half know, caught between a costume and a person.

Françoiz Breut

Songs and illustration · Brussels

Françoiz Breut, born in 1969 in Cherbourg, France, is a singer who trained as an illustrator, and she still draws children's books. She began by singing with Dominique A and has made her own records since 1997; she lives in Brussels. Her voice is close and plain, like someone talking to you from the next seat, and her songs leave room around every word. Her newest album, Vif!, came out in 2024.

Sara Anjargolian

Documentary photography · Los Angeles and Yerevan

Sara Anjargolian is a documentary photographer and a lawyer who has worked between Los Angeles and Yerevan, in Armenia. Her project How We Live, from 2010, is a show, a book and a film about families living in poverty in Armenia. Her pictures stay with everyday things: laundry on a line, a child's toy in the dust. Close up, those small things carry the whole story of how people make a home with very little.

Pia Camil

Installations built with the public · Mexico City

Pia Camil, born in 1980 in Mexico City, makes art that strangers help to build. She works with second-hand clothes, T-shirts and jeans, and with whatever people are willing to give away. In A Pot for a Latch, at the New Museum in New York in 2016, visitors could take an object from her shelves only by leaving one of their own. Up close, every shirt and trinket carries somebody's story, and the trading is the work.

Klára Hosnedlová

Sculpture, installation and performance · Berlin

Klára Hosnedlová, born in 1990 in the Czech Republic, lives and works in Berlin. Her sculptures, rooms and performances borrow from the modern buildings of Czech and Eastern Europe, such as the Ještěd Tower, and from the craft that went into them. She works in hand-blown glass, stainless steel, terrazzo, leather and fine embroidery in cotton thread. Close up, every surface is made so carefully that it feels like touch you can see.

Theaster Gates

Painting, clay, sound and buildings · Chicago

Theaster Gates, born in 1973, works in Chicago, where he also rebuilds empty buildings into places for art, books and music. He makes paintings from salvaged roofing and tar, fused with a hand torch, and vessels from clay. In A Heavenly Chord, a Hammond organ and seven speakers bring the sound of the Black church into the gallery. Up close, the tar paintings keep every scar of the roofs they came from.

Xu Bing

Printmaking, books and installations · Beijing

Xu Bing, born in 1955 in Chongqing, China, grew up in Beijing and was sent to work in the countryside for two years near the end of the Cultural Revolution. For Book from the Sky he carved more than four thousand printing blocks of Chinese characters he invented, so the book looks perfectly readable and no one can read it. He has also made art from tobacco, building rubble, twigs and surveillance-camera film. In this ink drawing of a hillside, the banks of earth already run like lines of writing.
